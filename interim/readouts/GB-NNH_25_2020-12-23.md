### Southwick
* Main Street in Southwick was the subject of a flood investigation (report title indicates a flooding incident occurred there and was investigated by the Lead Local Flood Authority). (p3)
* Internal flooding of more than five buildings in close proximity during the single flood event; excess surface water followed local ground levels to low points in and around properties. (p7)
* Concentrated internal flooding in the Main Street area of Southwick, with floodwater entering multiple nearby properties. (p9) [PAG]
* A small area of Southwick (including one property) was included within a Flood Alert for the minor tributaries of the Nene. The Flood Alert was issued on 23 December 2020 and there was no local Flood Warning coverage or telemetry in the area. (p10)
* {1990s, 1968}: Southwick is traversed by Southwick Brook which has caused historic flooding in the village. Flooding during construction of the Main Street bridge in 1968 and further flooding in the 1990s led to installation of a bypass culvert, bifurcation works and a concrete flood wall upstream of the bridge. When drainage systems are exceeded upstream, overland surface water follows the topography into lower parts of the village and along Main Street; parts of Main Street were previously at a lower elevation and were inundated when the bridge capacity was exceeded. The brook flows to the rear of gardens on Main Street and a nearby ditch and outfall (20m north of Main Street) convey field/woodland flows into the brook; the ditch was noted as largely inaccessible and maintenance was due. (p14) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Southwick Brook overtopped approximately 100 m upstream of Main Street bridge and, together with surface water from a blocked/exceeded ditch northwest of the bridge and an exceeded piped minor watercourse from Millennium Cottage, flowed onto Main Street and into properties. Water followed ground levels through rear gardens back to the brook. Streets, gardens and properties were inundated; one property reported water entering from both Main Street (front) and the brook (rear). (p16) [PAG]
* Southwick Brook overtopped and conduits (Main Street bridge and buried pipes) were exceeded, producing overland flows into Main Street and around properties; floodwater followed local ground levels to low points near properties. (p18)

### Main Street Bridge
* The bridge did not have capacity for the volume of brook flow; this caused water to deepen on the upstream side and contributed to overtopping and subsequent internal flooding of properties on Main Street. (p15) [PAG]
* The bridge lacked capacity for the volume of water in the brook during the event, causing water to deepen upstream and contributing to overtopping upstream of the bridge. This led to flows onto Main Street and contributed to internal flooding of adjacent properties. The existing bypass pipe and bifurcation arrangement influenced how high flows passed the structure. (p16) [PAG]
* The bridge lacked capacity for the volume of water in the brook, causing water to deepen upstream. (p17)
* {general}: The conduit at Main Street Bridge did not have capacity for the extreme rainfall flows and overtopped, contributing to overland flow of water onto surrounding ground and towards properties. (p19)

### Southwick Brook

* {general}: The brook overtopped/flowed through the village and caused internal flooding of properties on Main Street. (p12)

Outfall pipes were covered by high water, restricting discharge and exacerbating surface water ponding and flows onto Main Street. Pipes beneath Main Street and at the ditch northwest of Main Street Bridge and at Millennium Cottage were exceeded, contributing to surface water flowing onto Main Street. (p18)

### Environment Agency
* {general}: Environment Agency (EA) survey returns confirmed that 11 properties in Southwick were internally flooded during the event. The EA-recognised main river (Southwick Brook) overtopped approximately 100m upstream of Main Street bridge, causing internal flooding to one property there; combined flows then contributed to internal flooding of a further 10 residential properties. The EA has previously installed a concrete flood wall upstream of the bridge (installed in 1990s) which is referenced in relation to the brook overtopping. (p15) [PAG]
* {general}: Undertook surveys to identify properties which had flooded and shared information with the local authority; exploring whether meaningful Flood and Severe Flood Warnings could be created for Southwick. (p18)
* {general}: The Environment Agency worked with the LLFA and Emergency Planning Team following the surface-water flooding in Southwick to support the community (including flood wardens if recruited), encourage third-party asset maintenance, and consider maintenance/repair of third-party assets where justified to safeguard the public interest. (p23)
* {general}: Quant: 11 properties; 1 property; 10 residential properties (p15) [PAG]

### Highway Authority
* {general}: Highway drainage on Main Street (road gullies and associated highway drains) was overwhelmed during the event, allowing surface water to flow onto Main Street and combining with brook flows to enter doorways and airbricks, causing internal flooding to residential properties (total 11 flooded). The highway structure (Main Street bridge) lacked capacity for the flow, contributing to upstream deepening and overtopping of the brook. (p15) [PAG]
* Surface water flooding affected the highway catchment; highway drainage assets were identified as at risk/with potentially insufficient capacity, creating flood risk to properties and requiring cleansing, inspection and potential improvement works and consideration of overland flow routes. (p24)
* {general}: Quant: 11 properties (p15) [PAG]

### Northamptonshire Fire and Rescue Service

### EA Partnerships

### North Northamptonshire Highways
* Surface water flooding affected the catchment and prompted the North Northamptonshire highways team to assess asset capacity, consider more frequent inspection/cleansing and identify improvement works or overland flow routes to reduce flood risk to properties. (p24)

### North Northamptonshire Council
* {general}: As the Lead Local Flood Authority (LLFA) for the affected properties, NNC carried out Section 19 flood investigation duties for the flood incident, will maintain a register of assets that significantly affect flooding (surface runoff, groundwater or ordinary watercourses), and record flooding hotspots. NNC will seek support from other risk management authorities, communities and homeowners to report incidents and record assets. (p25)
