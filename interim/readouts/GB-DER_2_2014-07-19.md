### Derby
* Areas within Derby (notably Oakwood/Bishops Drive) experienced intense short-duration rainfall causing surface water flooding that led to internal property flooding and widespread highway and garden flooding. (p3)
* The urban surface water sewer network in the Oakwood area of Derby discharges to Oakwood Brook. During the intense rainfall of 19th July 2014 the network capacity would be exceeded and large volumes of runoff from paved urban areas were focused rapidly toward the sewer outfall and the brook, increasing downstream surface water flood risk and concentrating flow toward the base of the local valleys. (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* Part of the city (Oakwood) was impacted by a short-duration, high-intensity storm that produced rapid surface water runoff and caused the public surface water sewer serving a large urban catchment to be overwhelmed. This led to internal flooding of residential properties in Oakwood and widespread external flooding of gardens and highways in the affected neighbourhoods. (p12) [PAG, IMG]
* Intense, short-duration rainfall overwhelmed drainage and produced rapid runoff and overland flow toward Wood Brook and Oakwood Brook, causing significant surface water flooding of properties and highways across large areas of the east of Derby (including Oakwood). A cluster of streets between Bishops Drive and Wood Brook was particularly affected. (p17) [PAG]
* Surface water flooding occurred across many areas when highway drainage capacity was exceeded by a combination of highway and private surface water during the intense, isolated storm on the afternoon of 19th July 2014 (Oakwood was specifically affected). (p18)

### Derby City Council
* Identified and reported the significant cluster of internal and external property flooding and highway flooding in the Bishops Drive area as part of the formal investigation. (p5) [PAG]
* As Lead Local Flood Authority and Highways Authority, the Council coordinated Section 19 investigations into the flooding and acknowledged that highway drainage capacity was exceeded during the storm, resulting in surface water flooding of adopted highways and contributing to property flooding. (p17)
* As the local Highways Authority, the Council's highway drainage systems were exceeded on 19 July 2014 when combined highway and private surface water caused surface water flooding in many areas. The Council assessed that the maintenance condition was unlikely to have significantly affected the flooding and is undertaking feasibility studies for drainage improvements. (p18)
* Took reports of flooding from residents of Morefern Drive, Santolina Drive, Columbine Close, Bryony Close, Saffron Drive and Bishops Drive, carried out site visits shortly after the event and produced the investigation report as part of its FWMA duties; provided advice to residents on improving personal flood resilience. (p19)

### Oakwood Brook
* The public surface water sewer serving the investigation area discharges to Oakwood Brook; during the intense rainfall on 19th July 2014 a large quantity of surface water from the extensive urban catchment was focussed towards the sewer outfall into Oakwood Brook, contributing to the cluster of internal and external property flooding and highway flooding reported in the Oakwood area. (p6) [PAG]
* Public surface water sewer serving the investigation area discharges to Oakwood Brook; during the intense rainfall event (19 July 2014) a large quantity of urban runoff would have been routed rapidly to the sewer outfall and focussed at Oakwood Brook, increasing risk of exceedance at the outfall and surface water accumulation at the bottom of the local valley. (p7) [PAG, IMG]
* {general}: Oakwood Brook is an ordinary watercourse that forms the base of a local valley; during extreme rainfall and when the surface water sewer capacity is exceeded, overland flows and accumulated surface water are routed toward and into the Oakwood Brook valley. Areas adjacent to the brook are identified as being susceptible to inclusion in flow paths or zones of surface water accumulation during storm events. (p8) [PAG, IMG]
* Overland flow from overwhelmed drainage and green spaces was routed towards Oakwood Brook, causing it to contribute to significant flooding of nearby properties and highways. (p17)

### Highways Authority
* Severe highway flooding and external (highway and garden) flooding occurred on multiple streets in the Bishops Drive area (Bishops Drive, Saffron Drive, Columbine Close, Santolina Drive, Morefern Drive and Bryony Close). Bishops Drive experienced further extensive external (highway and garden) flooding and possible additional internal property flooding. (p3)
* Highway flooding occurred in the Bishops Drive area as part of the cluster of flooding in Oakwood; highway gullies and adopted highways drained to the surface water sewer system which was overwhelmed during the event. (p6)
* The capacity of highway drainage was exceeded by the intensity of the rainfall combined with private surface water, resulting in surface water flooding of highways across many areas; highway drainage could not contain overland flows from private areas and open spaces during the event. (p17)

### Wood Brook
* The Wood Brook had limited conveyance capacity such that excess surface water spilled where it could not enter the channel; anecdotal reports indicate detritus/illegal dumping in the watercourse may have reduced capacity and contributed to local flooding of adjacent gardens and properties. (p14)
* Overland flow and rapid runoff from intense, short‑duration rainfall routed towards Wood Brook, causing the brook to contribute to significant flooding of adjacent properties and highways, especially in the cluster between Bishops Drive and the brook. (p17)

### Chandlers Ford Recreation Ground
* Excess surface water (including runoff and sewer overflow) flowed over the recreation ground; flattened vegetation documents overland flow across the ground toward Morefern Drive, and water originating on the ground travelled to property boundaries. (p13)
