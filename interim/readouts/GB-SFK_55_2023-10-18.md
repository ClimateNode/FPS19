

# Wetheringsett-Cum-Brockford flooding 18 Oct 2023 - 21 Oct 2023

## Place names with coordinates

### Wetheringsett
* Part of the Wetheringsett-cum-Brockford parish which was severely impacted by flooding between 18–21 Oct 2023; natural and drainage flow routes were overwhelmed, overland flow convergence led to internal flooding of properties and disruption to local infrastructure and services. (p4)
* Wetheringsett was one of the main affected areas during Storm Babet, experiencing significant internal flooding to property and infrastructure from a combination of surface water runoff, overtopping watercourses and overwhelmed drainage/sewage systems. (p8) [IMG, PAG]
* Significant internal flooding to properties and village infrastructure in Wetheringsett. The village was identified as one of the main affected areas; floodwater originated from surface runoff, overtopping watercourses and overwhelmed drainage/sewer systems. Localised flooding was also reported in nearby streets (Station Road, Park Green and Weatherup Street). (p9) [PAG, IMG]
* Central Wetheringsett is mapped as having significant fluvial flood risk from local watercourses (and associated pluvial flow paths). The settlement is identified as a low-lying convergence point for flows and is expected to be a primary location to experience flooding during high rainfall. Wetheringsett lies toward the upper extent of the Flood Alert area (Flood Alert issued 20 October 2023) for the River Waveney / River Dove. (p13) [PAG, IMG]
* Significant flooding affected the parish area, inundating residential properties, roads and local infrastructure in Wetheringsett due to saturated catchment conditions and heavy rainfall. (p15) [PAG, IMG]
* The local area experienced significant surface water runoff and river overtopping causing internal flooding of properties; private sewerage works in the area continued to be affected in months following the event by high water levels, and possible infilling/lack of maintenance of watercourses prevented water egress in places. (p22) [IMG]
* During the event the village experienced both river (watercourse) flooding and significant surface-water/flash flows. At least 10 properties suffered internal flooding with internal depths of up to 500 mm. The watercourse entering the village from the east was overwhelmed and overtopped early, with water forcing its way around or over constrictions (including a weir, two small brick-arch bridges, a culvert under the old mill, and an engineered ~90° turn), adding floodwater onto the highway and into nearby properties. The central area west of the church (a topographic low) was the most impacted. High-gradient southern and western approach roads produced torrents of surface water across the highway that reached the central low spot. The culvert at the primary school overtopped once its capacity was reached. Community reports indicate some sections of the watercourse north of the village required maintenance and may have reduced channel capacity during the event. (p23)
* The central village junction experienced torrents of surface water from the south and west that travelled at high speed across the highway and pooled in the village low spot. An eastern watercourse overtopped at a culvert, contributing flood flows into the highway and surrounding properties. (p24) [IMG]
* Surface water from a large catchment overwhelmed the village watercourse and highway drainage, causing rapid-onset flooding due to steep topography; the village pumping station was submerged, the foul sewer system was overwhelmed and overflowed into gardens and highways, and properties experienced internal flooding. (p25)
* Natural areas and vegetation were inundated; muddy floodwater covered the ground and rose partway up tree trunks. Flooding was reported close to the church in Wetheringsett. (p28)
* An area in east Wetheringsett (near the village church/Church Street) experienced surface water inundation covering ground, vegetation and surrounding buildings; community members required a small boat to move through the flooded area, indicating significant local disruption to movement and access. (p29) [IMG, PAG]
* Domestic gardens experienced sewage escape following the storm; the village also had observed watercourse blockages and drainage defects (e.g. Church Street, Brockford Street) contributing to local flooding and drainage issues. (p34) [TAB, PAG]
* Investigation and planned works to improve the watercourse network through Wetheringsett (including investigating capacity at engineered turns) and feasibility work for Natural Flood Management features to attenuate water in the upper catchments. (p35)
* Surface-water flows affected the village, prompting proposed works (watercourse improvements and NFM in upper catchments) to improve the village's flood resilience. (p36)
* Quant: over 20 properties (across Wetheringsett-cum-Brockford) (p4)
* Quant: over twenty properties (parish-wide) (p9) [PAG, IMG]

### Suffolk
* Significant disruption to communities across the county between 18–21 Oct 2023 due to Storm Babet, with widespread fluvial and surface water flooding impacting infrastructure and services. (p4) [PAG]
* During 18–21 October 2023 Suffolk experienced the heaviest rainfall across East Anglia leading to saturated catchments, rapid river rises and significant flooding of roads and properties. The flooding caused substantial disruption to transport (road and rail) and prompted a major incident declaration by the Suffolk Resilience Forum on 20 October. (p6) [PAG]
* Parts of the Wetheringsett-cum-Brockford parish in Suffolk (notably central Wetheringsett and Brockford Street) were identified as low-lying convergence points for flow paths and were subject to fluvial and pluvial flood risk during the Storm Babet period. A Flood Alert covering Brockford and Wetheringsett was issued on 20 October 2023; catchment runoff and low-permeability soils contributed to rapid surface water flows into these low spots. (p13) [IMG]

### River Dove
* The river overtopped and breached its banks at several points east and west of the A140, causing flooding of adjacent land, inundation of nearby properties and contributing to flooding of transport routes (A140 and Brockford Road). The overtopping contributed to internal flooding of properties in the Brockford Street / Griffin Lane area and to flows travelling southeast to rejoin the river. (p17) [PAG, IMG]
* During 18 Oct 2023 - 21 Oct 2023 high flows in the River Dove exceeded the capacity of the small single-span masonry arch bridge carrying the river under the A140. Water flowed out of the channel upstream and around the bridge abutments, overtopping and flooding onto the A140 carriageway and onto surrounding properties on the west side of the A140 in north Brockford Street. (p19) [IMG, PAG]
* Flooding associated with the River Dove inundated grazing land adjacent to the channel (sites between Buces Hill and the caravan park at Brockford Road). Works on the river bank and material laid in the river bed for scour protection were noted close to a bridge; residents reported rocks from gabion baskets reducing capacity under/near the bridge, though highway inspections judged the gabions and the laid material to be for scour protection and unlikely to have a significant impact on conveyance. The floodplain continued to operate during the event (sites flooded during Storm Babet). (p20)
* Quant: 13 properties (p17) [PAG, IMG]

### A140
* The A140 was flooded and closed for several hours after the River Dove breached banks in multiple locations and adjacent watercourses/drainage were overwhelmed, causing floodwater to affect the highway. (p16)
* The A140 was closed for several hours after the River Dove breached its banks and adjacent watercourses were overwhelmed; closure and flooding caused significant traffic disruption and prevented emergency services from passing for several hours. (p17) [IMG, PAG]
* Floodwater was forced out of the River Dove channel by a limited-capacity bridge beneath the A140; water flowed out upstream around the bridge abutments and flooded the A140 carriageway and surrounding properties on the west side of the A140. (p18) [PAG]
* Floodwater from the River Dove overtopped/was restricted at the small masonry arch bridge and flowed out of the channel, inundating the A140 and flooding surrounding properties on the west side of the road. (p19) [IMG]
* The A140 was closed and rendered impassable for a period due to flooding from overwhelmed local watercourses and surface water runoff. (p22)

### Anglian Water
* The foul sewer system was overwhelmed during the event (and on multiple subsequent occasions). The piped system flowing to the village pumping station was a considerable depth underwater during the storm, and residents reported overflows of raw sewage from the piped network into gardens and highways. Anglian Water have completed remedial works and plan to implement additional monitoring to provide early warning of issues. (p25)
* Sewage escape into gardens of homes was reported following Storm Babet; Anglian Water carried out works to the rising main in response. (p33) [TAB]
* Carried out works on the rising main in the village and installed an early warning system after sewage escapes into gardens were reported. (p34) [PAG]

### Suffolk County Council
* SCC as LLFA investigated possible unconsented infilling of a watercourse and checked local watercourses for maintenance needs; a site visit was carried out and riparian ownership letters were sent to landowners as part of the post‑flood response. (p33) [TAB]
* As the LLFA, monitored progress on recommended mitigation actions, recommended and programmed drainage repairs (e.g. Church Street) and identified longer‑term measures to reduce flood risk in the parish. (p34)

### Environment Agency
* River level gauging stations recorded many flows close to or exceeding their highest on record and river systems rose rapidly across whole catchments during Storm Babet, contributing to flooding of roads and properties across Suffolk. (p6)
* Issued a Flood Alert for the catchment area ('The River Waveney from Diss and the River Dove to Ellingham, including Bungay') on 20 Oct 2023 at 11:44; the alert (advising flooding possible) was frequently updated and remained in force until removal on 17 Nov 2023 at 11:20. The text notes there was no Flood Warning in force for the upper catchment and that Station Road and Wetherup Street were not covered by the alert. (p12) [PAG]

### SCC Highway
* Required to respond to blockages of drainage features on Church Street and carry out repairs/clearance alongside riparian owners. (p35)

### Bridge
* A single-span masonry arch bridge (approx. 2.7 m wide) carrying the River Dove under the A140 acted as a flow restriction during the event. Community reports indicate the bridge capacity was insufficient for the volume of water, causing a significant portion of flow to leave the channel upstream and by the bridge abutments, leading to flooding of the road and nearby properties. The bridge is a relatively low point and susceptible to breaches if high volumes or blockages impede flow. (p19) [IMG]

### Buces Hill
* Grazing land adjacent to the River Dove between Buces Hill and the caravan park was subject to development works (plots, caravans, raised banks) and those sites flooded in Storm Babet; the floodplain continued to function during the event. (p20)
