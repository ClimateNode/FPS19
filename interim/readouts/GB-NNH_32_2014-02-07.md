### Gainsborough Road, Corby
* Overflow from a part-culverted Ordinary Watercourse led to banks overtopping and the public highway being affected; the road (a critical arterial route for Corby) was flooded and private property and commercial premises were also affected by the overflow. (p6) [PAG]

Debris build-up at culvert grilles exacerbated the flooding. The road is identified as a critical arterial route for the town, so the flooding had significant transport impact. (p7) [PAG]
* An ordinary watercourse overtopped and caused flooding across Gainsborough Road. The depth and velocity of flow affected the community and placed residential properties at high risk of internal flooding; the road (a critical arterial route) was affected. (p9) [PAG]
* Carriageway and footways became impassable to pedestrians and vehicles; adjacent public highway and private gardens were flooded when the local watercourse became full to capacity and overtopped its banks. (p12) [PAG]
* Public highway and adjacent private gardens flooded; flood water reached approximately three metres away from residential thresholds and an estimated depth at the area outside residences exceeded 500 mm. Bow waves from vehicles driving through the flood presented a danger of internal flooding to dwellings. (p13)

### Corby
* Corby is the town in which Gainsborough Road was the subject of a Northamptonshire County Council flood investigation (report title references Gainsborough Road, Corby). (p2)
* High water levels and strong flows in a part-culverted Ordinary Watercourse caused banks to overtop, resulting in flooding that affected public highways (notably Gainsborough Road and Oakley Road), private properties and commercial premises in the town. (p6) [PAG]
* Flooding of a part-culverted ordinary watercourse led to overtopping and overflow that affected key roads (Gainsborough Road and Oakley Road) in the town, and caused property and commercial premises to be affected. The flooding disrupted critical arterial routes for the town; an increased frequency of flooding is also noted. (p7) [PAG]
* Parts of Corby (notably Gainsborough Road and Oakley Road) experienced flooding on 7th February 2014 due to overtopping of ordinary watercourses, placing residential properties at high risk of internal flooding and affecting critical arterial routes. (p9) [PAG]
* Flooding affected open space and highways. (p11)
* Floodwater on Oakley Road reached a depth in excess of 200 mm, causing road flooding in Corby. (p14) [PAG]
* Heavy rainfall caused excessive flows in ordinary watercourses which raised water levels and overtopped banks, affecting adjoining property across Corby. Local drainage systems were unable to discharge to the watercourse, exacerbating flooding. Culverted sections of the watercourse were reported as possibly partially blocked or damaged, and debris grilles at culvert entrances became easily blocked, contributing to reduced conveyance and overtopping. (p18)
* Localised surface-water and sewer-related flooding associated with blocked culvert grilles and runoff/groundwater emergence. Culvert protection grilles required inspection and clearing during heavy rainfall and were a factor in local drainage performance; the event prompted a borough-wide flood risk management study. (p21)
* {21 Nov 2012, 24 Dec 2013}: Sections of Gainsborough Road in Corby were inundated, with floodwater covering roads and footpaths, reaching the fronts of terraced houses and disrupting normal road use and resident access. (p53) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Quant: depth exceeded 200 mm on Oakley Road (p14) [PAG]

### Oakley Road, Corby
* Affected by overflow from the Ordinary Watercourse when its banks overtopped; the public highway (an arterial route) was flooded and private property and commercial premises were impacted by the overflow. (p6)
* Flooding from the ordinary watercourses (which combine near Oakley Road) caused depth and velocity of flow affecting Oakley Road; residential properties were at high risk of internal flooding and the road (a critical arterial route) was impacted. (p9) [PAG]
* Carriageway and footways were impassable; surface water flowed out of the watercourse over the supermarket car park at the Gainsborough/Oakley junction at high velocity and into Oakley Road; drainage systems were unable to discharge and backed up, causing flooding at the supermarket area. (p12)
* An open section of watercourse immediately west of the supermarket junction overtopped, producing overland surface water flow that collected on Oakley Road; floodwater also collected in the supermarket car park and delivery bay. Flooding on Oakley Road overspilled to adjacent open space. (p13)
* The entrance to the supermarket car park on Oakley Road experienced flooding where surface water overland flow met standing flood water, creating waves and standing water at the entrance. (p65) [IMG, PAG]
* Entrance to supermarket car park and adjacent carriageway were heavily flooded, rendering vehicle and pedestrian access difficult and impassable; standing flood water covered the full width of the road at the car park entrance and interacted with moving overland flow. (p66) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface water overland flow met standing floodwater at the entrance to the supermarket car park on Oakley Road; flooding reached the car park entrance and extended along the road toward the south, impeding vehicular and pedestrian access to the supermarket entrance. (p67) [IMG, PAG]
* Standing floodwater covered the carriageway and adjacent areas at the Oakley Road / Gainsborough Road junction and extended into the supermarket car park and delivery area; water reached up to and over kerbs and the bases of traffic signs, rendering the road impassable to vehicles and pedestrians. (p68) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: depth exceeded 200 mm (p13)

### Lowry Close, Corby
* The single vehicular access point became blocked by flood water, preventing entry/exit; flooding occurred adjacent to the area due to drainage systems backing up into the locality. (p12)
* Flood water covered the front garden of a residential property; debris accumulated on the garden (possibly from foul water sources). (p13)
* Floodwater and water emerging from drainage (manhole/gullies) affected the area around Lowry Close at its junction with Gainsborough Road, with water flowing onto pavements and the carriageway. (p60) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface water pooling at the junction with Gainsborough Road with water bubbling up from a manhole cover and road gullies; nearby open watercourse north of Lowry Close was at high, muddy levels and at or near capacity, increasing the risk of overflow onto adjacent land. (p61) [IMG, PAG]
* Floodwater at the junction extended into Lowry Close; manhole bubbling and gully discharge indicate sewer surcharge and surface water affecting the street. (p62) [IMG, PAG]
* The junction of Lowry Close with Gainsborough Road was submerged, making the junction impassable and affecting access for pedestrians and vehicles. (p63) [IMG, PAG]

### Blake Road, Corby
* Area adjacent to Blake Road experienced flooding when drainage systems backed up because they could not discharge to the high watercourse levels, resulting in inundation of highway and nearby private land. (p12)
* A culverted watercourse running from Blake Road to the supermarket was implicated in the flooding; a partial CCTV survey has confirmed its route and condition over much of its length and a full survey and likely repair/replacement/clearance works are recommended to address its role in causing/propagating flooding to the highway and nearby properties. (p20)
* Entrance to the culvert at Blake Road was submerged, indicating local inundation affecting drainage infrastructure and contributing to road/area inundation. (p64) [IMG, PAG]
* Entrance to the culvert at Blake Road was submerged; floodwater overflowed onto adjacent pavement and field, submerging fences and vegetation. (p65) [IMG, PAG]
