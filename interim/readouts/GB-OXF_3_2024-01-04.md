## Place names with coordinates

### Hakka's Brook
* Hakka's Brook flooded in several locations and was identified as the cause of flooding in the area, contributing to internal flooding of properties in East Hagbourne. (p2) [TAB]
* The Hakka's Brook box culvert beneath Blewbury Road was noted as containing utilities; the report recommends considering relocating those utilities because they constrain the culvert and its ability to convey flow. (p3)
* Hakka's Brook (the main river) was inspected along its length downstream of Blewbury Road and landowners were to be contacted about riparian responsibilities as a consequence of river flooding associated with the 04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024 event. (p4)
* Hakka's Brook (ordinary watercourse through East Hagbourne) overflowed/took flow and transfers flow via a fixed side weir into a box culvert and an adjacent ordinary watercourse, contributing to the local flooding. (p7) [PAG]
* The Hakka's Brook flooded in multiple locations and breached its banks, contributing to nearby internal property flooding and highway inundation; some residents reported property ingress occurring before the stream breached, indicating high groundwater also played a role. (p11)
* Residents reported foul drainage being affected during the event, with flooding causing inability to flush toilets (surcharging/backflow of foul drains). (p12)
* River levels in the Hakka's Brook rose following heavy rainfall, contributing to flooding that impacted roads and properties in East Hagbourne. Channel capacity was reduced by high silt levels in the relief channel, a partially blocked culvert and highway drainage link, and vegetation downstream of Blewbury Road; utilities within the box culvert beneath Blewbury Road also reduced capacity during peak flows. * A side weir and overflow box culvert on Hakka's Brook provided an overflow route; the overflow structure and highway pipe link to a relief channel were observed, indicating flow routing during the flood event. (p16)

### Blewbury Road, East Hagbourne
* Blewbury Road is the location of the Hakka's Brook box culvert; the report recommends relocating utilities in the culvert and ensuring highway gullies and pipe links are clear so the road drainage can flow and reduce flood risk. (p3)
* Area downstream of Blewbury Road was inspected in relation to Hakka's Brook overflowing; recommendations include clearing highway gullies and pipe links to restore flow after flooding on 04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024. (p4)
* Sub-optimal flow conditions in channels downstream of Blewbury Road and inability for water to drain into the relief channel impeded highway drainage, contributing to flooding of the highway. (p11)
* The culvert beneath Blewbury Road and its outfall into the Hakka's Brook relief channel showed reduced capacity (silt, partial blockages and vegetation downstream) and contained utilities crossing the culvert; these issues reduced conveyance during peak flows and contributed to flooding impacts on the highway and nearby properties. (p15) [PAG]
* Vegetation and siltation in Hakka's Brook immediately downstream of the culvert under Blewbury Road contributed to flooding during 04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024; Environment Agency cleared vegetation from the brook downstream of the culvert to restore flow. (p18)
* Flooding affected Blewbury Road on 04–05 Jan 2024 due to an inability for water to drain away, contributing to local inundation. (p19)
* Blewbury Road was identified as the location of a box culvert carrying Hakka's Brook and utilities; relocation of utilities and culvert-related works were recommended because culvert/flow issues contributed to flooding at this location. (p25) [TAB]

### Mill Brook

During the East Hagbourne flood event this connection would have conveyed overflow downstream into the Mill Brook, increasing flows and potentially affecting downstream channel capacity. (p7)


## Topics without coordinates

### Highways
* Flooding occurred in the highway and was considered a contributing factor to the wider flooding in East Hagbourne. (p2) [TAB]
* Highway drainage links to the Hakka's Brook relief channel appeared partially blocked and obstructions within the highway drainage system likely reduced capacity and performance, contributing to road flooding in East Hagbourne; recommendation to survey highway drainage to assess condition and restore capacity. (p15)
* Blocked or deficient highway gullies and pipe links contributed to drainage failure at Blewbury Road; utilities running through the Hakka's Brook box culvert beneath Blewbury Road were identified as a concern and recommended for relocation to reduce flood risk. (p25) [TAB]

### Oxfordshire County Council
* OCC Flood Risk Management Team and OCC Highways are identified as consulting stakeholders and tasked with actions to manage flows: clear a relief channel, remove a disused bridge over the relief channel, consider relocating utilities through the Hakka's Brook box culvert beneath Blewbury Road, and ensure highway gullies and pipe links are clear and able to flow. (p3) [PAG]
* Highways surveyed and pressure‑jetted the highway drainage system to remove obstructions and are investigating options to improve highway drainage performance following flooding on 04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024; the County Council LLFA also provided funding to the Parish Council for desilting/removal works. (p18)
* Oxfordshire County Council Highways surveyed and pressure-jetted the highway drainage system in response to the flooding of 04–05 Jan 2024 to remove obstructions, and is investigating options to improve highway drainage performance; OCC also carried out a Section 19 investigation. (p19) [PAG]

### Culvert
* Utilities crossing the box culvert beneath Blewbury Road reduce conveyance capacity during peak flows and increase the risk of blockage, contributing to local flooding risk; recommendation made to contact utility undertakers to alter crossings to reduce blockage and increase flow capacity. (p16)

### Disused Bridge
* Relief channel beneath the disused bridge contained standing/muddy water and was partially obstructed by dense vegetation/overgrowth, indicating localised ponding and impaired conveyance during the flood event. (p17)
