## Place names with coordinates

### Ginge Brook
* Surcharge flows from Ginge Brook entered Moor Ditch over farmland, contributing to hydraulic overload of Moor Ditch and causing flood overspill onto farmland toward Milton and the bridleway to Steventon and contributing to property flooding. (p2) [TAB]
* Flood flows on the left bank downstream of the A34 are a concern; the report recommends modelling an alternative exceedance flow route for flood flows on the left bank of Ginge Brook and undertaking desilting at a recommended location to reduce flood risk. (p3)
* The downstream section overtopped its banks, with a greater volume discharging out of the right bank into farmland and towards Moor Ditch and Milton; site inspection in February found flood levels on the bridleway and adjacent fields up to 30 cm. (p9)
* Overbank flows from Ginge Brook occurred around its crossing under the A34, with lower channel capacity in a ~350 m section causing continuous out-of-channel flooding onto the right-bank farmland and onto the bridleway; water flowed towards Moor Ditch and Milton. (p10) [PAG]
* Lower channel capacity in a 350m section around the A34 crossing caused Ginge Brook to flood out of its right bank onto adjacent farmland; out‑of‑channel flows were present continuously during winter and at the peak of the January event these flows crossed under the A34 (via a culvert and an underpass), contributing to flooding of farmland and sending flows down towards Steventon and the Bridleway. (p11) [IMG, PAG]
* Right-bank out-of-channel flow from Ginge Brook overtopped the raised earth bank and ran overland southeast across fields and infrastructure, with flow running back towards Steventon down the bridleway and into ordinary watercourses. A low-gradient, heavily silted reach of approximately 350m either side of the A34 has reduced channel capacity and contributed to recurring flooding through winter 2023/24, including the January event. (p12) [IMG, PAG]
* Ginge Brook overspilled during the January/February flooding, discharging onto adjacent farmland and onto the bridleway between Milton and Steventon. (p15)
* Ginge Brook contributed to overspill flooding on both left and right banks between Steventon and Milton. Downstream of the Moor Ditch offtake the channel runs faster and was observed to be within-bank in places, while upstream of Drayton Park Golf Course a double-stepped weir (formerly a mill) and a downstream brick-arch culvert were present during high flows; the culvert was observed at full capacity on site (raising collapse risk that could exacerbate flooding). (p17) [IMG]
* Overbanking/elevated flows in Ginge Brook contributed to flooding of adjacent land during the January event and added to elevated flows in the Moor Ditch catchment, driving flooding impacts downstream (including the golf course and Moor Ditch). (p19)
* Overtopped its right bank between Steventon and Milton around the A34, producing out-of-channel flows across farmland and entering Moor Ditch via a culverted watercourse under the A34 and its underpass. These out-of-channel flows contributed to hydraulic overload and flooding at multiple locations in Milton and nearby communities. (p21)
* Overbank flooding/overspill from Ginge Brook onto adjacent farmland towards Milton and onto a bridleway to Steventon; desilting was recommended to reduce this overspill. (p27) [TAB]
* Interactions with Moor Ditch (obstructions, overspill locations and altered flow routes) contributed to overspill and flooding in the corridor between Steventon and Milton; recommended further investigation of obstructions, overspill locations and bank levels. (p30)

### Moor Ditch
* Hydraulic overload within Moor Ditch, combined with surcharge from Ginge Brook, caused flooding of nearby private properties and raised river levels that prevented surface water drainage from discharging to the river, contributing to flooding within Milton Park. (p2) [TAB]
* {01 Jul 2007 - 31 Jul 2007, 01 Jan 2014 - 31 Jan 2014, 01 Apr 2023 - 30 Apr 2023}: Recorded internal flooding occurred around Moor Ditch on multiple previous occasions (internal property flooding documented). (p8) [MUL]
* Received elevated flows routed from Ginge Brook toward Milton during the event; implicated in conveying floodwater through the Milton area (specific overtopping or inundation of the channel not separately quantified in the text). (p9)
* Received overland flows and flows via a culvert from Ginge Brook during the flood event; flows were entering Moor Ditch approx. 150 m south of the offtake and Moor Ditch levels through Milton were observed at the soffits of highway culverts. (p10) [PAG]
* {04 Jan 2024, 01 Feb 2024 - 29 Feb 2024}: The Moor Ditch offtake was 100PCSYM blocked by silt build-up during the January and February flooding, preventing flow through the 100 mm diameter culvert; as a result flows entered Moor Ditch via farmland and an alternate culvert under the A34 ~100 m to the south. Clearance in April restored base flow to the upstream end of Moor Ditch and would have marginally reduced levels in Ginge Brook prior to the flood peaks. (p15) [PAG, MUL]
* The Moor Ditch offtake was 100PCSYM blocked by silt at the time of the January and February flooding, preventing baseflow through the 100 mm culvert. Flows nevertheless entered Moor Ditch via farmland and a culvert under the A34 ~100 m to the south; clearance of the offtake restored visible flow in the channel and would have marginally reduced upstream levels in Ginge Brook prior to Ginge Brook overspilling into adjacent farmland and the bridleway between Milton and Steventon. (p16) [IMG, PAG]
* Silt build-up adjacent to the Moor Ditch offtake reduced conveyance at the offtake inlet; after silt clearance Moor Ditch was observed flowing. Moor Ditch interaction with Ginge Brook is linked to current overspill locations on both banks. (p17) [PAG]
* {04 Jan 2024, 01 Feb 2024 - 29 Feb 2024}: Elevated water levels in Moor Ditch during winter and the January event caused properties to flood in Milton (High Street and Pembroke Lane). High river levels also prevented discharge of site surface water drainage in Milton Park, resulting in internal flooding at multiple business units. (p19) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* High river levels in Moor Ditch prevented the discharge of surface water drainage systems that outfall to the river, causing surface water to back up and leading to internal flooding of business units within the adjoining business park. (p20) [PAG]
* Severe flooding along the Moor Ditch reached businesses either side of the channel at Harriers Park Industrial Estate; flooding also occurred at multiple locations through Milton where the culverted channel was overwhelmed. (p21) [PAG]
* The Moor Ditch offtake is the downstream connection point referenced in relation to overspill from Ginge Brook; desilting upstream of this offtake was recommended to reduce flood overspill onto nearby farmland and paths. (p27) [TAB]
* Described as interacting with Ginge Brook with obstructions and overspill locations that contributed to flooding between Steventon and Milton; recommended for further investigation of flow routes and bank levels. (p30)
* Quant: 10 private residential properties; 3 business units (p2) [TAB]
* Quant: approximately 150m (location of inflow south of offtake); up to 30cm deep (reported flow through an underpass during the flood event) (p10) [PAG]
* {04 Jan 2024, 01 Feb 2024 - 29 Feb 2024}: Quant: 100PCSYM blocked; 100 mm diameter culvert (p15) [PAG, MUL]
* Quant: 100PCSYM blocked (p16) [IMG, PAG]

### Milton
* Flooding of ten private residential properties in the village caused by hydraulic overload within the Environment Agency designated main river (Moor Ditch), combined with surcharge from Ginge Brook entering Moor Ditch. (p2) [TAB, PAG]
* {01 Jan 2024 - 31 Jan 2024}: Properties in Milton were flooded in January 2024. Some flooded properties were located outside Environment Agency Flood Zone 3 (i.e. lie within Flood Zone 1). Surface water flood maps identify medium-risk areas that include properties on High Street and Pembroke Lane. Northern and western parts of the site boundary (near Mill Brook and the main river) are within Flood Zones 2 and 3 and are at higher flood risk. (p6) [IMG]
* Parts of the village experienced flooding on 4 January 2024. Some properties that flooded were outside Environment Agency Flood Zone 3 (i.e. lie in Flood Zone 1) and for some it was their first recorded flood in over 40 years. (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* Properties in the village experienced flooding during the 4 January 2024 event; for some properties this was the first flooding in over 40 years and some properties inside the Environment Agency designated Flood Zone 1 recorded flooding for the first time. (p8)
* Downstream flows from Ginge Brook discharged toward Moor Ditch and Milton; inspections around the A34 and downstream section showed floodwater affecting adjacent fields and bridleway, indicating flooding reached areas affecting Milton. (p9)
* Flood flows from Ginge Brook and associated flows via Moor Ditch affected areas around Milton; residents reported flooding and a meeting with flooded residents was held. Moor Ditch levels through Milton were observed at the soffits of highway culverts, indicating high river/water levels affecting the settlement. (p10) [PAG]
* The bridleway between Milton and Steventon and nearby farmland around the A34 were flooded from out-of-channel flows from Ginge Brook during the January event, affecting access and adjacent land near Milton. (p11) [PAG]
* Milton is identified as being affected by overspill/flooding from Ginge Brook and Moor Ditch; the report discusses flood risk consequences to Milton and potential alternative flow routes to reduce flooding on the left bank of Ginge Brook downstream of the A34. (p17)
* {04 Jan 2024, 01 Feb 2024 - 29 Feb 2024}: Properties in the village flooded during winter events: specifically properties on High Street and Pembroke Lane were flooded in the January event and some also in the February event. Elevated river levels in Moor Ditch affected local drainage and business units in Milton Park. (p19) [MUL]
* Backing up of site surface water drainage (because outfall to Moor Ditch could not discharge due to high river levels) led to internal flooding at multiple units within the business park. (p20) [PAG]
* Hydraulic overload within the Moor Ditch catchment caused flooding at multiple locations on the culverted channel through Milton. Surface water drainage in Milton Park could not discharge to Moor Ditch because of elevated river levels, leading to flooding there. (p21) [PAG]
* Farmland towards Milton experienced flood overspill originating from Ginge Brook. (p27) [TAB]
* Flooding in Milton was linked to overspill and constrained flow from nearby main rivers (Ginge Brook and Moor Ditch). The weir at Milton Mill was identified as a feature affecting flows. Highway drainage capacity through Milton was also noted as contributing to runoff and flood risk to properties. (p29) [PAG]
* Subject to overspill and flooding related to the interface of Ginge Brook and Moor Ditch; recommended actions include modelling, maintenance of main river assets, and creating upstream flood storage/increasing floodplain capacity. (p30)
* Quant: 10 properties (p2) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 30 cm flood depth on bridleway (p9)

### Steventon
* Residents in the village were reported as flooded; a meeting with flooded residents and a full river walkover (upstream of Steventon along Ginge Brook) was held on 22 February following the flood event. (p9)
* Lower channel capacity in a ~350 m section around Ginge Brook's crossing under the A34 caused out-of-bank flows from the right bank onto adjacent farmland and onto the bridleway between Milton and Steventon. The bridleway and farmland were inundated, restricting access. (p10) [PAG]
* Left-bank flooding from Ginge Brook during the January event contributed to flows down the Bridleway and into ordinary watercourses, which in turn caused flooding to properties in Steventon. (p11) [PAG]
* Properties in Steventon were flooded when water ran out of Ginge Brook's left bank and flowed back towards the village along the bridleway and into ordinary watercourses. Resident repairs to low spots on the brook bank were insufficient to prevent erosion and continued out-of-channel flow. (p12) [PAG]
* Steventon is identified as being affected by overspill/flood risk from the interaction of Ginge Brook and Moor Ditch, with flood risk consequences reported for the village. (p17)
* Out-of-channel flows from Ginge Brook led to flows across farmland near Steventon and contributed to flooding affecting the community and downstream catchments. (p21)
* A bridleway to Steventon was subject to flood overspill from Ginge Brook. (p27) [TAB]
* Experienced overspill and altered flow routes from the interface of Ginge Brook and Moor Ditch leading to flooding in the area; identified as a location for further investigation and potential upstream flood storage/nature-based solutions. (p30)

### A34
* Flows from Ginge Brook passed through a culvert under the A34 and via an underpass; the underpass was reported to have been flowing up to 30cm deep during the flood event. Farmland and the bridleway around the A34 both upstream and downstream were observed flooding. (p10) [PAG]
* Out-of-channel flows from Ginge Brook crossed under the A34 in two locations during the peak of the flood event (via an ordinary watercourse culvert and an underpass), contributing to flooding of adjacent farmland upstream and downstream of the A34. (p11) [IMG, PAG]
* The reach of Ginge Brook in the vicinity of the A34 (approximately 350m either side) has a very low gradient and is heavily silted; this reduced conveyance led to silt deposition and recurring flooding at this location through winter 2023/24, contributing to the January 4th flood impacts. (p12)
* Siltation at the downstream end of the culvert under the A34 reduced effective channel capacity and impeded flow during the January/February flooding, increasing local flood risk; flows entered Moor Ditch via an alternate culvert under the A34 ~100m to the south. (p15) [IMG]

### Milton Mill Weir
* The Milton Mill weir and the brick‑arch culvert downstream influence flood flows; the report recommends modelling the flood‑risk impact of modifying the weir and checking the condition/requirement of the brick arch culvert to ensure it can pass flood flows. The Environment Agency is to provide advice to the landowner regarding the culvert. (p3)
* The modern course of Ginge Brook runs in what was previously the cut for Milton Mill; this man-made, shallower-gradient channel is heavily silted relative to other reaches, reducing conveyance and contributing to overbank/out-of-channel flow and recurring flooding through winter 2023/24, including the January 4th event. (p12)
* The Milton Mill weir and the brick-arch culvert downstream of the weir were identified as influencing flood flows; a check of the culvert condition and modelling of modifying the weir were recommended to assess and manage flood risk. (p27) [TAB]
* The weir at Milton Mill (no longer serving a working mill) influences river flow and was highlighted for modelling to understand how modifications could affect local flood risk and fish passage; the structure is implicated in local flood behaviour. (p30)

### Bridleway

### Moor Ditch Catchment
* Elevated river levels throughout the winter in the Moor Ditch catchment contributed to flooding of downstream properties (including on High Street and Pembroke Lane) and prevented normal discharge of surface water systems. (p19)
* Hydraulic overload within the Moor Ditch catchment contributed to flooding at multiple locations and prevented surface water drainage from discharging to the Moor Ditch, exacerbating flooding in Milton and Milton Park. (p21)

### Business Park
* Internal flooding occurred at multiple business units because site surface water outfalls to Moor Ditch could not discharge when river levels were high; site owners are planning installation of a pump to enable discharge to the downstream river when levels permit. (p19)

### Highway Authority
* Oxfordshire Highways to inspect, maintain and record highway drainage assets in Milton; assess capacity of highway drainage where insufficient capacity contributed to runoff and flooding of property, report unsuitable third-party drainage connections, and work with LLFA/community to clarify ownership/maintenance of nearby watercourses to reduce future highway-related surface water flooding. (p29)
