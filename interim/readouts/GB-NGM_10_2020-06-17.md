### Tottle Brook
* Water levels rose sharply during the intense summer storm and the watercourse overtopped, particularly at locations where the channel was restricted; this contributed to flooding in Wollaton Vale and Larchdene Avenue and to internal flooding of properties. (p4)
* Constrained and partly culverted channel adjacent to Larchdene Avenue and Wollaton Vale contributed to flooding that caused internal flooding of properties; unconsented channel alterations reduced capacity and conveyance through the channel, increasing flood risk to nearby properties. (p7) [PAG]
* {2012, 17 Jun 2020}: The watercourse can rise sharply due to numerous connections from private properties and surface water sewers; it has overtopped and caused external flooding (overtopping linked to flooding on nearby streets). The brook is the main channel with a bypass culvert to divert additional flows to protect lower properties. (p8) [MUL]
* Overtopping of Tottle Brook was the predominant cause of flooding, leading to inundation of adjacent properties. Surface water sewers discharge directly into the brook, producing a rapid rise in water level and causing backing up of water through the sewer network and onto highway infrastructure. Channel modifications and pinch points exacerbated the tendency to back up towards properties. (p10)
* Flooding associated with the Tottle Brook catchment was linked to property flooding in the study area; there is no flood warning system for the Tottle Brook and assets (e.g. trash screens and culverts) are inspected/cleaned by the Environment Agency. The LLFA recommends surveying the Tottle Brook and its assets to identify constraints that impact conveyance. (p11)
* The brook's capacity was exceeded by a short, high-intensity rainfall event, contributing to flooding of nearby properties (internal flooding on Larchdene Avenue and Wollaton Vale). (p15) [TAB]
* {general}: Predicted extent of river flooding follows the course of Tottle Brook from NW to SE, concentrating along the watercourse and widening through denser residential areas; the floodplain runs toward The Lake and Wollaton Park and places adjacent to the brook (streets and properties) are shown at risk of inundation. (p19) [IMG]
* {general}: The highest surface water flood risk is concentrated along Tottle Brook; flood-prone zones follow the brook's course through the residential area and feed into The Lake, causing deep surface water inundation at low points along the watercourse. (p20) [IMG]
* Quant: more than 5 properties reported internal flooding (p4)
* Quant: 19 properties (p7) [PAG]
* Quant: maximum rainfall intensity at location estimated as a 1 in 70-year return period (1.43PCSYM annual chance); sewers typically designed for a 1 in 30-year event (capacity exceeded) (p10)
* Quant: 19 properties internally; at least 3 properties externally (p11)
* Quant: 11 residential properties; 8 residential properties (p15) [TAB]
* {general}: Quant: Over 900 mm flood depth concentrated along the brook in the medium-risk scenario; moderate zones of 300–900 mm extend along its course into adjacent streets and green spaces (p20) [IMG]

### Wollaton
* Wollaton experienced an intense summer storm on 17 June 2020. Tottle Brook rose sharply and overtopped (particularly where the channel is restricted). The storm also overwhelmed the sewer system and highway infrastructure, causing additional surface water flooding and internal flooding of properties in the area. (p4) [PAG]
* Parts of Wollaton (specifically Larchdene Avenue and Wollaton Vale) experienced internal flooding of residential properties. (p7) [PAG]
* A highly localised thunderstorm moved northwest over Wollaton on 17/06/2020 producing intense rainfall that caused surface water / urban flooding in the Wollaton area and prompted the Section 19 flood investigation. (p9) [PAG]
* Thunderstorm was extremely localised to Wollaton; flooding occurred rapidly (within ~30 minutes of the storm reaching the area). Drainage systems were compromised by the intense rainfall (almost an average month's June precipitation fell over 2 days in Wollaton), resulting in flooding of properties and streets driven by overtopping of the Tottle Brook and surface water overland flow. (p10) [PAG]

### Tottle Brook Catchment
* The catchment area is the focus of the LLFA investigation and recommended actions (modelling and surveys). The flood event in the area led to investigation and recommendations to survey the Tottle Brook and associated channel assets to identify constraints and to develop a flood model to explore options to reduce risk. (p11)
* Short, high‑intensity rainfall caused the capacity of the Tottle Brook to be exceeded, which contributed to flooding of adjacent residential streets (Larchdene Avenue and Wollaton Vale). (p15) [TAB]
* The catchment is the focus for a flood warning system and for post-flood emergency maintenance and culvert cleansing by the Environment Agency, indicating flooding associated with Tottle Brook. (p16)

### Highway Services
