## Topics without coordinates

### Sovereign Grange
* On-site drainage systems were unable to cope with the rainfall, causing water to discharge out of the development to off‑site areas and drainage systems that could not manage the volume. (p7)
* The area immediately south of the Sovereign Grange Housing Development was the focus of the reported flooding during the event; the investigation focuses on this area and internal flooding of one property was recorded in the incident. (p9)
* Surface water drainage manholes and road gullies within the estate were surcharged and water flowed out of manhole covers and gullies onto the estate carriageway and beyond. Channels and soakaway sumps had a significant build-up of very fine silt, reducing drainage capacity and contributing to drainage system failure and overtopping. (p14)
* Build-up of fine silt in the development's drainage system (pipes and soakaways) reduced hydraulic capacity and contributed to failure of the surface water drainage. Surface water drainage pipework levels and gradients at the south of the site produced high heads and increased velocities, increasing tendency for the drainage system to surcharge; sharp bends, changes in gradient and challenging soakaway entry/distribution increased likelihood of surcharging and soakaway failure. (p15)
* On-site drainage systems were unable to cope with the rainfall; water flowed out of the on-site drainage to off-site areas and into older drainage systems. The failure of the development's drainage system is considered the primary source of flood water that contributed to the Kings Cliffe flood incident. (p16) [PAG]
* Surface water runoff from the Sovereign Grange development contributed to surface water flooding in Kings Cliffe. The drainage is reported to potentially allow high-velocity, high-volume flows that could lead to overtopping of manholes and road gullies and to surface water flowing onto Wood Road and other properties; developer required to investigate, redesign, renovate or replace drainage to prevent adding to flood risk. (p23)
* {general}: Drainage serving the development is identified for investigation, redesign, renovation or replacement to address runoff/drainage issues that create flood risk to the development and potentially downstream properties. (p26)
