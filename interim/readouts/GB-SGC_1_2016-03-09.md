

# Oldbury-On-Severn flooding 09 Mar 2016

## Place names with coordinates

### Oldbury-On-Severn
* Local flooding occurred in Oldbury-on-Severn; the report specifically investigates the flood incident affecting the village on 9 March 2016 and examines tidal influence (tide locking) using tide gauge data from Avonmouth and Sharpness to understand sources of the local flooding. (p2) [PAG]
* Oldbury-on-Severn was impacted by flood flows routed through the Oldbury Naite rhine during the event on 9 March 2016; assessment indicates these flows reached the village and produced the reported flooding. (p5) [IMG]
* The centre of Oldbury-on-Severn experienced elevated water levels on 9 March 2016 when high river flows coincided with a high tide. Surveyed high water and photographic evidence show peak levels in the village, with peak levels recorded below the crest of formal defences. (p9) [PAG]
* Oldbury-on-Severn experienced inundation during the 9 March 2016 event. Parish Council survey points in the village recorded high water levels (around 6.1–6.4 m) that were below the approximate crest of formal defences (~6.5 mAOD). The survey and photographic evidence document localized flooding of roads and properties within the village. (p10) [IMG, PAG]
* During the 9th March 2016 event there were instances of property and road flooding in Oldbury-on-Severn caused by high rainfall and overland flow routes. Specific properties (the Anchor Inn and Christmas Cottage on Church Road) were flooded; blocked drainage in the Anchor Inn garden was identified as a main cause, with high rhine levels potentially a contributing factor. (p11) [PAG]
* Property and road flooding occurred during the 9 March 2016 event. Properties at the Anchor Inn and Christmas Cottage, immediately south of the main watercourse on Church Road, experienced flooding driven mainly by overland runoff and a blocked drainage feature in the Anchor Inn garden (thresholds were above peak rhine levels so runoff rather than direct rhine overtopping was the primary cause). Runoff down Church Hill and Westmarsh Lane exacerbated flooding of this and adjacent areas; highway drainage condition and capacity contributed to impacts. (p12) [PAG]
* The village area is below the local Highest Astronomical Tide (~8.5mAOD) and experienced elevated water levels during the March 2016 flood, with fluvial–tidal interaction raising levels in the Rhine network and contributing to flooding in Oldbury-on-Severn. Upstream moor storage reduced peak levels. (p15) [PAG]
* Significant parts of Oldbury-on-Severn were vulnerable to elevated water levels when fluvial flows combined with spring tides. The Rhine network experienced raised water levels during the March 2016 event; Chapel Road reached around 6.0mAOD. Existing tidal flaps normally convey flows at low/intermediate tides but offer limited benefit when higher fluvial flows coincide with spring tides. Report notes pumping would need to move several cubic metres per second to materially reduce levels. (p16) [IMG, PAG]
* During the 9 March 2016 event floodwater produced raised water levels through Oldbury-on-Severn (around 6.0 mAOD). Peak flows during the event constrained levels in the village and determined the extent/duration of flooding; modelling of upstream storage and field attenuation was identified as relevant to reducing peak levels at Oldbury. (p17)
* Oldbury‑on‑Severn experienced elevated flood levels during the event; recommendations refer to flood wall overtopping, flood water passing onto roads, and options to reduce flood levels within the village (including using other rhine systems and increasing tidal gate capacity when high fluvial flows combine with spring tides). (p18)

### Oldbury Nate Rhine
* {01 Nov 2000 - 30 Nov 2000}: The lower reaches of the Oldbury Naite rhine (referred to as Lower Oldbury) have limited conveyance capacity; a 2002 review noted the lower reaches capacity (~14.4 m3/s) and identified this as roughly equivalent to a 1-in-10 to 1-in-20 annual probability flood (including a tide-lock allowance). The study highlighted capacity concerns and potential for overtopping in the catchment. (p10)
* Peak water levels were recorded in the Naite rhine during the 9th March 2016 event; these high levels may have been a contributing factor to flooding of nearby properties (for example Anchor Inn and Christmas Cottage). The report also flagged capacity concerns in the Upper Oldbury Naite/Rockhampton rhine area and recommended a hydraulic-model review and potential longer-term alleviation measures. (p11) [PAG]
* During the 9th March 2016 event the Rhine experienced tide-locking and high fluvial flows. Closure of the tidal flaps during the rising tide meant fluvial water could not escape for a period (~just under 3 hours), requiring storage within the Rhine and adjacent low-lying ground and raising water levels to the observed peak (~6.1mAOD at Church Road). This raised peak levels contributed to flooding of adjacent low-lying areas and properties. (p13) [PAG, IMG]
* Fluvial flows in the upper Oldbury Naite rhine transferred water into the low-lying upstream moor storage during the March 2016 flood, influencing downstream Rhine network levels and contributing to elevated flood levels in Oldbury-on-Severn. The availability of upstream moor storage reduced peak flows from the Oldbury Naite. (p15)
* The Oldbury/Naite rhine acted as a primary channel for floodwater during the March 2016 event; its capacity and connections to surrounding fields influenced peak water levels and routing of flows away from the moors. (p17)
* {01 Nov 2000 - 30 Nov 2000}: Quant: design capacity ~14.4 m3/s; roughly equivalent to a 1-in-10 to 1-in-20 annual probability (p10)
* Quant: Peak level ~6.1mAOD; ~110,000m3 of water required to be stored during the main tide-locking period; available volume between 4.5 and 6.1mAOD calculated at ~60,000m3; alternative storage estimate ~80,000m3 if tide timing differed. (p13) [PAG, IMG]

### Church Road, Oldbury-On-Severn
* Surveyed points and photographs around Church Road show elevated water levels during the 9 March 2016 event, documenting water reaching surveyed points on and adjacent to the road and nearby properties. (p9) [IMG, PAG]
* Flood water inundated locations along Church Road during the 9 March 2016 event. A Parish Council survey mapped and photographed high water at points on the road, showing localized inundation adjacent to properties (e.g., 'Cherry Trees' and 'Dean House'). (p10) [IMG]
* Area around the main watercourse experienced high water levels (peak flood level in the rhine recorded at Church Road) and overland runoff contributed to property/road flooding in the vicinity. (p12)

### Chapel Road, Oldbury-On-Severn
* The Parish Council produced a dedicated flood survey (Figure 9) for Chapel Road documenting that the road was affected by flooding during the event. (p10)
* Chapel Road experienced elevated water levels during the March 2016 flood; the peak water level in Chapel Road was approximately 6.0mAOD. (p15)
* Flood water passed onto the road via drainage connections prior to the flood wall overtopping, causing roadway inundation. (p18)

### Church Hill, Oldbury-On-Severn
* Runoff down Church Hill exacerbated flooding to nearby properties during the event; drainage in this area has been identified as a factor and subject to improvement works or further investigation. (p12)
* Runoff from the Church Hill area contributed to flooding; the report recommends investigating highway drainage at the Church Hill/Westmarsh Lane junction and restoring the former Cowhill Wharf rhine route to convey runoff from this area. (p18)

### Westmarsh Lane, Oldbury-On-Severn
* Runoff down Westmarsh Lane exacerbated flooding to nearby properties during the event; the highway drainage here has been noted for improvement. (p12)
* The Church Hill/Westmarsh Lane junction suffered from inadequate highway drainage during the event; investigation and potential improvements are recommended to reduce road flooding. (p18)

### Pickedmoor Brook
* The Pickedmoor Brook / Oldbury Naite rhine catchment drained toward Oldbury-on-Severn during the 9 March 2016 event; modelling/assessment indicates the catchment produced the flows that reached the village and caused the flood. (p5) [IMG]
* {general}: Mentioned in the Lower Severn IDB (2002) review of the Oldbury drainage system as the confluence point relevant to the capacity of the lower Oldbury Naite rhine; the report links the drainage system behaviour at and below the Pickedmoor Brook confluence to past overtopping and capacity concerns. (p10)
* Pickedmoor Brook contributed to the flooding risk in the area; the report recommends installing flow and water level gauges to monitor high flows during extreme events. (p18)

### Anchor Inn
* Survey points and annotated photographs south of Anchor Inn recorded as part of the 9 March 2016 survey show nearby high water exposure (e.g. an artificial gas vent at 5.93m). These elevations and the clustering of high-water survey points indicate the Anchor Inn area was exposed to the high tide/peak water during the event. (p9) [IMG]
* Property inundated by overland runoff from surrounding area; flooding was exacerbated by blocked drainage in the Anchor Inn garden (drainage improvements have since been undertaken). (p12)
* Quant: 1 property (p12)

### Greenwood House
* Surveyed elevations and survey photographs taken at and immediately east of Greenwood House show nearby culvert inverts (5.10m and 4.68m) and a local low point (5.56m). These survey points were recorded as part of the 9 March 2016 event and document high water exposure adjacent to the property; they indicate the property was in the area of peak tidal/ponded water during the event (peak regional high water elsewhere recorded at 6.10m AOD, below formal defence crests). (p9) [IMG]

### Pill Cottages
* Surveyed high water levels and photographs recorded at locations immediately west of and adjacent to Pill Cottages during the 9 March 2016 event show a nearby high water level of 6.10m and a beam underside level of 6.01m. These survey values document that tidal/high water reached elevations close to structural elements beside Pill Cottages during the event. (p9) [IMG]

### Roseland
* Survey measurements north of Roseland recorded during the 9 March 2016 event include culvert invert levels (5.29m) and a road centre level (5.80m), documenting elevated water levels and drainage structure elevations in the immediate vicinity and showing the property/road were within the zone of high water exposure during the event. (p9) [IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Rhine Network
* The rhine system (the local drainage channel/river) experienced elevated fluvial flows coincident with tide-locking on 9 March 2016, which increased water levels and contributed to flooding in the locality. The assessment describes how the tide gates and the period when they were closed interacted with high fluvial flows to raise peak levels within the rhine network. (p13) [IMG]
* {09 Mar 2016, 21 Nov 2016}: Tide-locking during peak fluvial flows raised water levels within the Rhine network, with fluvial flows being stored behind tidal controls and increasing Rhine levels substantially. The report estimates stored fluvial volume during the event at about 80,000m3, which could raise Rhine levels to around 5.8mAOD under an alternative tide timing; in March 2016 peak tide alignment produced higher Rhine levels than the November 2016 event. (p14) [PAG, MUL]
* The Rhine network experienced elevated levels due to fluvial flows and their interaction with tidal conditions during the March 2016 event; Rhine levels contributed to flooding in the locality but were moderated by storage in upstream moor areas. (p15)
* Other rhine systems conveyed flood water through and into Oldbury-on-Severn; their capacity and connections influenced flows passing through the village and contributed to local flooding. Recommendations sought to better utilise these rhines to reduce flows through Oldbury. (p18)
* Quant: Tide-locking period ~just under 3 hours; storage demand estimates given as ~110,000m3 (main period) and ~80,000m3 (if tide timing different). (p13) [IMG]
* {09 Mar 2016, 21 Nov 2016}: Quant: 80,000m3; around 5.8mAOD (p14) [PAG, MUL]

### Lower Severn IDB
* Non-return (flap) valves in Oldbury-on-Severn that were installed by the IDB were observed to be not fully functioning, with leakage through these structures. This reduced the effectiveness of the drainage/flow control system and increased flood risk; a survey was recommended to determine condition and whether valves need removal, replacement, repair or relocation. (p11)
* The drainage network managed by the IDB was implicated in attenuating and conveying flood water during high rainfall; a review was recommended to assess system performance and possible alterations to reduce flood levels in Oldbury-on-Severn. (p18)

### Severn Estuary
* Tidal levels in the Severn Estuary (from Avonmouth and Sharpness gauges) were analysed to understand periods of tide‑locking that relate to the local flooding at Oldbury-on-Severn; tidal influence therefore contributed to the flood incident investigated. (p2)

### Rockhampton Rhine
* {general}: Identified alongside the Upper Oldbury Naite rhine as an area of concern for the hydraulic capacity of the drainage system in the 2002 review, suggesting it is implicated in local flooding issues and candidate for longer‑term alleviation measures. (p10)

### Cowhill Wharf Rhine Route
* The former Cowhill Wharf rhine route was not conveying runoff; restoring it was recommended to convey runoff from the Church Hill area to reduce accumulation of runoff and local flood risk. (p18)
