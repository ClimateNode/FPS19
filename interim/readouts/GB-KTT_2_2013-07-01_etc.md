### Kingsgate Road Railway Bridge
* {July 2013, 10 Jun 2019}: The underpass was flooded during reported events; required emergency response and removal of water (TWUL attended to restart pumps and remove water). (p3) [MUL]
* {July 2013, 10 Jun 2019}: The Kingston underpass (section of Kingsgate Road beneath the railway bridge) flooded on multiple occasions, affecting a critical transport route. Flooding under the bridge made the highway infrastructure impassable and prompted Section 19 investigations. (p8) [IMG, MUL]
* {July 2013, 10 Jun 2019}: The Kingston underpass (A308 carriageway running under the railway) is the subject of the flood incident investigation — site where road underpass was inundated and investigated as a flood location. (p9) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* {11 Jul 2020}: The underpass flooded after heavy, prolonged rainfall (a topographical low point). Flooding led to closure of the highway at the site, diversion of traffic in Kingston town centre, removal of floodwater, cleansing of the highway and reopening in time for the morning rush hour. (p10)
* {10 Jun 2019}: Underpass inundated with deep standing water that covered the road at the lowest point, rendering the underpass impassable; traffic lights and road signage were partially submerged and traffic was disrupted. (p11) [IMG, PAG]
* {July 2013}: Surface water accumulation affected the underpass; the report links previous July 2013 flood events at the site to surface water flooding (site is within the underpass hydrological catchment shown on the map). (p13) [IMG]
* {general}: The underpass is located in Flood Zone 3 and, per the RoFSW map, lies within predicted surface-water flood extents (notably the 1-in-30 to 1-in-1000 year mapped extents). The report and map highlight significant surface water flood risk at the underpass and vulnerability of this transport asset to inundation and associated travel disruption. (Timing of actual inundation is not specified.) (p14) [IMG]
* {general}: The underpass is the lowest topographical point locally and is at increased risk of flooding when the nearby TWUL pumping station and surface water sewer network surcharge or fail; surcharging causes water to back up and flood the underpass and adjacent land. (p15)
* {general}: The report states a rainfall event resulted in flooding at the site identified as the Kingston underpass. (p17)
* {10 Jun 2019}: Rainwater ponded in the underpass during the June 2019 intense rainfall; the underpass flooded due to its position at a local topographical low and experienced greater severity of ponding than would be expected from the recorded TBR rainfall totals. (p18) [PAG]
* {general}: The highway under the railway bridge (Kingston Underpass) was closed due to flooding and a diversion route was put in place. (p20) [PAG]
* {July 2013}: Identified in the 2013 Kingsgate Road Railway Bridge Flood Investigation Report as being affected during the 2013 flood when the nearby TWUL pumping station failed, contributing to flooding at the railway bridge. (p21)
* {July 2013, 10 Jun 2019}: Multiple flooding reports affected the Kingston underpass and prompted a flood risk investigation; the underpass experienced flooding related to drainage failure requiring response actions. (p22) [PAG, MUL]
* {July 2013, 10 Jun 2019}: The underpass was inundated by surface water and sewer-related flooding. Flooding was exacerbated by proximity to the primary flow path and by failure of the nearby TWUL pumping station; pumps had to be re-started and water removed. (p23) [PAG, MUL]
* {general}: Site experienced flooding that prompted a flood risk investigation and led to recommended measures (liaison with Thames Water, investigation of pumping station failure, action plan development, consideration of automated traffic diversions/signage/alarms). (p24)
* {10 Jun 2019}: The FEH (Flood Estimation Handbook) analysis prepared for the Kingston Underpass shows short‑duration extreme rainfall and return‑period estimates used to assess the June 2019 event; the assessment concludes the June 2019 event at the underpass is approximately under a 1‑in‑5‑year return period, indicating a surface‑water flooding impact at the underpass for that event. (p26) [TAB]

### Kingston Upon Thames
* {July 2013, 10 Jun 2019}: The local authority carried out emergency response to flooding at the underpass and carried out Section 19 investigations into the July 2013 and June 2019 events. (p3) [MUL]
* {July 2013, 10 Jun 2019}: Kingston experienced flooding during the investigated incidents, with surface-water and sewer-related flooding affecting streets, properties and infrastructure; incidents led to investigation and post-2013 mitigation measures. (p5) [MUL]
* {July 2013}: Experienced two flooding incidents in July 2013 which triggered a Section 19 investigation by the local authority. (p7) [PAG]
* {11 Jul 2020}: Traffic disruption and delays in the town centre due to closure of the underpass and diversion routes; social media and local news documented vehicles in floodwater and public efforts to assist. (p10) [PAG]
* {10 Jun 2019}: Urban flooding occurred at the Kingston underpass within the town, causing local road inundation and traffic disruption on 10 June 2019. (p11) [IMG, PAG]
* {July 2013}: The hydrological catchment and RoFSW mapping show parts of Kingston Upon Thames at risk of surface water flooding; the report states previous flood events in July 2013 were attributed to surface water flooding affecting the area. (p13) [IMG]
* {10 Jul 2019}: The report records that fluvial flooding was reported in Kingston on 10 July 2019; the text also states it is unlikely that that particular incident can be attributed to fluvial sources, indicating uncertainty about the flood mechanism. (p14) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: A rainfall event assessed using the Hogsmill TBR resulted in flooding at the Kingston underpass site; the underpass was inundated following the intense 24‑hour rainfall. (p17) [IMG, PAG]
* {10 Jun 2019}: A Flood Alert and Flood Guidance Statement on 10 June 2019 identified the Beverly Brook area of Kingston upon Thames as at risk, stating flooding of highways and land was possible and urban areas were at risk, with associated transport disruption predicted. (p19) [TAB, PAG]
* {general}: Access to the town was affected by flooding-related closures (a crossing/route was closed to stop high-sided vehicles entering Kingston) and diversion routes were used, impacting town-centre access. (p21) [PAG]
* {July 2013}: Flooding at the Kingsgate Road railway bridge/underpass in Kingston led to closure/traffic diversions and prompted the 2013 Kingsgate Road Railway Bridge Flood Investigation Report and subsequent measures (diversions, signage, alarms reviewed). (p25) [PAG]
* {July 2013}: Quant: two flooding incidents (p7) [PAG]
* {10 Jun 2019}: Quant: 1 underpass affected (p11) [IMG, PAG]

### Thames Water
* {July 2013, 10 Jun 2019}: Thames Water (TWUL) attended the Kingston underpass site during flood events to restart pumps and remove floodwater as part of the immediate response. (p3) [MUL]
* {general}: Responded as a risk management authority to flooding in the area and is continuing collaboration on Drainage and Wastewater Management Plan investigations; pumping station failure is referenced as a potential cause/trigger to be mitigated. (p4)
* {July 2013, 10 Jun 2019}: Thames Water is recorded as a responsible authority contributing actions to the flood incidents, attending sewer-related flood issues and taking response measures during the events investigated. (p5) [MUL]
* {July 2013}: Failure of pumps at a nearby TWUL pumping station did not work as intended and is recorded as contributing to the flooding of the Kingston underpass during the July 2013 incidents. (p8)
* {11 Jul 2020}: The TWUL pumping station located close to the underpass failed during the flood incident, contributing to sewer/pumping failure associated with the underpass flooding. (p10)
* {general}: The TWUL surface water pumping station and associated sewer network have a history of failure; pumping station failures and subsequent sewer surcharge reduce network capacity and have caused sewer flooding risk to the lowest local point (Kingston underpass). (p15) [IMG]
* {July 2013, 10 Jun 2019}: TWUL assets contributed to the incident: a TWUL pumping station (approximately 85m west of the underpass) failed during the July 2013 flood event contributing to flooding at the Kingston underpass. A TWUL engineer re-started the pumping station at about 02:00 on 11 June 2019. The site is recorded for inclusion in TWUL's Drainage and Wastewater Management Plan investigations. (p21) [MUL]
* {July 2013, 10 Jun 2019}: The nearby TWUL pumping station failed during the July 2013 events and again required re-starting during the June 2019 event; TWUL attended the site to re-start pumps and remove floodwater. (p23) [MUL]
* {general}: Operator of the pumping station implicated in flood incidents; required to liaise with the LLFA, confirm actions taken to prevent pumping station failure, and produce an action plan to address future failures and area flooding (to be progressed via DWMP collaboration). (p24)
* {July 2013}: Quant: 2 flooding incidents (July 2013) (p8)
