### Cogenhoe
* {20 Dec 2012, 09 Mar 2016}: Flooding at Whiston Road in Cogenhoe occurred when watercourses could not convey rainwater, leading to overtopping and allowing surface water and groundwater to enter property basements; internal flooding of at least one building in Cogenhoe has been recorded (including repeated incidents over the last five years). (p7) [MUL]
* The area including Cogenhoe (specifically Whiston Road, 300 m east of the village) experienced internal flooding of a property during the 9 March 2016 event. (p9) [PAG]
* {2012}: Basement flooding occurred to properties on Whiston Road within Cogenhoe during the flood incident (affecting property basements). (p11)
* Community has experienced flooding and a formal Section 19 Flood Investigation Report was completed for the community; the investigation focused on flood risk and potential small-scale mitigation options which were found not to be viable for FCERM GIA funding. (p14)
* Surface water runoff from highways within Cogenhoe led to flood risk in the village; recommendations include cleansing of highway drainage throughout Cogenhoe and assessing capacity of drainage assets. (p18)

* Schematic flooding areas and main overland flow routes on the incident plan are located within the Cogenhoe area, concentrated near culvert inlets (01A and 02A), indicating localised surface water/overland flooding in parts of the village. (p32) [IMG]

## Topics without coordinates

### Anglian Water
* Public sewerage was identified as receiving additional sources of water and the capacity of Anglian Water's assets needs assessment because insufficient capacity could lead to sewer-related flood risk to properties; actions requested include asset capacity assessment and development of an asset plan to share with the LLFA and community. (p18)
* {general}: Sewer systems can become overwhelmed in extreme weather and result in sewer flooding (flooding from public sewer and combined sewer systems). (p23)

### Highway Authority
* Highway drainage assets were identified as having insufficient capacity to drain highway runoff, creating surface water flood risk to properties; recommended actions include regular cleansing and capacity assessments and consideration of improvement works where runoff from the highway leads to property flood risk. (p18)

### South Northamptonshire Council
* Surface water flooding in the vicinity of the flooding incident prompted SNC to review planning policies and drainage designs for developments, consider enforcement action where riparian owners are not maintaining ordinary watercourses, assist preparation of detailed drainage/asset plans to share with the LLFA and community, and carry out street cleaning to remove debris that could block highway drainage. (p18)

### Environment Agency
* The EA is tasked to work with the county emergency planning team and the LLFA to support the community following the flooding incident (coordination and response support related to the surface water flood event). (p18) [PAG]

### Northamptonshire County Council
* {general}: As the Lead Local Flood Authority (LLFA), NCC is responsible for investigating flood incidents under Section 19 of the Flood and Water Management Act (FandWMA). NCC must maintain a register of assets that have a significant effect on flooding (Section 21), recording ownership and state of repair, and keep a record of flooding hotspots. NCC will seek support from other risk management authorities, communities and individual homeowners to ensure flood incidents are reported and assets affecting flood risk are recorded. (p22) [PAG]
