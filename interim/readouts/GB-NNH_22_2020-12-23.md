## Topics without coordinates

### Anglian Water
* AWS are the statutory owner/operator of the public foul and surface water sewer systems serving the area containing the flooded property. Their surface water sewer network (including the sewer that runs along the southern boundary of the flooded property) is directly connected to the private drainage on the flood‑affected site and therefore AWS infrastructure was directly associated with the location of inundation. (p13)
* The AWS surface water sewer, into which private drainage from the car park outfalls, is reported or inferred to have backed up. Flood water was observed 'backing' up in the car park, rising from drainage features and then flowing overland into the basement (which has an external stepped entrance at the same level as the car park). The affected property used a temporary pump to remove floodwater from the basement. (p14)
* An anomaly/obstruction was identified within the AWS sewer that prevented a full survey and is thought to have affected downstream flow. Backup from the below-ground drainage flooded the adjacent car park and the water flowed eastward into the property's basement via the external stepped entrance. The basement was flooded; AWS carried out a partial survey of its infrastructure where accessible and further investigation/rectification is to be discussed between AWS and the occupier. (p15)
* Follow-up investigations by AWS into the surface water main sewer running through the property boundary found illegal alterations made during private surfacing works that may have contributed to the flooding; AWS provided recommendations to the customer to improve flow to their private section of surface water drainage. (p16) [PAG]
* An anomaly (an unexpected buried structure/obstacle) was identified within the AWS sewer that prevented a full survey. AWS infrastructure was reported clear where it could be surveyed. The sewer anomaly is thought to have contributed to restricted conveyance, allowing surface water to back up and surcharge into the car park and then flow into the basement of the property via an external stepped entrance, causing flooding of the property. (p17)
* Quant: 100,000 litres of flood water in the basement (p15)

### Northamptonshire Highways
* NH received a significant number of service requests county-wide during the flood incident; out-of-hours crews dealt with many drainage-capacity issues arising from the event. (p16)

### Environment Agency
* The Environment Agency liaised with the LLFA and other authorities to help determine the extent of flooding across the county during the response to the event. (p16)
