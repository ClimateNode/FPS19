

# Wolverhampton Metropolitan District flooding 12 Jun 2023

Reference document:<br>Floods and Water Management Act 2010 Section 19 - Investigation: Ettingshall Road/Birmingham New Road, Bilston, Wolverhampton<br>149\_Wolverhampton/Floods and Water Management Act 2010 Section 19 - Investigation.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Ettingshall Road, Wolverhampton Metropolitan District
* Carriageway inundated by surface water during the 12 June 2023 flood event; road surface was covered edge-to-edge with water high enough to partially submerge vehicle wheels, disrupting traffic and access to adjacent residences. (p1)
* Surface water from isolated heavy rainfall on 12th June 2023 caused internal flooding to properties on Ettingshall Road. (p5)
* Flooding occurred on the street on 12 June 2023. (p7)
* Intense, short-duration rainfall recorded at the monitoring location produced rapid surface water / flash flooding that contributed to flooding of local infrastructure and properties. (p18) [IMG]
* Surface water flooding on highways and footways affected highway drainage and sewer infrastructure; high‑velocity runoff inundated driveways and gardens and progressed into internal areas of properties (through thresholds and communal areas). Debris and building materials were washed down the catchment and blinded highway drainage. (p21) [PAG]
* Surface water inundation across the carriageway with highway drainage failure; adjacent driveways and residential properties were flooded. Sewer infrastructure was affected/surcharged contributing to flooding. (p22)
* Highway gullies and subsurface drainage on Ettingshall Road were overwhelmed during the storm on 12th June 2023; surface water flowed over/around gullies and along the highway to lower-lying properties, with water escaping from gullies and sewer manholes. Sensors have been placed inside gullies on Ettingshall Road to monitor water levels for operational response. (p24) [TAB]
* Previous measures (altered road/footpath profile and additional gullies) increased capacity but were insufficient; the storm produced volumes/velocities that would not have been contained by this infrastructure, indicating flooding/overwhelming of drainage on the road. (p25)
* Quant: 15 properties (across Ettingshall Road and Birmingham New Road) (p5)

### Birmingham New Road, Wolverhampton Metropolitan District
* Carriageway inundated by surface water during the 12 June 2023 flood event; urban street flooding disrupted vehicular movement and access to nearby properties. (p1)
* Surface water from isolated heavy rainfall on 12th June 2023 caused internal flooding to properties on Birmingham New Road. (p5)
* Flooding occurred on the street on 12 June 2023. (p7)
* A highly localised, short-duration intense storm occurred on 12th June 2023 with a pronounced rainfall spike around 18:15 recorded at the COWC Ettingshall Road gauge. The rapid accumulation of runoff from this event produced very large volumes of water across the catchment and is identified in the report as the primary cause of flooding to infrastructure and properties in the area. (p18) [IMG]
* Surface water inundation of the highway affecting carriageway drainage; driveways and nearby residential properties were flooded and sewer infrastructure was impacted/surcharged. (p22)
* Severn Trent’s modelling and assessments focused on the existing surface water sewer infrastructure within the Birmingham New Road environs and its downstream interactions, implying the sewer/surface water system in this area was affected by the storm. (p25)
* Quant: 15 properties (across Ettingshall Road and Birmingham New Road) (p5)

### Bilston
* Residential streets in Bilston (including Ettingshall Road and Birmingham New Road) were heavily affected by surface water/urban flooding on 12 June 2023, causing inundation of roads, disruption to vehicle movement and impacts to property access. (p1)
* At least 15 properties in Ettingshall Road and Birmingham New Road in Bilston suffered internal flooding from surface water following isolated heavy rainfall on 12th June 2023. (p5)
* Flooding occurred on 12 June 2023 affecting Ettingshall Road and Birmingham New Road in Bilston. (p7)
* Radar-derived rainfall estimates show the epicentre of a high-intensity rainfall band passed directly over Bilston at around 18:00 on 12 June 2023. This intense short-duration rainfall was associated with Met Office thunderstorm warnings and Flood Forecasting Centre warnings for isolated surface water flooding, indicating a risk of surface-water/flash flooding in Bilston. (p16)
* Quant: 15 properties (p5)

### West Midlands
* Isolated heavy rainfall on 12th June 2023 caused surface water flooding in parts of the West Midlands, resulting in internal flooding to properties and businesses. (p5)
* A belt of heavy convective rainfall moved northwest across the West Midlands on 12 June 2023 (approx. 17:00–20:00). The rainfall epicentre passed over Bilston around 18:00, producing intense downpours that the guidance and radar indicated could cause isolated surface water flooding in urban areas with the potential for significant impacts. (p15) [PAG]
* Rainfall radar over the West Midlands on 12 June 2023 shows spatially variable but locally very high intensities (cores of 30–100 mm/h in places). The Met Office issued thunderstorm warnings covering the West Midlands and neighbouring authorities, and the Flood Forecasting Centre warned of isolated surface water flooding with potential for significant impacts. (p16) [PAG, IMG]
* A localized, fast-moving belt of very intense rainfall crossed the West Midlands between about 17:00–20:00 on 12th June 2023. The radar-derived snapshot shows a concentrated band of very high rainfall intensity moving NW across the region (epicentre around Bilston/Wolverhampton at ~18:00). This produced highly localised extreme runoff potential and is the precipitating cause of reported urban/surface-water flood impacts in the area (likely rapid surface-water and flash flooding in low-lying urban catchments). (p17) [PAG, IMG]

### Wolverhampton Metropolitan District
* Wolverhampton (the Bilston area) experienced urban surface water flooding during the 12 June 2023 event, with streets inundated and transport/disruption impacts in the affected neighbourhoods. (p1) [PAG]
* The radar imagery places the epicentre of the high-intensity rainfall band over the Bilston/Wolverhampton area around 18:00 on 12th June 2023. Multiple image snapshots (18:00–18:20) show very high, highly localised rainfall cells passing over Wolverhampton and nearby neighbourhoods (including Penn Fields/Lower Gornal vicinity), producing conditions consistent with rapid urban surface-water and flash flooding in affected streets and low-lying areas. (p17) [PAG, IMG]
* Drainage capacity was overwhelmed by the intense storm on 12th June 2023. Surface water runoff was channelled by impermeable surfaces to the lowest points (the highway), overflowing the highway drainage and spilling from the highway into downhill properties; flows also escaped from sewer manholes and highway gullies. (p24) [TAB]

### Dudley Metropolitan District
* A localised, intense band of rainfall was centred on the Dudley–Tipton area on 12th June 2023 (highest intensities 30–100 mm/h shown on radar). This produced conditions for isolated surface water / flash flooding in the Dudley–Tipton area and was highlighted by the Flood Forecasting Centre as having potential for significant impacts. (p16)
* Radar snapshots show the central band of very high rainfall intensity running from Wolverhampton through Dudley and Tipton around 18:10 on 12th June 2023. This suggests Dudley was subject to intense, short-duration rainfall producing high runoff rates and a risk of localized surface-water/flash flooding in urban and valley locations within the borough. (p17) [PAG, IMG]

### Tipton
* Radar-derived rainfall intensity maps show the core of a short, very intense convective band passing directly over Tipton around 18:00 on 12 June 2023. This intense, short-duration rainfall had the potential to produce isolated surface-water/flash flooding in urban areas of Tipton; the report links this rainfall to FFC warnings of isolated surface water flooding with potential for significant impacts. (p16) [PAG, IMG]

### Meadow Lane, Wolverhampton Metropolitan District

## Topics without coordinates

### City of Wolverhampton Council
* As Lead Local Flood Authority, City of Wolverhampton Council produced a Section 19 report and led recovery actions following surface water flooding on Ettingshall Road and Birmingham New Road (Bilston). The council's Flood Risk Management team identified affected residents, provided help and guidance, and undertook remedial works to drainage infrastructure. (p5)
* Received resident reports and coordinated local response following the 12 June 2023 storm; issued flood questionnaires and collated testimony (including door-to-door evidence) which identified internal property flooding, external flooding to driveways/frontages/gardens, and highway flooding. Worked with highways and water company partners to request inspections of infrastructure and to record and share actions taken to address the flooding. (p23)
* City of Wolverhampton Highways had carried out gully inspections/cleaning on the morning of 12th June 2023 and found gullies fully operational a few hours before the storm, but during the storm the gully infrastructure was overwhelmed and unable to capture the extreme runoff, leading to highway overflow and spill into downhill properties; sensors in gullies now report warnings to operational staff for response. (p24) [TAB]
* Highways teams had cleaned gullies 6–8 hours before the storm and verified them operational, and installed previous road/footpath profile changes and additional gullies on Ettingshall Road, but the storm’s very large flows and velocities were not contained by the highway drainage. Gully-chamber sensors recorded rising water and triggered alerts leading to operational response. (p25)
* Quant: 15 properties (p5)
* Quant: up to 15 residential properties (p23)

### Severn Trent
* Severn Trent Water worked with the CWC Flood Risk Management team and CWC Highways during recovery to identify affected residents, provide help and guidance, and undertake remedial works to drainage infrastructure following surface water flooding that caused internal flooding to properties on Ettingshall Road and Birmingham New Road (Bilston). (p5)
* Undertook inspections and CCTV surveys of sewer infrastructure and carried out network modelling to assess how the sewer network responded to the intense rainfall; initial modelling did not fully replicate some in-person testimonies of internal property flooding, so further modelling assessment continued. (p23)
* Local public sewer and subsurface drainage capacity was overwhelmed during the 12th June 2023 storm, causing flows to escape from sewers and potentially via manholes; Severn Trent Water inspected and surveyed the public drainage network, assessed capacity, inspected for blockages in sewers and manholes, and undertook hydraulic modelling to identify required investment. (p24) [TAB]
* Severn Trent contacted affected properties to build a picture of impacts, conducted asset condition surveys to check the network for obstructions, and carried out hydraulic modelling of how their sewer and surface water network reacted to the storm, including assessments around Birmingham New Road and downstream connections to explore capacity or resilience improvements. (p25)
* Quant: 15 properties (p5)
* Quant: up to 15 residential properties (p23)

### Highways
* Inspected and undertook cleansing of highway gullies and drainage assets to restore function after the storm; investigation found highway drainage capacity was overwhelmed such that surface water runoff flowed along carriageways and, when drains surcharged, spilled from the highway into downhill properties. (p23)
* Quant: up to 15 residential properties (p23)
