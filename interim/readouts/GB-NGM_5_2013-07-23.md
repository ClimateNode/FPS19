### Strelley
* The flood event occurred at Woolsington Close within Strelley and was the subject of the Section 19 investigation. (p1)
* Flooding occurred in Strelley during the intense storm on 23 July 2013, including on Woolsington Close where properties experienced internal and external flooding. (p3)
* The intense convective storm that moved across the north of Nottingham City on 23 July 2013 affected the Strelley area; the flood event started during the intense rainfall at 17:00 on 23 July 2013. (p9)
* Areas of green open space in the wider Strelley area were saturated in the 24 hours before the main rainfall, increasing runoff and contributing to rapid overland flow and ponding that led to flooding at Woolsington Close. (p10)
* Road gullies and highway drains in the Strelley area were subject to the annual maintenance regime but were likely overwhelmed by surface water runoff during the event, contributing to ponding and flooding in the affected catchment (including Woolsington Close). (p15)
* Properties on Woolsington Close in Strelley were subject to internal and external flooding after a sequence of heavy rainfall events saturated the ground and overwhelmed drainage capacity. (p19)
* Quant: 10 properties (internal flooding); 2 properties (external flooding) (p3)
* Quant: 12 residential properties; 10 internal; 2 external (p19)

### Nottingham Unitary Authority
* Woolsington Close (the site of the investigated flood) lies within Nottingham and the event was investigated under Section 19 within this administrative area. (p1)
* An intense storm on the evening of 23 July 2013 caused flooding across the city, resulting in residential properties being flooded. (p3)
* The intense storm crossed the north of Nottingham City on 23 July 2013; the flood event began during this storm, indicating the area was affected by the resulting flood event. (p9)
* Nottingham City Council was required to respond to flooding in the Woolsington Close area: they submitted funding bids (including to Flood Defence Grant in Aid), commissioned surveys and optioneering studies, planned construction of a surface water management scheme, and placed gullies on a targeted cleansing regime to reduce repeat flooding. (p16) [TAB]
* A series of heavy rainfall events over the city saturated soils and filled underground drainage storage; a fourth event on 23 July 2013 caused drainage overload and led to property flooding, including 12 properties on Woolsington Close. (p19) [PAG]
* Quant: over 100 residential properties (p3)
* Quant: 12 residential properties; 10 internal; 2 external (p19) [PAG]

## Topics without coordinates

### Severn Trent
* Severn Trent Water stated the rainfall event exceeded the sewerage system design standard (1 in 30 year) and therefore classed the event as 'extreme', indicating the public sewer network's design capacity would have been exceeded and may have been overwhelmed during the intense storm. (p9)
* The public sewer network was described as being at or near maximum capacity during the event, preventing surface water from draining into the sewer system and contributing to surface water ponding and flooding; Severn Trent's sewer inspections/maintenance and the need for partnership with the Council are noted. (p15) [PAG]
* Severn Trent recorded the 23rd July 2013 flooding, classified the rainfall as 'extreme' exceeding sewer design standards, and undertook/committed to remedial actions (inspections, blockage removal) and ongoing monitoring following the event. (p16) [TAB]
* Severn Trent ensured the 23rd July 2013 flooding event was recorded on their systems, inspected and maintained the public sewer system (including blockage removal) to maintain serviceability, and committed to monitoring reported internal/external flooding incidents and storm characteristics to identify triggers for capital investment. (p17)
* As the sewerage provider, investigated the flooding in partnership with the City Council; determined the extreme rainfall did not meet their criteria for capacity improvements at this time and committed to continued maintenance of existing drainage and sewer systems to maximise performance during rainfall events. (p19) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 in 36 annual probability event; 1 in 30 year design standard exceeded (p9)

### Nottingham City Council
* Owned properties on Woolsington Close were flooded, sustaining downstairs internal and external damage to electrics, carpets, floors, doors, walls and furniture. (p14) [TAB]
* As the responsible authority for surface water and highway drainage, the Council's drainage assets (road gullies/highway drains) were overwhelmed during the event; the Council's maintenance regime and responsibilities were relevant to managing the impact and coordinating response and future works with Severn Trent Water. (p15) [PAG]
* The Council was directly involved in response and recovery actions after properties on Woolsington Close were flooded on 23rd July 2013: they targeted the area for flood risk improvements, submitted funding bids, commissioned surveys and optioneering studies, placed gullies on a targeted cleansing regime and planned construction works to divert surface water. (p16) [TAB]
* Acted as the local risk management authority for surface water and highway drainage during the event, investigated the flooding in partnership with Severn Trent Water, applied for Flood Defence Granting Aid to fund improvements for Woolsington Close, and is undertaking maintenance and preliminary design work to reduce future surface water flood risk. (p19) [PAG]
* Quant: 5 Nottingham City Council-owned properties affected (part of the 12 properties flooded on Woolsington Close) (p14) [TAB]

### Cranhill Road
* A section of Cranhill Road was included in the flood investigation area. Primary surface-water flow paths during the storm passed through and converged on this section, indicating surface water flooding and pooling in low-lying parts of the road. (p11) [IMG]

### Broxtowe Park Brook
* Identified as the only nearby watercourse that may have contributed to flooding of Woolsington Close during the event; drains the valley ~100 m north of Woolsington Close and could have conveyed excess water toward the area on 23rd July 2013. (p7)
