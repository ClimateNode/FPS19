

# Southwell flooding 18 Oct 2023 - 20 Oct 2023

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Southwell – Storm Babet, October 2023<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/southwell-s19-storm-babet-oct-2023.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Southwell
* Roads and properties in the town were flooded following intense rainfall on already-saturated ground, causing flooding to occur more quickly than would after a dry period. (p1)
* The Potwell Dyke (which runs through the town and under Church Street) and intense rainfall on saturated ground led to internal flooding of properties and flooding of roads; the central Church Street area experienced flooding similar to previous events. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Central area of the town experienced internal flooding associated with the Potwell Dyke during Storm Babet; town has a history of flooding which prompted a £5m catchment flood alleviation project. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Predicted Flood Zone 3 is concentrated along the main river through the southern part of Southwell (Figure 4). Figure 5 shows high surface water flood risk along the central watercourse through Southwell, indicating potential inundation of properties, roads and other infrastructure adjacent to the river and central watercourse during significant flood or heavy rainfall events. (p4) [IMG]
* Quant: 14 residential properties (p2) [IMG, PAG]

### Nottinghamshire
* Verified internal flooding to properties; attended sites and fed verified flood impact data back to RMAs and partners; delivered sandbags to affected areas; closed 77 roads across Nottinghamshire to promote road safety and avoid additional flood damage; responded to highway flooding in the catchment and carried out ditch clearance to reduce flooding risk in the Nottingham Road / Minster School area. (p6)
* Quant: 77 roads closed (p6)

### Newark and Sherwood
* The district experienced surface water flooding which then presented a continued risk of river flooding. Impacts described include evacuations of residents, delivery of flood protection equipment (aqua sacs), provision of emergency accommodation, visits to impacted communities, and post‑flood cleanup of roads, paths and removal of bulky waste from flooded homes. (p6)

### Church Street, Southwell
* The Potwell Dyke runs underneath Church Street; during Storm Babet this location experienced flooding similar to previous events and is within the area highlighted as having internal flooding, affecting properties in the vicinity of Church Street. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Church Street (where the Potwell Dyke runs underneath) experienced flooding during Storm Babet that manifested similarly to previous events; the location is identified for upstream storage reservoir and property flood resilience to reduce flood risk and internal flooding to properties. (p3) [IMG]

### Queen Street, Southwell
* {general}: Queen Street is shown on the surface water flood map (Figure 5) within medium-to-high surface water flood risk shading along the central watercourse, indicating the roadway and adjacent properties are at elevated risk of surface water inundation during heavy rainfall. (p4) [IMG]

### Burgage Lane, Southwell
* {general}: Burgage Lane is shown on the surface water flood map (Figure 5) adjacent to the central watercourse and within medium/low surface water flood risk shading, indicating an elevated risk of surface water inundation to the road and nearby properties during intense rainfall. (p4) [IMG]

### Upton Road, Southwell
* {general}: Upton Road is visible on the surface water flood map (Figure 5) near the central watercourse and is mapped within low-to-medium surface water flood risk zones, indicating potential for surface water to affect the road and adjacent properties during heavy rainfall. (p4) [IMG]

### Nottingham Road, Southwell
* Highway/road flooding occurred on Nottingham Road; response work included ditch clearance and other actions to reduce flood risk to the local area (including the nearby Minster School). (p6)

### Potwell Dyke
* Ran through the centre of town and under Church Street; during Storm Babet the Potwell Dyke contributed to flooding in the town centre, manifesting similarly to previous flood events. (p3)

### Minster School
* The Minster School area was at risk from flooding; authorities carried out ditch clearance and response actions to reduce flood risk to the school area. (p6)

### River Trent
* Predicted extreme river levels and a continued risk of river flooding following an initial surface water flooding phase; this risk prompted the council to coordinate in-person visits to high-risk communities and to continue recovery/cleanup actions. (p7)


## Topics without coordinates

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Acted in response to flooding: commenced cleanup of roads and paths and removal of bulky waste from flooded homes; supported/liaised with other agencies to find assistance; coordinated in-person visits to communities at high risk due to River Trent levels; commenced recovery activities including delivery of grant funding; conducted internal and multi-agency debrief; worked with parish/town councils to restock aqua sacs; progressing longer-term measures (surface water reservoir and property flood resilience) and supporting training and enforcement improvements. (p7)
