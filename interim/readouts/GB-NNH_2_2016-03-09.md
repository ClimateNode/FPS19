### Brigstock
* Subject of a Northamptonshire County Council flood investigation concerning flooding at Grafton Road. (p2)
* Surface water from heavy rainfall on a saturated catchment flowed to low points around private property in the village, resulting in internal flooding of at least one property on Grafton Road. (p7) [MUL]
* Location of a flood incident on Grafton Road in which a property was internally flooded; the property experienced repeated internal flooding events within the last five years. (p9) [PAG]
* A flooding incident occurred in the village on 9th March 2016. The British Geological Survey dataset indicates the incident was in an area with potential for groundwater flooding to occur at surface level; affected residents within the village were interviewed about the event. (p10) [PAG]
* {1998, 21 Nov 2012, 20 Dec 2012, 28 Jan 2013, 02 Oct 2013, 28 Jan 2014, 01 Jul 2014 - 31 Jul 2014, 09 Mar 2016}: Areas of the village experienced flooding from Harpers Brook and surface water. (p11) [MUL]
* Harpers Brook rose and flowed over its banks onto roads and private property in Brigstock; surface water followed natural contours to low points around properties, entering residences and causing internal flooding in nearby homes. (p12) [PAG]

Bow waves from vehicles driving through floodwater exacerbated internal flooding to properties. (p13)

### Harper's Brook
* {general}: Harpers Brook flows northwest–southeast through Brigstock and is identified on mapping as the watercourse adjacent to areas at high risk of fluvial flooding; it is therefore a potential flood source for the village. (p10) [PAG]
* {1998, 21 Nov 2012, 20 Dec 2012, 28 Jan 2013, 02 Oct 2013, 28 Jan 2014, 01 Jul 2014 - 31 Jul 2014, 09 Mar 2016}: High water levels in Harpers Brook caused overtopping onto local roads (notably Grafton Road and Sudborough Road) leading to highway flooding and internal flooding of properties. Harpers Brook flooding was the source of multiple incidents including internal flooding of properties and a community building, and repeated highway inundation that required sandbags and other temporary protection measures. (p11) [TAB, MUL]
* Water level rose (reported approximately six feet), overtopped banks and flowed onto roads and private property; a spillway channel came into partial use and the outflow contributed to deeper water on adjacent roads and properties. (p12)
* Water levels rose up against a building downstream of Grafton Road bridge, reaching two stone courses above the blue band of bricks on that building. (p40) [IMG, PAG]
* Areas of siltation were observed on Harpers Brook upstream of Grafton Road bridge following the flood event. (p41) [IMG, PAG]
* {1998, 21 Nov 2012, 20 Dec 2012, 28 Jan 2013, 02 Oct 2013, 28 Jan 2014, 01 Jul 2014 - 31 Jul 2014, 09 Mar 2016}: Quant: three residential properties; community building; flooding 3 feet deep in community building; three properties on Sudborough Road; one property on Grafton Road (p11) [TAB, MUL]

* Water flowing from Harpers Brook onto Sudborough Road accumulated in low areas and deepened until it entered residential properties, causing internal flooding; one household installed a flood gate across their frontage (not tested). (p12)

### Cricket Pitch
* {1998, 01 Jul 2014 - 31 Jul 2014}: The cricket pitch flooded on multiple occasions: in 1998 it flooded almost up to the pavilion; in July 2014 it flooded when saturated ground would not drain away. (p11) [TAB, MUL]

### Highway Authority
* The highway drainage and carriageway channels at the flood location were affected by floodwater. Northamptonshire Highways undertook regular drainage cleansing, identified assets with insufficient capacity for runoff from the highway and considered improvement works where drainage capacity could lead to flood risk to properties. (p21)

### Anglian Water
* Anglian Water Services was to assess sources of water entering the public sewerage system and the capacity of sewer assets following the incident, identifying any insufficient capacity that could lead to flood risk to properties. (p21)

### East Northamptonshire Council
* Following the flood incident ENC was required to review planning policies and drainage designs, consult with the EA and LLFA on planning applications, consider enforcement under the Land Drainage Act where riparian owners fail to maintain watercourses, and assist in preparing detailed drainage/asset plans to reduce flood risk to properties. (p21)

### Environment Agency
* The Environment Agency worked with the NCC Emergency Planning Team and the LLFA to support the community in response to the flood incident. (p21) [PAG]
