

# Abingdon flooding 04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Flood Investigation Report: Larkmead School, Faringdon Road, Abingdon, OX14 1AG<br>095\_Oxfordshire County/Larkmead-School-Flood-Report-v1.1-May-2024.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Larkmead School
* Flooding occurred at the school on 04-05 January 2024. (p1) [PAG]
* Floodwater from the Wildmore Brook overtopped its southern bank at three low spots and spread across the very flat northern school boundary into the site. Water entered buildings: the below-ground boiler room filled (water flowed down the steps and filled to a depth of approximately 1.5m), a heating and utility duct was inundated, the music department building and parts of the sports hall were flooded internally. Floodwater reached an underground power cable causing loss of power to half the school site and the inundation of boiler equipment caused loss of heating. The school closed for two weeks; recovery work continued as of March 2024. (p2)
* {general}: The school site was flooded by Wildmore Brook. The school is undertaking flood recovery works and has proposed resilience measures including raising the boiler room above expected flood levels and installing a temporary flood barrier at the top of the boiler room steps; further recommendations include assessing/raising low ground and the pump house wall, clearing culvert debris, and assessing/removing disused bridges adjacent to the school to reduce future flood impact. (p3) [TAB]
* The school site and its critical infrastructure were flooded, triggering a Section 19 investigation because a school (critical infrastructure) was impacted. (p6) [PAG]
* The school site was flooded during the event; the flood met the threshold for a Section 19 investigation because a school (critical infrastructure) was flooded. (p7) [IMG]
* Rising watercourses flowing across the site impacted the school; surface water runoff from heavy rainfall also contributed though onsite pumps operated throughout the event. The school's extremely flat ground levels made it prone to inundation. (p15)
* During the 04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024 floods, floodwater from Wildmore Brook entered a low spot on the school site and inundated parts of the grounds; photographic/site-visit evidence shows the flooding impacted an underground electricity cable on site. (p17)
* Wildmore Brook overtopped onto the Larkmead School site during the heavy rainfall event of 03–04 Jan 2024 causing site flooding. Damage and required permanent remediation works recorded at the school include installation of new heating plant, new electrical switchgear and several sub-mains, renewal of several low-voltage electrical systems damaged by flooding/power surge, renewal of carpet and vinyl floor coverings where impacted, and provision of new timber structures (now lifted by several inches to improve flood resilience). (p31) [PAG]
* Wildmore Brook exceeded its capacity and flooded onto the school site during 04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024. Flooding was extensive across the school with internal flooding occurring at locations where building thresholds are lower. Debris and litter were observed against downstream culvert/grills adjacent to the site (reported as not increasing flood risk to the school). (p32) [PAG]
* Floodwater reached the top of the boiler room steps and affected the boiler room; other school buildings (music department, sports hall and additional buildings) were identified as having been impacted or vulnerable and recommended for flood resilience measures. Low-lying ground and the pump house wall along the school's northern boundary were inundated and recommended to be raised. (p33)
* Floodwater from Wildmore Brook affected the school site: water reached the boiler room access (recommendation to install a barrier at the top of the boiler room steps), and flood resilience measures were recommended for the music department building, sports hall and other buildings. Low areas of ground and the pump house wall along the northern boundary of the site were affected and are recommended for raising; the school is progressing flood recovery and resilience works. (p34) [TAB, PAG]
* The school premises were affected by flooding and required recovery support following the event; external recovery support was engaged to assist the school. (p37)
* Quant: 3 points; approximately 1.5m (boiler room depth); half the school site (power loss); 2 weeks (school closure); planned recovery work in excess of £300k; total proposed resilience and recovery cost more than £1.2m (p2)

### Abingdon
* Flooding affected the location on 04-05 January 2024. (p1)
* The River Ock at Abingdon gauge recorded exceptionally high river flows (average daily flows over 16 m3/s), far above the long-term average, indicating a significant river flood event affecting Abingdon around the 04–05 January 2024 period. (p14) [IMG, PAG]
* River Ock levels rose following intense rainfall and high catchment runoff, leading to flooding that impacted roads and properties in locations around Abingdon. (p15) [IMG, PAG]
* River Ock and watercourses flowing into it (including the Wildmore Brook) rose and inundated roads and properties in multiple locations within Abingdon. Larkmead School was specifically impacted by flooding from watercourses across the site and by surface water runoff from the school grounds; the school's surface water pumps continued to operate so the school's own runoff likely made only a small contribution. The site's extremely flat ground levels increase its susceptibility to flooding. (p16) [IMG]
* Flooding occurred in several parts of Abingdon during 04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024 following very heavy rainfall; Wildmore Brook and surface water contributed to inundation of low-lying areas including the Larkmead School site. (p32)
* Quant: average flows of over 16 m3/s on 05 and 06 January 2024 (p14) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: average flows over 16 m3/s recorded at the River Ock at Abingdon gauge on 05 and 06 January 2024 (p15) [IMG, PAG]

### The Manor Preparatory School
* The school site is described as having large areas shown at risk from surface water (mainly low risk) and the report states the mapped main river (Larkhill Stream) was not the source of flooding on 04–05 Jan 2024. The text indicates local drainage features (notably Wildmore Brook with disused bridges) and surface water are likely to have raised water levels adjacent to the school and contributed to the flooding that affected the school site during 04–05 Jan 2024. (p9) [IMG]
* Flooding on 04–05 Jan 2024 caused the school to close for two weeks; flood recovery work was still affecting the running of the school in March 2024. Planned remedial works include relocating the boiler room. (p10) [IMG]
* Quant: closure for 2 weeks; more than £1.2m estimated recovery/remedial cost (p10) [IMG]

### Wildmore Brook
* Wildmore Brook (ordinary watercourse) overtopped its northern/site boundary and caused shallow out-of-bank flooding at three low spots into the Larkmead School site. Floodwater spread across the very flat site, entered an underground power cable (causing loss of power to half the site), flowed down steps into the below-ground boiler room filling it to about 1.5m, and entered a heating/utility duct; the loss of heating and internal flooding of buildings (music department and parts of the sports hall) led to the school closure and prolonged disruption. (p2)
* {01 Jan 2024 - 31 Jan 2024}: Wildmore Brook flooded (following a wet autumn and winter) and caused flooding of the school site. (p3) [TAB, PAG]
* Debris was present in the culvert on Wildmore Brook beneath the A34/Copenhagen Drive; the blocked/partially blocked culvert impeded flow and was identified for clearance to reduce/contribute to local flooding. (p4)
* The ordinary watercourse (Wildmore Brook, also called Larkmead Brook) flows along the northern boundary of the school and joins Larkhill Stream at the school entrance. Its flows are implicated in the flooding that affected the Larkmead School site. (p7)
* The text states there are disused bridges in Wildmore Brook that are likely restricting flows and increasing water levels upstream adjacent to the school during high flows; these restricted/high flows are described as a potential contributor to flooding affecting the school site. (p9)
* Mapping and analysis identify the highest flood risk concentrated along Larkhill Stream and adjacent low-lying areas; the stream was a focus of flooding risk during the 04–05 Jan 2024 event and likely contributed to local inundation of nearby land. (p10) [IMG]
* Overtopping of the Wildmore Brook’s southern bank overnight 04–05 Jan 2024 caused shallow out‑of‑bank flooding onto the adjacent school site. Floodwater reached an underground power cable causing loss of power to half the school, flowed down steps into the below‑ground boiler room filling it to approximately 1.5 m, inundated a heating/utility duct (causing loss of heating), and flooded the music department and parts of the sports hall. The impacts led to the school being closed for 2 weeks and ongoing recovery/remedial works (planned works estimated >£1.2m). (p11)
* Watercourses flowing into the River Ock, including Wildmore Brook, rose to levels that contributed to flooding impacts on roads and properties. (p15)
* {general}: Flooding from Wildmore Brook entered the site via a low spot and caused inundation that impacted an underground electricity cable. (p18) [IMG]
* Wildmore Brook (ordinary watercourse) overtopped and flooded onto the adjacent Larkmead School site during the heavy rainfall event on 03–04 Jan 2024. (p31) [PAG]
* Wildmore Brook (ordinary watercourse) exceeded its capacity following very heavy rainfall and flooded onto the Larkmead School site during 04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024. (p32)
* The brook is implicated in the flooding at the school; obstructions (including debris in a culvert and disused restricted concrete bridges adjacent to the school) reduced conveyance and contributed to flooding. Recommendations include clearing debris from the culvert under the A34/Copenhagen Drive and ensuring the brook is clear of significant obstructions. (p34) [TAB]
