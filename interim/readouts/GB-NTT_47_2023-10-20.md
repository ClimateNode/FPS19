

# Ompton flooding 20 Oct 2023

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Ompton – Storm Babet, October 2023<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/ompton-s19-storm-babet-oct-2023.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Ompton
* Village suffered a flood event caused by sustained heavy rainfall (Storm Babet) with flooding of roads and properties; ordinary watercourse (Gallow Hole Dyke) runs through the village and contributed to the event. (p1)
* Ompton experienced flooding of roads (Flash Lane, Newark Road, Shortwood Lane) and flooding of properties. The event caused internal flooding to residential properties and flooding of gardens and outbuildings in the village. (p2) [IMG]
* Residential properties experienced internal flooding; gardens and outbuildings were also flooded. Flooding was driven by surface water runoff converging on low points (highway junctions) and by the Gallow Hole Dyke rising and bursting its banks, producing higher flood levels than in 2019. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: Five residential properties (p2) [IMG]
* Quant: 5 residential properties (p3) [IMG, PAG]

### Nottinghamshire
* County experienced flooding that required emergency response actions: visited flood-affected residents and businesses, delivered sandbags, supported community flood signage schemes, closed roads to promote safety and avoid additional flood damage, shared resources with district/borough partners, led Section 19 flood investigation and coordinated delivery of the DEFRA Property Flood Resilience Repair Grant Scheme and recovery support. (p5) [PAG]

### Newark and Sherwood
* District experienced flood impacts: staff visited impacted communities and vulnerable individuals, assisted evacuations and provided emergency accommodation when necessary, delivered aqua sacs, helped clean up roads/paths and remove bulky waste from flooded homes, contacted affected individuals to identify humanitarian needs, coordinated in-person visits to high-risk communities as River Trent levels were predicted to be extreme, and is delivering grant funding to eligible residents. (p5)

### Flash Lane, Ompton
* Flash Lane was flood-affected (road flooding) as part of the Ompton event; the local area experienced flooding of roads and properties, with residential internal flooding and garden/outbuilding flooding reported in Ompton. (p2) [IMG]
* Highway surface water flowed south along the road during the event, affecting properties on Flash Lane. (p3)
* Quant: Five residential properties (p2) [IMG]

### Newark Road, Ompton
* Newark Road was flood-affected (road flooding) within Ompton; the event affected roads and led to property flooding in the village, including internal flooding of residences and flooding of gardens/outbuildings. (p2) [IMG]
* The Gallow Hole Dyke passes under Newark Road and the road/junctions form a low point where runoff concentrates. Water on the highway accumulated and flowed from Newark Road, contributing to property flooding and allowing dyke overtopping to affect nearby properties. (p3) [IMG]
* Quant: Five residential properties (p2) [IMG]

### Shortwood Lane, Ompton
* Shortwood Lane was flood-affected (road flooding) in Ompton; the wider Ompton flooding event led to internal flooding of residential properties and flooding of gardens and outbuildings in the village. (p2) [IMG]
* Highway surface water flowed north along the road during the event, affecting properties on Shortwood Lane. (p3)
* Quant: Five residential properties (p2) [IMG]

### River Trent
* Predicted extreme river levels from the River Trent elevated the continued risk of river flooding after an initial surface water flooding phase; this prompted coordinated in-person community visits and preparedness actions by the council. (p5)


## Topics without coordinates

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* As Lead Local Flood Authority responding to flooding from Storm Babet, the council coordinated a multi-agency debrief, worked with parish and town councils to restock community stores of aqua sacs, developed Flood Warden / Road Closure training, reviewed its Property Flood Resilience Programme, and worked with partners and landowners on riparian responsibilities and surface water management. (p6)
