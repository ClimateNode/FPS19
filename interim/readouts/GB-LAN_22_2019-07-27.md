

# Lancashire flooding 27 Jul 2019 - 01 Aug 2019

Reference document:<br>27 July – 1 August 2019 Flooding In Lancashire: Section 19 Investigation Report<br>069\_Lancashire County/section-19-flood-investigation-report-july-august-2019-flooding.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Lancashire
* Lancashire experienced flooding during the event spanning 27 July to 1 August 2019. (p1)
* Summer storms (27–29 July 2019) and further rainfall on 1 August 2019 temporarily overwhelmed local drainage systems across Lancashire, causing extensive flooding of properties and highways. Records show 102 properties flooded (91 homes and 11 commercial/agricultural properties), with 14 homes flooded internally to at least one habitable room; there is a report of one further property potentially flooded (possible total 103 properties). (p3) [MUL]
* Part of Lancashire experienced flooding during the event; the area covered by the Earby and Salterforth Internal Drainage Board was affected by floodwater. (p10)
* Widespread flooding occurred across large areas of the county as a series of intense rainfall events between 27 July 2019 and 1 August 2019, affecting multiple communities and prompting localised site inspections by risk management authorities. (p12) [PAG]
* Heavy rainfall across the county resulted in property flooding: one property in Fylde flooded on 27 July 2019; on 28 July 2019 a total of 25 properties were reported flooded within Lancashire (4 in western Lancashire across Fylde, Chorley and Preston; 1 in Pendle; 20 in Rossendale — all 20 in the town of Whitworth). (p13) [IMG]
* Widespread external flooding to residential properties across multiple districts within the county and standing surface water on main and residential roads, causing travel disruptions. (p15) [PAG]
* External flooding affected properties across multiple districts within the county and widespread standing water and travel disruption on the local road network. (p16) [PAG]
* Repeated bands of heavy rain across the county between 27 July and 1 August 2019 caused flooding; county council Highway Service inspected affected highway locations, removed silt from gullies and drainage systems, and flood waters subsided the following morning. (p17) [PAG]
* Extreme rainfall between 27 July and 1 August 2019 overwhelmed many drainage networks in parts of Lancashire, causing flooding that damaged homes and belongings and raised serious concerns for personal safety among affected people. (p19) [PAG]
* Extreme rainfall between 27 July and 1 August 2019 overwhelmed local drainage networks across the county, causing flooding of homes and business premises and damage to people's homes and belongings; significant concern for personal safety was reported. (p20) [PAG]
* Quant: 102 properties; 91 homes; 11 commercial/agricultural properties; 14 homes flooded internally; possible total of 103 properties (p3) [MUL]
* Quant: 1 property (27 Jul 2019); 25 properties (28 Jul 2019); 20 properties (in Whitworth, Rossendale) (p13) [IMG]
* Quant: 75 properties across the county (29 Jul 2019: 21 properties; 30–31 Jul 2019: 34 properties; 1 Aug 2019: 20 properties) (p15) [PAG]
* Quant: 5 properties (Chorley); 1 property (Hyndburn); 1 property (Preston); 2 properties (Ribble Valley); 1 property (Rossendale); 10 properties (West Lancashire); 8 properties (Whitworth) (p16) [PAG]
* Quant: 103 individual homes and business premises (p20) [PAG]

### Whitworth
* Concentrated property flooding in the town of Whitworth (southern/SE Rossendale) caused by the heavy, persistent rainfall on 28 July 2019. (p13) [PAG]
* Residential properties in the town flooded following heavy, localised rainfall. (p14) [PAG]
* External flooding to properties in Whitworth: 7 properties flooded on 29 July 2019 and 3 properties flooded on 30–31 July 2019 (these counts are reported as Whitworth-specific within the Rossendale totals). (p15) [PAG]
* At least 8 properties in Whitworth flooded during the event; a rest centre was opened for affected residents (it was later closed unused). Properties around Station Road, Millfold, John Street and Daniel Street and the football ground were affected and a flood warning was issued. (p16) [IMG, MUL]
* Homes and gardens in Whitworth were flooded by surface water during the event; affected residents engaged with investigations and local response efforts (including the Whitworth Flood Action Group). (p18)
* Intense rainfall on 28 July 2019 produced rapid runoff from open fields and moorland which was funnelled by estate roads and footpaths into a deep flow, causing particularly devastating flood damage in Whitworth. (p20)
* Quant: 20 properties (28 Jul 2019) (p13) [PAG]
* Quant: 20 properties on 28 July 2019; 7 properties on 29 July 2019; total 27 properties (p14) [PAG]
* Quant: 7 properties (29 Jul 2019); 3 properties (30–31 Jul 2019) (p15) [PAG]
* Quant: 8 properties (p16) [IMG, MUL]

## Topics without coordinates

### Highways
* The Highway Service responded during working hours to reports of standing water and other flood-related problems on the local road network. (p16)
* Starting the morning after the event, the county council's Highway Service attended every reported or known highway location affected by flooding to inspect conditions and remove silt from gullies and drainage systems; this response continued through the week. (p17)

### Environment Agency
* Issued a flood warning for Whitworth on 28 July 2019 in response to flooding that affected properties around Station Road, Millfold, John Street and Daniel Street and the nearby football ground. (p16) [MUL]

### United Utilities
* United Utilities carried out inspections and exercises on the public sewer network following the heavy rainfall to identify and address impacts on the sewer system during the flooding event. (p17)

### Yorkshire Water
* Carried out inspections and response activities on the public sewer network after the heavy rainfall to address blockages and maintain sewer flows. (p17)

### Wallbank Estate
* Heavy flows of surface water moved in a straight line through roads and ginnels on the estate, contributing to highway-related runoff affecting nearby homes and gardens; raised kerbs and other alterations were later installed in vulnerable locations to disperse flows and reduce future impacts. (p18)

### Together Housing
* Properties owned and managed by Together Housing were among the affected homes; the organisation considered making adaptations to more vulnerable properties to reduce future ingress of flood water. (p18)
