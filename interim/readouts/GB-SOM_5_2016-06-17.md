

# Wraxall flooding 17 Jun 2016

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: A37 Fosse Way, Wraxall, 17th June 2016<br>113\_Somerset County/Flood Investigation Report Wraxall.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### A37 at Wraxall
* {24 Aug 2012}: Surface water flooding of the A37 carriageway (Fosse Way is the A37) between Wraxall and Bengrove Farm, causing flooding of the highway. (p10) [MUL]
* {24 Aug 2012}: Surface water on the A37 where a low kerb allowed water to run over into a nearby property; this prompted construction of a Bitmac berm on the property frontage on 06/12/2012 to prevent highway runoff threatening the property. (p11) [MUL]
* Flood water on the A37 carriageway. SCC and landowners cleared the culvert inlet and outlet and SCC removed up to 450 mm of silt from the culvert build-up, which improved water flow through the culvert and provided relief to flood water on the highway, reducing the threat of flooding to nearby property. SCC also removed a willow tree above the culvert's inlet at the Lye Lane junction with the A37. Ditches adjacent to the A37 (north and south bound) between the property known as Coppits and the junction for Stone required considerable clearing and an annual maintenance programme was recommended. A 150 mm pipe running under the north-bound carriageway (approx. 70 m south of Leighbridge Lodge) was suspected by residents to be blocked; SCC arranged CCTV survey and jetting (works to be completed in 2016/17). (p14)

### Wraxall
* Area south of Wraxall: four properties were internally flooded to depths up to 300 mm; the highway was flooded to depths up to 400 mm. (p4)
* The A37 at the junction with Kilkenny Lane (in Wraxall) is described as a low point where catchment flows combine and cross the road. Highway drainage in South Wraxall is noted as susceptible to flooding and is scheduled for gully cleansing according to flood susceptibility. (p9) [PAG, IMG]
* {24 Aug 2012}: Surface water flooding of the A37 highway in the Wraxall area (highway carriageway flooded) as recorded in Somerset County Council's database. (p10) [MUL]
* Part of the area affected by the exceptional rainfall event on 2016-06-17; residents cooperated with the investigation and experienced flooding from rapid intense rainfall on saturated ground. (p13)

### East Pennard
* Experienced flooding associated with the exceptional short‑duration rainfall on saturated soils on 2016-06-17; residents in the East Pennard area were involved in the investigation and reported impacts from the event. (p13)

### Kilkenny Lane, Kilkenny
* {general}: A shallower channel runs eastward along Kilkenny Lane conveying combined flows from the slopes; adjacent fields in the Kilkenny area are often waterlogged in wet winter periods. The low point at the A37/Kilkenny Lane junction is where these flows cross the A37. (p9) [PAG]
* {general}: Fields in the Kilkenny area are often waterlogged in wet winter periods due to poor natural drainage and flat clayey soils; there is also a shallower drainage channel eastward along Kilkenny Lane and a low point at its junction with the A37 where runoff concentrates. (p10) [PAG]

### Lye Lane, East Pennard
* The flood event originated at the A37 junction with Lye Lane; surface water flooding from this location was central to the incident which directly affected four properties and prompted local correspondence and concern. (p8) [PAG, IMG]
* The culvert at the A37 / Lye Lane junction acted as a pinch point during heavy rainfall: inlet/outlet obscured by dense vegetation and ~300mm silt build‑up (≈25PCSYM capacity reduction), impeding flow and contributing to highway flooding and risk to nearby properties during the event. Subsequent clearance of vegetation, silt (up to 450mm cleared) and removal of a willow reduced flood threat and improved flow. (p13) [PAG]
