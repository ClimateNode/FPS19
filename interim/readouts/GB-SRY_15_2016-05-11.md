### Woking

This produced internal flooding of homes across the area, closure of three schools, at least one road closure in the Maybury area and major traffic delays/grid-lock; Category 1 responders (police, fire, ambulance), Woking Borough Council, Surrey County Council, Thames Water and the Environment Agency attended the incident to prioritise emergency works and assist residents. (p3)
* The flood event caused major traffic delays and grid-lock across the Woking area; internal flooding of properties occurred (45 properties reported across the incident) and three schools were closed. Category 1 responders attended the rapidly developing situation. (p4)
* On 11th May 2016 a localised convective storm stalled over Woking (approx. 11:00–13:30), producing exceptional short‑duration intensity rainfall that caused localized flash/surface-water flooding focused on parts of Woking (notably the Maybury area). (p7) [PAG]
* The report documents flooding that occurred in Woking during the 11 May 2016 event; surface water/urban flooding affected parts of the borough and triggered RMA response and post‑event assessment. (p9) [IMG, PAG]

### Rive Ditch
* Main channel reached capacity quickly and flooded surrounding areas; neighbouring sewer networks and gullies were overwhelmed and surcharged, preventing drainage and causing overland flows and flash flooding of low-lying areas. This led to internal property flooding, school closures and road closures with major traffic disruption in the Woking area. (p4)
* Flooding occurred along the Rive Ditch, contributing to urban flash flooding and surface water flows that led to internal property flooding and road closures in adjacent areas. (p5)
* Flooding occurred along the Rive Ditch on 11th May 2016; the Rive Ditch formed the linear watercourse along which the flash flood extent propagated, affecting adjacent urban areas (Maybury, West Byfleet, Sheerwater). (p6) [IMG, PAG]
* The Rive Ditch (an ordinary watercourse that becomes an adopted surface water sewer and then a main river) is implicated in localised flooding in the catchment. Sections are shown to be at risk of fluvial flooding (Flood Zones 2 and 3) and the channel/sewer network contributes to surface water flow routes that affected Maybury and West Byfleet during the reported flood event. (p9) [IMG, PAG]
* The Main River section overtopped during the event and flooded neighbouring residential area in West Byfleet; trash screens were inspected/cleared pre-incident and attended during the incident. (p12) [PAG]
* The Rive Ditch main river section overtopped, causing flooding of adjacent residential properties; residents reported property flooding adjacent to the Rive Ditch. (p13) [PAG]
* Trash screen at the junction with the Wey Navigation became completely clogged with debris; the ditch exceeded pipe capacity causing back-up and overtopping that flooded neighbouring residential areas; teams cleared debris continuously and Rangers pumped water from the Rive Ditch into the Basingstoke Canal to help reduce rising flood waters. (p14) [PAG]
* The pipe capacity was exceeded causing water to back up along the Rive Ditch. Debris built up against the trash screen and responders (BCA Rangers, the EA, fire services and Runnymede officers) repeatedly cleared the screen. BCA Rangers pumped water from the Rive Ditch into the Basingstoke Canal to reduce the rate of rising flood waters. (p15) [PAG]
* The Rive Ditch trash screen needed to be kept clear of debris by attending contractors during the event, indicating the watercourse was implicated in the fluvial flooding response. (p18)
* Overflow / flooding associated with Rive Ditch during the 11 May 2016 event that impacted nearby communities; data from the flood was used to refine the Rive Ditch flood alleviation scheme hydrological model. (p19)
* Quant: 45 properties; three schools; one road closure (p4)
* Quant: 45 internal property floods; 2 road closures (p5)

### Runnymede
* Runnymede Borough Council teams attended Rive Ditch on the morning of 11 May 2016 and cleared the trash screen at the junction with the Wey Navigation. The trash screen became completely clogged during the event and contractors continuously removed debris to try to restore flow; contractors worked through the afternoon with EA staff until water levels dropped to near normal. The ditch capacity was exceeded, causing water to back up along the Rive Ditch and residents reported property flooding adjacent to the ditch and flooding on Scotland Bridge Road. (p14)
