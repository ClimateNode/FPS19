

# Croydon flooding February 2014 - March 2014

## Place names with coordinates

### Reddown Road, Croydon
* Gardens at a number of residential properties in Reddown Road (which back onto the London to Brighton main line) were flooded for several weeks during the February–March 2014 event. (p5)
* Overland flows from the Bourne passed through an open ditch between the Brighton main line railway and the rear gardens of residential properties on Reddown Road during the February–March 2014 event, affecting the gardens adjacent to the ditch and conveying floodwater toward the drainage network. (p10)
* {July 1976, August 1981}: Recorded surface-water flooding incidents at Reddown Road: July 1976 — surface water flooding reported in the garden of one property due to 'blocked gullies'; August 1981 — surface water flooding reported at one property. (p12) [TAB, MUL]
* Back gardens of properties along Reddown Road were flooded when water escaped from the underground Merstham Bourne (culvert damage) and flowed through gardens; floodwaters reached depths up to 30 cm and persisted for several weeks before fully receding in late March 2014. (p16) [MUL]
* Merstham Bourne overflowed into residential gardens and patios in Reddown Road; gardens were inundated with standing water and an open culvert section was overwhelmed, contributing to water backing up toward the railway embankment. (p17) [PAG]
* Merstham Bourne flowed between residential gardens on Reddown Road, bringing high water adjacent to properties and vegetation. (p18) [IMG]
* {July 1976, August 1981}: Quant: one property (July 1976); one property (August 1981) (p12) [TAB, MUL]
* Quant: Numbers 20, 20A and 18 had water entering gardens; water also flowed along backs of Nos.16 and 8A; flood depths up to 30 cm (p16) [MUL]

### Coulsdon South Station
* Railway infrastructure around South Coulsdon Station was flooded for several weeks during the February–March 2014 event. (p5)
* {general}: The report states there was flooding at a location near Coulsdon South Station (the area of flooding is underlain by superficial deposits); specific impacts at the station are not detailed in the text. (p12)
* Flood flow paths (known and assumed) converge near Coulsdon South Station and the approximate flooded area is adjacent to the station; flooding occurred in the residential area immediately downstream of these flow paths, placing infrastructure and nearby developments at risk. (p16) [IMG, MUL]
* Flooding from an overwhelmed culvert flowing north towards Coulsdon South station inundated the railway, causing signals to trip and slow line speeds and disruption to rail services. (p17) [PAG]
* An overwhelmed culvert near the station caused pooling and led to flooding of the railway track (water discharged from the culvert beneath the line inundated the track). (p18) [IMG]

### Croydon
* Flooding associated with the Merstham/Coulsdon Bourne occurred in the south of the borough (South Coulsdon) during February–March 2014, affecting local railway infrastructure and residential gardens. (p5)
* Parts of Croydon were affected by the Merstham Bourne flooding in February–March 2014: gardens were flooded and transport infrastructure was disrupted (railway line flooded), with identified risks to residential properties and transport if maintenance is not carried out. (p6)

### Surrey
* Areas south of the Croydon borough boundary in Surrey experienced flooding near the north end of Merstham Tunnel during the event. (p17)

### Coulsdon
* Investigation focused on impacts from the ephemeral Merstham (Coulsdon) Bourne at the open ditch section near Coulsdon South Station where the bourne flows adjacent to the railway and rear gardens; the event involved overland/ephemeral watercourse flow into the surface water drainage network affecting this area. (p10)
* Localised flooding occurred in the Coulsdon area where the Merstham Bourne emerges from a damaged culvert; floodwaters affected residential gardens adjacent to Network Rail land and created a local flood risk that required monitoring by the Environment Agency because recent ground-floor flats nearby could be threatened. (p16) [IMG, MUL]
* Flooding affected gardens in residential areas (water up to 30 cm in gardens), coming very close to properties and posing a serious potential risk; residential buildings were not entered by floodwater. (p21)
* Quant: gardens flooded to depths up to 30 cm; 0 residential properties flooded (p21)

### Caterham
* {July 1976, August 1981}: Flooding from the Caterham Bourne has been recorded at the same dates as the Merstham Bourne incidents and is described as causing significantly more disruption than the Merstham Bourne. (p12) [MUL]

### Woodplace Lane, Croydon
* The railway line adjacent to Woodplace Lane was flooded as part of the February–March 2014 event, contributing to transport disruption. (p6) [TAB]
* Railway adjacent to Woodplace Lane was flooded as part of a 530 metre flooded section caused by an overwhelmed culvert, contributing to signalling trips and service disruption. (p17)

### Netherne Drive, Croydon
* The railway line at/near Star Bridge (Nethern Drive) was within the 530 m stretch flooded during February–March 2014, causing disruption to rail infrastructure. (p6) [TAB]
* A 530 metre length of railway between Woodplace Lane and Star Bridge (Nethern Drive) was flooded when an overwhelmed culvert allowed the Merstham Bourne to flow north. Flooding caused signals to trip, slow line speeds and disruption to rail travel. (p17)

### Brighton Road, Purley
* The Bourne’s route follows the line of the A23/railway valley; during the February–March 2014 event flows were conveyed along this corridor into the surface water drainage network toward Purley Cross, with valley topography and concentrated runoff exacerbating flow accumulation along the A23/railway corridor. (p10)

### Marlpit Lane, Croydon
* Experienced flow from the Merstham Bourne connecting to the sewer causing foul/sewer-related flooding; tankers were deployed to try to remove water at Marlpit Lane but were ineffective while the bourne continued to flow; works were carried out on open ditch sections to increase capacity and storage. (p19) [TAB]


## Topics without coordinates

### Merstham Bourne
* Ephemeral watercourse that flowed during the event; flooding associated with the Bourne affected South Coulsdon. Flood risk is linked to permeable underlying geology allowing groundwater to rise and cause the Bourne and local springs to flow. (p5)
* Overflow of the Merstham Bourne during February–March 2014 caused local flooding that affected gardens and contributed to flooding of the railway line between Woodplace Lane and Star Bridge (Nethern Drive). Unclear ownership and lack of maintenance at this location were identified as possible contributing factors increasing flood risk. (p6) [TAB]
* Flooding around the Bourne during the February–March 2014 incident prompted responses by significant landowners (Network Rail) and the relevant risk management authorities. (p7)
* Following heavy rainfall in February–March 2014 the ephemeral Merstham (Coulsdon) Bourne flowed along its channel through the valley at the base of the A23/rail corridor. Overland flow ran in an open ditch between the Brighton main line railway and the rear gardens of properties on Reddown Road, inundating the open section near Coulsdon South Station and entering the surface water drainage network toward Purley Cross. (p10)
* {general}: Historic records held by the council indicate flooding from the Merstham Bourne is minimal in the archive, but the bourne is known to rise (often concurrently with the nearby Caterham Bourne) and has been associated with high groundwater conditions that contribute to local flooding. (p12)
* A damaged/cracked culvert carrying the Merstham Bourne discharged water through the ground by the railway embankment, causing flow along the rear gardens of Reddown Road properties (Nos. 20, 20A, 18, 16 and 8A). Floodwaters up to 30 cm deep were observed in the gardens and remained for several weeks, not fully receding until late March 2014. The EA attended circa 19 February 2014 and monitored levels because rising floodwater could threaten newly built ground-floor flats nearby. (p16)
* The Merstham Bourne overflowed between gardens on Reddown Road and the railway embankment; its flow overwhelmed a culvert, flooding gardens and inundating the railway and causing signals to trip and rail service disruption. (p17) [PAG]
* High flows in the Merstham Bourne flowed between residential gardens and overwhelmed a culvert, contributing to flooding of nearby railway infrastructure. (p18) [IMG, PAG]
* Flow in Merstham Bourne overwhelmed the culvert/ditches, continued to flow through the culvert beneath the railway and contributed to flooding at the railway line and Marlpit Lane; tankers were deployed to remove water at Marlpit Lane but were ineffective while the bourne continued to flow; works were carried out on open ditch sections to enlarge capacity and extra storage, and emergency works were undertaken on the damaged culvert. (p19)
* {February 2014 - March 2014, Feb–Mar 2014}: Flooding from the Merstham Bourne inundated gardens (approaching properties) to depths up to 30 cm, did not enter residential properties, and caused flooding to the railway line that produced signal failures and service disruption on the main London to Brighton Line. (p20) [PAG]
* Flooding from the Merstham Bourne inundated gardens close to residential properties, coming very near to entering homes and posing a serious potential risk. (p21)
* The Merstham Bourne was a local source of flooding during the reported event. Flooding associated with the bourne affected properties/residents (residents reported flooding) and assets along its route, and flows into a culvert under Marlpit Lane — triggering recommendations to clarify land ownership, survey the channel, and coordinate maintenance of railway (NR) and other assets along the bourne. (p22) [TAB]

### Network Rail
* Open ditch sections and culverts of the Merstham Bourne traverse Network Rail land; a crack or damage to the culvert adjacent to the railway embankment allowed water to gush out of the ground, contributing to flooding of neighbouring gardens. Network Rail land therefore was a pathway/source for escaped floodwaters and is identified as having riparian responsibilities. (p16) [MUL]
* Flooding of the railway infrastructure: a culvert was overwhelmed causing water to inundate a stretch of railway, signals tripped and resulted in reduced line speeds and travel disruption; flooding also occurred south of the Borough boundary near the north end of Merstham Tunnel in Surrey. (p17)
* Flooding of the railway track where a culvert beneath the line became inundated; Network Rail provided a channel above the culvert to redirect flow, engaged pumps to remove water from the track, implemented emergency works on the damaged culvert and commenced an investigation into the material, diameter and condition of the part-lined culvert following the railway. (p19) [TAB, MUL]
* {February 2014 - March 2014, Feb–Mar 2014}: Held principal responsibility for maintenance of the ditch and culvert through their land; contractors attended to implement emergency repairs to a damaged culvert. Subsequently carried out widening of the open ditch and created an additional channel above the damaged culvert; investigating the condition and size of a culvert beneath the railway. Flooding impacted the railway line causing signal failures and service disruption on the London to Brighton Line. (p20)
* As riparian owner NR carried out response activities to manage and respond to the Bourne flooding; issues in coordination and maintenance responsibilities were noted arising from the event. (p21)
* Quant: 5 properties adjacent to NR land flooded (p16)
* {February 2014 - March 2014, Feb–Mar 2014}: Quant: gardens flooded to depth up to 30 cm (p20)

### Croydon Council
* As LLFA, attended the flood site, assessed risk to residents, directed clearing of trash screens, delivered sandbags to affected properties, deployed tankers to attempt water removal at Marlpit Lane, and monitored groundwater and flows during the incident. (p19) [TAB]
* Croydon Council (as the lead local authority) carried out response activities to manage and respond to flooding from the Merstham Bourne; the event highlighted needs for ongoing watercourse maintenance and improved communication with Network Rail over flood management and riparian responsibilities. (p21) [PAG]

### Environment Agency
* Provided strategic oversight and groundwater monitoring: supplied weekly updates on groundwater levels to Croydon Council and attended the site at Coulsdon South Station to ensure flood risk was being addressed. (p19) [TAB]
* {February 2014 - March 2014, Feb–Mar 2014}: Attended the site with a strategic overview role (no main watercourses at the site) and participated in the response to flooding from the Merstham Bourne. (p20)

### Merstham Tunnel
* Flooding was experienced near the north end of the Merstham Tunnel (south of the Croydon borough boundary), affecting the railway corridor in that area. (p17)

### Thames Water
* Responsible for the culvert in Marlpit Lane where the bourne connects to the sewer and for managing foul (sewer) flooding there; contractor attended site on 19 February 2014 to investigate solutions to a damaged culvert; carried out works on open ditch sections to enlarge capacity and provide extra storage; reported no specific operational issues recorded during the incident. (p19) [TAB, MUL]

### Brighton Main Line
* Railway line experienced flooding which caused signal failures and service disruption on the main London to Brighton Line during the event. (p21)
