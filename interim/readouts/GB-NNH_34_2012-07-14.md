### Grendon
* The flood on Blackmile Lane (within Grendon) caused internal flooding of properties and closure of the highway; records indicate similar flooding has occurred at this location in the past. (p4)
* The village experienced flooding during the event of 14 July 2012: significant rainfall over the local catchment produced runoff that caused chest-deep flooding on Blackmile Lane, required emergency services assistance, closed the highway and led to internal flooding of nearby properties. (p5)
* Blackmile Lane, on the northern edge of Grendon Village, experienced surface water/flash flooding that led to residents being evacuated (31 properties) and temporary sheltering at Grendon Hall. (p6)
* Parts of Grendon (specifically Lower End and the area around Hall Farm) experienced flooding where main rivers and ordinary watercourses converged, creating the deepest flood point in the area. (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* {Easter 1998, 14 Jul 2012}: The civil parish area contains the low-lying catchment around Blackmile Lane where flooding concentrated near the confluence of main rivers (deepest flooding marked on the LiDAR-derived map). Recorded localised flooding events affecting this locality include Easter 1998 (channel capacity exceeded) and heavy rainfall affecting the catchment on 14 July 2012. (p8) [IMG, MUL]
* Assessed the main river and identified out-of-bank flows: two upstream locations where Grendon Brook overtopped its channel at natural low points; wrack lines showed out-of-bank flows that followed the channel to relatively shallow depths. Concluded a small amount of water left the channel but high water levels and limited channel capacity may have contributed to increased surface water in the catchment. (p12) [MUL]
* The Grendon area experienced a short, intense rainfall event between midnight and 04:30 on 14 July 2012 (44 mm reported), which contributed to saturation of the local catchment. (p22)
* There had been flooding in the area on 14 July 2012; a flood warning covering Grendon was issued at 06:52 on 14 July 2012. (p26)
* {general}: Identified as a designated Environment Agency flood warning area that follows the main watercourse through central Grendon; residential and farm buildings near the watercourse are shown as being at risk. (p27) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: more than 5 properties (p4)

* Quant: flood depth just under 1 m at the deepest point affecting Lower End and Hall Farm (p7) [IMG, PAG]

### Grendon Brook
* Grendon Brook is one of the main rivers that converges near Hall Farm/Lower End and contributed to flooding at that confluence. (p7)
* {Easter 1998, 14 Jul 2012}: Grendon Brook (a main river close to Blackmile Lane) is associated with the area of deepest flooding shown on the map; river reaches follow low-lying zones and contributed to localised flooding when channel capacity was exceeded (recorded at Easter 1998) and during the heavy rainfall event that saturated the catchment prior to 14 July 2012. (p8) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* {general}: The river came out of its channel at two naturally occurring low points in the field during the flood event; out-of-bank flows followed the channel course to shallow depths. It was concluded only a small amount of water left the channel, though limited channel capacity due to high water may have contributed to surface water in the catchment. (p12)
* {01 Apr 2012 - 14 Jul 2012, 14 Jul 2012}: Grendon Brook is included in the discussion of the Grendon Brook catchment being saturated by repeated intense rainfall leading up to and including the event on 14 July 2012. (p22) [MUL]
* Investigations found only two small sections where a shallow depth of water came out of Grendon Brook (shallow out-of-bank flow); these were considered too small to account for the overall flooding experienced. (p23) [PAG]
* Quant: flood depth just under 1 m at the confluence (deepest point) (p7)
* {general}: Quant: two locations upstream (p12)

## Topics without coordinates

### Environment Agency
* Surface water flowed across fields and channelled onto Blackmile Lane, causing pooling and flooding at the junction with Main Road (maximum pooling depth about 1 m). Access to properties (including flooded ones) was obstructed for several hours. (p10)
* EA site surveys identified two upstream locations where Grendon Brook came out of its channel at naturally occurring low points; wrack lines indicate out‑of‑bank flows that closely followed the river course to relatively shallow depths. EA concluded only a small amount of water left the channel, though limited channel capacity may have contributed to surface water in the catchment. Drains and the Gibbards Arm channel were heavily silted as a result of the flood event. (p12)
* {01 Jul 2012 - 31 Jul 2012}: Investigations established there was little flooding from the river; only two small sections of the Grendon Brook experienced shallow overspill. The surge of water was attributed to intense rainfall over the catchment. Five flood storage reservoirs (at Denton, Bozeat and three at Yardley Hastings) operated as designed: penstocks remained at fixed positions with no manual or automatic operation, water backed up behind reservoir embankments, emergency spillways were not used, and no sluice gates opened. (p24) [PAG]
* {14 Jul 2012, 19 Jul 2012, 23 Jul 2012}: Received a report on 19 July 2012 about a tree in the watercourse linked to flooding on 14 July 2012; the Agency arranged removal of the tree on 23 July 2012 and closed the call. (p27) [IMG, MUL]
* Quant: approximately 31 properties; 1m (p10)
* Quant: two locations upstream (p12) [MUL]

### Grendon Brook Catchment
* {01 Apr 2012 - 14 Jul 2012, 14 Jul 2012}: The catchment was described as being saturated by regular periods of intense rainfall in the period from April 2012 to 14 July 2012; a short, intense storm on 14 July 2012 (44 mm in 4.5 hours in the Grendon area) contributed to saturation of the Grendon Brook catchment. (p22) [MUL]
* {14 Jul 2012, 21 Nov 2012 - 28 Nov 2012}: Catchment was saturated in July and November 2012 so rainfall rapidly entered natural and artificial drainage systems, producing excess surface water flowing over ground and contributing to flooding collecting in the village low point. (p23) [MUL]
* A surge of water caused by the sheer volume of rain over the catchment was identified as the cause of the flooding experienced (the report states the surge from intense catchment rainfall, not river overtopping, explained the amount of flooding). (p24)
* A flood warning for the Grendon Brook Catchment (including associated communities) was issued at 06:52 on 14 July 2012; warnings are issued based on water levels in the Flood Storage Reservoirs. (p26)
* Quant: 263 contacts registered to receive this warning; 31 contacts reported unsuccessful on 14 July 2012. (p26)

### Gibbards Arm

* Drains and the channel of the Gibbards Arm were heavily silted as a result of the flood event. A small culvert on the Gibbards Arm was completely submerged and there was flooding over a significant length of highway downstream of that structure. (p12)
* A small culvert on the Gibbards Arm was completely submerged; there was flooding over a significant length of highway downstream of the structure. (p13)
* The volume and rate of runoff exceeded the capacity of Gibbards Arm, contributing to an accumulation of water that resulted in flooding of some properties when combined with overwhelmed local drainage networks. (p14)
