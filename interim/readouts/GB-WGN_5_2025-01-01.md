### Platt Bridge
* Platt Bridge was impacted by widespread flooding on 1 January 2025. The report identifies multiple sources affecting the area including fluvial (river) flooding, surface water flooding and sewer flooding across the study areas (Templeton Road and Bickershaw Lane). (p11)
* The rainstorm on 1 January 2025 was the main driver of observed flooding in Platt Bridge. Sources included extreme rainfall, overtopping of Hey Brook and Brookside Brook Drain, groundwater emergence in the Bickershaw Lane area, and highway drains and sewers surcharging. Receptors included internal flooding, submersion of the Templeton Road pumping station, resident displacement, loss of possessions, and negative health impacts. The event resulted in internal flooding of residential and commercial properties in the area. (p12)
* Fluvial flooding from Hey Brook, interacting with surface water, sewer and groundwater mechanisms, resulted in flooding of local properties and businesses, flooding of road networks, submersion of Templeton Road pumping station, disruption to local services, resident displacement, loss of possessions, and negative mental and physical health impacts for the local community. (p13) [PAG]
* The 1 January 2025 flood event inundated two distinct parts of Platt Bridge: the Bickershaw Lane area (Bickershaw Lane, Keats Way, Simpkin Street) and the Templeton Road area (Templeton Road, Walthew Lane, Platt Street). Each area was affected by different flooding mechanisms (not specified in the provided text). The report does not provide flood depths or specific internal water ingress routes for affected properties. (p17) [IMG]
* {general}: Platt Bridge and its central area are shown on the EA mapping to lie within higher fluvial flood risk zones, with the highest (darkest blue) flood extents concentrated adjacent to the main river and watercourses near Templeton Road. The area is included within detailed hydraulic modelling for 1PCSYM and 0.1PCSYM annual probability events as part of the Hindley Flood Risk Management Scheme. (p24) [IMG, PAG]
* Modelled fluvial flood extents (1PCSYM and 0.1PCSYM annual probability) affect parts of the Platt Bridge study area. Flood-prone areas are concentrated around the main river and extend into residential and recreational zones within the Platt Bridge area. (p25)
* Surface water flood extents (1PCSYM and increasing in 0.1PCSYM events) cover or partly cover multiple streets in the north-west of the study area associated with Platt Bridge (including Elm Street, Templeton Road and other local streets). Sewers in Platt Bridge were constructed prior to 1980 and may be of lower capacity, increasing the risk of sewer flooding during larger rainfall/flood events. (p27) [IMG, PAG]
* {29 Oct 2000, 26 Dec 2015}: Multiple recorded flood incidents in the Platt Bridge area: 18 properties identified as flooded when Hey Brook overtopped the right bank (26 December 2015). Surface water contributed where the culvert/highway drain on Platt Street was severely blocked by tree roots, exacerbating impacts during high receiving watercourse levels. Residents reported widespread historic flooding in the vicinity of properties (questionnaire responses: just over 78PCSYM of respondents had experienced flooding prior to 1 January 2025). (p28) [TAB]
* External property flooding affected the Platt Bridge area (residents on Bickershaw Lane reported external flooding); local mitigation (scrapes) have been installed upstream but are reported ineffective/inundated by groundwater. (p30)
* Internal flooding of residential properties caused by floodwater entering from a low point on the right bank of Hey Brook and spreading westwards into the Templeton Road area. The combined sewer and surface water sewer systems became overwhelmed and specific gulleys/manholes were observed to have surcharged (first manhole to surcharge along the highway drain noted near Platt Street). A pumping station lost power within the flooded zone, likely exacerbating flooding. Confirmed internally flooded properties: 10 properties on Templeton Road, 9 properties on Walthw Lane, and 2 properties on Platt Street. (p44) [IMG, PAG]
* Extreme rainfall in Platt Bridge and the surrounding area led to increased fluvial flows and the combined and surface water sewerage systems becoming overwhelmed. (p45)
* Water from Hey Brook backed up via the highway drain outfall and surcharged highway gullies on Platt Street (first gulley to surcharge was outside the Post Office). Drone footage shows flood extents across the Templeton Road area. Residential properties and businesses were flooded, causing house repair costs, replacement of possessions, higher energy bills for drying (dehumidifiers/heaters), displaced residents moved to temporary accommodation, lost sentimental belongings and significant stress/mental-health impacts. (p47)
* Extreme, double-peaked rainfall in Platt Bridge increased fluvial flows and overwhelmed the combined sewer system, contributing to flooding across the area (combined sewer overwhelmed and high fluvial flows). (p51)
* Platt Bridge area experienced flooding that generated a high volume of emergency calls (calls increased significantly at 08:25). Response was prioritised to the area, multiple nearby roads were closed, and Wigan Council transported residents from Platt Bridge to a rest centre and provided emergency accommodation. (p56)
* The centre of Platt Bridge was the focus of the emergency response: multiple calls and reports of residents in distress led to prioritisation of Forward Incident Officers; Electricity North West isolated supply to the whole area and later supplied a generator at the Platt Bridge McDonalds to support people; emergency services and Wigan Council representatives were active during and immediately after the flooding. (p57) [TAB, PAG]
* Internal flooding of properties across the settlement: at least 35 residential properties and 11 commercial properties were internally flooded. Flooding drivers included Hey Brook overtopping (fluvial floodwater flowing north and impacting streets) and overwhelmed combined and surface water sewer systems with submerged outfalls leading to highway drainage backing up. (p59) [PAG]
* Fluvial flooding from Hey Brook, which combined with surface water, sewer and groundwater mechanisms, caused flooding across the area. There were road closures; flooding of the post office, supermarket and other local businesses; internet connectivity issues due to access difficulties to telecommunications masts; residents displaced and living in temporary accommodation; damage to homes and loss of possessions; negative physical and mental health impacts reported. (p60) [PAG]
* Fluvial flooding from Hey Brook led to high water levels that submerged the outfall of a highway drain, causing the drain to back up and surcharge upstream in Platt Bridge. Flooding in the area was the result of interacting mechanisms (river overtopping combined with surface water, sewer and groundwater contributions), increasing local flood risk and reducing community resilience without a local Flood Warning. (p61) [PAG]
