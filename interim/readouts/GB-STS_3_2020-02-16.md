## Topics without coordinates

### Dale Brook
* Runoff from the ~1.8 km2 steep catchment reached the watercourse quickly during the storm, causing levels in Dale Brook to rise rapidly. Dale Brook receives two surface-water sewer outfalls behind properties on Newton Leys and this rapid rise contributed to the flooding that affected properties behind Newton Leys on 16th February 2020. (p21) [PAG]
* Rapid increase in streamflow during the rainfall on 16 February 2020 produced elevated flows in Dale Brook. Modelled hydrographs estimated peak runoff equivalent to an event with a return period between 1 in 2 and 1 in 5 years, though local reports indicate the flooding experienced was more severe than the modelled magnitude. Flooding was exacerbated by saturated soils, lack of vegetation and obstructions in the floodplain which increased runoff and rapid release of flood water. (p24) [IMG]
* Dale Brook conveyed rapid runoff from saturated fields and increased urban surface water (including flows from a 675mm Severn Trent Water outfall), increasing channel volume and contributing to flooding of downstream areas including the Newton Leys footpath. (p25) [IMG]
* Conveyed rapid runoff from ploughed agricultural fields and urban outfall during the event; culvert capacity was exceeded, causing the brook to back up and spill out of the channel onto an adjacent footpath. Culvert constriction potential noted at two footpath crossings and debris/trash screen condition observed. (p27) [PAG]
* Dale Brook overtopped/was unable to contain high flows during the extreme event, spilling into gardens on Newton Leys and contributing to internal property flooding. (p33)
* Quant: 7 properties (p21) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 in 2 years; 1 in 5 years; 2-year and 5-year peak flow thresholds (p24) [IMG]
* Quant: 7 residential properties (p33)

### Staffordshire County Council
* Staffordshire County Council, as Lead Local Flood Authority, undertook a Section 19 investigation into flooding at Newton Leys following Storm Dennis; the report summarises the extent of flooding reported, likely causes and actions completed or proposed to manage the flooding of highways and properties. (p6) [PAG]
* Received a significant number of emergency telephone calls reporting localised flooding across the Borough (including Burton-on-Trent); coordinated the multiagency response (established Strategic and Tactical Co-ordinating Groups) and mobilised the Town Hall as a rest centre on standby. (p30)
* Acted in role of LLFA to coordinate response — worked with a local councillor to inform residents and landowners following the flood event. (p32)

### Dale Brook Catchment
* The catchment experienced an intense rainfall event that produced high river flows; a hydrograph was modelled for the observed rainfall and peak flows were compared to estimate the flow return period (heaviest rainfall estimated between a 1 in 2 and 1 in 5 year event). (p23)
* Saturated soils and ploughed agricultural land produced rapid runoff and high flow accumulation in the catchment; these rapid, coincident peaks from rural and urban parts of the catchment increased flows feeding Dale Brook and drove the downstream flooding. (p25) [IMG]

### East Staffordshire Borough Council
* Following the event, collected information from residents who experienced flooding to process Flooded Property Claims to help fund repairs for damages caused by the 16 February 2020 flood. (p30)
