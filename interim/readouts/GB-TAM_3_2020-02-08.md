

# Hyde flooding 08 Feb 2020 - 09 Feb 2020

## Place names with coordinates

### Hyde
* Flooding occurred in Hyde during the event dated 8–9 February 2020; the report concerns flood impacts at King Edwards Court, Hyde. (p1)
* Localised surface water flooding in the town, including extensive external flooding and internal flooding of six properties on King Edwards Court. (p7)
* Affected by the same 8–9 February 2020 event; King Edwards Court in Hyde experienced internal flooding of six properties. (p10)
* The south‑east Hyde area (King Edwards Court) experienced surface water/flash flooding caused by intense rainfall and runoff from Werneth Low, resulting in internal flooding of properties, resident displacement and subsequent clear-up operations. (p13) [PAG]
* Residential properties in the locality were flooded internally and required clean-up operations after flood waters receded. (p14) [IMG]
* External flooding was reported to one property on Mottram Old Road. The town experienced downstream impacts from intense runoff from Werneth Low that contributed to culvert surcharge and local flooding. (p18)

### King Edwards Court, Hyde
* Internal flooding of properties on the street and extensive external flooding; a culvert at this location was partially blocked, reducing flow through the underground drainage and contributing to the flooding. (p7)
* Six properties at King Edwards Court were flooded internally as a result of the flooding on 8–9 February 2020. (p10)
* Runoff from intense rainfall on Werneth Low overwhelmed an unnamed culverted watercourse running under the estate. Floodwater entered the estate via an inlet near 41 King Edwards Court, flowed through gardens via culverts and emerged in a grassed area near King Edward Road. Six properties were flooded internally and two sets of residents were displaced. Operatives carried out a clear-up operation to remove debris from the roadway; Tameside MBC was alerted to the event on 10 February 2020. (p13) [PAG]
* Internal flooding of residential properties; water surcharged from a manhole near the court and flowed through the roadway requiring a debris clear-up operation. (p14) [IMG]
* An underground drainage system surcharged at a manhole at the side of 59a King Edwards Court due to a hydraulic restriction caused by a flagstone in the pipe, causing localised flooding including internal flooding to properties. (p15) [PAG]
* Debris (including part of a flagstone) was washed into and lodged within a culvert running through King Edwards Court, significantly reducing channel capacity. The blockage caused the culvert to surcharge and contributed to flooding of nearby properties. (p18)
* Quant: 6 properties (p7)
* Quant: six properties (p10)
* Quant: six properties; two sets of residents displaced (p13) [PAG]

### Stalybridge

* Quant: 1 property (p17)

### King Edward Road, Hyde
* The drainage outfall at King Edward Road was part of the inspected drainage route; a piece of flagstone was found in the manhole near King Edward Road during the investigation, indicating involvement in the flooding/drainage failure chain. (p14) [PAG]
* A piece of flagstone was found in a manhole near King Edward Road that significantly reduced flow through the underground drainage system; this restriction contributed to downstream surcharge and localised flooding at King Edwards Court. The flagstone was removed and subsequent heavy rainfall produced no further issues. (p15) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 piece of flagstone (approx. 350 mm x 425 mm) (p15) [PAG]

### Mottram Old Road, Millbrook
* External flooding to a property caused by a blockage in a culvert on an unnamed watercourse under Mottram Old Road which caused the watercourse to surcharge. (p17)
* Quant: 1 property (p17)

### Acorn Avenue, Hyde
* An inlet structure on Acorn Avenue is shown as the start of the culvert/flow path that conveyed rainfall runoff, contributing to the surcharging manhole and subsequent residential flooding. (p14) [IMG]

### Werneth Low
* Intense rainfall on the evening of 8 February to the morning of 9 February 2020 ran off Werneth Low via a number of watercourses, including the unnamed culverted watercourse that runs under King Edwards Court, contributing to internal flooding of properties on the estate; operatives carried out a clear-up on 10 February after floodwater receded. (p13) [PAG]
* Intense rainfall fell on Werneth Low on the evening of 8 February into the morning of 9 February 2020. Runoff flowed off the hill via culverts and washed debris into downstream systems, which contributed to culvert blockage, surcharge and downstream flooding in King Edwards Court. (p18)
* Quant: 6 properties flooded internally; 2 sets of residents displaced (p13) [PAG]

### North Wales

### Tameside Metropolitan Borough Council
* As Lead Local Flood Authority and highways authority, TMBC had duties during the 8/9 February 2020 event to investigate flooding, manage highway drainage and oversee other risk management authorities and landowners in relation to the surface-water flooding that affected the area. (p9) [PAG]
* No action taken during the flood because TMBC were not informed until 10 February 2020. After the event (11–13 Feb) carried out clean-up including cleaning highway drainage system and road sweeping, inspected the inlet structure, spoke to residents to establish which properties were affected and to what extent, commissioned a Section 19 report and arranged CCTV investigation; liaised with the Environment Agency and United Utilities about the site. (p16) [TAB, PAG]

### Highways
* Highway drains and associated inlet structures were cleaned/cleared (critical inlet structures cleaned on 6 Feb; highway drainage system cleaned and road sweeping carried out 11–13 Feb). No action during the flood because not informed until 10 February 2020. Recommendation to continue maintaining efficient operation of highway drains. (p16) [TAB]

### United Utilities
* Named as a responsible operating authority to liaise with the EA and TMBC regarding the site; recommended to continue to maintain efficient operation of surface water and combined sewers and to ensure main river watercourses are suitably maintained. (p16) [TAB]

### Environment Agency
* Responded and liaised with Tameside Metropolitan Borough Council and United Utilities during and after the 8/9 February 2020 flooding; recommended review of Flood Alert trigger levels for the area and advised that main river watercourses be suitably maintained. (p16) [TAB]
