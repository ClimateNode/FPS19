### Raunds
* The flood incident at Brick Kiln Road / North Street in Raunds (9 March 2016) resulted in internal flooding of a property in Raunds; the cause is described as heavy rainfall on a saturated catchment with drainage unable to convey water, producing overland flow to low points near properties. (p7)
* Flood incident on 9 March 2016 led to internal flooding in the town: causes reported include agricultural runoff, surface water runoff and ordinary watercourse flooding. The investigation recorded internal flooding and repeated incidents at the Brick Kiln Road/North Street location. (p9) [PAG]
* Raunds was affected during the event: the town is crossed by a Main River (the Hog Dyke) and associated watercourses and ditches that connect to drainage in the town. The report records an internally flooded property in Raunds (on North Street). (p10)
* Heavy rainfall on a saturated catchment caused drainage systems across Raunds to be unable to collect and convey rainwater to larger watercourses, resulting in excess surface water following natural contours to low points around private properties. Drainage systems (including ditches in the south verge) became backed up or overtopped, main drainage systems were fully surcharged causing carriageway ponding and domestic drainage backups with reported internal flooding of property(ies). A reported blockage where a ditch enters a pipe immediately north of Furnells Close caused local backing up of the verge ditch. (p13) [PAG]
* Main drainage systems at low points were fully surcharged causing ponding in carriageways; surcharge led to domestic drainage backing up and reported internal flooding of property(ies). (p14)
* Site of flood incidents prompting a Section 19 investigation by the LLFA (NCC); the report discusses identifying causes and recording assets and hotspots related to flooding in Raunds. (p16)

* Quant: over 70 properties; over 200 reports of flooding (p9) [PAG]

### High Street, Raunds
* The Main River entrance to the culvert under High Street was heavily obstructed by dense vegetation and overgrowth, preventing access and restricting inspection/maintenance. The blockage would have reduced culvert conveyance during the 9th March 2016 flood, increasing local flood risk at the culvert inlet. (p71) [IMG, PAG]
* The Main River was actively flowing into the culvert entrance beneath High Street; the inlet showed turbid, sediment-laden water and visible debris at the culvert mouth, indicating elevated river flows affecting the culvert. (p72) [IMG, PAG]

### Brooks Road, Raunds
* A watercourse flows into the north of Raunds along Brooks Road and this watercourse is part of the Main River through Raunds. The report attributes the March 2016 event to ordinary watercourse flooding (alongside surface water and agricultural runoff), so the watercourse along Brooks Road was implicated in the ordinary watercourse flooding during the 9 March 2016 event. (p10)

### Brook Street, Raunds
* The Main River through Raunds is joined near Brook Street by a second Main River from Butts Road. The report identifies ordinary watercourse flooding as a cause of the March 2016 event, so the Main River route joining near Brook Street was implicated in ordinary watercourse flooding during that event. (p10)

### Mackenzie Road, Raunds
* The watercourse flows south through the centre of Raunds parallel with High Street, Brook Street and MacKenzie Road and then flows to the River Nene. The report attributes the March 2016 event to ordinary watercourse flooding among other causes, so the watercourse running parallel to MacKenzie Road was implicated in the ordinary watercourse flooding during the 9 March 2016 event. (p10)

### Furnells Close, Raunds
* {09 Mar 2016, 25 Apr 2016}: The ditch in the south verge was reported blocked at the point where it enters a pipe immediately north of Furnells Close; the blockage caused the ditch to overtop and made a significant contribution to surface water flowing onto Brick Kiln Road and towards North Street. During the 25 April investigation ditch systems were observed to back up at the same location due to the reported blockage (though rainfall on that date was not enough to overtop). (p12) [MUL]


## Topics without coordinates

### East Northamptonshire Council
* ENC was implicated in managing flood risk related to recent/ongoing developments (notably on Brick Kiln Road) and in addressing ordinary watercourse maintenance where failures contributed to surface water flood risk to properties; ENC is to review planning, contact developers and use enforcement powers as required. (p22)

### Environment Agency
* The Environment Agency worked with the NCC Emergency Planning Team and the LLFA to support the Raunds community response and local flood risk management following the flooding. (p22) [PAG]

### Anglian Water
* Issues relating to water entering the public sewerage system and the capacity of sewer assets were identified as contributing to flood risk to properties; AWS was asked to assess sources of inflow and asset capacity and consider improvements where capacity is insufficient. (p22)
