

# Crick flooding 09 Mar 2016

## Place names with coordinates

### Danes Way, Crick
* Surface water flooding impaired drainage and affected critical infrastructure at Danes Way; flooding persisted and affected critical infrastructure for a period in excess of three hours from the onset of flooding. (p7)
* Heavy rainfall on a saturated catchment overwhelmed drainage systems and watercourses at Danes Way (DIRFT East), causing water levels to rise and flooding of the area. Pumping operations and the installation of a larger culvert for Crick Brook (over‑pumping during works at the northern end of Danes Way/A5 roundabout) may have altered flow, affecting the volume and direction of overland flooding. (p15) [PAG]
* The plan labels an “Approximate catchment area contributing to Danes Way Watercourse”, indicating runoff routed to the Danes Way watercourse and its role in the flooding around Crick on 9th March 2016. (p32) [IMG]
* Overland flow routes converged toward Danes Way during the incident; schematic areas of flooding are shown adjacent to the road. Water was pumped from a watercourse onto Danes Way. Culvert replacement works (Q1, Q2, Q3) were identified along Danes Way and nearby watercourses and an attenuation pond is located to manage excess water. (p33) [IMG]
* Road surface showed pooling and standing water, producing a wet reflective carriageway that could impede vehicle movement and indicates drainage was overwhelmed at this location. (p48) [IMG, PAG]
* Paved pedestrian walkways and the adjoining road were flooded: standing water pooled along walkways adjacent to a fenced industrial/commercial site and water flowed over a sloped footpath toward gates and drains. Pedestrian access was made impassable or difficult and water encroached onto the carriageway, prompting on-site personnel and roadwork/traffic management activity. (p49) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface-water flooding caused ponding and water pooling on the paved access and roadway at Danes Way, obstructing pedestrian and vehicular access and creating a safety hazard; caption notes water being pumped/over-pumped from a nearby watercourse onto the road. (p50) [IMG, PAG]
* Danes Way was heavily flooded with water covering both lanes and extending onto the pavement, rendering the road impassable. Emergency response used pumps and sandbags and deployed a blue barrier to manage and redirect floodwater. (p51) [IMG, PAG]
* Roadway and adjacent sidewalk were inundated; vehicular traffic was affected (truck driving through floodwater); flood warning signage was deployed and personnel in high-visibility clothing were present, indicating an official response/monitoring. (p52) [IMG, PAG]
* Road surface covered by standing water across both lanes, making the road hazardous or potentially impassable and disrupting normal travel; vehicles seen halted or proceeding with difficulty. (p56) [IMG]
* Floodwater covered the carriageway, extending across both lanes and making the road hazardous or impassable; vehicles were observed driving through standing water and emergency responders in high-visibility clothing were present managing the situation. (p61) [IMG]
* Surface water flooding affected Danes Way and the adjacent business park area, leaving the carriageway and site areas wet/partially inundated and prompting on-site inspection checks during the incident. (p65) [IMG, PAG]
* Roadway was inundated by water from a nearby watercourse overflow; vehicles were driving through standing/flowing water and emergency pumping operations were in place discharging muddy water onto the carriageway to manage levels. (p72) [IMG, PAG]
* Pathway and roadside adjacent to Danes Way were inundated: the pathway was almost completely submerged and rendered impassable; floodwater encroached onto the grass verge and partially covered the carriageway, requiring a warning sign and staff presence and forcing vehicles to drive through standing water. (p73) [IMG, PAG]
* Danes Way was completely submerged by floodwater, with the road surface and curbs obscured; the street was impassable to vehicles and pedestrians and water encroached onto pavements. (p74) [IMG, PAG]
* Discharge headwall and culvert entry under Danes Way (including underground attenuation outlet) were documented as conveying runoff during the event. The headwall and culvert outlet were partially filled with water and discharging to a reinforced channel; stone rip-rap and vegetation were present at the outlet for erosion control. (p80) [PAG]
* Quant: 3 culvert replacement sites (Q1, Q2, Q3); 1 location where water was pumped from the watercourse onto Danes Way; 1 attenuation pond (p33) [IMG]

### Crick
* Surface water flooding at Danes Way in DIRFT East (within Crick) resulted from heavy rainfall on a saturated catchment and affected critical infrastructure in the area for a period in excess of three hours. (p7)
* The flood investigation covers Danes Way and the DIRFT area in Crick where overland flow converged toward roads and watercourses; schematic flooding areas and culvert issues were identified within the Crick area, and attenuation measures were shown on the plan. (p33) [IMG]

### Parklands, Crick
* Roadway flooding in the Parklands area with significant standing water on the carriageway near a roundabout; hazardous driving conditions demonstrated by a large truck travelling through floodwater. Floodwater also extended onto adjacent grassy land, partially submerging a wooden fence and depositing debris. (p53) [IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### A428
* Floodwater inundated the A5/A428 roundabout, with water levels high enough to impede vehicle movement and create a hazard for road users. A vehicle was seen partially submerged at the junction, indicating transport disruption at the roundabout. (p54) [IMG]
* Surface water affected the carriageway at the A5/A428 roundabout — photographs from the 9th March 2016 flood incident show wet road surfaces and traffic cones on the roundabout, indicating the carriageway was impacted by flood-related surface water and traffic management was in place. (p69) [IMG]
* The A5/A428 roundabout and connecting carriageways were covered by floodwater and made impassable to normal traffic; vehicles were stopped or driving through deep water and traffic was disrupted. (p71) [IMG]

### Crick Brook
* Mapped as a local watercourse that collected runoff from its catchment and contributed to the flooding incident affecting the Crick area on 9th March 2016; shown on the plan with a delineated contributing catchment. (p32) [IMG]

### Culvert
* Concrete culvert under the car-park access road was observed conveying water during the event. Interior contained flowing water and plant debris; the exit is embedded in a concrete embankment and was discharging to the downstream channel. Standing water, algae/moss and minor debris were noted inside and at the outlet, and an overgrown downstream exit channel and nearby attenuation features received the discharge. (p80) [PAG]
