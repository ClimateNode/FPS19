### Ginge Brook
* Out-of-channel flows on the left bank (upstream of Church Lane) crossed fields and entered ordinary watercourses through the village; out-of-bank flow between Steventon and the A34 routed flows down a bridleway toward Milton Lane and into downstream watercourses. These actions contributed to internal flooding of properties and businesses within Steventon. (p2) [PAG]
* Ginge Brook produced flood spill locations and had reduced conveyance due to silt/obstructions; flood storage upstream of Steventon is proposed to reduce future flooding risk to the village and surrounding areas. (p3) [TAB]
* The Environment Agency mapping shows the highest flood risk (Flood Zone 3 and dark blue surface-water risk) confined around the channel boundary of Ginge Brook, indicating the brook is a focal location for river flooding that affects adjacent land and properties (and likely contributed to the January 2024 impacts). (p7)
* {general}: The channel boundary of Ginge Brook is mapped as the area of highest surface water flood risk; flooding is concentrated along the brook and adjacent low-lying areas. The Ginge Brook is designated as a main river running across the southern end of Steventon and is culverted in places under roads and property accesses, creating local flood pathways. (p8) [IMG, PAG]
* Ginge Brook broke its left bank during the flood event, producing out-of-channel flows that crossed fields into an ordinary watercourse; the main river caused flooding at multiple culverts and bridges (including under the railway and a public footbridge whose deck was observed at soffit level). (p12) [IMG]
* {general}: Flooding from Ginge Brook inundated the adjacent bridleway between Steventon and Milton; flood levels persisted into the February site visit. (p14)

* Properties flooded from lack of capacity of culverts or open channel at multiple locations through Steventon, with water backing up at multiple culvert inlets. Channel siltation reduced cross-sectional area in sections (notably around the rail culvert by Stocks Lane and at the A34), contributing to the flooding. (p16)
* Overtopping on the left bank south of Mill Farm flooded adjacent farmland; flood flows crossed the farmland into a parallel ordinary watercourse, adding cross-catchment flows that, together with already high levels, led to internal flooding of a property on Vicarage Road and contributed to flooding through the centre of Steventon. (p17) [IMG, PAG]

The section is heavily silted and repeatedly flooded over the 2023/24 winter. (p21) [IMG, PAG]

### Steventon
* Multiple locations within the village experienced internal flooding of residential properties and businesses. Flooding originated from the main river Ginge Brook (out-of-channel flows on the left bank upstream of Church Lane) and from a number of ordinary watercourses through the village; channel restrictions contributed to the event. Out-of-bank flows between Steventon and the A34 routed down a bridleway towards Milton Lane and into downstream watercourses; inundation of the pumping station on Milton Lane caused local foul water flooding at that location. (p2) [PAG]
* Flooding driven by Ginge Brook was identified as affecting Steventon; report recommends assessing upstream flood storage on Ginge Brook and investigating obstructions/silt to reduce local flood risk. (p3) [TAB]
* Many properties in the village were affected by flooding during the January 2024 event; many of the affected properties are located within Environment Agency Flood Zone 3 (high probability of river flooding). (p7) [IMG]
* {general}: Most of the village is shown at low risk of surface water flooding, with small areas at medium risk and the highest risk confined adjacent to the Ginge Brook. Medium and low risk areas extend into built-up parts of the village (notably near Mill Street and Station Road), indicating potential for surface-water/urban flooding in those locations. (p8) [IMG]
* Residential properties and local roads were flooded; flooding incidents in the village included property inundation and road closures during severe storms. (p9) [MUL]

Flooding occurred at multiple culverts and bridges through the village (including highway culverts, the rail crossing and a public footbridge). (p12) [IMG]
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 01 Feb 2024 - 29 Feb 2024}: Multiple highway culverts within Steventon reached capacity during the January event. The culvert under High Street (adjacent to Station Yard) produced flooding that affected residential and commercial properties and contributed flows to ordinary watercourse flooding around the Causeway/Milton Lane junction. Downstream, the bridleway alongside Ginge Brook showed flood levels up to 30 cm during a February site visit (over a month after the 4 Jan event). (p14) [MUL]

* Raised river and watercourse levels contributed to flooding through the centre of Steventon; the central area experienced localised fluvial flooding driven by Ginge Brook and linked ordinary watercourse flows. (p18) [IMG]

Foul/sewer flooding was also reported at the bottom end of the village. (p20) [PAG]

Flooding from these flows contributed to property flooding during the January 2024 event and was present repeatedly over the 2023/24 winter. (p21) [IMG, PAG]

Flooding originated from Ginge Brook and a number of ordinary watercourses through the village; out‑of‑channel flows on the left bank of Ginge Brook upstream of Church Lane crossed fields and entered an already at‑capacity ordinary watercourse through the village, contributing to local inundation. Culvert restrictions and silt contributed to capacity issues and downstream flooding. (p26) [PAG]

### Moor Ditch
* Moor Ditch is identified as having obstructions and silt affecting flow, with existing flood spill locations around the A34 that require further investigation — indicating it contributed to local flooding risk. (p3) [TAB]
* During the 4–5 Jan 2024 flood event, flows entered Moor Ditch approximately 150 m south of the offtake via overland flow from Ginge Brook and via a culvert under the A34; flows were reported to pass through a downstream underpass up to 30 cm deep. (p15)
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 01 Feb 2024 - 29 Feb 2024, 16 Apr 2024}: The main river offtake into Moor Ditch was blocked by silt and was not flowing during the flood event (and remained blocked in February); the inlet was inspected and silt was cleared on 16 April to restore flows into the upstream end of Moor Ditch. (p23) [PAG, MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 01 Feb 2024 - 29 Feb 2024}: The main river offtake feeding Moor Ditch was silted and not flowing during the flood event (4–5 Jan 2024) and again in February 2024. The offtake was inspected on 16 Apr 2024 and silt blocking the inlet was cleared, which restored flow into the upstream end of Moor Ditch. Photographs document silt build-up adjacent to the inlet before clearance and active flow after clearance. (p24) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Offtake inlet experienced silt build-up and required silt clearance; Moor Ditch was observed flowing after the offtake inlet clearance and contributed to overspill locations on both left and right banks between Steventon and Milton. (p25) [PAG]
* Out-of-channel/overspill flows adjacent to Moor Ditch contributed to flooding that affected Steventon and other communities; investigation of the interface between Ginge Brook and Moor Ditch and alternative routes for overspill flows is recommended. (p26) [PAG]
* Obstructions and high silt levels in Moor Ditch around the A34 were identified as restricting flow and causing/relating to flood spill locations; further investigation and clearance were recommended. (p32) [TAB]
* Quant: flow depth up to 30 cm; location ~150 m south of offtake (p15)
