### Harpole
* Flooding at School Lane in Harpole is the subject of the Northamptonshire County Council flood investigation report. (p2)
* Village location of the flood incident (School Lane, Harpole). Internal flooding of one property occurred in Harpole as part of the event: surface water ran to low points around private property and entered the building. (p7)
* Village location of the School Lane flood incident in which one property experienced internal flooding. (p9) [PAG]
* West Harpole experienced surface water flooding associated with a local, primarily culverted, watercourse and blocked/silted field ditches that led to flooding of at least one property on School Lane. (p10) [PAG]
* A watercourse through Harpole (primarily culverted in places) contributed to property flooding; riparian and culverted sections (including pipe connections from smaller watercourses) were linked to the flood that affected a property in the village. (p11)
* Heavy rainfall over a saturated catchment caused drainage systems to be ineffective in Harpole, resulting in excess surface water flowing to low points around private properties (notably at School Lane). Emergency action reduced the impact to a residential property. (p14)
* Location of the flood incident described in the report; the event affected Harpole on the date of the investigation. (p18)
* A flood incident on 9th March 2016 produced surface water and overland flow from surrounding fields that moved toward and into Harpole. The investigation map marks five flood incident locations clustered on the northern and eastern outskirts and shows probable overland surface water flow routes and main drainage channels conveying water into the village, highlighting vulnerable areas. ## Topics without coordinates

### Highway Authority
* Flooding affected the highway drainage and carriageway channels within the incident location, requiring the highway authority to undertake regular drainage cleansing, assess asset capacity, identify areas with insufficient capacity that were causing flood risk to properties, and plan improvement works and asset-sharing with the LLFA and community. (p22)

### Anglian Water
* Flooding led to additional water entering the public sewerage system and raised concerns about asset capacity; Anglian Water was required to assess sources entering the sewers, identify assets with insufficient capacity that could create flood risk to properties, and develop a detailed asset plan to share with the LLFA and community. (p22)
