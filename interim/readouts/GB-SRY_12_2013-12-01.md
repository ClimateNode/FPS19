

# Staines-Upon-Thames flooding December 2013 - February 2014

Reference document:<br>S19 Flood Investigation Report River Ash and Knowle Green Area<br>126\_Surrey County/126\_2015\_Surrey County/S19-Report-River-Ash-Area.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Staines Reservoir Aqueduct
* The aqueduct was a focus of flood-control operations; the Environment Agency worked with Thames Water under the Ash Protocol to control flooding at the Staines Reservoir Aqueduct. Thames Water carried out repairs to the aqueduct following the event and will agree revised operating arrangements for when nearby river levels are high. (p2)
* Staines Reservoir Aqueduct (asset associated with the reservoir) rose above its banks and received overflow from fluvial water; operation of assets on the Staines Reservoir Aqueduct was implicated and recommended for revised operating arrangements. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* The aqueduct rose above its banks in areas around Leacroft and Priory Green, contributing to road and internal property flooding; fluvial water also overflowed into the aqueduct via county ditch connections. (p4)
* High Thames levels prevented normal discharge of tributaries; those tributaries backed up and spilled into the Staines Aqueduct at various points, producing backflow/spill within the aqueduct and forming part of the flooding experienced in the Staines-upon-Thames area. (p5)
* The Staines Reservoir Aqueduct overtopped (into the River Ash at the Crooked Billet side weir) and overtopping at multiple points contributed to increased flows in the River Ash and local flooding. (p6)
* Identified as a key asset requiring active management during the flooding event; the aqueduct was managed by Thames Water staff to reduce flood risk to the asset and surrounding area. (p9)
* During the February event the Aqueduct was fed solely with floodwater originating from the County Ditch and Wraysbury River. Thames Water intake gates at Hythe End were set to control levels and were fully closed on 9 February. Operations on the Aqueduct (including Moor Lane sluice gate) were managed as part of flood control: pumping from Birch Green pumping station was increased to control flood levels, Moor Lane sluice gate was instructed to be 50PCSYM closed (completed by 00:30 on 12 February), flows began to recede on 12 February afternoon so no further closure was required, and the gate was raised and pumping reduced when levels fell. (p10) [MUL]
* The Staines Reservoir Aqueduct partially collapsed following the flood event, causing significant damage; it was taken out of service for repairs and remained out of service throughout the summer of 2014. (p11) [PAG]
* Subject to high river levels and risk of overtopping during the February 2014 flood event; operational changes made to reduce flood risk including removal of a sluice gate, installation of new isolation valves on the siphon under the railway, Thames Water to close valves when aqueduct levels reach a trigger before overtopping, and the aqueduct was added to the SCC Flooding Asset Register to highlight its importance to flood risk. Thames Water will run annual tests of communications and operations. (p12)
* Water from various watercourses getting into the aqueduct caused levels to rise and spill over into the River Ash, contributing to the sharp rise in river levels that led to flooding. (p13)
* The report states that water from various watercourses entering the aqueduct caused levels to rise and spill into the River Ash, likely contributing to the sudden rise in river levels and downstream property flooding in Birch Green when combined with other factors. (p14)
* Quant: over 80 properties; at least 11 properties (p4)
* Quant: pumping increased from 270 million litres-a-day to 350 million litres-a-day and later to 420 million litres-a-day; Moor Lane sluice gate 50PCSYM closed by 00:30 on 12 February (p10) [MUL]

### River Ash
* Unprecedented winter 2013/14 rainfall caused fluvial flooding from the River Ash that affected the upstream section in the vicinity of Leacroft and Priory Green; prompted operational interventions (EA overrode sluice gate; Thames Water adjusted pumping and sluice settings) to control flows. (p2)
* Upstream section rose dramatically and overtopped its banks; high river levels persisted for a number of days and caused fluvial flooding into nearby areas, including overflow into lakes, a county ditch and the Staines Reservoir Aqueduct. (p3) [PAG]
* The upstream section rose rapidly and overtopped its banks in the vicinity of Leacroft and Priory Green, causing fluvial flooding that led to road flooding and internal flooding of some properties (including on Leacroft, Leacroft Close and Raleigh Court). Elevated levels persisted for several days before receding. (p4) [PAG]
* Flows in the River Ash were increased due to overtopping from the Staines Reservoir Aqueduct (including at the Crooked Billet side weir), contributing to flooding in nearby areas. (p6) [IMG]
* River Ash overtopped/caused flooding at Priory Green and the surrounding area. (p8)
* Flooding from the River Ash affected Priory Green and surrounding areas; the EA overrode the sluice gate controls (which manage flow from the River Colne into the River Ash) during the February 2014 event to regulate flows and reduce flooding. (p9) [PAG]
* Flow into the River Ash was actively controlled by an EA-owned sluice gate during the February event. The gate was closed manually on 9 February to minimise flood flows entering the Ash (downstream levels were low so it later automatically re-opened to restore base flow). The gate was closed again on 11 February and prevented from automatically re-opening (reducing base flow), then reopened on 13 February to allow a small flow to prevent damage from a dry riverbed. Flows on the Ash began to recede on the afternoon of 12 February. (p10) [PAG, MUL]
* Flood alert and flood warning issued (10 February 2014) for the River Ash covering the Borough of Spelthorne, including Ashford and Staines (and named areas Birch Green, Knowle Green, Littleton and Shepperton). River level thresholds on the River Ash were reduced after the February 2014 event so that flood warnings will be issued at an earlier stage. (p12) [PAG]
* A sharp rise in water levels on the River Ash led to property flooding in the Birch Green area; opening of a sluice gate and water from other watercourses entering the aqueduct contributed to rising levels and spillover into the River Ash. (p13) [PAG]
* Quant: over 80 properties; at least 11 properties (p4) [PAG]

### River Thames
* High levels in the River Thames were identified as a source of the flooding that propagated into the River Ash, Staines Reservoir Aqueduct and surrounding areas, contributing to overtopping and subsequent road and property flooding. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* {December 2013 - February 2014, 01 Dec 2013 - 28 Feb 2014}: Exceptional winter rainfall kept the River Thames running at capacity, producing very high river levels on the Lower Thames (the highest in over 65 years) that prevented tributaries discharging and contributed to flooding around Staines-upon-Thames. (p5) [PAG]
* Very high River Thames levels prevented tributaries from discharging to it, causing those tributaries to back up and contribute to flooding in the Staines-upon-Thames area. (p6) [PAG]
* High river flows prompted EA flood notices including a flood alert (24 December 2013) and flood warnings (7 February 2014) and a severe flood warning for the reach at Staines and Egham on 9 February 2014. Thames Water closed its River Thames intake during the high flows and only reopened it after EA agreement on 17 February 2014. (p11) [MUL]
* High river levels on the Thames triggered EA flood alerts and warnings covering Staines-upon-Thames (including a 24 Dec 2013 flood alert for River Thames from Datchet to Shepperton Green, a 7 Feb 2014 flood warning for Staines and Egham and a 9 Feb 2014 severe flood warning), indicating the Thames contributed to flood risk in the Staines area. (p12) [PAG, MUL]
* The River Thames was included in updated modelling and is described as influencing flood risk to communities between Datchet and Teddington (including Spelthorne / Staines); the River Thames Scheme is intended to reduce that flood risk. (p13)
* {winter 2013/14}: Quant: more than 65 years (p5) [PAG]

### River Colne
* Complex multi-channel Lower Colne system experienced backwater because high Thames levels prevented normal discharge; tributaries and channels backed up, spilling into the Staines Aqueduct and contributing to the flooding seen in Staines-upon-Thames during the winter 2013/14 event. (p5)
* Flows from the River Colne were controlled via an EA-owned sluice gate to manage flood levels in the River Ash; the Environment Agency overrode the sluice gate's automatic settings during the February event to control flooding into Priory Green and surrounding areas. (p9)
* The EA-operated sluice gate controlling flow from the River Colne to the River Ash was manipulated to minimise flood flows from the Colne entering the Ash: closed on 9 February and closed again on 11 February (later reopened on 13 February to restore base flow to the Ash). (p10) [PAG, MUL]
* {general}: Included in updated flood modelling to better represent interactions between watercourses (with the River Ash, Aqueduct, Colne Brook and County Ditch) that contribute to flooding mechanisms affecting the Staines area. (p13)

### Moor Lane, Staines-Upon-Thames

(p10) [PAG, MUL]
* The Moor Lane sluice gate was operated as part of flood management: it was 50PCSYM closed by 00:30 on 12 February, and was raised on 14 February to allow pumping to take place. (p11) [MUL]

### Surrey
* {general}: The area (within Surrey) is shown to be at risk of surface water flooding in 1 in 30, 1 in 100 and 1 in 1000 return period events. The area lies within the extent of the EA's Historic Flood Map indicating previous flooding by rivers and/or groundwater. A nearby wetspot indicates historic highway flooding, and Surrey County Council records previous instances of property flooding nearby (internal or external). (p7)

### Spelthorne
* Communities in Spelthorne (including Staines and areas such as Birch Green) were affected by flood risk and property flooding associated with the River Ash rising; the River Thames Scheme is described as reducing flood risk to communities including Spelthorne. (p13)

### Birch Green
* Overtopping of the River Ash (and raised levels in the Staines Reservoir Aqueduct linked to high Thames/Wraysbury levels) caused fluvial flooding in the Birch Green area affecting property grounds, garages and outbuildings and contributing to internal property flooding in the local area. (p4)
* Birch Green was a location where the aqueduct overtopped significantly, contributing to increased flows and local flooding in the area. (p6)
* Property flooding occurred in the Birch Green area as a result of a sharp rise in water levels along the River Ash; water from nearby watercourses entering the aqueduct spilled into the River Ash and contributed to the rapid rise in levels that led to the property flooding. (p13)
* Quant: over 80 properties affected (combined with Knowle Green); at least 11 properties experienced internal flooding (across Birch Green and Knowle Green) (p4)

### Staines-Upon-Thames
* {winter 2013/14}: The Staines-upon-Thames area experienced flooding when tributaries and drainage channels could not discharge into the Thames (which was running at capacity). High groundwater (saturated ground) further prevented drainage, and backup of water led to spills into the Staines Aqueduct at multiple points, causing flooding in the town. (p5) [PAG]
* The Staines-upon-Thames area experienced flooding caused by a combination of saturated ground, high rainfall and high River Thames levels that forced tributaries to back up and spill, producing local flooding. (p6) [IMG]
* The focus of the flood response moved to the Staines area in early February 2014; EA field teams managed local assets (including the River Ash sluice gate) to minimise flooding at Priory Green and surrounding areas. (p9)
* Staines was within the area covered by River Thames and River Ash flood alerts/warnings (including a severe flood warning for River Thames at Staines on 9 February 2014). The EA had personnel on site in the Staines area from 10-14 February to record flooded properties and extent. (p11) [PAG, MUL]

### Knowle Green
* Overtopping of the River Ash (and raised levels in the Staines Reservoir Aqueduct linked to high Thames/Wraysbury levels) caused fluvial flooding in the Knowle Green area affecting property grounds, garages and outbuildings and contributing to internal property flooding in the local area. (p4)
* Knowle Green was included in EA flood warnings for the River Ash (10 February 2014) and is within the area for which river level monitoring thresholds were reviewed and reduced, so warnings will be issued earlier for this locality. (p12) [PAG, MUL]
* Quant: over 80 properties affected (combined with Birch Green); at least 11 properties experienced internal flooding (across Birch Green and Knowle Green) (p4)

### Ashford

(p3) [IMG, PAG]

### Thames Water
* Thames Water's asset (the Staines Aqueduct) was identified as a key asset needing management during the December 2013–February 2014 flooding; the company worked (through its asset) to manage flood risk to its infrastructure. (p9) [TAB]
* Thames Water's intake and pumping operations were directly affected by floodwater in the Aqueduct: Hythe End intake gates were in automatic mode then fully closed; the Aqueduct was being fed with floodwater from County Ditch and Wraysbury River; Thames Water increased pumping from Birch Green pumping station (from 270 to 350 and then to 420 million litres-a-day) to control flood levels; they implemented a 50PCSYM closure of the Moor Lane sluice gate and later raised it. These actions carried a high risk of damage to the Aqueduct and pumping system and risked deterioration of water quality in the Staines Reservoirs. (p10) [PAG, MUL]
* {December 2013 - February 2014, summer 2014}: Operated flood-control measures during the event (sluice gate at The Moor Lane was 50PCSYM closed by 00:30 on 12 February and pumping was later adjusted). Thames Water closed its River Thames intake during the event and reduced pumping (to one pump) on 14 February due to low Aqueduct levels; the intake was permitted to reopen on 17 February. Following the flood event Thames Water discovered the Staines Reservoir Aqueduct had partially collapsed, causing significant damage and putting the aqueduct out of service for repair through the summer of 2014. (p11) [PAG, MUL]
* {Following the February 2014 flooding incident}: Thames Water will take full ownership of aqueduct assets and carry out monitoring and operational actions during high river levels: close new isolation valves when aqueduct levels reach a trigger (before overtopping), run annual tests of communications and operations, and operate assets when alerted by the EA to manage flood risk. (p12)
* Thames Water was involved in operating assets around the aqueduct during the flood; the report identifies communication issues between Thames Water and the Environment Agency during the incident. Thames Water committed to run regular tests to ensure communications and operations around the aqueduct are adequate for high water level events. (p14)
* Quant: pumping increased from 270 million litres-a-day to 350 million litres-a-day and later to 420 million litres-a-day; Moor Lane sluice gate 50PCSYM closed by 00:30 on 12 February (p10) [PAG, MUL]

### Environment Agency
* Opened its incident room on 23 December 2013 and coordinated response through to February 2014; deployed field teams to manage flood assets, clear screens and river blockages, and overrode the automatic settings on the River Ash sluice gate to control flows and reduce local flooding. (p9) [MUL]
* The Environment Agency deployed flood data recorders and flood ambassadors in the Staines-upon-Thames area from 10-14 February to collect data on flooded properties, flood extent, water levels and (where possible) source and depth of flooding; the EA also issued flood alerts and warnings covering the Staines area during the event. (p11)
* The Environment Agency had flood risk management duties for the Main River and carried out detailed actions during and after the February 2014 flooding event in the area. (p13)
* The Environment Agency will carry out improved local modelling (including accounting for the aqueduct) to better understand the river-level responses that contributed to the flooding; the report also notes communication issues between the EA and Thames Water during the flooding incident. (p14)
