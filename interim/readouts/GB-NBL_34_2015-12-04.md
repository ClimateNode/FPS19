

# Ovingham flooding 04 Dec 2015 - 06 Dec 2015

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Ovingham<br>091\_Northumberland/Ovingham\_FIR\_2015.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Ovingham
* {04 Dec 2015 - 06 Dec 2015, 26 Dec 2015, 05 Jan 2016 - 06 Jan 2016}: The village experienced flooding from the main river (Tyne overtopping) and from an ordinary watercourse (Whittle Burn) — residential and business properties were flooded; surface water runoff from agricultural fields threatened properties (no internal flooding from that runoff). Areas adjacent to the main river, especially near Ovingham Bridge, were particularly affected. (p1) [IMG, MUL]
* Flooding damaged Ovingham Bridge (debris, damaged/temporary scaffolding and foundations) and affected the river bed and bridge foundations; fallen/unstable trees were removed upstream of Ovingham Bridge. Sewers were impacted leading to CCTV survey, cleansing and relining; repair of a surface water outfall and installation of a flap valve on a combined sewer overflow were proposed. Property-level resilience grants were made available to help residents install defences and increase property resilience. (p2) [TAB]
* {04 Dec 2015 - 06 Dec 2015, 26 Dec 2015, 05 Jan 2016 - 06 Jan 2016}: Quant: 12 dwellings; 4 businesses (p1) [IMG, MUL]

### River Tyne
* The River Tyne swelled and overtopped its banks/defences during Storm Desmond, causing flooding in areas adjacent to the river (concentrated around Ovingham Bridge and the central part of the village). Flooding and river action contributed to heavy damage to Ovingham Bridge which required closure and repairs. (p1)
* The interaction between the River Tyne and the Whittle Burn was identified for further investigation; the EA will update the Tyne model to include recommendations on investigating this interaction and its implications for flooding. (p2) [TAB]
* Quant: 12 dwellings; 4 businesses; Ovingham Bridge heavy damage requiring closure (p1)

### Ovingham Bridge
* {04 Dec 2015 - 06 Dec 2015, 26 Dec 2015, 05 Jan 2016 - 06 Jan 2016}: The bridge sustained heavy damage during the winter 2015–16 flood events and was closed; repairs (debris removal, scaffolding and foundation repairs) and studies of the river bed and foundations were carried out. (p1) [IMG, MUL]
* Bridge was damaged by flood-related debris and high flows; debris removed, temporary scaffolding and foundations repaired, fallen/unstable trees removed upstream. A study assessed damage to the river bed and bridge foundations and hydraulic-modelling scenario runs were completed for the bridge and temporary scaffolding. (p2) [TAB]
* Extensive physical damage to the bridge: supports and railings visibly damaged, with large piles of debris (branches, wood, metal fencing) lodged against the structure, indicating strong flood flows and likely structural compromise requiring repair and cleanup. (p3) [IMG]
* {04 Dec 2015 - 06 Dec 2015, 26 Dec 2015, 05 Jan 2016 - 06 Jan 2016}: Quant: 1 bridge (p1) [IMG, MUL]

### Whittle Burn
* Localised flooding issues around the Whittle Burn prompted RMAs to investigate the watercourse and to propose provision of a localised flood warning system; actions also included investigating whether live reservoir (Whittle Dene Reservoir) levels could be provided to residents. (p2) [TAB]

### Kielder Water
* {01 Nov 2016}: Subject of flood management actions: the EA met to discuss reducing the maximum water level to provide greater flood water attenuation; reservoir modelling was transferred to Aquator to determine an acceptable maximum water level; any viable changes to the maximum water level would be implemented from 1 November 2016. (p3) [PAG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Northumbrian Water
* NW carried out CCTV survey, cleansing and relining of some sewers in Ovingham following flood impacts; proposed repair of the surface water outfall and installation of a flap valve on a combined sewer overflow to reduce future flooding risk. (p2) [TAB]
