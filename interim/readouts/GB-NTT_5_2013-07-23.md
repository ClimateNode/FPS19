

# Hucknall flooding 23 Jul 2013

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Flooding In Hucknall On the 23 July 2013<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/hucknall-sec19-committee-report.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Hucknall
* The town experienced flooding as part of the flooding event on 23 July 2013. (p1)
* Hucknall experienced major flooding during the intense rainfall on 23 July 2013; local roads and carparks and properties were affected as part of the wider county impacts. (p4) [PAG]
* The town experienced flooding from multiple sources during the event: Baker Lane Brook and Titchfield Park Brook overtopping, surface water runoff, and combined sewer surcharge. Impacts included internal flooding of commercial premises in the town centre and internal flooding to residential properties (including 8 properties at Thoresby Dale), plus flooding reported on Portland Road, St Johns Crescent, Story Gardens and Arden Close (Arden Close exacerbated by sewer surcharge). (p5)
* During the extreme rainfall event of 23 July 2013 surface water runoff far exceeded the design capacity of the drainage systems in Hucknall, causing surface water flooding across the town. Contributory factors cited include impermeable ground after a dry period, increased hard surfacing of front gardens (raising runoff), and historic development that has interfered with natural flow routes. (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* The extreme rainfall on 23 July 2013 produced surface water runoff far in excess of the town's drainage design capacity, causing flooding across Hucknall. (p8)
* {01 Jul 2013 - 31 Jul 2013}: Experienced flooding risk during the July 2013 event from excess flows in Baker Lane Brook affecting Thoresby Dale and downstream areas; prompted an S19 investigation and the design of a highway-incorporated relief culvert and further feasibility studies to reduce future flood risk. (p10)
* Quant: 8 properties (p5)

### Nottinghamshire
* Intense short-duration rainfall on 23 July 2013 caused major flooding across the county, affecting properties, roads and carparks. (p4)
* Quant: 600 properties (p4)

### Thoresby Dale, Hucknall
* The brook overtopped at Thoresby Dale. Surcharged brook flows combined with flow from the Wighay tributary, surface water from the Leisure Centre and highway runoff at the junction of Thoresby Dale and Perlethorpe Drive, resulting in internal flooding to residential properties. Floodwater had limited ability to flow through to the River Leen. (p5)
* Thoresby Dale was flooded due to surface water runoff from the Leisure Centre car park and adjacent housing; surcharge from combined sewers within the Baker Lane Brook catchment may also have contributed to the flooding. (p6)
* Quant: 8 properties (p5)

### Perlethorpe Drive, Hucknall
* Surcharged flows from Baker Lane Brook combined with the Wighay tributary, surface water from the Leisure Centre and highway runoff converged at the junction of Thoresby Dale and Perlethorpe Drive, resulting in internal flooding of properties and restricted discharge toward the River Leen. (p5)
* Quant: 8 properties (p5)

### Portland, Hucknall
* Surface water flooding from Portland Road combined with overtopping of Titchfield Park Brook caused flooding reported on Portland Road, St Johns Crescent, Story Gardens and Arden Close; flooding at Arden Close was further exacerbated by surcharge from the combined sewer network. (p5) [PAG]
* Flooding was reported at Portland Road. Flooding was caused by the Brook overtopping (combined with surface water runoff from Portland Road) and evidence suggests surcharge from a combined sewer in the Titchfield Park Brook catchment contributed to flooding opposite Brookside. (p6)

### Brookside, Hucknall
* Surface water flows between Brookside Road and Jenny Burton Way contributed to flooding near the recreation ground and to areas south of Leen Valley Way. (p6)

### Ogle Street, Hucknall
* Baker Lane Brook overtopped its banks at Ogle Street; flows entered and passed through the town centre contributing to internal flooding of commercial premises. (p5)

### St Johns Crescent, Hucknall
* Flooding was reported at St Johns Crescent associated with the Brook overtopping its banks and combined surface water runoff south of the culverted sections near the recreation ground. (p6)

### Story Gardens, Hucknall
* Flooding was reported at Story Gardens associated with the Brook overtopping its banks and combined surface water runoff south of the culverted sections near the recreation ground. (p6)

### Arden Close, Hucknall
* Flooding was reported at Arden Close resulting from the Brook overtopping and surface water runoff; the flooding at Arden Close was further exacerbated by surcharge from the combined sewer network. A partially above-ground sewer pipe near Arden Close also acted as a small dam limiting flow away from the Close. (p6)

### Jenny Burton Way, Hucknall
* Surface water flows between Brookside Road and Jenny Burton Way contributed to flooding near the recreation ground and to areas south of Leen Valley Way. (p6)

### Titchfield Park
* The Titchfield Park Brook (limited capacity, with blocked trash screens) quickly overtopped its banks and, combined with surface water from Portland Road, caused flooding in the area south of the culverted sections near the recreation ground. Reported flooding locations linked to this were Portland Road, St Johns Crescent, Story Gardens and Arden Close. Flooding at Arden Close was further exacerbated by surcharge from the combined sewer network. (p5)
* The map labels 'Titchfield Park Brook' (western side) showing both culverted (within built-up area) and open channel sections. During the extreme rainfall event of 23rd July 2013 the catchment experienced surface water runoff far in excess of system capacity; culverted/open sections in this area are therefore likely to have been subject to excessive flows and may have been overwhelmed, contributing to local surface water flooding in and around the park area. (p7) [IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Baker Lane Brook
* The brook overtopped its banks (including at Ogle Street and Thoresby Dale). Overtopping caused flows through the town centre contributing to internal flooding of commercial premises and, where surcharged flows combined with tributary and surface runoff at the junction of Thoresby Dale and Perlethorpe Drive, resulted in internal flooding to residential properties. Floodwater had limited ability to flow through to the River Leen. (p5)
* Baker Lane Brook is shown on the map with both culverted and open channel sections through the central catchment. Given the report that the 23rd July 2013 extreme rainfall produced surface water runoff far exceeding drainage capacity, these sections of Baker Lane Brook in built-up areas are likely to have experienced excessive flows and may have contributed to or been affected by local surface water flooding. (p7) [IMG]
* {01 Jul 2013 - 31 Jul 2013}: Brook surcharged during the event; excess flows from Baker Lane Brook contributed to flooding risk downstream (Thoresby Dale and downstream area). Trash screens were cleared and culvert CCTV surveys undertaken as part of the response; a relief culvert has been designed to take excess flows during storm events. (p10)
* Quant: 8 properties (p5)

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* The report records that the County Council, as Lead Local Flood Authority, investigated and reported on the flooding event in Hucknall; no specific physical impacts to the council as an asset are described in the text provided. (p1)
* Responded to a flooding event by initiating and coordinating emergency planning procedures, providing emergency response crews to assist in management of the flooding, participating in the police-led Tactical Coordinating Group, leading the Section 19 Flood Investigation, and commissioning feasibility and design work for flood relief (including a town-centre culvert) across the affected catchments. (p9)

### Wighay Tributary
* The Wighay tributary was surcharged and its flows combined with Baker Lane Brook, surface water from the Leisure Centre and highway runoff at the junction of Thoresby Dale and Perlethorpe Drive, contributing to internal flooding of properties. (p5)
* Quant: 8 properties (p5)

### Severn Trent
* Surcharge from Severn Trent Water combined sewers may have contributed to flooding at Thoresby Dale and to flooding on Portland Road opposite Brookside. (p6)
* Provided emergency response crews during the flood event and investigated the hydraulic operation and standards of public sewers that were reported to have surcharged during the event. (p9) [PAG]

### Ashfield District Council
* Responded to the flood event by initiating emergency planning, providing emergency response crews (including clearing trash screens), actively engaging in the Section 19 Flood Investigation, participating in local community response and resilience measures, attending NCC flood warden training, and promoting/managing Repair and Renew grants to support affected properties. (p9)
* Quant: 65 properties (p9)

### Baker Lane Brook Culvert
* {2017}: A flood relief culvert (to take excess flows from Baker Lane Brook during storm events) has been designed to be incorporated into the Hucknall Town Centre Improvement Scheme to reduce flood risk to Thoresby Dale and downstream areas. (p10)

### Baker Lane Brook Catchment
* The Brook quickly overtopped its banks after trash screens blocked culvert entrances, which prevented water entering culverts and contributed to flooding of nearby streets (areas south of the culverted sections, including the recreation ground vicinity, and reported flooding at Portland Road, St Johns Crescent, Story Gardens and Arden Close). (p6)

### Titchfield Park Brook
* In the Titchfield Park Brook catchment the majority of surface water is captured by a combined sewer and evidence suggests surcharge from this combined sewer contributed to flooding on Portland Road opposite Brookside. (p6)

### Hucknall Catchment
* Topography and impermeable ground in the Hucknall catchment, together with the extreme rainfall of 23 July 2013, generated surface water runoff that exceeded drainage capacity and contributed to flooding in Hucknall. (p8)
