

# Maplebeck flooding 18 Oct 2023 - 20 Oct 2023

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Maplebeck – Storm Babet, October 2023<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/maplebeck-s19-storm-babet-oct-2023.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Maplebeck
* Flooding of roads and properties in Maplebeck caused by intense rainfall following a prolonged wet period that saturated the ground, leading to faster onset of flooding. (p1)
* Internal flooding affected dwellings in the central area of the village (highlighted on the map). Five residential properties experienced internal flooding; additional properties had flooding to gardens and outbuildings; the public highway was rendered unpassable. The village sits in a natural bowl, increasing its susceptibility to surface water flooding. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Internal flooding in the village (Maplebeck sits in a natural bowl resulting in surface water ingress to built areas). The mapped surface-water flood extents correlate with flooding experienced during Storm Babet. Local surface-water assets (trash screens on culverts, highway gullies, below-ground piped system) were reported as of potential concern during follow-up meetings. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 5 residential properties (p2) [IMG, PAG]

### Nottinghamshire
* County-level response to flooding included 24-hour emergency planning support, delivering sandbags, closing roads to protect safety and avoid further flood damage, sharing resources with district/borough partners, liaising with parish councils/community groups/affected residents and businesses, and initiating the Section 19 Flood Investigation to establish causes, mechanisms and severity of impacts. (p5)
* Quant: 77 roads (p5)

### Newark and Sherwood
* Identified as a category one responder under the Civil Contingencies Act with responsibilities to respond to emergencies; therefore involved in planning and response to the flooding caused by Storm Babet (text links the council to emergency planning and control/reduction of flood impacts). (p4)
* Staff and council services were mobilised to respond to flooding across the district: visiting impacted communities and vulnerable individuals, helping evacuations, delivering aqua sacs, providing emergency accommodation, coordinating clean-up of roads and removal of bulky waste from flooded homes, identifying humanitarian needs and delivering recovery grant funding to eligible residents. (p5)

### Church Lane, Maplebeck
* Surface water flooding in the central area of Maplebeck rendered the public highway unpassable; Church Lane is within the centrally flooded area shown on the map. (p2) [IMG]

### Maplebeck Road, Caunton
* Surface water flooding affected the central village area and made the public highway impassable; Maplebeck Road is shown as bounding/within the highlighted flooded area. (p2) [IMG]

### The Beehive Inn
* Located within the centrally highlighted area subjected to internal flooding, the building and its immediate surroundings were affected by surface water inundation. (p2) [IMG]

### River Trent
* Reported predicted extreme levels of the River Trent created a continued risk of river flooding that prompted the council to carry out in-person visits to high‑risk communities to reinforce the need to prepare and take immediate action; this risk influenced the local flood response and recovery planning. (p5)


## Topics without coordinates

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Played a central role in responding to the Major Incident caused by Storm Babet: initiated a Community Flood Recovery Grant to support individuals affected by internal flooding; attended sites after flooding to verify internal flooding and offer support, guidance and advice to affected residents and businesses; gathered and shared information with other risk management authorities and partners; visited flood-affected residents and businesses to establish sources of internal flooding. (p4)
* Coordinated in-person visits to communities at high risk due to predicted extreme River Trent levels; led recovery activities once risk passed including delivery of grant funding to eligible impacted individuals/communities; supported restocking of community aqua sac stores; ran a multi-agency debrief and is developing training, signage and Property Flood Resilience work for communities affected by Storm Babet. (p6)

### Highway Authority
* Provided site-based presence and investigations immediately following the flood event; responsible for maintenance of public highway drainage assets (text records concerns raised about hydraulic effectiveness of surface water assets serving the village, including trash screens on culverts, highway gullies and below-ground piped systems and a commissioned investigation as part of follow-up). (p4)
