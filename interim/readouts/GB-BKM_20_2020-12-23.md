## Place names with coordinates

### Thornborough
* {general}: Areas of the village are at fluvial, surface-water and groundwater flood risk: most of the village is Flood Zone 1 but Lower End and the area around Thornborough Mill are Flood Zone 3 (≥1PCSYM annual chance); heightened surface water flood risk along the watercourses with many immediate areas at risk from a 3.33PCSYM (1 in 30) annual chance rainfall event and more widespread surface-water flooding predicted at 0.1PCSYM AEP (1 in 1,000); potential for high groundwater levels across large areas including the central village green and streets (High Street, Back Street, Palmers Moor, Chapel Lane, The Green). (p5)
* The village experienced flooding during the 23 December 2020 storm (flooding began around 15:00), with overtopping of local watercourses, converged overland and surface water flows and groundwater emergence causing internal flooding of homes, resident displacement, loss of possessions and negative mental and physical health impacts. (p6)
* Internal flooding to residential properties within the settlement, triggering a Section 19 investigation. (p13)
* The village was impacted by flooding during the December 2020 event (23 December 2020); this prompted a Section 19 flood investigation and collection of evidence (photos, questionnaires, rainfall and drainage data) to understand causes and impacts. (p14)
* Overbank flows and surface-water overland flow affected multiple locations in the village during the event: flooding at Lower End Bridge and Hatchet Leys Bridge, Tonne Brook beginning to overtop at the High Street, overland flow from fields accumulating on Back Street, and high flows at the inlet to Thornborough Pond. (p22) [PAG, IMG]
* {1902, 1935, March 1947, April 1998, July 2000, 2006, July 2007, 23 Dec 2020}: Repeated flooding of the village from ordinary watercourses, surface water and the River Great Ouse across multiple events: 1902 (whole village flooded, several houses flooded by rapid ingress), 1935 (storm — brook at Bridge Street burst its banks, forced down a brick wall, water rose rapidly to around 6 ft and entered houses to ~1 ft depth; many properties affected), March 1947 (overtopping of the River Great Ouse led to ordinary watercourses overtopping and High Street/Back Street and the village green being flooded to about 6 ft), April 1998 (heavy rainfall led to very high river levels and road/ford flooding), July 2000 (internal/external flooding affecting the village), 2006 (two properties reported flooded), July 2007 (around 10 properties flooded; River Great Ouse believed to have flooded the Thornborough Mill area), and the reported event on 23 December 2020 when the village was the subject of the investigation. Impacts described include inundation of streets (High Street, Back Street, The Green), internal property flooding, damage to carpets and furniture, and significant depths of standing water in parts of the village and at the ford on Back Street. (p23) [PAG, IMG, TAB, MUL]
* The village experienced overtopping of the River Great Ouse and subsequent overtopping of ordinary watercourses; properties across the centre and Lower End were flooded (internal and external flooding reported), with roughly ten properties cited in some accounts and reports that up to 17 or 18 houses had water entry. (p24)
* Following the flood event on 23rd December 2020, local watercourses (notably Cowerde Brook) caused flooding issues in the village. Transport for Buckinghamshire cleared gullies and culverts in the adopted highway. The Buckingham and Ouzel IDB cleared a section of channel upstream of Lower End bridge to increase capacity, removed a concrete in‑channel weir on the upstream face of Hatchet Leys Bridge, and cleared the stream between bridges. Fly‑tipped debris in the highway ditch beside the IDB‑maintained watercourse was identified as obstructing flow and a potential contributor to flooding. A section of watercourse flows to a pumping station alongside Lower Road. (p29)
* Flooding occurred in the village beginning at approximately 15:00 on 23 December 2020; floodwaters affected the village and later receded. (p33) [TAB, PAG]
* Flooding began in the village at approximately 15:00 on 23/12/2020. Internal flooding was observed on Bridge Street at 16:00 with water coming up through the floorboards of a property. Lower End Bridge and Hatchet Leys bridge became impassable; roads were flooded and closed (including High Street). Residents deployed sandbags on Lower End and High Street. Flood water in the village had receded by about 23:00 on 23/12/2020. (p34) [TAB, PAG]
* Extreme rainfall on an already saturated catchment caused multiple ordinary watercourses to overtop in the village (at least seven locations) and produced overland flow pathways, resulting in widespread flooding across Thornborough on 23 December 2020. (p36) [PAG, IMG]
* Multiple local watercourses overtopped in the village: Thorn Brook flooded the ford and footpath on Back Street (water pooled on the road, reaching the second railing); Tonne Brook overtopped near the pond and flowed through the grounds of Thornborough Manor; the pond overtopped and flooded the road at Location 2; Bridge Street experienced overtopping to the first white railing; a culvert near the Old Manor surcharged and backed water onto High Street; blocked culvert/culvert debris caused flooding behind the Maltings. (p37) [PAG]
* Extreme rainfall on a saturated catchment and overtopping of ordinary watercourses caused widespread local flooding in the village. Ordinary watercourses overtopped in at least seven locations, producing overland flow paths that flooded streets and low-lying areas; groundwater emerged from springs behind the Maltings and on High Street; culvert surcharge and blocked drains exacerbated flooding in the village. (p38) [PAG, IMG]
* High River Great Ouse levels occurred in the village, with the south bank overtopped at Thornborough Mill. Groundwater emerged from a spring behind the Maltings on Bridge Lane (Location 14) and from the ground on the High Street (Location 9). Overland runoff from saturated fields (including around Stonelands Farm) flowed onto roads and into rear gardens on Lower End and High Street and 'poured' onto Bridge Street. A manhole cover near Location 2 lifted with flood water, and sewer backup caused water to enter toilets on Bridge Street near Location 17. (p39) [PAG]
* Converging overland flows, overflows from watercourses and groundwater ingress caused widespread localised flooding across the village. Flooding led to vehicles becoming stranded and flooded at the Back Street / Lower End junction, significant inundation of the High Street (residents had to wade to cars), and multiple properties flooded internally (including a property on Hatchet Leys Lane, a property on High Street and the Two Brewers pub). (p40)
* Village-wide flooding affected multiple properties (internal and external) and infrastructure: properties flooded internally and externally, roads inundated (including Lower End, Back Street Ford, Bridge Street, High Street), vehicles stranded requiring rescue, and some residents cut off while floodwaters persisted. (p41)
* Footpaths located within the village floodplain were inundated during the event; this flooding did not present a significant hazard to residents. (p43)
* Flooding in Thornborough caused damage to property and affected people/communities, prompting a high-level appraisal of mitigation measures (improvements to data collection and modelling, flood warning and incident management, community resilience, asset maintenance and flood risk management schemes). (p52) [PAG]
* Properties in Thornborough were affected by flooding during the event on 23 December 2020. The investigation recommends Property Flood Resilience measures for at-risk properties and community-level actions (a Community Flood Toolkit and a flood-preparedness information pack) to reduce future impacts. (p54) [TAB, PAG]

Some residents deployed door barriers which successfully prevented water ingress; others, despite landscaping intended to redirect flows, still experienced floodwater approaching their properties. A blocked ditch below the solar farm at Thornborough Grounds contributed to overland flows in the Thornborough Mill area. Homeowner resilience measures prevented internal flooding in some cases. (p61) [PAG]
* Overland flow from farmland to the south of the village caused external flooding around properties off High Street; properties avoided internal flooding due to resilience works carried out by homeowners. (p62) [PAG]
* On 23 December 2020 the village experienced intense surface water and river-related flooding. At least five residential properties suffered internal flooding. Multiple ordinary watercourses and culverts were overtopped in the village, roads became unpassable, drains were blocked and overwhelmed, and a manhole cover lifted with flood water near properties. Water backed up from sewers into toilets (reported on Bridge Street). Concentrated overland flows and runoff from saturated surrounding fields contributed to flooding across streets. At the junction of Back Street and Lower End at least 13 cars became stranded and flooded requiring Fire Brigade attendance. Residents leaving the village hall had to wade through significant flooding on the High Street during an episode starting between 19:00–20:00 and lasting roughly five hours, causing major disruption and distress. (p64) [PAG]
* The Thornborough village catchment (Cowerde Brook) produced an elevated flow response to heavy rainfall on 23rd December 2020 while soils were saturated, giving an estimated flow return period of about 5–20 years. This led to increased flood risk to the village and prompted recommendations for community resilience (Flood Action Plan, Community Flood Toolkit) and Property Flood Resilience at highest-risk properties.
