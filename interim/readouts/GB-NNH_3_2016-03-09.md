### Lyveden Brook
* Lyveden Brook was implicated in the event: ordinary watercourse flooding from the brook contributed to flooding of residential areas near its confluence with the River Nene. Upstream of Stoke Doyle Road a severe narrowing at a large tree, a length of barbed wire across the channel and a build-up of debris were observed, restricting flow and likely exacerbating flooding. Parts of the western developed area drain to public sewers that discharge to the brook, increasing flow into the channel during the event. (p10)
* On the morning of 9th March 2016 the depth of water in the brook increased by approximately two metres above usual, causing the banks to be overtopped and water to flow across rear gardens of properties on the north and east edge of the Warren Bridge residential estate. Water levels in gardens and the brook subsided in the late afternoon after the rain stopped. (p13)
* Water levels in the brook rose during the event and contributed to higher garden and upstream water depths. Debris (barbed wire) trapped upstream reduced conveyance and caused the channel to become deeper upstream; the narrow, twisting channel downstream limited flow away to the River Nene, contributing to raised water levels. (p14)
* Shown with evidence of high water levels; produced overbank and overland flow routes that contributed to schematic flood areas and inundation of nearby rear gardens and properties. Narrowing of the channel and a barbed‑wire obstruction were identified as localised pinch points that increased water levels and localised flooding. (p33) [IMG]
* A barbed-wire fence crossing the brook trapped debris after high flows, creating an obstruction that impeded water flow and contributed to flooding of adjacent banks and nearby gardens/properties. (p46) [IMG]
* Quant: over 70 properties flooded internally; over 200 reports of flooding (p10)
* Quant: two metres deeper than usual (p13)
