* {12 Jun 2016}: During a heavy downpour surface water flowed along High Street from east and west onto the south-side footway. Water collected at a low point where gullies and underground pipework could not accept the flow; depth increased until it flowed under a gate east of an affected property, across paved external areas to gardens and reached the property's front doorway, entering the building. Sandbags were used to try to limit ingress; water later receded via the footway gully. (p13) [PAG]

## Topics without coordinates

### Highway Authority
* {general}: Northamptonshire Highways has duties over highway drainage and maintenance; highway drainage systems are designed to drain surface water run-off from the highway catchment only. Historic highway drainage may be of inadequate standard and can become the responsibility of the highway authority; the authority has powers to construct drains and discharge water, affecting how surface water flooding is managed from roads and adjacent areas. (p17)
* {general}: Northamptonshire Highways' drainage assets (carriageway channel and highway drains) are described as subject to blockages or insufficient capacity, leading to runoff that causes flooding of the carriageway and creates flood risk to properties; the authority is instructed to cleanse drains, assess capacity and consider improvement works where required. (p23)

### Northamptonshire County Council
* {general}: As the Lead Local Flood Authority (LLFA), NCC investigates flood incidents under Section 19 of the Flood and Water Management Act, maintains an asset register of structures affecting flooding (Section 21), and keeps a record of flooding hotspots across the county. NCC can suggest causes and make recommendations to mitigate flood risk but does not have the mandate or funding to force other risk management authorities to act. (p17)
