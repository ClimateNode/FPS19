

# Kettering flooding 16 Aug 2020

## Place names with coordinates

### Waverley Road, Kettering
* Intense, short-duration rainfall overwhelmed surface water drainage, causing excess surface water to flow over ground to low points around properties on Waverley Road. This led to internal flooding of a garage that is integral to the main occupied building; the garage has experienced internal flooding on more than one occasion in the last five years. (p6)
* Internal flooding to a garage that is integral to the main occupied building; the garage has flooded on multiple occasions (including the incident that triggered this investigation). (p8)
* {general}: Flooding affected properties on Waverley Road: 52 Waverley Road flooded through the garage and into the rear garden; properties 38-50 Waverley Road experienced flooding to rear gardens. (p11) [TAB]
* Relief culvert under Waverley Road has unclear ownership; a 225mm diameter surface water public sewer runs beneath Waverley Road and was implicated in the incident when the adjacent 300mm pipe was severed and connected to East Brook, altering the drainage connection in the Waverley Road corridor. (p15) [PAG]
* Surface water from intense rainfall overwhelmed a road gully and exceeded the dropped kerb, flowing across the footpath and into an integral garage. One property on Waverley Road flooded internally and other properties experienced flooding to their rear gardens. (p17)
* Surface water flowed over dropped kerbs and down through the integral garage at 52 Waverley Road, then into the rear garden where depth increased and water spread northwards into neighbouring rear gardens and properties. (p18)
* Properties at the rear of Waverley Road were affected by the surcharging East Brook culvert; the severed/misconnected surface water sewer discharges directly into the culvert, and lids on the relief culvert have been observed to blow off on Waverley Road when the East Brook is surcharged. (p19) [PAG]
* The road flooded when intense heavy rainfall exceeded surface water drainage capacity. The East Brook culvert in Waverley Road surcharged and flooded out at locations where the culvert cross-sectional area reduced, causing manholes to surcharge and water to flow over ground into low points around nearby properties. (p21)
* Surface water was present on the carriageway and adjacent driveway as documented in photographs; the wet pavement at the street entrance indicates Waverley Road was affected by surface water/urban flooding. (p36) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface water from a failed/blocked drainage route pooled at the road end and was associated with a collapsed area adjacent to inspection covers and a gully, indicating drainage failure and local inundation affecting the carriageway/entrance area. (p37) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: more than one occasion in the last five years (p6)
* Quant: 1 garage (p8)

* Quant: 225mm diameter surface water public sewer; 225mm diameter foul water public sewer; 300mm diameter pipe severed (p15) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 property flooded internally (p17)
* Quant: 1 integral garage flooded (p18)

### Kettering Rugby Club
* Surface water runoff from the rugby club grounds was reported to have flowed toward Waverley Road and contributed to overtopping of the kerb and subsequent flooding of properties on Waverley Road. (p17)
* The low point at the junction with the Rugby Club entrance experienced overtopping; the manhole cover to the East Brook culvert just inside the Rugby Club entrance was surcharged and was discharging floodwater to surface. (p18)
* The gully/ surface water connection at the Rugby Club entrance was not connected into the Anglian Water system (it abutted externally to the 300mm system) and had been damaged; this restriction prevented free discharge of surface water into the main sewer and contributed to drainage problems. The connection has since been repaired. (p19) [PAG]
* The Rugby Club's surface water connection had been damaged (restricting discharge into the main surface water sewer) and the club entrance was included in the area where blocked gullies contributed to flooding; the damaged connection was investigated and subsequently repaired following coordination between AWS and Local Authorities. (p20)
* Surcharge flooding from an East Brook manhole at the Rugby Club entrance far exceeded system capacity, overwhelming the surface water drainage. The gully connection from the Rugby Club was not connected to a suitable discharge system and was unable to convey surface water effectively; overtopping occurred over dropped kerbs and the footpath at the entrance. (p21)
* Surface water reached the club entrance/driveway area; photographs show water on the paved entrance and nearby manhole covers, indicating the entrance was affected by the flooding and access was likely impeded. (p36) [IMG, PAG]
* The vehicle entrance/manhole within the Rugby Club showed significant surface damage, cracking and vertical displacement around the manhole with water pooling on the surface, indicating inundation and structural damage to the entrance associated with drainage/culvert failure. (p37) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 300mm surface water system (adjacent) (p19) [PAG]
* Quant: 3 gullies (p20)

## Topics without coordinates

### East Brook Culvert
* {general}: The East Brook Culvert has been recorded as being exceeded in previous flooding events and is linked to flooding affecting properties on Waverley Road (floodwater through a garage into the rear garden at 52 Waverley Road and flooding to rear gardens at 38–50 Waverley Road). Some other previous flooding in the area is reported to have arisen from highway-only runoff rather than the culvert. (p11)
* Manholes associated with the East Brook culvert (one inside the Rugby Club entrance and one at the rear of Waverley Road) were reported surcharged and were discharging water to surface, contributing to local flooding of gardens and property. (p18)
* Convergence of culvert legs reduces flow capacity causing a restriction and surcharge; the cut-off of the 300mm surface water sewer discharges directly into the culvert, contributing to surcharging and local flooding; relief culvert arrangement has allowed lids to blow off when surcharged. (p19)
* The East Brook culvert surcharged at points where the culvert reduced in cross-sectional area (in Waverley Road and at the rear of a property where two legs converge). Flow capacity was exceeded at these points, causing the respective manholes to surcharge and flood out. (p21)
* The culvert manhole and surrounding surfacing exhibited cracking, subsidence and water pooling, and annotations indicate collapse and likely blockage/failure in the culvert/drainage connection contributing to local surface water flooding. (p37) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Quant: 1 property (52 Waverley Road); 38–50 Waverley Road (addresses listed) (p11)
* Quant: 2 manholes surcharged (p18)
* Quant: 900mm diameter pipe; 975mm diameter pipe; cross-sectional area reduced from 1.39m² to 0.64m²; 300mm surface water sewer (p19)

### Anglian Water
* Anglian Water (AWS) public sewer infrastructure in the area was involved in the incident: records describe a 225mm surface water sewer and a 225mm foul sewer along Waverley Road/alongside 52 Waverley Road; on-site investigation found a 300mm surface water pipe (recorded by AWS) had been severed and now connects to East Brook. (p15)
* An Anglian Water surface water main (300mm) had been severed and reconnected to a relief culvert; AWS investigations confirmed the severed main and a damaged Rugby Club surface water connection that restricted discharge, issues which contributed to local drainage failure and were subject to CCTV investigation and subsequent repairs. (p19)
* Identified an unmapped relief section of the East Brook culvert which allows lids to blow off Waverley Road when the East Brook is surcharged; found an AWS surface water main had been severed and reconnected into the new culvert and the surface water connection from the Rugby Club was damaged, restricting free discharge into the main surface water sewer. Confirmed issues by CCTV and passed details to Local Authorities for repair; worked with Local Authorities to enable repair of the Rugby Club connection. (p20) [PAG]
* Quant: 225mm diameter surface water public sewer; 225mm diameter foul water public sewer; 300mm diameter pipe severed (p15)
* Quant: 300mm surface water main (p19)

### East Brook
* {general}: Previous exceedance of the East Brook culvert has been associated with flooding in the local catchment (i.e. the culvert being exceeded contributed to past flooding incidents). (p11)
* The East Brook culvert physically severed a recorded 300mm surface water public sewer, causing the pipe to now connect to the brook; the brook’s culvert interaction with the sewer was a causal feature of the drainage failure observed during the incident. (p15)
* Convergence and capacity reduction within the East Brook culvert system contributed to reduced conveyance and surcharging, with flows discharging to surface and causing local flooding of gardens and properties adjacent to the culvert. (p18)
* Convergence of East Brook culvert legs at a manhole created a hydraulic restriction that caused the culvert to surcharge; a 300mm surface water sewer was cut off and discharges directly into the East Brook culvert at the rear of Waverley Road properties, contributing to localised flooding and causing manhole/lid blow‑offs when the culvert surcharged. (p19) [PAG]
* Quant: 300mm diameter pipe severed (p15)
* Quant: 900mm diameter pipe; 975mm diameter pipe; convergence reduced approximate cross-sectional area from 1.39m² down to 0.64m²; 300mm surface water sewer (p19) [PAG]

### Northamptonshire Highways
* {17 Aug 2020}: Attended as an emergency on 17th August 2020 to respond to reports of flooding caused by blocked gullies; the NH maintenance contractor cleared 3 gullies between No. 15 to 52 and the Rugby Club entrance. All gullies were reported operational on leaving; on arrival gullies had approximately 75PCSYM silt levels. (p20)
