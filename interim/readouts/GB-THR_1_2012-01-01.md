### Quebec Road, Tilbury
* The Quebec Road sewer network and local surface water drainage were identified for hydrological modelling to determine capacity and to identify options to reduce flood risk in the Quebec Road area. (p3)
* {general}: Area is situated in EA Flood Zone 3 (tidal) and the northern part of Quebec Road is identified as being at risk of surface water flooding; LiDAR and site observations indicate this area lies at the base of a natural depression which promotes ponding/overland flow during surface water flood events. (p6) [PAG]
* {01 Sep 1993 - 30 Sep 1993, 01 Jul 1994 - 31 Jul 1994, 01 Dec 1994 - 31 Dec 1994, 01 Mar 1996 - 31 Mar 1996, 01 Apr 1998 - 30 Apr 1998, 01 Jun 2002 - 30 Jun 2002, 01 Sep 2002 - 30 Sep 2002, 01 Jun 2003 - 30 Jun 2003, 01 Aug 2011 - 31 Aug 2011, 01 Nov 2011 - 30 Nov 2011, 01 Jan 2012 - 31 Dec 2013, 2013}: Repeated flooding of the road and adjacent properties caused by surface water drainage reaching capacity and surcharging in the road; highway gullies unable to drain and property surface/roof drains unable to discharge, resulting in water rising above kerb height and flooding properties (west side internally, east side externally). (p7) [MUL]
* Properties on Quebec Road were flooded due to surcharging/backing up of the AW surface water sewer, caused by a restricted outfall and localised high silt levels in the East Dock Sewer. (p9) [PAG, MUL]
* {general}: Area has propensity to flood in extreme rainfall events (per Thurrock SWMP); recommendations target reducing surface runoff/increasing drainage capacity to reduce flood risk to residents. (p10) [PAG]

The flooding prompted a formal Section 19 Flood Investigation and recommendations for sewer upsizing, combined EA/Anglian Water modelling and surface water management measures. (p11)

(p8) [PAG, MUL]

### East Dock Sewer
* Silt build-up downstream of the Anglian Water (AW) sewer outfall identified; short-term maintenance recommended to remove silt which is affecting sewer performance and contributing to flood risk. (p3)
* Channel was highly vegetated and demonstrating limited flow, creating blockages that contributed to flooding. The EA undertook vegetation clearance and desilting works and carried out a topographic survey to determine bed levels. Survey work also identified a localised high silt level around the outfall of the AW sewer. (p8) [MUL]
* Heavily vegetated and silted condition restricted flow and the AW sewer outfall, contributing to surcharging of the sewer system and flooding of nearby properties; Environment Agency carried out vegetation clearance and targeted desilting downstream of the AW outfall. (p9) [MUL]
* {general}: Sections of the sewer are under-capacity causing surcharging of the remainder of the system; limited discharge into the East Dock sewer and potential for blockages to be flushed through to it increase flood risk in the local drainage network. (p10) [PAG]

### Thurrock Council
* As Lead Local Flood Authority and highways authority, Thurrock Council is tasked to combine sewer-network and river modelling and surface water mapping to determine drainage capacity and identify options to reduce flood risk in the Quebec Road area. (p3) [PAG]
* Ferry Road retaining wall condition was affected by flood-related drainage issues and limited flow in the adjacent East Dock Sewer; its condition impacted the viability of undertaking maintenance works and raised concerns about the wall's structural integrity, prompting a joint site visit and a recommendation to determine ownership and maintenance responsibility. (p8) [MUL]

### Anglian Water
* {general}: The sewerage network serving the area is described as undersized; a 2003 modelling study identified the last three lengths of 375 mm diameter sewer as under-capacity, resulting in surcharging of the remainder of the system. Localised blockages and standing water in pipes and drains may be flushed through to the East Dock Sewer, increasing the likelihood of sewer surcharging and flooding. (p10)

### Thurrock Highways
* {general}: Standing water in pipes and drains (even after prolonged dry periods) suggests localised blockages in the highway drainage. Thurrock Highways is required to undertake condition surveys and fully cleanse highway drainage to prevent blockages being flushed into the East Dock Sewer and to reduce the potential for flooding in the Quebec Road area. (p10)
