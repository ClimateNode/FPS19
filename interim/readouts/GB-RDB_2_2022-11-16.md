

# London Borough of Redbridge flooding 16 Nov 2022 - 17 Nov 2022

Reference document:<br>Westwood Recreation Ground: Section 19 Flood Investigation<br>100\_Redbridge/westwood-recreation-ground-section-19.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Westwood Recreation Ground
* Site subject of a Section 19 flood risk investigation, indicating flooding occurred at the recreation ground that prompted the investigation. (p1)
* The on-site watercourse exceeded its capacity and overtopped its banks causing significant fluvial flooding within the recreation ground that extended into properties backing onto the park. A trash screen at the point where the watercourse enters a culvert became blocked by debris carried by floodwaters, which exacerbated the flooding; floodwaters receded once the trash screen was cleared. (p3)
* Significant inundation of the recreation ground during the 2022 incident, with flood water extending across both the eastern and western sides of the park; most significant flooding occurred in the western section just beyond the pond and trash screen. The 2022 incident was associated with internal flooding of local properties and external flooding reports. (p6)
* The recreation ground and surrounding properties experienced flooding during the November 2022 rainfall event; attendees at a 5 June 2023 roadshow reported internal and/or external flooding associated with the 16–17 November 2022 incident. Photos of flooded sites were collected and the location was used in GIS mapping, desktop assessment and a site visit to identify overland flow routes and flood mechanisms. (p7)
* The recreation ground was the location of the reported flood incident. Seven Kings Water passes through the site into a central pond and then through a trash screen into a culvert beneath the southern half of the ground; this watercourse behaviour was associated with flooding of the recreation ground during the event. (p10) [PAG]
* Seven Kings Water overtopped its banks during heavy rainfall, causing significant flooding across the recreation ground: the pathway east of the watercourse was covered, the south-western section of the park (beyond the culvert) was flooded, and floodwaters extended towards houses that back onto the park (Chester Road, Spencer Road, Westwood Road). (p11) [IMG]
* Flooding of the recreation ground (including the south‑western section beyond the culvert). The downstream pond/trash screen became blocked with debris carried from upstream, which exacerbated flooding within the park; after contractors cleared the trash screen flood waters were observed to recede. (p13) [PAG, IMG]
* Floodwaters within the pond/park were exacerbated by a trash screen at the downstream end of the pond becoming blocked with debris carried from upstream. Blockage increased flooding within Westwood Recreation Ground; after the trash screen was cleared by contractors flood waters were observed to recede. (p14) [PAG, IMG]
* Flooding occurred within the recreation ground during the 16–17 November 2022 event when Seven Kings Water emerges from a culvert at the northern boundary and becomes an open channel and pond within the site. Surcharging of the culverted watercourse/surface water sewer contributed to inundation through the body of the recreation ground, with flow passing a trash screen and culverting beneath the southern half towards High Road. (p16) [PAG, IMG]
* Overland surface water / ponding affecting the western side of the recreation ground; adjacent properties reported flooding during the 16–17 November 2022 event. (Report also notes that, given the low rainfall return period of this event, surface water was considered an unlikely sole source of the flooding.) (p17) [IMG, PAG]
* The recreation ground was affected by flooding during the 16 Nov 2022 - 17 Nov 2022 event. Seven Kings Water (a Main River) enters the northern part of the ground and is culverted through the southern half; the report notes the western side of the ground and surrounding properties lie within the EA RoFSW 0.1PCSYM extent and that properties reported flooding during the November 2022 event. The site topography, high water table and urbanised catchment were identified as factors increasing flood risk in the area. (p18) [PAG, IMG]
* Seven Kings Water (a Main River) overtopped its banks and flooded the low-lying area of Westwood Recreation Ground. The trash screen was reported blocked by debris (branches, leaves, detritus), which reduced the watercourse hydraulic capacity and contributed to the scale of flooding; the majority of flooding occurred beyond the trash screen. Flood levels were observed to recede after the trash screen was cleared. Reported fluvial flooding is attributed to the event on 17 November 2022 and affected properties within Flood Zone 3 adjacent to the recreation ground. (p19)
* Flooding within the recreation ground caused by the Seven Kings Water reaching and overtopping within the park as a trash screen at the pond became blocked by debris, reducing hydraulic capacity and increasing flood extent. Once the trash screen was cleared flood levels were observed to recede. (p20) [PAG, IMG]
* The culverted watercourse within Westwood Recreation Ground exceeded hydraulic capacity and overtopped its banks, causing flooding of the recreation ground and surrounding properties. A blocked trash screen (debris, branches, leaves) reduced culvert capacity and exacerbated flooding. High groundwater table reduced infiltration capacity, increasing flood impact. (p21) [PAG]
* Westwood Recreation Ground flooded during the 17 November 2022 event: high water levels prevented safe unblocking of the trash screen; pumps were used to remove water from the ground (17–20 Nov). Post‑event works at the ground included tree removal around the trash screen, pond desilting, increased debris‑cleaning frequency, installation of three river flood sensors, and plans to raise the bund and reduce hardstanding to improve protection and infiltration. (p22) [TAB]
* The recreation ground and the immediate surroundings were inundated during the 16–17 Nov 2022 event; surrounding properties experienced internal and external flooding. The EA attended the site (attempted trash-screen clearance) and later recorded flood extent and flooded property data. (p23) [TAB, PAG]
* The watercourse within Westwood Recreation Ground exceeded its capacity and overtopped its banks during the event; the trash screen at the point where the watercourse enters the culverted system was reported blocked, which exacerbated the fluvial flooding. Multiple surrounding properties experienced internal and external flooding as a result. (p24)
* Quant: 8 properties (internal flooding); 10 properties (external flooding) (p3)
* Quant: 8 properties internally flooded; 10 reports of external flooding (p6)
* Quant: ten reports of external flooding; eight reports of internal flooding (p13) [PAG, IMG]

### London Borough of Redbridge
* A trash screen on the waterway within Westwood Recreation Ground is identified in recommendations for more frequent maintenance to help reduce future flood risk. (p4)
* Properties and land around Westwood Recreation Ground experienced flooding during the November 2022 event; attendees reported internal and/or external flooding. A survey, desktop GIS mapping and a site visit were carried out to investigate overland flow routes and flood mechanisms at the recreation ground. (p7) [TAB, PAG]
* Westwood Recreation Ground experienced significant flooding when Seven Kings Water overtopped its banks, covering the pathway to the east of the watercourse and flooding the south‑western section of the park beyond the culvert. Floodwaters extended towards and reached houses bordering the western side of the park. The trash screen at the downstream end of the pond became blocked with debris, exacerbating flooding; once Redbridge contractors cleared the screen, flood waters were observed to recede. (p12) [PAG]
* Intense, concentrated rainfall during 16–17 Nov 2022 (three distinct peaks, with the largest on the evening of 16 Nov and early 17 Nov) contributed to the flood event affecting Redbridge; rainfall data from nearby gauges were used to characterise the event and its return period for the Westwood Recreation Ground catchment. (p15) [PAG]
* Fluvial flooding from Seven Kings Water overtopped its banks and flowed into the low-lying area of Westwood Recreation Ground beyond the trash screen. The trash screen was reported blocked by debris which reduced hydraulic capacity; the majority of flooding occurred beyond the trash screen. Flood levels receded after the trash screen was cleared. Properties that reported flooding were located within Flood Zone 3. (p19) [IMG, PAG]
* The borough experienced fluvial flooding on 17 November 2022: the watercourse in Westwood Recreation Ground overtopped, flooding the recreation ground and surrounding properties. Blocked trash screen and high groundwater table increased flood severity and reduced infiltration. (p21) [PAG]
* A flood on 16–17 November 2022 caused internal and external flooding of multiple properties surrounding Westwood Recreation Ground and triggered a Section 19 investigation by the borough. (p23) [PAG]
* Quant: 10 reports of external flooding to properties; 8 reports of internal flooding (p12) [PAG]

### Spencer Road, London Borough of Redbridge
* Properties along Spencer Road back onto Westwood Recreation Ground and were affected by floodwaters from Seven Kings Water overtopping, with floodwater extending towards these houses. (p11) [IMG]
* During the 17 November 2022 event a manhole chamber on Spencer Road exhibited very high water levels, suggesting a partial blockage in the surface water/sewer system; this was reported to TWUL. Although no sewer flooding was reported, the high manhole level indicates the sewer network was under stress and could contribute to future sewer flooding if overwhelmed. (p21) [PAG]
* Flooding occurred along Spencer Road during the flood event; the London Fire Brigade reported flooding and requested attendance from the Environment Agency. (p23) [TAB]
* A sewer network blockage on Spencer Road was reported and is being investigated in relation to the flood event. (p24)

### Chester Road, London Borough of Redbridge
* Properties along Chester Road back onto Westwood Recreation Ground and were affected by floodwaters from Seven Kings Water overtopping, with floodwater extending towards these houses. (p11) [IMG]
* Flooding occurred along Chester Road during the flood event; the London Fire Brigade reported flooding and requested attendance from the Environment Agency. (p23) [TAB]

### Westwood Road, London Borough of Redbridge

## Topics without coordinates

### Redbridge Council
* Redbridge contractors cleared debris from the blocked trash screen at the downstream end of the pond in Westwood Recreation Ground after an initial unsuccessful attempt due to health and safety concerns; clearance was followed by observed recession of flood waters. (p13) [PAG, IMG]
* Redbridge contractors attended the site to clear debris from the trash screen at the downstream end of the pond. Contractors were initially unable to clear the screen due to health and safety concerns; later the council contractors cleared the screen and flood waters were observed to recede. (p14) [IMG, PAG]
* Redbridge (the local authority) responded to flooding at Westwood Recreation Ground on 17 November 2022: officers attended (~09:20), found water levels too high to safely unblock the trash screen, ordered pumps and pumped water out of the recreation ground (17–20 Nov), and assisted affected residents with accommodation and welfare. Post‑flood actions included tree removal around the trash screen, pond desilting, increasing debris‑cleaning frequency (monthly → fortnightly during autumn/winter), purchase/installation of three river flood sensors, and plans to raise the park bund and reduce hardstanding. Redbridge also hosted public engagement on planned works (roadshow 6 Jun 2023) and progressed an environmental improvement scheme in partnership with the Greater London Authority. (p22) [TAB, PAG]
* The local authority produced the Section 19 flood investigation following the event and set out conclusions and recommendations: the watercourse overtopped and a blocked trash screen exacerbated flooding; recommended actions include increased trash screen clearance, pond maintenance upstream, enforcement against fly-tipping, increased site monitoring, and liaison with TWUL regarding sewer investigations. (p24) [PAG]
* The Seven Kings Water channel through the recreation ground is constrained — confined to a rectangular concrete channel for most of the ground and culverted beneath the southern section — which limits hydrological capacity and resilience during flood events. The report recommends deculverting the southern section, extending banks and adding aquatic vegetation to increase capacity and resilience. (p26) [PAG]

### Thames Water
* Flooding was most significant in the western section just beyond the pond and the trash screen, indicating the trash screen area was affected by flood water. (p6)
* The TWUL sewer network in the Westwood Recreation Ground catchment conveyed flows via culverts and large-diameter sewers that discharge through the site and towards South Park; the network was part of the flood flow paths during the 16–17 November 2022 event and likely contributed to flooding at the recreation ground and downstream. (p16) [IMG]
* TWUL (Thames Water) was identified for follow-up to investigate the sewer network blockage on Spencer Road that relates to the flood event. (p24)

### Seven Kings Water Catchment
* The Seven Kings Water catchment was the hydrological context for the event: the watercourse associated with this catchment exceeded capacity and overtopped, delivering debris and detritus downstream that blocked the trash screen within Westwood Recreation Ground and exacerbated the fluvial flooding which extended into adjacent properties. (p3)
* Seven Kings Water overtopped its banks on both sides during the heavy rainfall, the overtopping covered a park pathway and was the cause of flooding that affected Westwood Recreation Ground and nearby properties. (p11)
* Quant: 8 properties (internal flooding); 10 properties (external flooding) (p3)

### Environment Agency
* During the 16–17 Nov 2022 event EA staff responded: a Field Team attended and attempted to clear a trash screen but could not fully clear it due to health and safety concerns; Community Information Officers and a drone operator later attended to record flood extent and flooded property data. The EA issued a Flood Guidance Statement (16 Nov) and a flood alert for the Middle River Roding area (raised 17 Nov). (p23) [TAB]

### London Fire Brigade
* During the 16–17 Nov 2022 event the LFB reported flooding along Spencer Road, Westwood Road and Chester Road and requested EA attendance. (p23) [TAB]
