

# Woodford Halse flooding 21 Nov 2012

## Place names with coordinates

### Woodford Halse
* Intense rainfall on a saturated catchment caused excess surface water to flow overland along natural contours to low points in the village, resulting in internal flooding of properties. (p5) [PAG]
* Intense, short‑duration rainfall onto a saturated catchment caused excess surface water to flow overland following natural contours to low points in the village; historic drainage was ineffective, resulting in internal flooding of properties in the village. (p6) [PAG]
* Internal flooding of residential properties across four separate locations in the village: eight properties flooded internally in one clustered location; at least one property flooded internally at a second location; at least one property flooded internally at a third location; properties at a fourth location are at high risk of regular flooding. (p8)
* Localised ponding at the road junction of Scrivens Hill and Byfield Road during heavy rainfall deepened until water flowed back into and inundated a residential property. (p13)
* Residents of Woodford Halse were identified in the report as being at risk during the Flood Incident; guidance and actions for preparing properties and community resilience (e.g. signing up to Flood Warning Direct, property-level protection, community flood plans, flood wardens) were recommended. (p15)
* Intense short‑duration rainfall onto a saturated catchment overwhelmed existing drainage systems and watercourses. Silt and debris were observed blocking inlets, preventing satisfactory flows; excessive flows overtopped and backed up drainage, causing water to flow to low points in the village and into other drainage systems unable to cope. (p16)
* Drainage systems and watercourses were unable to convey excessive flows from higher ground, causing overtopping and water to flow or back up to low points in the village; land drainage overspilled onto the public highway and into private property, creating flood risk to properties. (p17)
* {general}: Areas in and around Woodford Halse experienced surface water flow and pooling on streets (e.g. Farndon Road / Pool Farm Court) and had debris-obstructed culverts on watercourses and the Main River Cherwell (increasing local flood risk). (p36) [IMG]

### South Street, Woodford Halse
* Riparian-owned ditches on South Street are culverted at the low point adjacent to No.35 South Street; the ditch system overtopped at this point and water flowed overland from the culvert low point toward the boundaries of properties on Cobbley Close. (p10)
* Riparian-owned ditches at a low point adjacent to No.35 South Street overtopped, causing overland flow toward the boundaries of properties on Cobbley Close. The headwall to the piped system was very shallow and the pipe diameter (~150 mm) was likely insufficient, leading to overtopping during the event. (p11) [PAG]
* An area of the public highway at the road low point became flooded. (p12)
* Flood water collected at the low point of South Street into a culverted ditch and formed a flow path that continued away from South Street. (p40) [IMG]

## Topics without coordinates

### Thames Water
* The Thames Water grille at the culvert entrance (Ash Way/Pool Street to River Cherwell) was reported blocked regularly with debris; the culvert/open watercourse was not free flowing and contained substantial debris and vegetation, contributing to impaired conveyance and local flood risk. (p11)
* Water entering the public sewer and limited sewer/asset capacity is identified as contributing to flood risk to properties; Thames Water is asked to assess sources entering the sewer system and asset capacity and consider improvement works where these cause flood risk. (p18)

### Woodford Halse Catchment
* Significant rainfall on 21 November 2012 (c.32mm) across the catchment led to saturated ground and triggered surface water flooding incidents in the catchment, contributing to multiple local flooding events described in the report. (p10)

### Highway Authority
* {general}: Highway drainage assets in the village have insufficient capacity causing runoff/overtopping of the public highway and creating flood risk to properties; recommendation for regular highway drainage cleansing and capacity improvements to prevent overtopping. (p18)

### Daventry District Council
* Piped culverts and ordinary watercourses in the village lack hydraulic capacity or maintenance, leading to overtopping risk to the public highway and properties; recommendation to confirm ownership/maintenance responsibilities and consider replacing conduits to allow free flow in extreme events. (p18)

### Environment Agency
* Support to instate, train and liaise with a Flood Warden and the production of a village community flood plan to help mitigate and respond to surface water runoff and groundwater emergence affecting the village. (p18)
