## Place names with coordinates

### Kenley Treatment Works
* Kenley Water Treatment Works was at significant risk from Caterham Bourne flows and rising groundwater; relief efforts concentrated on preventing high floodwaters reaching the works to avoid a shutdown (which would have had wider consequences). High-volume pumping and other measures were deployed to protect the works; the situation became critical during the incident and was a focus of multi-agency response. (p7) [MUL]
* The Waterworks was the focus of protection efforts during the flood: defences were erected and pumps installed to protect the asset; Thames Water assisted and attended sites of foul (sewer) flooding associated with the event. (p8)
* Heavy and prolonged rainfall threatened the Kenley Water Treatment Works; significant pumping was required to prevent its closure and to avoid property flooding. This pumping increased volumes entering the sewer system as routes were sought to remove water from the risk area. (p23) [TAB]
* High flows in the bourne caused the culverted watercourse to surcharge and flood the water treatment works in February 2014; floodwaters reached the main building and the works were threatened with shutdown. Emergency response included erection of demountable flood defences, sandbags and high‑volume pumps, and pumping floodwater away from the site along Godstone Road to an open section at Purley Cross. Pumping was impeded by a wall constructed since the 2001 flood. (p31) [IMG]
* Water levels at the Waterworks reached critical levels on 14 February 2014, triggering the temporary commencement of the evacuation plan. (p34) [IMG]
* Water was pumped from Kenley WTW into an open section of the bourne north of the railway as an emergency management measure; this pumping directed water into the watercourse to help relieve downstream pressure during the flood response. (p35) [IMG]
* Rising floodwater in the Kenley area threatened to compromise the integrity of the Kenley Water Treatment Works. High-volume pumps were deployed to remove floodwater from the bourne around the WTW to a point downstream with greater capacity; water was also pumped from the balancing pond at Purley Oaks into a separate drainage system. Flood-fighting operations were maintained 24 hours a day to protect the WTW and local communities. (p43)
* Kenley Water Treatment Works experienced surcharging issues and flood risk; sandbags and demountable flood defences were provided and the site was monitored as 'stable' during the event. (p54) [TAB, MUL]
* Water levels rose above critical on 14 February causing pumping to run at maximum capacity; significant groundwater pumping was undertaken to lower the water table. Control was escalated to London Gold and evacuation plans (for up to 220 properties) were drawn up but later reversed. Environment Agency pumps later replaced LFB high-volume pumps and the site status was later recorded as 'Green'. (p55) [TAB, MUL]
* Flood water management required on site: pumping operations were deployed and coordinated, site status recorded as 'Green', and water levels reported stable during monitoring. (p56) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: approximately 47,000 people; hundreds of properties (p7) [MUL]
* Quant: 47,000 homes; 3 high-volume pumps (p31) [IMG]
* Quant: 220 properties (evacuation planned); 9 properties evacuated (p55) [TAB, MUL]
* Quant: 2 pumps (p56) [TAB, PAG]

* Kenley Water Treatment Works (WTW) at Godstone Road was threatened with shutdown due to inundation from the Bourne and rising groundwater; boreholes at Kenley required increased abstraction to try to reduce groundwater levels. (p41) [PAG, MUL]

The London Fire Brigade was notified on 6 February 2014 that rising flood water in the Kenley area threatened the integrity of the WTW. (p42) [PAG, MUL]
* Rising floodwater in the Kenley area threatened the integrity of the Kenley Water Treatment Works, prompting high-volume pump deployments, sustained LFB flood management activities and a dedicated police presence; a critical situation at the Kenley Water Treatment Works required temporary transfer of incident management. * Kenley WTW had surcharging issues; measures including sandbags and demountable flood defences were put in place and the works were reported as stable under ongoing monitoring. (p54) [TAB, MUL]

### Croydon
* Significant flooding around the route of the Caterham Bourne (Kenley and Purley). Residential properties were affected at Bourne Park Close, Brighton Road, Dale Road, Foxley Hill Road, Godstone Road, Lansdown Road and Purley Park Road. Bourne Park flooded (temporary flood storage created) and the Bourne View Flood Storage Area was used though some issues were identified. (p6) [MUL]
* Council coordinated a multi‑agency response, mobilised pumps and temporary defences to protect communities; number of flooded properties was reported as low due to early mobilisation; active short‑ and long‑term recovery and mitigation planning (clearance, dredging/de‑silting, inspection, investigating Dale Road, and catchment‑scale alleviation/attenuation measures). (p8)
* Significant flooding across the London Borough of Croydon between December 2013 and January 2014. Flooding resulted from high groundwater (which drove the Caterham Bourne), foul sewer flooding due to groundwater ingress and emergency pumping stressing the sewer system, and surface water flooding from heavy rainfall and saturated soils. Croydon Council declared a Major Incident on 6 February 2014 and implemented a large pumping operation to protect homes, businesses and critical infrastructure. (p9)
* The borough experienced extreme rainfall in December 2013 and January 2014 which led to flooding issues investigated by the Council (Croydon Borough-wide flood investigation covering impacts attributed to surface water/urban runoff and watercourse surcharging). (p19) [TAB, PAG]
* The bourne moves into the more urbanised parts of Croydon; exceptionally high groundwater in December 2013–January 2014 led to high fluvial flows, ingress of groundwater into the foul sewer network, saturated soils and extreme pressure on surface water drainage, contributing to urban flooding in Croydon. (p21) [PAG]
* Widespread high groundwater led to basement and garden flooding across the borough. Roads flooded due to bourne water surcharging from culverts (e.g. Dale Road/Godstone Road) and relief pumping; a row of commercial businesses between Kenley Hotel and Westbourne Florist experienced internal flooding as the road filled with water. Emergency pumping and flood protection measures were mobilised to protect properties. (p26) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* Areas within the London Borough of Croydon along the Caterham Bourne were investigated because they experienced flooding. Gardens in Bourne Park Close were reported flooded with foul sewage (photograph captioned July 2014), prompting remedial action (new fencing) following the event. (p28) [PAG, IMG]
* Experienced widespread flooding in December 2013–January 2014; early mobilisation of pumps and use of temporary attenuation areas prevented significant property flooding; multi‑agency response and a major incident declaration were used to protect communities. (p44)
* Communities in Croydon were threatened by flooding associated with the Caterham Bourne and combined sources of surface water/urban flooding; mitigation options and maintenance programmes are proposed to reduce future threats. (p46)

### River Caterham Bourne
* The Caterham Bourne (a groundwater‑fed intermittent watercourse) emerged from multiple flow paths in 2014, combined at Wapses Lodge Roundabout and produced heavy flows along the A22 through Whyteleafe, Kenley and Purley before discharging to the River Wandle at Waddon, contributing to flooding of properties and Bourne Park (and activating existing flood storage features). (p6)
* {June 1967, August 1967, July 1976, August 1981, February 1988, August 1988, October 2000 to March 2001, July 2007, December 2013 - March 2014}: The Caterham Bourne has repeatedly surcharged (culverts and manholes), causing road and property flooding. High groundwater led to the bourne flooding in winter 2000–2001; water emerged alongside Woldingham Road and flooded the A22 (up to 2 feet deep), combining with foul sewer surcharging and resulting in closure of the A22 for around a month. Historic incidents include culvert surcharging, flooded basements and commercial premises (timber yard), and property flooding at locations such as Bourne Park Close and Saito Close (blocked trash screen). (p19) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* Following exceptional rainfall in December 2013–January 2014 the Caterham Bourne experienced high flows and groundwater resurgence that contributed to flooding within the bourne catchment and prompted the Caterham Bourne Flood Investigation and EA groundwater alerting. (p20)
* Exceptionally high groundwater levels in the underlying chalk caused the bourne to rise during December 2013–January 2014. The bourne's rising produced springs and increased flows, leading to high fluvial flows downstream and contributing to flooding in the Wandle catchment. (p21) [PAG, MUL]
* Open sections of the Caterham Bourne experienced fluvial-type flooding during the event: large flows overwhelmed banks (notably at Bourne View, Harris Academy and Kenley Cricket ground). Culverts lacked capacity, causing manholes to surcharge and water to back up and overflow upstream. Trash screens and other structural assets became blocked by debris and household waste, causing water to back up. Flooding was compounded by high groundwater and surface-water runoff which blocked drainage infrastructure and placed extreme pressure on the surface water drainage network, with emergency pumping exceeding capacity. (p22) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* {December 2013 - April 2014}: The bourne flowed significantly following the winter groundwater rise and continued to flow well after groundwater flooding subsided; sustained flow persisted until late April 2014 and contributed to observable flows along downstream locations (for example alongside Kenley Cricket Club). (p24) [PAG]
* {December 2013 - April 2014, July 2014}: The bourne continued to flow significantly after groundwater levels peaked; sustained flow persisted until late April 2014, with residual flow observed alongside Kenley Cricket Club in July 2014 while upstream sections were dry. Groundwater emergence was reported at other non‑monitored locations across the catchment during winter 2013/14. (p25) [PAG, MUL]
* The Caterham Bourne watercourse was the subject of a Section 19 flood investigation following the December 2013–January 2014 flooding; strategic locations along its corridor were investigated because the watercourse contributed to flooding incidents within the mapped corridor. (p28) [PAG, IMG]
* Groundwater emergence on higher ground flowed toward and into the Caterham Bourne channel. High groundwater entered the sewer system and contributed to sewer surcharging, moving foul water into the bourne and driving flooding downstream that affected Bourne Park, the recreation area, residential gardens and a commercial site. (p29) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* The Bourne experienced very high flows in early 2014, causing the culverted channel to surcharge and flood out of its banks (including at the Bourne View culvert). The trash screen upstream required regular clearing of household waste and debris to keep the Bourne flowing. Channel alignment (two sharp 90° bends) contributed to flows spilling out of bank when flows were high. (p30) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Received pumped water from Dale Road and Kenley WTW into an open section north of the railway; acted as the conveyance/temporary storage route during the response while flows fluctuated from upstream. (p35) [IMG]
* Flooding from the Bourne inundated the Kenley water treatment works and a wide area, threatening assets and prompting coordinated flood defence and removal efforts. (p42) [PAG]
* Rising floodwater in the bourne threatened local infrastructure; high-volume pumps were deployed to remove floodwater from the bourne around the Kenley WTW and discharge it downstream where capacity was greater. (p43)
* The Bourne has risen and flooded intermittently (historical and during the investigated event), contributing to flooding around the watercourse and threatening nearby communities; problems include silted open sections, blocked culverts and trash-screens that exacerbate flood flows. (p46)
* Experienced exceptional flooding during February 2014 and March 2014 that generated large volumes of water and triggered a major emergency response; the Bourne's flooding contributed to internal flooding of a small number of properties' ground floors. (p48) [MUL]
* The Bourne caused flooding in its catchment during the December 2013–January 2014 event, prompting recommendations for a catchment-scale flood alleviation scheme and community engagement to address property flooding and reduce future risk. (p49) [TAB]
* Flooding in the Caterham Bourne catchment during December 2013–January 2014 led to significant flood risk in the catchment and prompted multi‑agency recommendations for future management (including culvert sizing, engagement with Network Rail over assets along the Bourne, and catchment management options). (p50) [TAB]
* {June 1967, August 1967, July 1976, August 1981, February 1988, August 1988, October 2000 to March 2001, July 2007, December 2013 - March 2014}: Quant: 2 feet deep; 1 m deep; closure of the A22 for around a month (p19) [TAB, PAG, MUL]

### Purley Oaks Pond
* The pond rapidly filled as bourne flows increased and was not draining; significant pumping was required to move water into the downstream drainage network. The outlet pump was later found to be non‑functional. (p7)
* The pond, functioning as a relief area for the surface water sewer, filled to dangerously high levels because outflow was restricted by blockages and a malfunctioning pump. The pond required intervention (significant pumping by emergency services) and it overtopped on 14 February 2014, causing shallow highway flooding. (p23) [TAB, MUL]
* {December 2013 - March 2014, July 2014}: Balancing pond did not drain as intended during the flood, requiring emergency pumping of water back into the downstream drainage system. The pond filled to capacity during the February 2014 flood and overtopped onto the highway on 14 February 2014 causing shallow flooding. Temporary defences were erected along the western edge of the pond. The pond outlet pump was later found to be malfunctioning due to a missing power connection. (p36) [IMG, MUL]
* Purley Oaks Pond flooded and was used as a temporary attenuation area to prevent property flooding during the December 2013–January 2014 event. (p44)
* Purley Oaks Pond reached maximum capacity; pumps failed after heavy rain and additional pumps were installed and operated to reduce levels (pumping operations carried out to relieve the pond). (p54) [TAB, MUL]
* Water levels increased slightly; pumping operations at the pond were handed to EA/LBC pumps and LFB high-volume pumps were replaced by EA pumps during the response. (p55) [TAB, MUL]
* Reported elevated water levels which were later described as stable during the post-event monitoring. (p56) [TAB]

### Caterham Bourne Catchment Basin
* The groundwater-fed Caterham Bourne emerged from multiple flow paths (notably in Woldingham east of the railway aqueduct and near Caterham Station), combined at Wapses Lodge Roundabout and produced significant flooding along its route through Kenley and Purley. Groundwater emergence and high bourne flows, with combined contributions from surface water and surcharging sewers, caused residential and commercial flooding along the A22 corridor (including flooding at Bourne Park Close, Brighton Road, Dale Road, Foxley Hill Road, Godstone Road, Lansdown Road and Purley Park Road). Bourne Park experienced flooding that required creation of a temporary flood storage area; the Bourne View Flood Storage Area (constructed after 2001 flooding) functioned as storage but experienced issues with trash screen blockages, capacity and fluctuating water levels. (p6)
