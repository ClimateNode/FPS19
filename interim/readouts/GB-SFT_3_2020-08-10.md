

# Thornton flooding 10 Aug 2020 - 11 Aug 2020

## Place names with coordinates

### Thornton
* The report identifies an intense, short-duration thunderstorm on the night of 10th/11th August 2020 that overloaded drainage across the borough and produced surface-water/urban flooding in Thornton; Westbourne Avenue (in Thornton) was specifically affected and has experienced flooding annually over the last four years. (p3)
* The thunderstorm caused surface water / flash flooding at least at the named Westbourne Avenue properties in Thornton; local drainage runs to a watercourse that traverses Thornton from Runnels Lane to Brook Road. (p4)
* A very intense, short-duration rainfall cell produced over 20 mm of rain in about 25 minutes (radar peaks ~140 mm/hr), and the text states this occurred 'at the time of the flooding' in Thornton—i.e. the intense rainfall caused flooding in Thornton. (p6) [IMG, PAG]
* Short, intense thunderstorm produced rapid onset surface water flooding: rainfall exceeded drainage capacity causing ponding on the highway and flow into properties/driveways that are lower than the road. Manholes were observed to bubble (sewer surcharge) indicating drainage system overload. Overall described as external flooding with minor impacts but potential ongoing wellbeing impacts for residents. (p7) [IMG]

### Westbourne Avenue, Thornton
* Westbourne Avenue experienced flooding during the intense short-duration storm on the night of 10/11 August 2020; the incident triggered a Section 19 investigation. The street has been affected by flooding each year in the last four years; drainage was temporarily overloaded. (p3)
* Properties numbered experienced surface water / flash flooding confined to their front and back gardens and to garages. Several driveways slope down from the road to the garages, providing a flow path for runoff into garage areas. The area drains to a conventional surface water sewer that discharges to a local watercourse. (p4)
* Flooding was confined to the front and back gardens and garages of some properties. The properties are either level with the road or have driveways that slope down from the road to the garages, which facilitated runoff and ingress into garages and gardens. Drainage in the area discharges to a local watercourse running through Thornton. (p5)
* High-intensity rainfall caused ponding on the highway; road-level water drained toward adjacent properties because houses and driveways are lower than the road. Manhole outside No.49 and a manhole in the garden of No.45 were observed to bubble, indicating surcharge of the surface water/drainage system. Blockage was later found in the downstream watercourse section connected to the surface water sewer. (p7)

## Topics without coordinates

### United Utilities
* Surveyed a length of the surface water sewer on Westbourne Avenue down to the culvert after the event; found no obvious defects that would have exacerbated flooding in heavy rain. Involved in post-event asset inspection related to the surface water flooding and sewer surcharge (manhole bubbling). (p7)
* United Utilities were required to check that systems were operating correctly and to clear blockages as part of the post-flood response; they were asked to investigate options to alleviate the flooding alongside Sefton. (p9)

### Sefton Council
* Carried out a CCTV investigation on the watercourse section of the network following the incident and discovered a blockage; officers also conducted follow-up visits and distributed sandbags to affected properties. Their investigations linked a downstream watercourse blockage to exacerbating the surface water flooding. (p7)
* Following the flood event, Sefton checked and cleared the local drainage system and the affected watercourse; the watercourse was confirmed to be flowing freely. Sefton is tasked to investigate options to alleviate the flooding and to clear any blockages and inform riparian owners of their responsibilities. (p9)

### Highways Authority
* The Highways Authority (Sefton) has responsibility for draining the local highway network of surface water where it creates a nuisance. As part of the response to the flood event, maintenance actions (checking/clearing roadside drainage and ditches where required) were identified to ensure highway drainage is fit for purpose. (p9)
