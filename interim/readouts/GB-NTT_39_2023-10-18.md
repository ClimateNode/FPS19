

# Lowdham flooding 18 Oct 2023 - 20 Oct 2023

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Lowdham – Storm Babet, October 2023<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/lowdham-s19-storm-babet-oct-2023.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Lowdham
* During Storm Babet residential properties and businesses experienced internal flooding; additional properties suffered flooding to gardens and outbuildings. Flooding followed a period of saturated ground and intense rainfall. (p2) [PAG]
* Experienced widespread flooding from a combination of Beck overtopping and surface water (overland) flows; properties, roads (including Epperstone By-Pass and Main Street), gardens and outbuildings were affected and the local surface water drainage system was overwhelmed. (p3) [PAG, IMG]
* Lowdham village experienced significant flooding produced by a combination of saturated catchment runoff, overland flows and the Cocker Beck overtopping. Initial flooding in the village (reported around 09:00) affected areas via water flowing through the Magna car park toward cottages on Southwell Road (source uncertain); later, the filled lagoon overtopped (circa 14:00) and contributed further flooding. The combined high river and surface water levels overwhelmed the surface water drainage system and there were subsequent sewage/discharge issues in parts of the village. (p4) [IMG, PAG]
* The Cocker Beck overtopped contributing fluvial (river) flooding to the village. Surface water was evident on Ton Lane West and Ton Lane East caused by foul water rising from the Severn Trent sewer system; Ton Lane East required a road closure. After the beck began to recede, sewage came up through drains in Blenheim. Drainage was slow down Old Tannery Dyke primarily because a double culvert after Harrison's garage is too high and too small. An Environment Agency-led reservoir scheme upstream is planned to store Cocker Beck flows to reduce future fluvial flooding. (p5) [PAG, IMG]
* Community experienced flooding leading to council visits to impacted homes and vulnerable individuals; some residents were evacuated and provided emergency accommodation; communications and multi-agency drop-in events were held to inform residents; council undertook cleanup of roads and paths and removal of bulky waste from flooded homes; recovery actions including grant funding delivery to eligible residents were commenced. (p9) [PAG, MUL]
* Quant: 135 residential properties and businesses (p2) [PAG]
* Quant: 135 residential properties and businesses internally flooded; additional unspecified number of gardens and outbuildings flooded (p3) [PAG, IMG]

### Cocker Beck
* {1999, 2007, 2012, 2013, 2019, 2020}: The Cocker Beck (referred to as a beck) runs through Lowdham and is classed as a main river; it forms part of the village catchment and is presented in the context of the village's significant flood history. (p1) [MUL]
* The Cocker Beck was overwhelmed by runoff, over-topped its banks and spilled out at points along its route; this overtopping contributed to widespread flooding of properties, roads and open spaces in the catchment and caused filling and subsequent overtopping of the cricket/football pitch lagoon. (p3) [PAG]
* The Cocker Beck was overwhelmed by runoff from saturated fields and overtopped at multiple points along its route. Over-topping and spill from the beck, plus large overland flows, filled the cricket/football pitch lagoon which then overtopped in all directions. River and surface water combined to overwhelm the local surface water drainage system and contributed to significant flooding through the village and adjacent areas; roads (including Epperstone By-Pass and Main Street) acted as channels for the flows. (p4) [IMG, PAG]
* Flood Warnings were issued for the Cocker Beck at Lowdham; the Environment Agency carried out patrols and cleared blockages in the area before and after peak flows. (p9)
* Quant: 135 residential properties and businesses internally flooded (village-wide impact to which Beck overtopping contributed) (p3) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 lagoon overtopped; 1 road closure (Ton Lane East); 2 culverts (double culvert) (p4) [IMG, PAG]

### Newark and Sherwood
* {1999, 2007, 2012, 2013, 2019, 2020}: Contains the village of Lowdham which has a significant history of flooding. The report records historic flood events affecting Lowdham including 1999 (when 300 properties were flooded), and further events in 2007, 2012, 2013, 2019 and 2020. The Environment Agency has funded a flood alleviation project for the catchment and flood storage works were constructed on the cricket pitch in 2000 following the 1999 flooding. (p1) [MUL]
* The district experienced flooding impacts requiring an intensive response: council staff visited impacted communities and vulnerable individuals, prepared to support evacuated residents, and operated an extended staffing response during the flood event. (p8)
* {1999, 2007, 2012, 2013, 2019, 2020}: Quant: 300 properties (p1) [MUL]

### Nottinghamshire
* Significant flood impacts across the county including internal flooding of properties (verified on site), deployment of sandbags, county-led visits to affected residents and businesses, collation and publication of verified flood impact data, and the closure of roads to promote safety and avoid additional flood damage. (p8)
* A Flood Alert was issued for River Trent Tributaries in Nottinghamshire covering the area on 19/10/2023, triggering multi-agency responses and local community warnings. (p9)
* Quant: 77 roads (p8)

### Southwell Road, Lowdham
* Cottages on Southwell Road were reached by floodwater that flowed from the Magna car park area; Southwell Road is within the area highlighted as affected by internal flooding. (p3) [PAG, IMG]
* {2021}: A scheme to improve surface water and highway drainage in the Southwell Road area was completed in 2021 to address surface water flooding issues. (p10)

### Ton Lane, Lowdham
* Significant road surface water was evident on Ton Lane West and Ton Lane East prior to the beck overtopping due to foul water rising from the Severn Trent sewer system; the flooding on Ton Lane East required a road closure. (p4)
* Quant: 1 road closure (p4)

### Main Street, Lowdham
* Main Street acted as a channel for overland surface water flows, contributing to the spreading of floodwater through the village and overwhelming local drainage. (p3) [PAG]

### Blenheim Avenue, Lowdham
* After the beck began to recede, sewage/wastewater came up through the drains in Blenheim, indicating sewer surcharging and local flooding of drains. (p4)

### Old Epperstone Road, Lowdham
* Isolated flooding occurred on Old Epperstone Road; Nottinghamshire County Council responded to the isolated flooding and continue to consider options to reduce the risk to the area. (p8)

### Willow Holt, Lowdham
* {2021}: Surface water and highway drainage improvements were completed in 2021; Nottinghamshire County Council is resolving ownership concerns with parts of the piped system in the Willow Holt area and is working with affected residents. (p10)

### Dover Beck
* {1999, 2007, 2012, 2013, 2019, 2020}: Dover Beck is described as a main river running to the north of Lowdham and is included among the watercourses serving the village in the context of its flood history. (p1) [MUL]

### Epperstone by-Pass
* The Epperstone By-Pass acted as a channel for overland flows during the storm, contributing to the movement of large volumes of surface water through the village and to the flooding of properties and other roads. (p3)

### Magna
* Floodwater was observed coming through/around the Magna area (Magna car park) and flowing towards cottages on Southwell Road; a retaining wall from the roundabout to the Magna was reported to be leaking during the event. (p3) [PAG, IMG]

### Magna Charta Pub

### River Trent

### Nottinghamshire County Council

### Magna Car Park

### Severn Trent
* The Severn Trent sewer system is reported to have lacked capacity during the event, with foul water rising from the sewer system onto roads (e.g., Ton Lane) and sewage later coming up through drains (for example in Blenheim), contributing to the observed flooding. (p4)

### Old Tannery Dyke
* Floodwater was slow to drain down Old Tannery Dyke because the double culvert after Harrison's garage was too high and too small, contributing to prolonged local flooding and delayed recession. (p4)

### Harrison's Garage
* The double culvert after Harrison's garage was reported as too high and too small, restricting flow and contributing to slow drainage and local flooding. (p4)
