### Earls Barton
* Named as the subject of a Northamptonshire County Council flood investigation report (The Square, Earls Barton). No specific flood impacts are described in the provided excerpt. (p2)
* Moderate rainfall and ineffective drainage produced excess surface water that flowed to low points on the public highway and around private properties in Earls Barton (The Square), resulting in internal flooding of at least one property and repeated internal flooding incidents noted. (p6) [PAG]
* Surface water from moderate rainfall collected and flowed along natural contours to low points on the public highway at The Square, causing internal flooding of at least one property within Earls Barton. (p7) [PAG]
* The village contained the flood incident at The Square where internal flooding of one property occurred. (p9) [PAG]
* The town centre experienced surface water flooding during the investigated event. Blocked road gullies were observed during the Flood Incident Investigation which contributed to poor highway drainage. Runoff from private car parks and hardstandings on West Street and High Street flowed onto the public highway, increasing surface water conveyance into the centre. (p10)

Surface water flowed from higher ground (private property and highway) following natural contours into low points of the village, causing flooding of low-lying areas and temporary impacts to properties and businesses. ### High Street, Earls Barton
* Surface water sewers from High Street flow towards The Square. Several road gullies on High Street were seen to be blocked with leaves and silt during the Flood Incident Investigation. Limited local drainage on private car parks and hardstandings allowed runoff to reach the public highway, increasing surface water flow toward the town centre. (p10)
* {general}: Surface water runoff from High Street (together with West Way) flowed down carriageways, overtopped kerbs at the corner of Station Road and The Square and contributed to surface water entering a property at The Square on 16/09/15. Recurrent kerb overtopping from High Street was reported approximately six times per year over the preceding five years. (p11) [TAB, PAG]
* Surface water runoff flowed from High Street from north to south across the crossroads junction toward the corner of Station Road and Broad Street, contributing to accumulation at the low point. (p12)
* Blocked road gully adjacent to the kerb (partially blocked by fallen leaves) was documented during the flood incident investigation, reducing drainage capacity on the downhill approach to The Square and linked to surface water flooding impacting the roadway and junction. (p40) [IMG, PAG]
* Blocked road gully reduced drainage capacity, causing surface water to pond on the carriageway and contributing to highway flooding/run-off during the flood event. (p45) [PAG]
* {general}: Quant: approximately six times every year for the last five years (p11) [TAB, PAG]
