

# South Somerset flooding 28 Jun 2021

## Place names with coordinates

### Tatworth
* Multiple flooded properties clustered in the village; flooded roads nearby and associated road damage, causing local transport disruption. (p8) [IMG]
* Property flooding affecting homes and businesses (property damage and loss of belongings; disruption to trading). (p9)
* Several houses in Tatworth (in the area of Fore Street near the Loveridge Lane junction) were flooded. Water ran overland across fields to the west and north, entering properties at the rear. Manhole covers were reported lifted by the force of the water and the road profile was changed by flowing water. Debris/rubbish was observed along watercourse banks and partially cleared by the council land drainage team. (p15)
* Councillors delivered sandbags to the village (sourced from the Town Hall) and helped some (including very elderly) residents upstairs to safety; councillors assisted in clearing flood debris from people's properties. (p30)
* Low-lying areas received large volumes of runoff which collected at low points; the village was also threatened by rising water levels in brooks running through the settlement, causing local inundation and flood risk. (p32) [MUL]

### Chard
* Extensive flooding across the centre of town on 2021-06-28 with multiple flooded properties, road damage and flooded roads causing transport disruption. (p8) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* Multiple parts of Chard were extensively flooded during the storm: the area around Furnham Road Industrial Estate, Beeching Close Industrial Estate and Chard reservoir saw extensive flooding; businesses in Furnham Road Industrial Estate were flooded internally (some units in Beeching Close also affected); local topography and overland flows from nearby residential areas (e.g. Coker Way) fed water into these locations. Additionally, parts of Glynswood (within Chard) experienced culvert blockage/backing up that flooded open space adjacent to the Academy and leisure centre, and a property reported about 2 feet of flooding against a back door and surcharging from a foul sewage manhole. (p12)
* On 28 June 2021 Chard experienced extremely intense rainfall (officers reported visibility down to ~5 yards). The central area was effectively blocked to emergency services, who set up control points on either side of the town to reach those affected. (p20) [IMG]
* Extensive surface water flooding across Chard and surrounding villages during the 28 June 2021 event. Intense local rainfall (95 mm in 2.5 hours recorded in Chard between 17:00–19:30 BST on 2021-06-28) produced flooding that was more extensive and deeper than shown on 1PCSYM (1-in-100 year) flood maps. (p22) [PAG]
* Heavy surface water (pluvial) flooding on 2021-06-28 concentrated in the town centre and low-lying areas due to overland flows from surrounding hills (a 'bowl' effect). Significant flooding of streets and property, flows in transit caused damage to roads and blocked drains. (p23)
* Saturated shallow groundwater and intense rainfall combined with surface runoff to generate significant overland flows that entered the Furnham Road industrial estate in Chard, causing flooding to the industrial area. (p24) [TAB]
* Considerable mud and debris on roads requiring deployment of sweepers and street cleaners; carriageways cleared of debris; arisings from road clearance were deposited temporarily at the Chipping landing on the nearby A30. (p26)
* Chard was heavily affected on 2021-06-28: roads could not be crossed safely, houses required pumping out with high‑capacity pumps, sewers/piped drainage were overwhelmed, emergency services received many calls for help (prioritisation required), and parts of the town were managed from forward command points due to inaccessibility. (p28)
* Flooding occurred in the High Street and Furnham Road; water entered houses on the High Street (residents moved upstairs); the main route through town was closed. (p29)
* Surface water from surrounding farmland and slopes ran into the town, flowing through streets, gaining speed and debris, overwhelming gullies and drains and causing widespread street and property flooding across Chard. (p31)
* Modelling of flood flows corresponds with reported experience of water movement in the town during the event; flood flows moved along mapped directions and caused local inundation consistent with the rainfall event. (p32)
* During the 2021-06-28 event the centre of Chard became impassable — the town centre was not readily crossable, with roads cut by floodwater due to intense rainfall, constrained drainage capacity and local topography. (p34)
* {general}: Residents report that the local area in and around Chard has experienced repeated and ongoing flooding. Concerns are raised about surface water drainage from nearby developments (Blackdown Heights and Holbear) and the potential for increased downstream flooding; the community continues to suffer flood impacts. (p37) [PAG]

The Haymaker pub flooded when surface water came from the opposite field onto the road, down the eastern side into the pub car park and through gardens; surface water also entered the pub via the side entrance — a small drain under the road was functioning but exceeded capacity. (p14)
* {general}: Recorded historic episodes of flooding affecting the village (listed in District Council historic flooding records). (p17) [TAB]

* High volumes of rainwater flowed down sloped roads, inundating roads and collecting in low points; local areas were threatened by rising levels in brooks through the village. (p32)
* A sudden rise in water level occurred in Wadeford during the event (possible containment breach alleged), with multiple local drainage assets (culverts, gullies, drains) reported near Goblin Hollow, between Burnside House and Coach House, and around the Haymaker Pub requiring inspection, clearing or repair. (p35) [PAG]

### Furnham Road, Chard

Culverted watercourses running from and approaching Furnham Road were reported as blocked or damaged, creating bottlenecks that contributed to local flooding. Surface water entering the sewage system and blocked drainage pipes between gullies also contributed to the sewer system becoming filled/overwhelmed. (p19)
* The central area around Furnham Road was impassable to emergency services during the intense rainfall on 28 June 2021; control points were needed either side of Chard to access affected people. (p20)

* Significant overland flows entered the Furnham Road industrial estate as a result of poor local infiltration (mudstone near surface and saturated sands/gravels), causing surface-water/overland flooding of the industrial estate. (p24) [TAB]
* Area around Furnham Road experienced flooding and drainage problems during the event, prompting recommendation to develop appropriate drainage and flood mitigation schemes. (p35)

* A culvert and trash screen in the open area were identified for owner confirmation and inspection after the flood; they require checking for damage or blockage and repair/clearing as necessary. (p35)
