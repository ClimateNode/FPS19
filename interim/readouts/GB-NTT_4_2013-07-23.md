

# Eastwood flooding 23 Jul 2013

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Eastwood 23rd July 2013<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/eastwood-section-19-report.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Nottinghamshire
* Major flooding affected parts of the county on 23 July 2013. The intense short-duration rainfall exceeded drainage capacity and produced widespread surface water / overland flow flooding. The July 2013 event included internal flooding of properties across the county; within Eastwood five properties were recorded as suffering internal flooding caused by overland flow, surcharged highway drains and flooded ordinary watercourses. The event was concentrated around the Beauvale Brook catchment. (p1)
* Quant: 5 recorded properties (within Eastwood) (p1)

### Eastwood
* Internal flooding of residential properties caused by intense rainfall overwhelming drainage; contributing sources were overland flow, surcharged highway drains and flooding of ordinary watercourses (Beauvale Brook catchment). (p1) [PAG]
* Residential areas experienced flooding driven by fluvial runoff from the Beauvale Brook and Brinsley Brook, surface water runoff from surrounding fields and footpaths, and overwhelmed highway drainage gullies. Water flowing off the hills to the north and east followed natural topography and was channelled into roads and residential streets. (p2) [IMG]
* Quant: 5 recorded properties (p1) [PAG]

### Coach Drive, Eastwood
* Particularly affected residential street located near the confluence of the Beauvale and Brinsley Brooks; experienced flooding when water from the brooks, surface runoff from surrounding fields and channelled road/footpath flows entered the residential area. (p2) [IMG]

### Greenhills Road, Eastwood
* Residential area on Greenhills Road was particularly affected by flooding. Floodwaters arrived from fluvial sources (Beauvale Brook and Brinsley Brook), pluvial surface water runoff from surrounding fields, footpaths and roads, and from highway drainage where gullies were at/above capacity. Blocked culverts, trash screens and silt/overgrown vegetation in the watercourses contributed to the impacts. (p2) [IMG]

### Beauvale Brook
* Identified as the primary fluvial source of the July 2013 flooding in the catchment; sections were heavily silted and overgrown, with culverts and trash screens blocked by debris, which impeded flow and contributed to overtopping and flooding into adjacent residential areas. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Subject of ongoing inspection/monitoring by NCC because it could obstruct flow and contribute to flooding; NCC will continue to inspect its condition as part of flood-resilience actions. (p4)

### Brinsley Brook
* Contributed to fluvial flooding in the area (runs parallel to the A608 and is a tributary of the Beauvale Brook). Investigations found heavy siltation, overgrown vegetation and blocked culvert trash screens that impeded flow and contributed to flooding of nearby roads and properties. (p2) [IMG]
* Subject of ongoing inspection/monitoring by NCC because it could obstruct flow and contribute to flooding; NCC will continue to inspect its condition as part of flood-resilience actions. (p4)


## Topics without coordinates

### Highways Authority
* Highway drainage became surcharged during the intense rainfall and the surcharged highway drains were identified as one of three contributing sources that led to internal flooding of properties in Eastwood. (p1)
* Quant: 5 recorded properties (p1)

### A608
* The A608 (which runs alongside the Brinsley Brook) was identified as particularly affected during the event; the proximity of Brinsley Brook, fluvial flow and surface water/runoff contributed to flooding in this corridor and nearby residential streets (Coach Drive, Greenhills Road). (p2) [IMG]
