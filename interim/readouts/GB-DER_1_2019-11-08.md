## Place names with coordinates

### River Derwent
* Heavy, persistent rainfall over the Derwent headwaters on 6–7 Nov 2019 produced high river response in the River Derwent, which contributed to flood events affecting downstream areas including Derby on 8 November 2019. (p16) [PAG]
* Heavy rainfall over the Derwent catchment generated a strong river response that propagated downstream and contributed to the high river flows observed at downstream gauges and into Derby. (p17) [PAG]
* High river flows in the River Derwent and its tributary (Folly Brook) overtopped banks. Fluvial water entered a low-gradient, poorly maintained ditch and then the highway drainage outfall (which lacked a non-return valve), allowing river water to backflow into gullies and emerge at the A61 Pektron Island roundabout causing highway flooding. Flood flows from the overtopping also followed Folly Brook onto Haslams Lane, then onto the Rugby Club grounds and Darley Fields. The Pektron Island area was submerged and the roundabout required temporary closure. (p21) [PAG]
* High river flows caused the River Derwent to overtop its banks; fluvial water entered a nearby low-fall, poorly maintained ditch, forcing ditch levels above the highway drainage outfall and driving floodwater into the highway drainage network. (p22) [PAG]
* Fluvial flooding from the River Derwent is implicated in seepage through flood defences around Darley Abbey Mills and is represented by the recorded flood outline that follows the river, contributing to internal flooding and groundwater emergence at nearby properties. (p26) [PAG, IMG]
* High river levels led to seepage through unsealed joints in partially constructed sheet pile flood defences on the riverbank (behind Old Tomlinson Yard). The seepage required pumping of water from the construction site back to the River Derwent and contributed to surface water reaching City Road when a pump failed. (p28) [PAG, IMG]
* The River Derwent overtopped its banks (south of Causey Bridge) and sent floodwaters into exceedance channels and across adjacent urban areas, contributing to flows that reached Sowter Road and collected in the Sowter Road underpass. (p29) [IMG]
* Rising levels in the River Derwent caused overtopping of riverbanks and seepage through the riverside flood wall. These failure modes produced substantial flooding in the Exeter Place / Meadow Road area (seepage and overtopping), contributed to flooding along Meadow Lane (overtopping near the footbridge), and prompted local mitigation (sandbags, penstock operation elsewhere). (p32) [PAG, IMG]
* The River Derwent overtopped its banks at the footbridge, constricting flows and causing fluvial flooding onto Meadow Lane. Overtopping flooded the road and led to internal flooding of nearby commercial properties and the evacuation of one commercial property. (p34) [PAG, IMG]
* The River Derwent overtopped its banks (starting by the footbridge, which acted as a constriction), causing fluvial flooding of the Meadow Lane industrial area (flooding of the road and internal flooding of commercial properties). High river levels also caused the surface water sewer to back up, which led to manhole/gully flooding and inundation of highway locations away from the river (including Pentagon Island and Eastgate gullies). (p35) [PAG]
* The River Derwent overtopped its banks during the event, flooding the floodplain to the south of the railway tracks. Overtopping and high river levels contributed to flood water flowing into Chaddesden Sidings (via an underpass and via backing up into connected drainage features). (p39) [PAG, IMG]
* The River Derwent overtopped its banks and flooded the floodplain south of the railway, allowing water to flow into Chaddesden Sidings via an underpass (the protective flood bund was absent/eroded). High river levels also caused river flows to back up into a railway culvert and the connected surface water sewer, contributing to flooding north of the railway. Elevated river levels prevented outfalls (including a flap-valved ditch outfall) from discharging: the ditch filled and overtopped, flooding an adjacent commercial car park, and a separate surface water outfall was prevented from discharging causing pluvial flows to back up and flood one commercial property on Raynesway. (p40) [PAG]
* Elevated river levels in the River Derwent during the event prevented connected outfalls (flap valves) and surface water systems from discharging. This led to ditch filling and overtopping into an adjacent car park, backflow along surface water pipes causing internal flooding of a commercial property, and contributed to flows backing up through a railway culvert and surface water sewer into areas north of the railway (contributing to flooding and pump station inundation reported by STW). (p41) [PAG, IMG]

### Derby
* Internal flooding at multiple commercial properties and one residential property near the city border; disruption to critical infrastructure including closure of main roads, failure of a pumping station, and flooding of an electricity sub-station resulting in blackouts. (p7)
* Flooding occurred across the city within twelve investigation sites (many located adjacent to the River Derwent), affecting urban areas and infrastructure and prompting this Section 19 investigation. (p9) [IMG, PAG]
* Flooding affected multiple sites across the city during the investigated event; the majority of the twelve sites considered are located close to the River Derwent and its tributary/ordinary watercourse network. Privately owned ditches connecting to the river were reported as being affected during the flood event. (p10) [PAG, IMG, TAB]
* Flood events occurred in Derby on 8 November 2019 driven by high river flows from the River Derwent following intense rainfall over the river's headwaters. (p16) [PAG, IMG, TAB]
* River Derwent peak flows propagated downstream to Derby. The River Derwent at St Mary's Bridge recorded a record high peak flow during the event, producing very high river levels that were associated with the flood incident in Derby on 8 November 2019. (p17) [PAG, IMG]
* On 8–9 November 2019 fluvial flooding from the River Derwent propagated downstream into Derby. Exceptionally high river levels overtopped and/or caused seepage through some flood defences, produced groundwater flooding in topographically low areas, and led to the sewer network backing up/surcharging from river water ingress to outfalls. These combined mechanisms produced the recorded flooding across the city; DCiC mapped flood outlines and managed response. (p20) [PAG]
* Flooding on the A61 at Pektron Island (within the Derby urban area) caused significant highway inundation and required response from Derby City Council. (p21) [IMG, PAG]
* Parts of Derby (central area) experienced river-driven flooding impacts during the event: seepage through partially constructed/unsealed sheet pile flood defences at the River Derwent bank led to water being pumped from the construction site back to the river; pump failure allowed floodwater to flow onto adjacent streets (notably City Road) where sandbags were deployed to protect properties. (p28) [PAG, IMG]
* Widespread impacts across the city including flooded and closed roads that delayed power restoration and blackouts caused by flood-induced substation failure. (p38)
* Flooding of the surface water drainage system caused backing-up at a Western Power underground sub-station yard gully, resulting in internal flooding of the sub-station, failure of the switchgear and a circuit failure at 21:56 on 8 Nov that produced blackouts across large areas of Derby. Power was restored in stages, with the final stage completed at 23:25 on 8 Nov; restoration was delayed in places due to flooded/closed roads. (p39) [IMG]
* Internal flooding of properties across the city; significant flooding on several main highways causing road closures; disruption to the electricity supply network causing a blackout in Derby. (p43)
* Derby experienced flooding during 8–9 November 2019 from the River Derwent and from surface water/runoff. Multiple locations were affected: Haslams Lane acted as an active floodplain, Sowter Road functioned as an exceedance channel, and City Road flooded due to incomplete flood defences. Surface water network connectivity and outfalls (including into the River Derwent) contributed to local inundation. (p44) [PAG]
* Quant: 12 sites; one residential property (p7)
* Quant: highest peak on record across an 85-year period; estimated 75–95 year return period (ESS analysis); OCOR review estimated 60–95 year return period (report range 50–150 years) (p20) [PAG]
* Quant: water depths of approximately 500mm (p21) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: depths up to 50mm; two pumps used (4“ and 6"), 6” pump failed (p28) [PAG, IMG]

### Severn Trent
* The surface water drainage system (STW) discharged to the River Derwent and, lacking a non-return valve at the outfall, experienced back-up when river levels were high; this back-up transmitted high water levels into the highway drainage system and contributed to manhole/gully flooding away from the river (e.g. Pentagon Island and Eastgate gullies). (p35) [PAG]
* The STW surface water sewer backed up during the event and is described as a contributing source of flooding in Chaddesden Sidings, exacerbating inundation of roads and internal flooding of properties. (p38)
* Severn Trent Water infrastructure contributed to and was affected by flooding: the STW surface water sewer backed up (allowing river water to flow back via a culvert into Chaddesden Sidings), and the Downing Road pumping station became flooded during the event causing pump failure which likely exacerbated local flooding. STW later repaired the pump, carried out cleansing works to reduce foul surcharging, and upgraded/raised the pumping station control panel to improve flood resilience. (p40) [PAG, IMG]
* Quant: flood depths up to 1.5 m (p38)

### Eastgate Underpass
* {1931}: Fluvial flooding from the River Derwent produced a 250–300 m wide stream through the city centre, inundating properties. (p11)
* The underpass was inundated when the connected surface water drainage system backed up due to high river levels; highway drainage gullies at low points flooded and the underpass was flooded by 14:40 on 8 Nov 2019. (p35) [PAG, IMG]
* The underpass flooded (gullies and low points inundated) as a result of the surface water drainage network backing up; reported flooded by 14:40 on 08/11/2019. (p36)
* During the 8–9 Nov 2019 event surface-water and fluvial back-up affected locations including Eastgate Underpass; the report recommends investigating non-return valves, pumping or other measures and consideration of reinstating a flood-defence bund as a temporary measure. (p44) [TAB]

### Car Park
* Standing floodwater covered the commercial property car park on 8 November 2019, extending across the visible paved area up to the buildings. Parked vehicles had wheels partially submerged and emergency vehicles and a trailer were within the inundated area, restricting site access and operations. (p42) [IMG]

### A61
* The highway drainage outfall at the A61 north of the Pektron roundabout was identified as a location of concern during the 8–9 November 2019 flood event; the report recommends investigating the benefit of installing a non‑return valve on the highway drainage outfall and understanding interactions between the watercourse and the drainage network in this area. (p7)
* The A61 at Pektron Island experienced significant highway flooding when fluvial flows entered the highway drainage system and emerged through gullies, leading to closure of the roundabout. (p21)

### Our City Our River
* During the flood on 08 Nov 2019 - 09 Nov 2019, sheet piling flood defences being constructed as part of the OCOR scheme along the riverbank behind Old Tomlinson Yard had been installed but not finished or sealed; this allowed seepage through the sheet piles during the river flood. (p27)
* The exceedance channel constructed as part of the OCOR scheme was used by floodwaters during the event; because of the channel levels south of St Marys Bridge, floodwaters entered the channel from the south and collected in the Sowter Road underpass rather than following the intended bypass route. (p29)
* The flood defence (historic bund forming part of the Derby Flood Protection Scheme at Chaddesden Sidings) was either removed or eroded prior to the November 2019 event, leaving a gap in the defences. This allowed River Derwent to overtop and flow through the underpass into Chaddesden Sidings during 8–9 November 2019, contributing to flooding of the A52 and Chequers Road and internal flooding of nearby commercial properties. (p39) [PAG, IMG]

### Highways Authority

### Folly Brook
* Folly Brook, another ordinary watercourse in Derby, is identified in the report as part of the local drainage network associated with the 08 Nov 2019 - 09 Nov 2019 flooding. It is therefore implicated in the local surface water/river-related impacts during the event and in interactions with privately owned ditches that were affected. The report does not provide site-level details of overtopping or specific property impacts for Folly Brook. (p9)
* Folly Brook overtopped its banks; flood flows followed the brook's line, broadening out onto Haslams Lane and contributing to inundation of the Rugby Club grounds and Darley Fields. (p22)

### Derby City Council
* Derby experienced fluvial flooding on 8–9 November 2019 caused by exceptionally high River Derwent levels. Flood defences were overtopped and/or experienced seepage in locations, groundwater flooded topographically low areas, and river water ingress caused the sewer system to back up/surcharge. Local impacts managed by the council included an internally flooded residential property on Ford Lane (resident had to be rescued via an upstairs window), closure of the train track at that location with trains reversing back to Derby Station, and flooding of caravans in a caravan park adjacent to the railway. The peak river level recorded at Derby St Mary’s gauge was the highest on record (across an 85-year record). (p20)
* Quant: one residential property (p20)

### A52
* Flooding of the A52 slip road at Pentagon Island due to back-up of the surface water/highway drainage system; the A52 slip road (Nottingham-bound) was closed at 23:20 on 8 November and reopened at 04:26 on 9 November after the water cleared. (p35) [PAG, IMG]
* Flooding in the Chaddesden Sidings area resulted in the A52 being inundated and closed from 21:45 on 08 Nov 2019; it was reopened on 09 Nov 2019. (p38)

### Old Tomlinson Yard
* River flooding on 08 Nov 2019 - 09 Nov 2019 affected the riverbank behind Old Tomlinson Yard where incomplete sheet piling defences allowed seepage through unsealed piles; recorded flood outlines for this location are uncertain and did not match the observed flooding. (p27)

### Western Power Distribution
* Surface water drainage backed up causing water to flood out of the yard gully into the underground sub-station, leading to rising internal water levels and failure of switchgear. The circuit failed at 21:56 on 08 Nov 2019, causing blackouts across large areas of Derby; power was restored in stages with final restoration completed at 23:25 on 08 Nov 2019. Restoration was delayed by flooded and closed roads elsewhere in Derby. (p38)

### Downing Road Pumping Station
* Failure of the Downing Road pumping station contributed as one of three sources of flooding into the Chaddesden Sidings area during the event, exacerbating carriageway and property flooding. (p38)

### Railway Culvert
* An existing railway culvert under the railway, connected into the surface water drainage system, allowed river water to flow back into the drainage network and flood north of the railway into Chaddesden Sidings. The culvert's open upstream end enabled backflow from the river; it may previously have been blocked or was unknown prior to this event. (p39) [PAG, IMG]

### Network Rail
* Railway infrastructure (a culvert) was implicated in the flooding event when surface/fluvial waters affected its function, contributing to inundation risk at the adjacent underpass; Network Rail was recommended to investigate the culvert and consider reinstating a flood-defence bund as a temporary measure. (p44) [TAB]
