

# Stotfold flooding 04 Jul 2015

## Place names with coordinates

### Pix Brook
* Debris was blocking the brook causing water to enter residential properties; flow was restored after debris was cleared. The blockage and associated flooding prompted deployment of responders and mitigation measures. (p5)
* During the intense rainfall event on 4 July 2015 a significant quantity of debris (vegetation, timber and watercress) was mobilised and conveyed along Pix Brook to downstream locations, contributing to blockage risk at the twin culvert and requiring debris management measures. (p6) [PAG]
* {04 Jul 2015, 24 Jul 2015, 13 Aug 2015}: Debris blockage of the Pix Brook twin-culvert caused surface water to accumulate upstream and contributed to internal and external flooding in the affected area. Residents reported being affected internally or externally on 4th July 2015; debris blocking the culvert on 24th July 2015 caused surface water to accumulate on Brook Street/Coppice Mead. IDB undertook maintenance and a 2015 project to reduce culvert blocking and enable safe debris removal. (p7) [MUL]
* On 4th July 2015 heavy rainfall produced high flows in the Pix Brook; debris (predominantly watercress) accumulated at the trash screen at the Brook Street road junction, overwhelming the screen and blocking the twin-culvert highway culvert. This caused upstream water levels to rise rapidly, overtopping the brook banks and flooding the local area, resulting in internal and external flooding, risk to life and obstruction of access. (p8)
* Trash screens at Brook Street, Valerian Way and other structures became blocked and were cleared following the flood; debris removal was necessary to restore flow. (p9) [PAG]
* Flooding of Pix Brook on 4 July 2015 led to blockages at trash screens and other structures (notably at Brook Street and Valerian Way), contributing to local flooding that prompted emergency attendance by the Internal Drainage Board to clear/remove screens and a Section 19 investigation coordinated by Central Bedfordshire Council. (p10)
* Pix Brook is described as having a significant debris load (sources include a nearby conservation woodland, watercress growth and garden/back-channel debris adjacent to Brook Street) that can block culverts and reduce channel capacity during flood flows. Specific issues cited include potential debris blocking at the A507 culvert, sediment accumulation at the Brook Street highway bridge reducing discharge capacity, and interaction with the Pix Brook Reservoir flood gates — all contributing to flooding risk in the locality. (p12) [TAB, PAG]
* The Pix Brook overtopped/caused local flooding in Stotfold on 4 July 2015. Debris from adjacent areas (notably watercress and tree branches) was identified as blocking the channel and contributing to flow obstructions along the banks, increasing flood risk to nearby properties and land. (p13)
* Identified as a prime source of debris (including watercress and woody branches) that was transported and accumulated in the channel and at outfalls, increasing debris loading and contributing to blockages during the 4 July 2015 flood; prompted recommendations for trash screens, maintenance, baffle hedges and other controls to reduce debris entering the channel. (p14) [PAG]
* High flows in the Pix Brook caused the brook to burst its banks, inundating adjacent woodland and mobilising woody debris which was carried downstream (identified as the source of debris found at Brook Street). (p15)
* Vegetation (identified as watercress) from upstream woodland was dislodged and flowed downstream, with similar weed observed having been pulled out at Brook Street — indicating vegetation/debris transport by the flood flows and potential for local channel/blockage issues. (p17) [PAG]
* Flooding occurred along the Pix Brook corridor on 4 July 2015. The post‑event annotated map identifies culverts (Hitchin Road, Brook Street), IDB trash screens (Brook Street, Valerian Way), a standalone reservoir and control gates, and the proximity of Letchworth sewage works as relevant to the event and response. The report notes actions to increase retention in the lower floodplain and the need to intercept debris before the two channels meet. (p18) [IMG]
* {04 Jul 2015, 16 Jul 2015, 17 Jul 2015}: The brook became blocked by debris and by a recently installed trash screen/bridge, forming a dam that prevented flow and caused the brook to overflow into gardens, garages, vehicles and streets. (p19) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* {04 Jul 2015, 16 Jul 2015}: The brook course was blocked by debris and a recently installed trash gate/trash screen which collected debris and was not cleared, causing the brook to be unable to convey storm flows. These blockages led to overflow and flooding of adjacent gardens and streets on 04/07/2015 and during heavy rainfall in mid-July 2015. (p20) [PAG, MUL]
* Quant: 12 properties; two properties (p5)

### Brook Street, Stotfold
* Debris blocking Pix Brook at Brook Street caused water to enter residential properties; Fire & Rescue were on site and deployed aqua sacs to limit flood water entering properties; IDB operatives and Highways/grab lorry cleared debris and the brook was reported to be flowing again; Brook Street trash screen was cleared/removed by IDB. (p5)
* {04 Jul 2015, 24 Jul 2015, 13 Aug 2015}: Surface water accumulated on Brook Street (and Coppice Mead) when debris blocked the twin-culvert downstream, causing obstruction of access (estimated 24 hours) and contributing to internal/external flooding reported by residents on 4th July 2015. (p7) [MUL]
* On 4th July 2015 the trash screen at the road junction on Brook Street became blocked by debris during high flows, preventing flow into the highway culvert and causing overtopping and flooding of the highway/local area and obstruction of access. (p8)
* Trash screens at Brook Street became obstructed during the flood; the Internal Drainage Board attended on 4th July to assist in clearing the trash screen(s). (p10)
* Accumulation of sediment and debris in the Brook Street highway bridge and the adjacent back channel reduced the discharge capacity of the highway asset and was identified as a debris source that could contribute to flooding of the gardens adjacent to the back channel. (p12) [TAB]
* Debris accumulated in the back channel and at Brook Street (the report states the majority of the debris at Brook Street came from nearby woodland), reducing channel capacity and contributing to flooding and debris entry into gardens during the 4 July 2015 event; recommended inspection, removal of debris sources and surveying of the highway bridge for sediment. (p14) [PAG]
* Deposition/accumulation of debris at Brook Street caused by flood flows in the Pix Brook; the report states the majority of debris at Brook Street originated from the nearby woodland washed into the brook. (p15)
* High flows dislodged vegetation (identified as watercress) which was pulled out at Brook Street and transported downstream, indicating strong flood flows and debris movement. (p16)
* Brook Street is shown on the 4 July 2015 annotated map as the location of a highway culvert and an IDB Brook Street trash screen; these features are identified as relevant to flood flow and debris interception during the event. (p18) [IMG]
* {04 Jul 2015, 16 Jul 2015, 17 Jul 2015}: Overflow from the brook (exacerbated by a blocked trash/gate at the Brook Street bridge) caused flooding down Brook Street and into adjacent gardens and properties. Reports note water overflowing into Coppice Mead and previous events where water rose high enough to threaten properties; the fire brigade was called to help divert road water on an earlier occasion. (p19) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* {04 Jul 2015, 16 Jul 2015}: A blocked trash screen/trash strainer at the Brook Street bridge on 04/07/2015 caused the Pixy to overflow directly into back gardens and down Brook Street into Coppice Mead. Heavy rainfall on 16/17 July 2015 also caused overflow into back gardens. The floodplain was ineffective and the brook course was blocked so no relief flow was available. (p20) [MUL]
* Quant: 12 properties; 2 properties (electricity isolated) (p5)

### Stotfold
* The town was affected by flooding associated with the Pix Brook system during the event, with debris loading and potential culvert blockages reducing channel/discharge capacity and contributing to urban flood flows through Stotfold. Recommendations target community resilience and local asset interventions to reduce future flood impacts. (p12) [TAB]
* The town was the focus of the investigation into the 4 July 2015 flooding; recommendations address debris management, asset performance, community resilience and measures to reduce flood impacts in Stotfold arising from Pix Brook blockages. (p14)
* Stotfold is included in the annotated map of the 4 July 2015 Pix Brook flood corridor. The map highlights the Stotfold stretch of Pix Brook and associated infrastructure (culverts, trash screens) and notes South Stotfold as a new development within the mapped flood extent. (p18) [IMG]

### Coppice Mead, Stotfold
* {04 Jul 2015, 24 Jul 2015}: Flood water accumulated on Brook Street/Coppice Mead when debris reportedly blocked the twin-culvert downstream; residents reported internal and external flooding at the flood location on 4th July 2015. Access was obstructed for an estimated 24 hours. (p7) [MUL]
* Raised water levels at the point where the Pix's two channels re-converge backed up along the old channel and into properties on Coppice Mead; properties are at a considerably lower level than surrounding land so experienced inundation from the backed‑up channel. (p17)

### River Ivel

### Brook Street Bridge

## Topics without coordinates

### The Bedfordshire and River Ivel Internal Drainage Board
* IDB operatives attended the Pix Brook event and cleared debris from trash screens (Brook Street and Valerian Way). They removed the Brook Street trash screen under instruction to restore flow after blockages in the brook that were causing flooding. (p5)
* Debris was conveyed to the IDB gantry and debris prevention grid (trash screen) during the 4 July 2015 event, which the screen was intended to intercept to prevent blocking of the twin culvert and to allow safer maintenance/clearing. (p6)
* {04 Jul 2015, 24 Jul 2015}: The IDB coordinated a 2015 project to reduce the risk of the Brook Street twin-culvert blocking and to provide a safe environment for debris removal (design retained discharge capacity). They carry out annual watercourse maintenance and monthly checks of known flood hot-spots (including the Brook Street twin-culverts), reported constraints to hand work in some reaches due to access, and produced a technical report into the flood event. (p7) [MUL]
* Attended the incident on 4th July to assist clearing trash screens at Brook Street, Valerian Way and other structures; removed a trash screen following police instruction; produced a post-event report assessing causes, scale and contributing factors and outlining remediation options. (p10)
* The IDB is identified as the responsible body for the Pix Brook watercourse and for managing debris and vegetation that contribute to blockages during flood flows. Actions described include liaising with landowners to reduce debris sources (conservation woodland and watercress), reviewing inspection and maintenance regimes, considering trash booms and trash screens (per EA guidance), removing unconsented planting, surveying Brook Street bridge for sediment, and reviewing monitoring of flood gates at Pix Brook Reservoir to manage flood risk. (p12) [TAB]
* The Internal Drainage Board is the operating authority for the Pix Brook (an IDB watercourse). Residents were advised to report flood incidents to the IDB; the IDB may remove or require removal of unconsented obstructions and reclaim costs. The report recommends the IDB liaise with landowners (conservation woodland, AWS sewerage works, adjacent farmland) to reduce debris that caused channel blockages. (p13) [PAG]
* Quant: 12 properties; 2 properties had electricity isolated (p5)

### Central Bedfordshire Council
* High flows in the Pix Brook on 4th July 2015 caused debris (watercress) to accumulate on the trash screen at the Brook Street road junction, blocking the highway culvert. The blocked screen caused upstream water levels to rise rapidly, overtopping the brook banks and producing internal flooding of properties and external flooding of gardens/grounds in Stotfold, with access obstructed for an estimated 24 hours and a reported risk to life. (p8)
* As Lead Local Flood Authority, coordinated a Section 19 flood investigation following the 4th July flood, undertook community engagement and post‑flood site appraisal, and made recommendations for future flood risk management. (p10)
* On 4th July 2015 accumulated sediment and debris reduced the discharge capacity at the Brook Street highway bridge (a CBC asset), compromising its performance during the flood and increasing local flood risk; culvert bridges were noted as vulnerable to debris blockage during the event. (p12) [TAB]
* Central Bedfordshire Council (CBC) is identified as a body to be contacted to record details of flood incidents and to provide advice to the Town Council and residents following the Pix Brook flooding. (p13) [PAG]
* Accumulated sediment and debris at the Brook Street highway bridge reduced the discharge capacity of this CBC asset during the 4 July 2015 flood, contributing to local flooding; the report also links refurbishment/operation of Pix Brook Reservoir flood gates to flood management and possible blockages. (p14)
* {04 Jul 2015, 16 Jul 2015}: Reports identify a council-installed/trash gate/trash screen in the brook that collected debris and was not cleared, which contributed to blockage of the channel and consequent flooding of nearby properties on 04/07/2015 (and related blockages noted on 16/07/2015). (p19) [TAB, MUL]

### Bedfordshire Fire and Rescue Service
* Attended the site, reported debris blocking Pix Brook which was allowing water to enter residential properties, and deployed aqua sacs to limit flood water entering properties. (p5)
* Quant: 12 properties (p5)

### Anglian Water
* Two surface-water outfalls that are under adoption with Anglian Water Services discharge directly into Pix Brook upstream of the flood location and are cited as possibly exacerbating flooding; nutrients from the sewage treatment works' base flow promoted prolific watercress growth that increased mobilised vegetation during the flood, worsening blockages in the brook. (p6)

### Environment Agency
* Provided assistance to residents during the emergency via the Floodline service on the day of the incident and advised on jurisdiction of structures (that they were under CBC/IDB rather than EA). (p10)

### Pix Brook Reservoir
* Flood gates at the Pix Brook Reservoir were identified for refurbishment and for the introduction of monitoring (e.g. remote telemetry) to provide early warning of blockages that could exacerbate flooding; the reservoir gates were therefore implicated in the flood response and risk management following the event. (p12) [TAB]

### CBC Highways
* High debris loads and accumulated sediment during the 4 July 2015 flood compromised the performance and discharge capacity of culvert/bridge assets (including the A507 road bridge and associated culverts), reducing conveyance and worsening flood impacts on the highway network. (p14)
