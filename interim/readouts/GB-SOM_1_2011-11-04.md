

# Bruton flooding 04 Nov 2011

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Cuckoo Hill, north Bruton, Somerset<br>113\_Somerset County/Flood Investigation report Bruton.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Cuckoo Hill, Bruton
* Described as the origin of the flood event that affected properties in Bruton; the event originated at Cuckoo Hill and led to flooding impacts on local properties. (p5)
* A significant volume of surface water travelled down Cuckoo Hill during the event and entered The Pastures development via the new junction. The flow overwhelmed road drainage, ran over the development amenity space and into properties in Eastfield, contributing to flooding of residential properties. (p7)
* Highways surface-water run-off from Cuckoo Hill contributed additional significant volume to the flood flows reaching the A359, increasing local roadway/urban flooding. (p8)
* Area experienced flooding caused by overland surface water runoff associated with the unusual rainfall on 2011-11-04 and land management practices (maize stubble). (p13)
* Agricultural surface runoff from adjacent fields overtopped the field ditch and outfall where it meets road drains at Cuckoo Hill; the ditch had insufficient capacity so overtopping contributed to flooding reaching the road drainage system and contributed to flooding of the garden of an adjoining property. (p17) [TAB]
* A reception ditch and diversion culvert at Cuckoo Hill were unable to cope with agricultural field runoff. The diversion pipe entrance is only ~10 in (25 cm) diameter, providing limited capacity to discharge the volumes that collected in the reception ditch. The immediate sub-catchment draining to the ditch was estimated at 8 ha (within a 32 ha forage-maize field). For a 12-hour 30 mm event the intercepted rainfall was estimated at 2,400 m³, with ~70PCSYM absorption leaving an estimated 1,680 m³ of captured field runoff; if 50PCSYM of that required drainage the discharge pipe would need an average capacity of ~70 m³/hour. The report therefore identifies the culvert/pipe and reception ditch at Cuckoo Hill as being overwhelmed by surface-water/field-runoff during the event and requiring greater discharge capacity or diversion works. (p22)

### Bruton
* Affected by the flood event originating at Cuckoo Hill; the event affected a significant number of properties in Bruton and generated substantial correspondence and local concern. (p5)
* Surface water travelling down Cuckoo Hill entered The Pastures development and flowed into Eastfield, flooding properties in Bruton and causing evacuation of vulnerable residents; between 10 and 15 homes across the affected area were flooded. (p7) [PAG]
* Intense short-duration downpours and saturated ground produced surface-water flooding in Bruton. Surface water and an overwhelmed field ditch ran overland onto the A359; between 10 and 15 homes in Bruton were flooded to varying degrees. Sheltered housing in Eastfield (Yarlington Homes) required evacuation of vulnerable elderly residents. (p8) [PAG]
* Intense short-duration rainfall and saturated ground produced large surface water run-off from agricultural land (maize stubble) which overwhelmed a field ditch and then the highway drainage on the A359. Turbid runoff covered the carriageway of the A359 at Bruton, impeding traffic and requiring an emergency/response presence; highway drainage was completely overwhelmed by flows originating off-field and from Cuckoo Hill. (p9) [PAG, IMG]
* On 2011-11-04 North Bruton experienced surface water flooding driven by an unusual rainfall pattern and runoff from farmland (notably maize stubble) north of the A359. Overland flows and overwhelmed highway/local drainage led to floodwater entering residential areas (including Cuckoo Hill, The Pastures and Eastfield) and caused damage to property; local drainage defects (blocked watercourse opposite the cemetery and a low headwall) exacerbated the flooding. (p13)

### Eastfield, Bruton
* Properties in Eastfield were flooded when surface water from Cuckoo Hill passed through The Pastures; sheltered housing (operated by Yarlington Homes) required evacuation of a number of vulnerable elderly residents. Between 10 and 15 homes across The Pastures and Eastfield were affected. (p7)
* Surface water travelled overland from The Pastures across open space and down roads, continuing through Eastfield and flooding residential properties; pathway surfaces were damaged with gravel deposits from the flood. (p10)
* Surface water flowed through Eastfield (from The Pastures open space and via Brue Avenue), entering the residential area and flooding houses. (p11) [PAG, IMG]
* Private surface water runoff reached the highway drainage in Eastfield in volumes significantly in excess of the system's design capacity; highway drainage was overwhelmed and ditch clearance and drainage investigations were planned. (p12)
* Experienced surface water / overland flow flooding during the event on 2011-11-04 resulting from an unusual rainfall pattern combined with land management factors; identified for further drainage and mitigation actions. (p13)
* A localised flooding event occurred at Eastfield (near Bruton) that was investigated as being influenced by surface runoff from adjacent agricultural land (Whaddon Farm fields). (p18)

### Brue Avenue, Bruton
* Surface water continued through Eastfield and flooded a number of houses on Brue Avenue. (p10)

### Whaddon Hill, Bruton
* Fields to the east of Whaddon Hill drained in a south-westerly direction into a headland ditch area between fields 1 and 2. Post-harvest maize stubbles and silty loam soils produced waterlogged headland areas with standing water and poor drainage, producing surface runoff that contributed to a localised flooding event at Eastfield (near Bruton). (p18)

### The Pastures
* Floodwater flowing down the A359 reached the junction of the Bellways Development; because of the carriageway profile the water left the road and entered the new access to the development, conveying significant volumes of surface water into the site after highway drainage was overwhelmed. (p9) [PAG]
* Surface water from the A359 overwhelmed highway drainage and flowed down the carriageway into the new access to The Pastures. Estate road drainage captured water until the attenuation tank filled and then overflowed, adding to overland flow across the development's open space and directing water toward properties in Eastfield. Deposits of gravel from damaged pathways corroborate local accounts of this overland flow. (p10)
* The Pastures housing development's adopted surface water drainage system and attenuation tanks were overwhelmed by runoff originating outside the site during the 2011-11-04 event, resulting in flooding impacts to the development and its properties. (p12)


## Topics without coordinates

### Bellway Homes
* The Bellways development 'The Pastures' was entered by surface water via the new junction on 2011-11-04; a number of properties within the development were flooded, the road drainage was overwhelmed and water flowed over amenity space into Eastfield. Between 10 and 15 homes across The Pastures and Eastfield were affected. (p7)
* The Bellway Homes development at The Pastures had its surface water drainage and attenuation tanks overwhelmed by runoff from outside the site during the 2011-11-04 intense rainfall; Bellway was identified to be involved in discussions to identify and implement further flood protection measures for the development and properties. (p12)

### Yarlington Homes
* Properties (sheltered housing) in Eastfield operated by Yarlington Homes were flooded during the event; a number of vulnerable elderly residents were evacuated as a result. (p7)

### South Somerset Council
* Following flooding on 2011-11-04 the District Council (as Local Drainage Authority) arranged clearance works at the headwall of the watercourse draining farmland adjacent to the A359 to identify further improvement works and commit resources to localised maintenance. (p12)

### Highway Authority
* The highway drainage system on the A359 and in Eastfield received private surface water volumes far beyond design capacity; the Highway Authority committed to dig out ditches on the A359, investigate the highway drainage location and condition, and carry out required repairs. (p12)

### Wessex Water
* Wessex Water's adopted surface water drainage system and attenuation tanks within The Pastures (Bellway development) were overwhelmed by water originating from outside the site; the system had been designed to convey only water generated within the development and was exceeded. (p12)

### Whaddon Farm
* Surface run-off and standing water in the headland between fields 1 and 2 on the farm contributed to a localised flooding event at Eastfield near Bruton. After late-October maize harvest the stubbles were left undisturbed; fields lying east of Whaddon Hill drain south‑westerly toward an open headland ditch and, where a boundary ditch is absent, water accumulated in a muddy, waterlogged headland strip. Silty loam soil in field 2 and evidence of poor drainage/surface capping reduced infiltration and increased overland flow, which was identified as a contributing source of runoff to the localised flood on 2011-11-04. (p18) [IMG]
