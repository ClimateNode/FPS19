### Hillingdon Council
* Torrential rain/standing water caused widespread surface water flooding across the borough on 28 July: many roads temporarily flooded (some impassable, including the A40); London Underground stations and lines in the Hillingdon area were closed; approximately 29 properties flooded internally with some residents unable to return and placed in temporary accommodation; vehicles became stranded (six cars reported stuck in floodwater in Uxbridge) and people were rescued from floodwater. (p3)
* Surface water built up at a low point on the A40 adjacent to Hillingdon Underground Station after intense short-duration rainfall overwhelmed drainage. The A40 was closed, causing diversions and long tailbacks across Hillingdon; visible pooling beneath a bridge/overpass was photographed. (p7) [TAB]
* Carried out highway drainage actions related to the event: responsible for gully cleaning and jetting, removed debris where gullies were blocked, checked Thames Water surface water outfalls and reported impeded flows to Thames Water; provided advice to residents and reviewed gully prioritisation. (p8) [TAB]
* During the flood event gullies on adopted highways were blocked by leaves and debris washed in by heavy rain; some gullies could not be accessed for cleaning because parked cars covered them. Council officers found Thames Water surface-water outfalls impeded by silt and vegetation and reported these to Thames Water. The Council carried out gully cleaning (annual schedule) and introduced a revised gully prioritisation system to target clearing at high-priority sites. (p9)
* Council Highways attended on 28 July, used a tanker to remove floodwater until approximately 16:00, identified the locations of the blocked surface water sewer and reported the problem to Thames Water; gullies had been checked previously and found clear. (p10) [TAB]
* The council inspected the gullies for which it is responsible following the incident and confirmed they were running clear. (p11) [TAB]
* Residents attempted to contact the Council to report the roadway flooding; Council recorded anecdotal reports and has actions to investigate the maintenance regime of the landowner of the ordinary watercourse in response to the flood. (p12) [TAB]
* Surface water flooded the highway at Glebe Avenue; flood water flowed across the road and entered residential properties. The Council undertook response and management actions including annual gully clearance, prioritising clearance works on the Ickenham Stream and raising issues with Thames Water and the Environment Agency. (p13) [TAB]
* {November - December 2014}: Carried out and scheduled maintenance to reduce flood risk: gullies are cleared annually and contractors were prioritised to clear the Ickenham Stream to ensure it is flowing properly (work scheduled for November/December 2014). (p14)
* Hillingdon Council monitored the flooding incident and provided guidance to residents (recommended drawing up Flood Plans and products to protect homes from airbrick ingress). (p16) [TAB]
* Hillingdon Council recorded the surface water flooding event affecting Regents Avenue and Windsor Avenue and referred the matter to Thames Water as part of their response. (p17) [TAB]
* The council responded to the surface water flooding on Cowley Road; actions recorded include checking gullies to ensure they were clear. Flooding led to Cowley Road being closed to buses with tailbacks and diversions. (p18) [TAB]
* {general}: Council actions recorded in response to the flooding at Wood End Green Road: yearly gully clearance (gullies last checked 8 Oct 2014), continued liaison with Thames Water, and provision of resident guidance on disposal of fats/grease and flood-proofing measures. (p19) [TAB]
* Council response actions following the highway flooding included investigating drainage, noting soakaways were full, and creating a new soakaway to reduce future extent of flooding; council maintenance (gully clearance) and responsibilities are described. (p20) [IMG, TAB]

* Quant: 4 households (p13) [TAB]

### Thames Water
* Surface water sewer was overwhelmed/blocked during the heavy rainfall causing flooding across access roads and into properties on Bedford Road, Clyfford Road and West End Road. Thames Water attended after the event, were unable to complete a CCTV survey because the line was blocked, commissioned a line clean and cut back tree roots, and committed to monitoring and future response. (p8) [TAB]
* The surface-water sewer serving the gullies was found to be blocked (tree-root ingress) which prevented completion of a CCTV survey. Thames Water commissioned a line clean, cut back tree roots, and said it will monitor and respond to future reports. The sewer blockage impeded flows from gullies and was addressed as part of the post-flood investigation. (p9)
* Attended the site after Council; identified a blockage in the surface water sewer caused by tree roots that led to the road and footpath flooding. The blockage required pipe excavation and replacement. (p10) [TAB]
* The Thames Water drainage network discharges into the Ickenham stream at the end of Glebe Avenue; residents were advised to contact Thames Water to check whether network capacity was an issue for the flooding event. (p11) [TAB]
* Thames Water attended the site in response to the flooding; work included partial clearance of the surface water sewer main and actions to address jammed manhole covers. The Thames Water network was implicated because surface water and sewer issues contributed to flooding and drainage discharge to the Ickenham Stream. (p13) [TAB]

### West London
* Torrential rain on 28 July produced rapid surface-water/flash flooding across West London (notably Uxbridge and Hillingdon). Large areas of standing water temporarily flooded many roads (some impassable, including the A40), London Underground stations and lines in the Hillingdon area were closed, vehicles became stranded (six cars reported stuck in Uxbridge) and properties were flooded internally (approximately 29 properties in Hillingdon). Some residents remain unable to return home and are in temporary accommodation; a number of people were rescued after being stranded in floodwater. (p3)
* Road flooding on High Road/Austins Lane in Ickenham with vehicles driving through deep water and splashing; nearby houses on Church Place and Tweedale Grove were nearly flooded. (p12) [IMG]
