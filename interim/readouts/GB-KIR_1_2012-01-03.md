* Internal flooding to the property's basement kitchen. Floodwater entered the property from a disused coal store adjacent to the basement steps through a point source in the coal store wall approximately 2 m below ground level. Investigation concluded no known drainage system contributed and the water is presumed to have come from raised groundwater. (p3)

### Kirklees Council

* Kirklees Council's Private Drainage Team attended and investigated flood observations (dye-tested drainage serving the block). They observed water entering the foot of the external stairwell during the inspection; recorded details of nearby incidents are noted on council records. (p10) [PAG]

### Yorkshire Water
* {03 Jan 2012 - 05 Jan 2012, 06 Jan 2012, 11 Jan 2012, 23 Jan 2012}: Attended site to investigate water entering a cellar/kitchen (odourless, colourless). Carried out dye tests and CCTV surveys: found no defect in the public sewer immediately outside the property (surveyed 28 metres downstream), identified a partial restriction 60 metres downstream (assessed as not connected to the flooding at the property), and found debris in the surface water brick culvert in Carlinghow Lane. Concluded the most likely cause of the flooding was groundwater rather than a public sewer failure. (p11) [PAG, MUL]

### Culvert
* {09 Jan 2012, 23 Jan 2012}: A surface water culvert in the footway adjacent to Box's buildings was dye-tested but dye was not observed downstream, indicating a possible obstruction. A later CCTV inspection found debris in the surface water brick culvert in Carlinghow Lane; this slight restriction was assessed as not having caused the flooding at the nearby property. (p11) [MUL]
