### River Thames
* {March 1947, early 2003, 01 Jan 2014 - 28 Feb 2014}: The River Thames produced extremely high water levels during major fluvial events cited in the report. The March 1947 flood produced very high Thames levels at Caversham and contributed to extensive flood damage; the early 2003 and 2013/14 events also produced high river levels linked to properties being internally flooded in Reading. (p7) [MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024}: River flows rose rapidly due to high surface runoff and saturated catchment conditions associated with Storm Henk (4–5 January 2024), leading to flow volumes and velocities that exceeded channel capacity. This created conditions that can cause overbank flooding of urban areas adjacent to the Thames and contributed to the risk of property flooding in areas closest to the main watercourse. (p8) [PAG]
* {01 Jan 2024 - 31 Jan 2024}: High antecedent rainfall and surface water runoff increased flows in the Thames and associated watercourses, contributing to elevated river flows and downstream passage of water during the January 2024 flood event. (p9)
* River levels in Reading exceeded the property‑flooding threshold and reached their highest measured levels since 1947, causing flooding of low‑lying land and creating conditions for property flooding in the borough. (p11) [IMG]
* Elevated Thames river levels threatened direct flooding of the area; a raised/bunded area south of Christchurch Ditch prevented direct Thames flooding but also impeded drainage back to the Thames as levels fell during the January 2024 event. (p13)
* High River Thames levels prompted a Flood Alert (31 Dec 2023) for the reach between Mapledurham and Sonning and a Flood Warning (5 Jan 2024) for properties from Scours Lane to Caversham Lakes. River flooding contributed to overtopping and impeded drainage in Lower Caversham, resulting in exterior and basement flooding at the eastern end of Queens Road from the evening of 5 Jan and internal ground-floor flooding of properties on 7 Jan. Basement water levels had fully receded by 15 Jan (basement inundation lasted 10 days). The raised/bunded ground south of Christchurch Ditch protected from direct Thames inundation but slowed drainage back to the Thames as levels receded. (p14) [PAG]
* High River Thames flows and out-of-bank conditions contributed to fluvial water entering local drainage (Christchurch Ditch) and driving floodwater into Lower Caversham. This fluvial influence formed part of the cause of internal and external flooding in Queens Road and neighbouring streets. (p15) [IMG]
* Flooding of land adjacent to the River Thames made the Thames Path, public green spaces and play areas temporarily unavailable. (p17)
* River Thames flooding during the January 2024 event caused internal flooding to residential properties in Reading borough and was the primary source of the flood event affecting the area. (p20) [PAG]
* High river levels on the River Thames exceeded channel and floodplain capacity and were a primary cause of the January 2024 flooding that led to internal flooding of properties in Reading borough. (p21) [PAG]
* River levels rose to record/near-record heights, flooding the Thames Path, Christchurch Meadow and the metal path under Reading Bridge; high river level contributed to Christchurch Ditch filling and downstream overflow into gardens and paths. (p23) [TAB]
* Elevated river levels (exceeding previous record) were reported and the Thames received overflow from local channels/ditch, contributing to backwater and riverside flooding impacting adjacent paths and properties. (p24)
* Quant: 7.26m AD (threshold exceeded on 05 Jan 2024); 7.54m AD peak on 07 Jan 2024; property‑flooding threshold 7.25m AD (p11) [IMG]
* Quant: 10 days (p14) [PAG]
* Quant: 16 residential properties reported internal flooding; 3 commercial properties reported internal flooding; an additional 33 residential properties reported garden/outbuilding flooding; 1 additional commercial property reported external flooding; 2 properties reported loss of electricity; flood duration (for basements) 10 days (p15) [IMG]

### Queens Road, Caversham
* Exterior and basement flooding began late evening 5 January; internal ground-floor flooding of several properties occurred on 7 January. Floodwaters flowed via residential gardens, public footpaths and roads into Queens Road and then extended to adjacent streets. Basement water levels had fully receded by 15 January, giving a total flood duration of 10 days. (p14)
* Properties at the eastern end experienced exterior and basement flooding beginning late evening 5 Jan, with internal ground-floor flooding of multiple properties on 7 Jan. Floodwater reached fronts of properties due to exceedance of surface water drainage causing street pooling. Sandbags and airbrick covers were provided by RBC on 7 Jan to mitigate rising water. Residents reported basement flooding potentially linked to rising groundwater through river gravels; basements receded by 15 Jan. (p15) [PAG]
* Local surface water drainage network in Queens Road was checked and cleared by the RBC Highways Team as a post‑flood intervention to restore drainage capacity following the flood event. (p18)

### Lower Caversham
* {general}: Lower Caversham has experienced rising River Thames water levels such that artificially raised ground has been created to provide informal protection from those rising levels. The area is identified as being close to the main watercourse and thus at risk of property flooding if river/channel capacity is exceeded. (p8)
* {01 Jan 2024 - 31 Jan 2024}: Residents reported flooding of basement areas that may be indicative of groundwater flooding through river gravels; this flooding occurred concurrently with the January 2024 event, though the direct contribution of groundwater is uncertain. (p10)
* Mapped flood extents show inundation of low-lying areas adjacent to the river in Lower Caversham during the January 2024 event; public consultation was held for affected residents indicating the area experienced flood impacts. (p12) [IMG, PAG]
* Rapid onset flooding in January 2024 gave residents little time to prepare. Surface water drainage capacity was exceeded in parts of the area. Sandbags were provided on 7 January 2024 which helped prevent water entering the front of properties but were too late to prevent flooding of gardens and outbuildings that had already begun. Late or no flood warnings were reported by some residents. (p13)
* Residential properties at the eastern end of Queens Road experienced exterior and basement flooding from late evening 5 Jan; internal ground-floor flooding of several properties occurred on 7 Jan. Basement water levels fully receded by 15 Jan (total flood duration 10 days). Flooding arose from exceedance of Christchurch Ditch capacity and culvert blockages by debris, with floodwater flowing via gardens, footpaths and roads into Queens Road and then to Mill Road, Coldicutt Street, Champion Road, Piggott's Road and Mill Green. Sandbags provided on 7 Jan reduced front-of-property inundation but were too late to prevent gardens/outbuildings already flooding. A raised bund to the south of Christchurch Ditch slowed drainage back to the Thames, prolonging inundation. A boat stuck on Caversham weir downstream of Heron Island was reported and may have impeded flows during the event. (p14) [PAG]
* Floodwater from Christchurch Ditch and the River Thames entered the area via residential gardens, footpaths and roads causing internal and external flooding. Basements and ground floors were flooded; front gardens and outbuildings were also inundated. Stantec modelling and a resident sketch show converging flow paths across Lower Caversham during the event. Basement water levels had fully receded by 15 Jan (total reported flood duration of ~10 days). (p15) [IMG, PAG]
* Area was included within the scope of River Thames flood alerts and reviews following the January 2024 River Thames flood event affecting Reading borough. (p20)
* Floodwaters, including flow from Christchurch Ditch northwards, affected Lower Caversham; RBC cleared debris and blockages in ditches within this area as part of the response. (p21) [PAG]
* Lower Caversham received floodwater flowing northwards from Christchurch Ditch, resulting in inundation of the low-lying area and impacts to nearby properties as reported by residents. (p22)
* Surface water flooding affected roads and pavements in Lower Caversham (notably Queens Road at the entrance opposite Coldicutt Street), impeding vehicular and pedestrian movement in the residential area. (p32) [IMG, PAG]
* Residential streets in the area were inundated: roads and pavements (notably Queens Road and the Queens Road/Mill Road junction) were submerged, front gardens were reached by floodwater and routes were rendered impassable for pedestrians and vehicles. (p33) [IMG, PAG]
* Residential streets in Lower Caversham (including Queens Road and Mill Green) experienced flooding. Inundation extended into Caversham Meadow and Christchurch Ditch (photo from Reading Bridge documents extent), impacting roads, pavements, parked vehicles and local access. (p34) [IMG, PAG]

### River Kennet
* {01 Mar 1947 - 31 Mar 1947}: The River Kennet is cited alongside the Thames as having very high water levels during the March 1947 flood, contributing to extensive flood damage in Reading and the Thames Valley. Parts of the Kennet within the RBC area form navigable sections of the Kennet and Avon Canal (maintenance responsibility noted). (p7)
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024}: High antecedent rainfall and the heavy rainfall from Storm Henk caused saturated soils and large volumes of surface-water runoff into the River Kennet, leading to river flows and levels rising rapidly and exceeding channel capacity, increasing flows downstream and the risk of inundation of adjacent floodplain and urban areas south of Reading. (p8)
* {01 Jan 2024 - 31 Jan 2024}: Rapid runoff and high groundwater/baseflow increased flows in the Kennet and its associated streams, with ephemeral streams flowing when groundwater rose; these processes likely contributed to the January 2024 flood event in the Kennet/Upper Thames area. (p9)
* River Kennet levels rose above the normal range and produced sustained high flows through January, contributing to elevated river levels upstream of Reading. (p11) [PAG]
* River levels at the Theale gauge exceeded the normal range and rose to a notable peak, indicating the Kennet was in flood and contributed to elevated river levels and mapped flood extents through Reading. (p12) [PAG, IMG]
* Residents in Southcote reported that sluice gates on the River Kennet may have been deployed, releasing water into the adjacent floodplain and contributing to unusually rapid local flooding and overtopping of ditches/culverts. (p13)
* Overbank flooding associated with the River Kennet prompted Flood Alerts and was linked to flooding risk for properties adjacent to the river; the River Kennet / Holy Brook flooding is the context for reported residential flooding in the downstream Reading/Southcote area. (p15)
* Water accumulating in the River Kennet floodplain flowed back northwards towards Circuit Lane (via under the railway bridge), causing backflows that contributed to internal property flooding; the River Kennet area was also the subject of Flood Alerts/Warnings during the period. (p16) [PAG]
* Flooding caused damage to river banks and adjacent footpaths in Katesgrove; areas adjacent to the River Kennet were recorded as flooded and contributed to temporary unavailability of nearby recreational facilities. (p17)
* Flooding of the River Kennet contributed to bank and footpath damage adjacent to Katesgrove; the affected section is designated as part of the Kennet and Avon Canal and falls under CRT responsibility. (p18)
* High river levels on the River Kennet, together with the River Thames and associated watercourses, contributed to flooding that affected residents and properties in Reading borough. (p21)
* Flooding from the River Kennet inundated its floodplain and flowed northwards beneath Briscoe's Bridge. (p29)
* Backflow floodwater was present on the River Kennet floodplain north of Briscoe’s Bridge, inundating wooded floodplain areas with standing/backwater and causing saturated ground and fallen tree debris. (p39) [IMG]
* Quant: 1.78m AD peak on 06 Jan 2024; normal range top 1.05m AD; levels remained above normal from 01 Jan - 27 Jan 2024 (p11) [PAG]
* Quant: Maximum recorded level 1.78m AD on 6th January 2024; levels remained above the normal range from 1st - 27th January 2024. (p12) [PAG, IMG]
* Quant: 21 residential properties; 3 commercial properties (p21)
