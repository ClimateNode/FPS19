

# Irthlingborough flooding 09 Mar 2016

## Place names with coordinates

### Station Road, Irthlingborough
* Heavy rainfall caused surface water drainage and watercourses to be unable to convey rainwater, producing excess surface water that flowed to low points around private property and resulted in internal flooding of properties on Station Road. (p7)
* Internal flooding of properties occurred on Station Road. The report states internal flooding of more than one property has been experienced on more than one occasion in the five years prior to the investigation. (p9) [MUL]
* Flooding occurred near Station Road, close to the roundabout junction with the A6 Finedon Road. Several businesses on the north and south sides of Station Road, immediately west of the A6, were affected by the flood. (p10)
* Station Road was flooded by flowing surface water (flow from the west) during the same overtopping events, impeding access and requiring placement of sandbags on the kerbline during responses. (p12) [TAB, MUL]
* The carriageway between the businesses was flooded before the overland flow from the watercourse started; water overtopped the lowered kerbs on the south side of the carriageway and flowed off the highway towards private property; attempts were made to unblock road gullies. (p14)
* Flooding occurred on Station Road when heavy rainfall and overwhelmed drainage/watercourses caused excess surface water to flow to low points around private properties. (p15) [PAG]
* Heavy rainfall on a saturated catchment caused drainage systems and watercourses to be unable to collect and convey rainwater, resulting in excess surface water flowing over ground into low points around private properties on Station Road. Sections of upstream watercourse that had not been recently maintained were identified as a significant contributory factor; limited records of underground drainage suggest historic/private systems may have exacerbated the incident. (p16)
* Flooding of the carriageway (overland flow) occurred on Station Road; lowered kerbs are noted at the location of carriageway flooding. (p33) [IMG]
* Standing floodwater covered the carriageway and part of the footway; responders placed a continuous line of sandbags across the road to control/divert water flow and protect the nearby traffic features, disrupting normal vehicle and pedestrian movement. (p42) [IMG]
* Floodwater extended from the building edge across the sidewalk and into the carriageway, covering the pavement and affecting parked vehicles and pedestrian movement on Station Road. (p43) [IMG, PAG]
* Station Road carried the flow route of flood water during the event; wet pavement is visible, an approximate route of a below-ground culvert under the carriageway is shown directing flow, and unused lowered kerbs on the south carriageway channel are visible along the flood route. (p53) [IMG, PAG]
* Floodwater flowed across the carriageway along Station Road via the identified flow route and kerb channels, inundating the road surface and affecting access between the businesses either side of the road. (p54) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: more than one property; more than one occasion in the last 5 years (p7)
* Quant: 4 businesses (p10)

### Irthlingborough
* A flood incident on 9 March 2016 (Station Road, Irthlingborough) caused surface water to flow to low points around private properties, resulting in internal flooding of more than one property and repeated internal flooding events within the preceding five years. (p7)
* Properties in Irthlingborough (specifically on Station Road) experienced internal flooding, with internal flooding of more than one property on multiple occasions. (p9) [PAG, MUL]
* The east of the town (Station Road area adjacent to the A6/Finedon Road) experienced flooding which affected commercial properties; local ordinary watercourse and culvert blockages/surcharge contributed to the incident. (p10)
* The flood incident at Station Road in Irthlingborough was caused by heavy rainfall on a saturated catchment, with drainage systems and watercourses unable to convey rainwater effectively; this led to excess surface water flowing overland to low points around private properties within Irthlingborough. (p16)
* The town experienced a flood incident (the report refers to 'this flood incident' affecting Irthlingborough). The event prompted advice and actions for property owners and communities regarding riparian responsibilities, maintenance of ponds/ditches/open drainage and community resilience measures. (p17)
* Flooding incidents occurred in Irthlingborough that prompted a Section 19 investigation by the LLFA and the recording of assets and flooding hotspots affecting the area. (p19)
* The town was the location of the flood investigated in this report and was affected by flooding during the event on 9th March 2016. (p21)
* A flood incident occurred in the town; the mapped catchment is described as contributing runoff to the flood location within Irthlingborough (map annotation indicates the flood incident location is within the outlined catchment). (p32) [IMG]
* Quant: more than one property; more than one occasion in the last 5 years (p7)
* Quant: 4 businesses (p10)

### Finedon Road, Irthlingborough
* The Finedon Road stream (an ordinary watercourse) flows southeast to Station Road/A6 and immediately upstream of Business 2 enters a culvert that runs beneath Business 2, Station Road and beneath the car parks of Businesses 3 and 4. The culvert/open channel downstream was in poor condition with high silt and debris, affecting flow out of the culvert and contributing to the flood incident that affected adjacent businesses. (p10)
* Quant: 4 businesses (p10)

### Drayton Place, Irthlingborough
* The culvert flows west–east through private property south of Drayton Place and the open watercourse downstream of the Drayton Place culvert is described as upstream of the flood incident, indicating the channel near Drayton Place was involved in the flood event; vegetation and debris were noted in the channel which may have restricted flow. (p56) [IMG]
* Headwall at the entrance to the Drayton Place culvert was heavily obstructed by vegetation, fallen branches and litter, restricting flow and contributing to the upstream flood incident. Obstruction likely increased local flood risk and impeded inspection/access to the culvert. (p58) [IMG]

### Addington Road, Irthlingborough
* Entrance to the culvert under Addington Road was photographed with vegetation and debris partially obscuring the inlet; the upstream watercourse immediately downstream of Addington Road was densely vegetated/overgrown. These conditions were documented in relation to the flood incident and indicate restricted conveyance at the culvert during the event, with floodwater following a flow route across nearby Station Road (between Business 2 and Business 3). (p55) [IMG]

### Lilley Terrace, Irthlingborough
* The outlet of a culvert in public open space immediately north of Lilley Terrace is identified as part of the watercourse upstream of the recorded flood incident; the culvert outlet and adjoining channel were implicated in the upstream flow contributing to the incident. (p56) [IMG]

### Robb Close, Irthlingborough
* The culvert entrance/exit immediately southeast of Robb Close was partially obstructed by dense vegetation, leaves, branches and litter, restricting flow from the culvert and contributing to upstream surface water flooding during the incident. (p57) [IMG]

### Fernmoor Drive, Irthlingborough
* The culvert entrance immediately north of Fernmoor Drive (headwall) was heavily overgrown and blocked by natural debris and litter, restricting the watercourse and contributing to the upstream flooding at the incident location. (p58) [IMG]

### River Nene
* {general}: The River Nene flows immediately south of the incident area; Flood Zones 2 and 3 associated with the River Nene extend to the south of the area where the flood incident occurred, contributing fluvial flood risk to the town. (p10)

### Northamptonshire County Council
* As Lead Local Flood Authority, NCC carried out a Section 19 investigation into flood incidents in Irthlingborough, maintains a register of assets affecting flooding from surface runoff, groundwater or ordinary watercourses, and records flooding hotspots; NCC can suggest causes and make recommendations but does not have the mandate or funding to enforce action by other risk management authorities. (p19)
