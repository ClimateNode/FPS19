### Surrey
* Exceptional rainfall in winter 2013/14 caused predominant surface water runoff and high groundwater levels, resulting in flooding across Surrey (including internal property flooding within the boundary of Epsom and Ewell). (p6)
* Widespread flooding across Surrey from a range of sources, including high groundwater levels; some areas exceeded previous groundwater flood records. (p8) [IMG, PAG]
* Surrey experienced widespread flooding during Winter 2013/14 from multiple sources, explicitly including groundwater flooding as groundwater levels rose very high; storm events during Dec 2013 contributed to sharp rises in river flows and flooding. (p9) [IMG, TAB]
* Widespread flooding occurred across the county during the winter 2013/14 event, prompting strategic actions and involvement from Risk Management Authorities (RMAs) described in the report. (p12) [PAG]
* Widespread flooding across the county in Winter 2013/14: roads were flood affected and in some places carriageway was extensively damaged and assessed for resurfacing (Project 400); roads were cleansed and re-opened; trees, debris and ditches were cleared to restore drainage; emergency pumping was used to remove flood waters to protect residents, property and infrastructure; call centre operated to handle residents' queries and grants/assistance were administered for those flooded. (p13)
* Widespread major flooding across the county caused by a combination of surface water and fluvial (river) sources; this led to internal property flooding in places (for example the ~10 incidents in Epsom and Ewell). Lack of maintenance of riparian watercourses/ditches contributed to the impact. (p18)
* Historic flooding across Surrey during the winter 2013/14 event; data on those flood incidents was gathered to highlight areas at risk, prioritise drainage maintenance and support business cases for major flood defence schemes. (p19) [PAG]
* Quant: approximately 10 incidents of internal property flooding in Epsom and Ewell (p6)
* Quant: 10 incidents of internal property flooding (in Epsom and Ewell) (p18)

### Leatherhead
* The major storm of 23–25 December 2013 produced an unusually large single-day rainfall event for the Leatherhead area (the 23 Dec event was estimated at a 1-in-9 year annual chance). The prolonged wet period (60 days) at Leatherhead was estimated at a 1-in-96 year return period, contributing to sharp rises in river flows and to the fluvial flood warnings affecting the region. (p9) [IMG, MUL]
* Quant: 1 in 9 year (single-day event); 1 in 96 year (60-day rainfall return period) (p9) [IMG, MUL]

### Epsom
* Internal property flooding occurred during winter 2013/14. Multiple sites in and around Epsom are identified on the EA updated Flood Map for Surface Water (uFMfSW) as susceptible to surface water flooding. Surface water flow routes from Epsom Downs towards Epsom town centre affected streets aligned with those routes (examples given: Ashley Road, Church Street; Worple Road bisects flow routes toward the bottom of the hill). (p16) [PAG]
* Quant: 6 of the 7 sites (p16) [PAG]

### Stoneleigh
* One site in Stoneleigh is identified on the EA uFMfSW as susceptible to surface water flooding; it lies on a surface water flow path through local residential streets (Walsingham Gardens, Amberley Gardens, Thorndon Gardens) with predicted flow following topography north‑westerly towards Auriol Park. The sub area experienced internal property flooding in winter 2013/14 and this Stoneleigh site was one of the Section 19 sites. (p16)
* Quant: 1 site (p16)

### Church Street, Epsom
* Internal property flooding occurred on Church Street during the sub area event; emergency responders (Surrey Fire and Rescue) attended properties to pump water, isolate electrical supplies and advise residents. (p16)
* Carried out resurfacing (Project Horizon) which altered road topography to improve surface water flow to highway gullies as a mitigation/response to flooding. (p17)
* Quant: 1 additional gully constructed; 2 highway gullies cleansed (p17)

### Ashley Road, Epsom
* Internal property flooding occurred on Ashley Road during the sub area event; emergency responders (Surrey Fire and Rescue) attended properties to pump water, isolate electrical supplies and advise residents. (p16)
* Gullies identified as requiring maintenance following the flood event; extensive gully cleansing and jetting operations carried out on assets along the road. (p17)

### Amberley Gardens, Stoneleigh
* Located on a surface water flow path for the Stoneleigh Section 19 site; the area is identified on the EA updated Flood Map for Surface Water as susceptible to surface water flooding and is on the predicted flow route which fed towards Auriol Park. The Stoneleigh site was one of the sub-area sites where internal property flooding occurred during winter 2013/14. (p16)
* Quant: 1 site (Stoneleigh Section 19 site) (p16)

### Thorndon Gardens, Ewell
* Located on a surface water flow path for the Stoneleigh Section 19 site; identified on the EA updated Flood Map for Surface Water as susceptible to surface water flooding with predicted flow following local topography towards Auriol Park. The Stoneleigh site experienced internal property flooding during winter 2013/14. (p16)
* Quant: 1 site (Stoneleigh Section 19 site) (p16)

### Pitt Place, Epsom
* Access to Pitt Place experienced flooding; investigation attributed this to inadequate drainage layout and topography within the property. An additional external gully was constructed outside Pitt Place as a remedial measure. (p17)
* Quant: 1 additional gully constructed (p17)

### Worple Road, Epsom
* Worple Road was identified as a site intersecting surface water flow routes and was part of the sub area where internal property flooding occurred during the event. (p16)

### Walsingham Gardens, Stoneleigh
* Walsingham Gardens lies on a surface water flow path in Stoneleigh that was identified as susceptible to surface water flooding; the Stoneleigh site was part of the sub area where internal property flooding occurred. (p16)

### Tattenham Corner Road, Epsom
* Identified location for a deepbore soakaway installation as a mitigation measure to address localised flooding (part of the Wetspots Capital Programme). (p19)

### Auriol Park
* Predicted as the down‑slope destination of surface water flows from the Stoneleigh flow path (flows from Walsingham Gardens / Amberley Gardens / Thorndon Gardens were predicted to follow topography towards Auriol Park). The Stoneleigh site is associated with internal property flooding during winter 2013/14. (p16)
* Quant: 1 site (Stoneleigh Section 19 site) (p16)

### Surrey County Council
* {winter 2013/14, December 2013 - January 2014}: As lead RMA for surface water flooding, SCC closed roads for public safety, coordinated response actions (Surrey Fire and Rescue pumped water from properties and provided advice), inspected affected highways and drainage assets and carried out remedial works and gully cleansing where applicable; administered Repair and Renew grants and operated a call centre during the event. (p6)
* As the Lead Local Flood Authority and highways authority, inspected flood affected roads and arranged defect repairs (contracted to Kier); assessed flood-damaged carriageways for inclusion in the Project 400 resurfacing programme; cleansed and re-opened roads; provided and recycled sandbags to slow ingress to properties; cleared trees, debris and carried out ditching works to restore drainage; operated a call centre during the flooding; administered Repair and Renew grants for residents and businesses that were flooded. (p13)

SCC cleared trees and debris, carried out ditching works to restore drainage function, provided sandbags, operated a call centre during the floods, and administered the Repair and Renew Grant to support property protection works after properties were flooded. ### South East England

### Environment Agency
* During the winter 2013/14 flooding the Environment Agency operated the flood alert and flood warning service, operated flood risk management assets, carried out flood mitigation works, participated in Strategic and Tactical Command Groups, and supported community flood/emergency planning. (p12)
* Quant: cleared 860 blockages and storm damage incidents; reported 1087 pollution incidents; 125 flood Ambassadors visited 95 locations; 70 flood data recorders sent to more than 100 locations (p12)

### Thames Water
* Thames Water implemented winter arrangements and scaled resources during the winter 2013/14 event to maintain customer services, protect assets vital for service delivery and resume any disrupted services; the report notes other relevant actions were carried out across Surrey. (p12) [PAG]
* Deployed flood barriers and sandbags at TW water and wastewater sites; carried out regular checks of unmanned sites; optimised sewer network by investigations and sewer cleaning; increased number of engineers to investigate flooding reports; Network Engineers visited internally flooded properties where sewer flooding was the primary cause; undertook wide-scale clean ups of flooded properties and provided sewer flooding information to affected properties. (p13) [PAG]

### Surrey Fire and Rescue Service
* Pumped water away from properties during the flooding to reduce damage and provided advice to residents as part of the immediate flood response. (p6)

### Highway Authority
* {December 2013 - January 2014, 01 Feb 2014 - 28 Feb 2014}: Roads and carriageways were flooded and damaged by the Winter 2013/14 floods; officers inspected flood-affected roads, defect repairs were undertaken by contractors, extensive areas of carriageway damaged by the flooding were assessed for inclusion in the Project 400 resurfacing programme, roads were cleansed and re-opened, trees and debris were cleared and ditching works carried out to restore drainage function. (p13) [MUL]
