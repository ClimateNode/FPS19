### The Square, Earls Barton

* Internal flooding crossed the threshold of a property located at The Square; the property experienced internal flooding during the incident and has experienced internal flooding on more than one occasion in the last five years. (p9) [PAG]

### Broad Street, Earls Barton
* Described as the low point where surface water sewers discharge and identified on flood mapping as high risk; several road gullies on Broad Street were seen to be blocked with leaves and silt during the flood investigation, implicating Broad Street in the surface water flooding observed. (p10) [PAG]
* {Approx 2011, 14 Sep 2015}: Road flooding documented (roads flooded but no properties pre-2011); approximately 2011 a property on Broad Street was internally flooded by surface water from the street; on 14/09/2015 the south footway at the low point of Broad Street was flooded by surface water from Broad Street, The Square and Churchill Road. (p11) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* Surface water continued east along Broad Street to the low point opposite Churchill Road, with water reported flowing out of road gullies at low areas; overtopping of kerbs at the low point flooded the footway and the road and footways at low points of Broad Street were flooded. (p12)
* Arrangement of kerbs and levels allowed surface water flowing off the carriageway to reach the footway and enter doorways of properties, making areas vulnerable to surface water flooding during the event. (p13)
* Floodwater covered the road and intersection, extended onto sidewalks and up to residential doorsteps on the south side of Broad Street, impeding vehicle movement and pedestrian access; parked vehicles were partially in standing floodwater. (p37) [IMG, PAG]
* Water emerging from a kerbside (offlet) gully and flowing onto the road and pavement, causing localised surface water flooding that pooled along the pavement and extended into the carriageway, affecting pedestrian movement and vehicle passage and posing a hazard. (p38) [IMG, PAG]
* Localised footway flooding on the south side of Broad Street opposite Churchill Road, with water pooling around a drain and wet pavement causing pedestrian inconvenience and indicating ineffective drainage/blockage. (p42) [IMG, PAG]
* Footway flooding on the south side of Broad Street opposite Churchill Road. Roof drainage downpipe discharging into a blocked highway gully contributed to surface water accumulation on the pavement, creating localized flooding and risk of water ingress to adjacent properties. (p43) [IMG, PAG]

### Earls Barton
* Moderate rainfall exceeded local drainage capacity so surface water followed contours to low points on the public highway and around private property in Earls Barton, causing internal flooding of at least one property. (p6) [PAG]
* Moderate rainfall and inadequate drainage led to excess surface water flowing to low points on the public highway and into private property, causing internal flooding (at least one property affected). (p7) [PAG]
* The Square, within Earls Barton, experienced internal flooding of a property on 16th September 2015; the incident is part of repeated internal flooding incidents in the village over the last five years. (p9) [PAG]
* Flood incident investigation of the town identified surface water flooding issues during the event: public and surface water sewers conveyed flows into the town centre (The Square/Broad Street), road gullies were observed blocked and runoff from hardstanding and private car parks was seen entering the public highway. (p10)
* Moderate rainfall could not be collected or conveyed by drainage. Surface water flowed from private property and the highway on higher ground, following natural contours to low points in the village, causing flooding of low-lying areas, footways and properties; highway drainage and public sewers were relied upon and were vulnerable to blockage and exceedance. (p13) [PAG]
* Surface water overtopped kerbs and flowed towards private property; highway drainage capacity was identified as insufficient in areas creating flood risk to properties. Actions recommended include regular highway drainage cleansing, asset capacity assessments and review of carriageway/kerb/footway levels to reduce overtopping and runoff to properties. (p20)
* Quant: 1 property (p6) [PAG]
* Quant: one property (p7) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 property (p9) [PAG]

### High Street, Earls Barton
* Surface water sewers flow from High Street to The Square; several road gullies on High Street were observed blocked with leaves and silt during the flood investigation, and runoff from private car parks and hardstanding was seen to flow onto the public highway during the incident. (p10)
* Surface-water runoff from higher ground on High Street contributed to flows along carriageways and over road gullies; this runoff overtopped kerbs at the corner of Station Road and The Square and contributed to accumulation and entry of surface water into nearby property (notably on 16/09/2015). Repeated overtopping from High Street (and West Way) has been reported historically. (p11) [TAB, PAG]
* Surface water flowed from High Street north to south across the crossroads junction toward the corner of Station Road and Broad Street, contributing to accumulation of water at that junction. (p12)
* {general}: A road gully at the edge of High Street was observed blocked by leaves and debris during the flood-incident investigation, reducing drainage capacity at the junction with The Square and contributing to localised surface water ponding / runoff. (p40) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Blocked road gullies were observed, reducing roadside drainage capacity and contributing to surface water accumulation/run-off on the carriageway. (p45) [PAG]
* Quant: Approximately six times every year for last five years (p11) [TAB, PAG]
