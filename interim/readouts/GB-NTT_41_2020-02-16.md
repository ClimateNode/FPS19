

# Lowdham flooding 16 Feb 2020

## Place names with coordinates

### Lowdham
* Extensive flooding occurred in the village during Storm Dennis; Nottinghamshire County Council’s Adult Social Care and Public Health Team evacuated a vulnerable person to a place of safety. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Parts of the village experienced significant flooding caused by very high flows in the Cocker Beck and heavy rainfall: internal property flooding, flooding of gardens and curtilages, and inundation of the public highway. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Widespread flooding of properties, roads and open spaces across the village caused by combination of Cocker Beck overtopping and surface water runoff. Resulted in internal flooding of properties, flooding to gardens, curtilages and the public highway. (p4) [IMG, PAG]
* Areas of Lowdham experienced surface water flooding; the Newton Close / Blenheim Avenue estate was severely affected by overland surface water. Highway drainage for that area is served by a surface water pumping station which early investigations indicate was operating to design standards during the event. (p5) [IMG]
* Watercourses and drainage assets (riparian-owned) in Lowdham were attended to and maintained as part of the flood response; works were carried out to manage flood water and drainage assets following the event. (p7)
* {01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019, 01 Jan 2020 - 31 Jan 2020, 01 Feb 2020 - 29 Feb 2020}: Local flood storage (cricket pitch) filled during flooding; Environment Agency carried out drainage works (enlarged pipe, flap valve and penstock) so storage could be drained faster; ongoing works and surveys (surface water/highway drainage improvements, private drainage survey on Blenhein Avenue) are being progressed to reduce future impacts. (p8) [MUL]

### Cocker Beck
* The Cocker Beck (a Main River through the Lowdham catchment) rose to its highest recorded level (Lowdham Grange gauge peak 1.54m) on 16 February 2020 and caused a significant flood event in Lowdham, resulting in internal flooding of properties, flooding of gardens/curtilages and inundation of the public highway. (p3) [IMG]
* The Cocker Beck overtopped on 16 Feb 2020 and, combined with surface water runoff, was a primary source of widespread flooding across Lowdham that led to internal property flooding, flooding of roads and open spaces. (p4) [IMG]
* The watercourse was overwhelmed by runoff from saturated upper catchment, over-topped its banks and spilled out at points along its route, contributing to extensive overland flows that overwhelmed local surface water assets and caused widespread flooding in the catchment. (p5) [IMG, PAG]
* Cocker Beck (Main River) had raised river levels that required use of the cricket pitch flood storage area; flood storage water was subsequently drained via a penstock once the river level reduced. (p7)
* Quant: 93 reported incidents of internal property flooding (p3) [IMG]
* Quant: 93 reported incidents of internal property flooding (across Lowdham) (p4) [IMG]

### Newton Close, Lowdham
* Marked on the map as part of the larger cluster of internal property flooding east of the Cocker Beck; residential properties and immediate streets were affected by floodwater from the overtopped Beck and surface water. (p4) [IMG]
* Severely affected by overland surface water (runoff) that flowed across the estate; area was inundated by surface water which overwhelmed local drainage. Highway drainage for this area is served by a surface water pumping station, which investigations indicated was operating to design standards during the event. (p5) [PAG]
* Newton Close is shown within the map's shaded predicted surface water impact zone. The investigation concludes flooding in the area resulted from an excessive volume of rain falling onto already saturated ground, leading to surface water accumulation affecting the street and adjacent residential blocks. A nearby surface water pumping station (shown on the map) was reported to have been operating to its design standards during the event. (p6) [IMG]

### Blenheim Avenue, Lowdham
* Severely affected by overland surface water (runoff) that inundated the estate; local surface water overwhelmed drainage assets in the area. (p5) [PAG]
* Blenheim Avenue is in the mapped Newton Close / Blenheim Avenue area where predicted surface water impact zones are concentrated along roadways. The map shows surface water accumulation adjacent to this major road and a Surface Water Pump located nearby; flooding in the area is attributed to extreme rainfall on saturated ground, causing potential road inundation and surface water affecting the highway and nearby properties. The pumping station was reported to have been operating to its design standards during the event. (p6) [IMG]
* {01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019}: Nottinghamshire County Council committed to survey the private drainage network and pump system on Blenhein Avenue to establish operational condition and interaction with Lowdham's wider surface water drainage, indicating issues during the flood event. (p8)

### Willow Holt, Lowdham
* {01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019}: Surface water/highway drainage improvements are planned for the Willow Holt area by Severn Trent Water and Nottinghamshire County Council in response to the flooding experienced during the event. (p8)

### Epperstone by-Pass
* The Epperstone By‑Pass acted as a channel for large overland flows during the event and was inundated, conveying water through the catchment and exacerbating local flooding. (p5)


## Topics without coordinates

### Environment Agency
* Operated a drainage arrangement (penstock) in the cricket pitch flood storage area to quickly drain stored flood water by gravity once Cocker Beck river levels reduced, creating storage capacity for further rainfall; responsible for maintenance and management of the Cocker Beck (Main River). (p7)
* {01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019, 01 Jan 2020 - 31 Jan 2020, 01 Feb 2020 - 29 Feb 2020}: Environment Agency staff operated a new drainage arrangement (penstock and enlarged pipe/flap valve) in the cricket pitch flood storage area to rapidly drain floodwater once the Cocker Beck receded; the penstock was opened and successfully emptied the storage area, greatly increasing available storage capacity for subsequent rainfall. The EA also carried out vegetation clearance and repaired walls around the storage area and on Station Street. (p8) [MUL]
* {01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019, 01 Jan 2020 - 31 Jan 2020, 01 Feb 2020 - 29 Feb 2020}: Quant: emptied within six hours; previously could take up to 36 hours to drain (p8) [MUL]

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* {15 Feb 2020 - 16 Feb 2020}: Coordinated response to Storm Dennis including commissioning response work (road closures, signs, sandbags) via contractor, welfare checks in the community, and evacuation of a vulnerable person to safety; involved in declaring/operating under a major incident response. (p2)
* {2018, 16 Feb 2020}: Worked with the Environment Agency and Trent Rivers Trust from 2018 to deliver Natural Flood Management measures in the upper Cocker Beck catchment to hold back and store surface water runoff; committed to survey the private drainage network and pump system on Blenhein Avenue to assess operation and interaction with Lowdham's wider surface water drainage; administers Flood Warden and Community Flood Signage schemes to support local flood response. (p8) [PAG, MUL]
* {15 Feb 2020 - 16 Feb 2020}: Quant: 51 road closures (facilitated); over 70 flood signs (placed); over 4,000 sandbags (delivered); 1 vulnerable person evacuated; major incident declared at 07:00 on 16 February 2020 (p2)

### Cocker Beck Catchment
* {01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019, 01 Jan 2020 - 31 Jan 2020, 01 Feb 2020 - 29 Feb 2020}: High river levels and surface-water runoff in the upper Cocker Beck catchment contributed to filling the cricket pitch flood storage area; when the river level receded the penstock allowed gravity drainage of the storage area. Natural Flood Management measures are being installed in the upper catchment to hold back and store surface water. (p8) [MUL]
* {01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019, 01 Jan 2020 - 31 Jan 2020, 01 Feb 2020 - 29 Feb 2020}: Quant: emptied within six hours; previously took up to 36 hours (p8) [MUL]
