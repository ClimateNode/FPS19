## Place names with coordinates

### Old Course River Nene
* {general}: The old course River Nene is the main watercourse flowing through March and is shown by Environment Agency mapping to present flood risk to the town. (p4)
* River-related flooding divided affected areas into locations north and south of the Old Course River Nene; associated with the area-wide flooding that generated 67 flood reports (20 internal property floodings) from 23/24 December 2020 and reports from over 50 streets in March. (p8) [TAB, PAG]
* Surface water flooding occurred in the area north of the Old Course River Nene. (p9)
* Outfalls to the Old River (Old River Nene) were identified where high silt levels or high water levels are impacting flood risk management; investigations were planned to identify where these factors contributed to flooding in the area. (p10) [TAB]
* Surface water flooding occurred south of the Old Course River Nene caused by high groundwater, blocked ordinary watercourses and a culvert, low spots causing ponding, cross‑connections between drainage systems, overwhelmed drainage networks and spring/overland flows; resulted in localised ponding and inability for surface water to drain effectively. (p12)
* Quant: over 50 streets; 67 flood reports; 20 internally flooded properties (p8) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 1 internally flooded property (p10) [TAB, PAG]

### Fenland
* {2006, 2009, 2012, 2014, 08 Aug 2014 - 09 Aug 2014, 2016, 2018, December 2020}: Contains the town of March which frequently experiences flooding with considerable internal flooding historically; large areas of March are shown to be at significant risk from surface water, and EA mapping also shows flood risk associated with the old course River Nene affecting the area. (p4) [MUL]

### East Anglia
* Intense rainfall on an already saturated catchment (notably on 23 December 2020) overwhelmed surface water drainage. Excess surface water flowed over ground into the public sewer network, causing the sewer system to surcharge and flood. Saturated ground and a high water-table exacerbated runoff and the inability of drainage to convey water. (p6) [PAG]

### Brewin Avenue, March

### Butt Avenue, March

## Topics without coordinates

### Anglian Water
* Engaged to investigate and implement measures to remove highway surface water entering the public sewer network following surface-water flooding of highways; part of coordinated post‑flood response. (p11)
* Surface water entry into the foul sewer network and risk of foul sewer surcharge prompted operational and mitigation works: CCTV surveys, silt and root removal from surface water systems (Upwell Road), capping a historic overflow from surface water to the foul network (Eastwood Avenue) to prevent surcharge, planned cleaning of foul sewers around Gaul Road, and planned works to increase capacity at a surface water manhole at the end of Green Street. (p12) [TAB, PAG]
* {December 2020, June 2021}: Carried out cleaning and remedial works to sewer and surface water systems: cleaning foul sewers on and around Gaul Road; CCTV survey and removal of silt and roots from the surface water system on Upwell Road; capping a historic surface-water-to-foul overflow in Eastwood Avenue to prevent foul network surcharge; planned works at the surface water manhole at the end of Green Street to increase surface water sewer flows. (p13) [MUL]
* {general}: Anglian Water ran the 'Keep it Clear' campaign addressing sewer blockages that cause flooding and pollution incidents; by working with communities in high‑risk areas they helped reduce blockages and therefore flood/pollution incidents. (p18)
* {general}: Quant: more than half of sewer blockages that cause flooding and pollution incidents; blockages reduced by an average of more than 50PCSYM where communities engaged with the campaign (p18)

### Cambridgeshire County Council
* Surface water flooding north of the Old Course River Nene prompted local authority/LLFA action: issuing letters to riparian owners advising maintenance responsibilities; funding surveying in the area to support integrated modelling; collaborating on a data agreement; and updating guidance on riparian responsibilities. (p10) [TAB]
* As the Lead Local Flood Authority (LLFA), Cambridgeshire County Council investigated the December 2020 flooding in March. CCC holds a digital flood model of March and is working with Anglian Water to combine their models to better represent flood flows in the town. CCC has commenced comprehensive surveying and mapping of drainage assets and is collaborating with Anglian Water, Fenland District Council and other Risk Management Authorities on evidence, planning advice and drainage improvements (including input to the Broad Street town-centre project) to achieve flood‑risk betterment. (p15)

### Fenland District Council
* Investigated installation of a sump pump to manage basement flooding at County Hall following the December 2020 flood. (p11)

### Cambridgeshire Highways
