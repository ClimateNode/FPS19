## Place names with coordinates

### Stonelea Road, Sywell
* Heavy rainfall on a saturated catchment, together with insufficient or absent drainage, caused excess surface water to flow over ground following natural contours to low points around private property. This resulted in internal flooding of a property on Stonelea Road; internal flooding of one property has been experienced on more than one occasion in the last five years. (p7)
* Internal flooding of a property (water entered the building). The incident on 9th March 2016 caused internal flooding of one property; the same property has experienced internal flooding on more than one occasion in the previous five years. (p9)
* The area is identified on NCC Flood Toolkit as being within an area of varying surface water flood risk; the street (residential area including later housing on Palmer Grove) was within the scope of the local flood investigation relating to the 9th March 2016 event. (p10)
* {general}: A historic ditch to the rear of gardens that intercepted agricultural runoff was removed (prior to 2006) and replaced with an underground land drain (2006). Residents reported the ditch outlet was piped east (to either a public surface water sewer or the west/central watercourse). It was also reported that, during later development, a land drain across the north of Stonelea Road was demolished and not reinstated; this drain is believed to have been the connecting drain from the historic ditch. These drainage alterations changed local runoff routes and are recorded in the area’s flood history. (p11)
* {03 Nov 2012, 04 Nov 2012, 09 Mar 2016}: Surface water runoff from agricultural fields to the north flowed under the rear garden fence across the rear garden into the house and garage, causing internal flooding of a property. (p12) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* Surface water from agricultural fields flowed under the rear garden fence across the garden towards the house and garage, ponded at the rear of the building and entered air bricks. Water flowed under the ground floor (audible from inside), rose over the rear door threshold causing damage to the door frame and flooring, and caused internal flooding of the garage. Floodwaters later receded by continuing to flow into the airbricks. (p13)
* Surface water flooding associated with inadequate or ineffective surface water drainage from recent developments affected properties on Stonelea Road, creating flood risk to those properties. (p22)
* Overland surface water flow converged from the west and southwest toward a central low point adjacent to Stonelea Road, producing a schematic flood area on the map. Historic ditches and possible outlet pipes connected to the area but were indicated as insufficient during the incident, contributing to localized inundation at/near Stonelea Road. (p30) [IMG]
* Floodwater pooled and flowed along the side and rear of a residential property, entering the garden and reaching the house threshold; water encroached onto the patio and interior threshold, outdoor furniture was submerged and a submersible pump was deployed to remove water. (p38) [IMG]
* {general}: Waterlogging and soil compaction in the field at the rear of Stonelea Road caused patchy crop growth and visible bands of poor establishment adjacent to gardens and fences, reducing agricultural productivity. (p45) [IMG, PAG]
* Field at the rear of Stonelea Road experienced waterlogging and soil compaction that damaged crop growth; photographed areas show large bare or thinly vegetated patches consistent with localized pooling/runoff adjacent to residential properties. (p46) [IMG, PAG]
* Rear garden of an affected residential property was saturated; air brick located just below the door threshold and a linear drainage channel present directly in front of the rear door on the patio, indicating water accumulation at the base of the property. (p48) [IMG, PAG]
* Rear entrance/patio area of a residential property on Stonelea Road showed water staining and evidence of surface water affecting external thresholds and drainage features (air brick and drain noted at rear of the affected property). (p49) [IMG, PAG]
* Saturated ground at the rear of a garden of an affected property on Stonelea Road, indicating soil saturation and shallow inundation of garden area. (p50) [IMG, PAG]

### Sywell
* At Stonelea Road in Sywell, heavy rainfall on a saturated catchment with insufficient drainage led to excess surface water flowing to low points around private property and caused internal flooding of at least one property in the village. (p7)
* Residents in the village were affected by the flooding incident on 9th March 2016; affected residents and a parish representative were interviewed as part of a Flood Incident Investigation on 14th June 2016. (p10) [PAG]
* Heavy rainfall on a saturated catchment caused excess surface water to flow overland from agricultural fields to low points around private properties in the village. Several properties backing onto the fields experienced garden and garage flooding, including the affected property on Stonelea Road. (p13) [PAG]
* The investigation map locates a schematic flood area within Sywell where multiple overland flow routes and historic drainage features converged, indicating localized surface water flooding in the village (central low point near Stonelea Road) during the incident. (p30) [IMG]
* Quant: 1 property (p7)

### Westlea Road, Sywell
* The area is identified on NCC Flood Toolkit as being within an area of varying surface water flood risk; Westlea Road was included in the local assessment and investigation following the flooding incident on 9th March 2016. (p10)
* {03 Nov 2012, 04 Nov 2012, 09 Mar 2016}: Runoff from agricultural fields caused flood water to affect rear gardens and garages on Westlea Road; three properties had gardens and garages affected in November 2012, and a garage of a property flooded on 09/03/2016. (p12) [TAB, MUL]
* Fields to the rear of Westlea Road were waterlogged with standing water and muddy, waterlogged tracks; this surface water was adjacent to and influenced the boundary with nearby residential properties. (p39) [IMG, PAG]
* {03 Nov 2012, 04 Nov 2012, 09 Mar 2016}: Quant: three properties; one garage (p12) [TAB, MUL]

### Palmer Grove, Sywell
* {general}: Palmer Grove is described as draining to soakaways. During its construction it was reported that a land drain across the north of Stonelea Road (believed to connect the historic ditch to the east) was demolished and not reinstated; this alteration to the local drainage network is recorded in the report and relates to surface water runoff routing in the area. (p11)
* Surface water flooding associated with inadequate or ineffective surface water drainage from recent developments affected properties on Palmer Grove, creating flood risk to those properties. (p22)

### Holcot Lane, Sywell
* A piped watercourse on Holcot Lane is shown connecting to the schematic flood area and formed part of the flow network during the incident; pipe/culvert sections and outlet routes from Holcot Lane are implicated in conveying water toward the flooded area and may have been overwhelmed or contributed to inundation. (p30) [IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Anglian Water
* Public sewer network received additional water contributing to flood risk; AWS were tasked to assess sources entering the public sewer, assess asset capacity where insufficient capacity was contributing to property flood risk and to prepare an asset plan to share with the LLFA and community. (p22)

### BCW
* BCW was asked to review planning policies and drainage designs for developments on Stonelea Road and Palmer Grove, to consult with EA and the LLFA on planning to reduce flood risk, and to use enforcement powers where riparian owners fail to maintain ordinary watercourses — actions prompted by flooding impacts at these developments. (p22)

### Environment Agency
* Environment Agency was asked to work with local emergency planning and the LLFA to support the community, reflecting its role in responding to surface water flooding impacts in Sywell. (p22) [PAG]
