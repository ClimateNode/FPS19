### Clifton Hampden
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Multiple locations within the village/parish experienced flooding on 4 January 2024. Five residential properties and one business were internally flooded by water emerging from a channel in upstream farmland (exacerbated by channel restrictions and lack of maintenance). A property and a business on the High Street (adjacent to the right bank of the Thames) were internally flooded due to high river levels. Flooding between these areas reached homes north of Abingdon Road but did not cause internal flooding there. The five properties flooded by the channel were flooded again on 18 February 2024. (p2) [MUL]
* Within Clifton Hampden there were four separate locations of property flooding in the January 2024 event. Location 1 was flooding associated with an unnamed ordinary watercourse and its tributary culvert through Oxford Road, Courtiers Green and Watery Lane. Location 3 (Terrace of Cottages on Abingdon Road between Clifton Hampden and Burcot) and Location 4 (properties on High Street) were also affected; the High Street properties were flooded by the River Thames. Most affected properties were in Flood Zone 1, while those on High Street are in Flood Zone 3. (p7) [IMG]
* Four separate locations within the village experienced property flooding on 4th January 2024; sources included an unnamed ordinary (culverted) watercourse running through the village and, for the High Street area, the River Thames. (p8) [IMG, PAG]

* {general}: Repeated flooding of the village: a property on the south side of the Thames (Location 4) has flooded periodically since its construction in the 1840s due to proximity to the river; in 2003 five properties at Location 1 flooded from surface water runoff from adjoining farmland and from blockages in culverted sections of an unnamed watercourse. A culvert on Watery Lane believed to be a restriction was replaced and upsized in 2011. (p11) [IMG, PAG]

A culvert restriction on Watery Lane (compromised by tree roots) was identified and later replaced/upsized. (p12) [MUL]

Dye testing and invert level surveys identified sections with reduced/slow flow in both publicly and privately owned culverts; these constrained flows were identified as contributing to local flooding risk and were flagged for further CCTV inspection and jetting. (p20)
* The main unnamed watercourse that runs through the village overtopped/escaped its channel during the January 4, 2024 event, causing water to come out of the channel toward the road and prompting subsequent improvement works to reduce further runoff onto the highway. (p21) [PAG]

* Culvert restriction and reduced conveyance during the storm on 4 January 2024. A 450mm culvert and the confluence of two culverted watercourses on Watery Lane provided limited capacity and a lower invert outfall (~200mm lower than the brick culvert it discharges to), reducing flow conveyance and contributing to hydraulic inefficiency visible during inspection. (p25) [IMG, PAG]

Recommendations were made to prevent future root ingress (reinforcement or tree removal). (p26) [PAG]
* A 450mm culverted watercourse experienced root ingress and capacity issues leading to backing up of the watercourse and surcharged flooding from manholes on Watery Lane; subsequent works after the event included cutting out the root mass, installing new grated manhole covers and reconstructing the downstream headwall.
