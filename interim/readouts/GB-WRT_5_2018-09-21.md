* {general}: Tidal influence and coastal surge from the Irish Sea are described as contributing to elevated high tides in the Mersey Estuary that drive tidal/coastal flooding risk to low-lying areas of Warrington. (p23) [TAB]

### Atherton Quay
* {general}: Properties and commercial/industrial units around Atherton Quay are at risk of flooding from the Mersey Estuary during high tides; industrial units behind the railway embankment at Bank Quay Station are also identified as at risk from estuary tidal inundation. (p24) [TAB]

### River Ditton
* {general}: The River Ditton and its tributaries are identified as the associated watercourse for the Ditton catchment flood alert and are described as a source of flooding affecting Ditton and surrounding areas (including Huyton, Widnes and Penketh). (p24) [TAB]

### Bollin Catchment
* {general}: The River Bollin catchment (including the Rivers Bollin, Dean, Pedley, Birkin and Mobberley Brooks) is identified as a flood alert area and as a source of flooding that may affect nearby towns such as Knutsford, Wilmslow, Hale, Macclesfield and Bollington. (p24) [TAB]

### Ditton Catchment
* {general}: The Ditton catchment (including Ditton, Logwood Mill, Fox's Bank, Stewards, Netherley Brooks and tributaries) is identified in a flood alert as being at risk from river flooding; the catchment is therefore described as capable of causing or experiencing river flooding. (p24) [TAB]

### Mersey Catchment
* The catchment was the subject of a Flood Alert for the 20–23 September 2018 rainfall period, with a list of brooks and places (Fishington, Bradley, Sow, Keckwick, Lumb Brooks; Warrington, Runcorn, Lymm) identified as at risk of river flooding. (p25) [TAB]

### Gateworth Sewage Treatment Works
* {general}: The Gatewarth STW monitoring location showed a noticeable increase in groundwater conditions in response to Storm Ali and Storm Bronagh; overall groundwater levels were noted as high for the time of year. (p28) [TAB]
