

# Grafton Underwood flooding 25 Nov 2012

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Grafton Underwood, 25th Novermber 2012<br>090\_North Northamptonshire/Grafton-Underwood-FIR.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Grafton Underwood
* Internal flooding of residential properties caused by overland flow of excess surface water following intense rainfall on a saturated catchment; drainage systems ineffective so water flowed over ground following natural contours into properties. (p5) [PAG, MUL]
* Intense, short-duration rainfall on a saturated catchment caused excess surface water to flow overland following natural contours into properties, resulting in internal flooding. Flooding was reported to have caused internal flooding of properties and had occurred on more than one occasion in the previous five years. (p6) [PAG]
* Internal flooding of properties: two properties had ground-floor internal living areas flooded by surface water overland flows; one property experienced a back-up of effluent into a ground-floor toilet (sewer/back-up). (p8) [MUL]
* Surface water run-off from agricultural land to the east flowed into rear gardens and into properties. Run-off from fields overtopped drainage ditches and flowed onto the highway at the junction of Warkton Road and Grafton Road. Water backed up at the junction of Grafton Road and Geddington Road causing flooding of the road junction and failure/damage to the tarmac footway. Reverse flow flooded the rear garden and patio of a property at the northeast of the village (did not reach the dwelling threshold). Culvert blockages in the highway at the junction were evidenced and culverts beneath Geddington Road operated unsatisfactorily, allowing water onto the highway. A property at the east had its conservatory flooded to a shallow depth and water flowed to the kitchen; works (trench, driveway reshaping) were carried out to divert flows. A property at the southeast experienced approximately 50mm of water in the kitchen (groundwater emergence) and temporary rear patio inundation; another southeast property had a ground-floor toilet flooded. (p10) [PAG]
* Residential properties in the southeast of the village were flooded: one property had groundwater emerge through weaknesses in the floor slab causing approximately 50mm of water in the kitchen and surface water runoff from fields to the east temporarily flooded the rear patio; another property experienced water backing up and flooding a ground floor toilet; the garden and patio of the property referenced in 3.3.2 flooded to a depth of approximately 4. (p11) [PAG]
* Properties in Grafton Underwood were flooded after intense, short-duration rainfall on a saturated catchment. Existing drainage systems and watercourses were unable to cope, causing water to flow into other areas and overwhelm local drainage infrastructure, resulting in flooding of properties. (p14) [PAG]
* Properties in the village were flooded after intense short-duration rainfall on a saturated catchment. Existing drainage systems and watercourses were unable to cope, causing water to flow into other areas and into properties; surface water and emergence of groundwater occurred within dwellings. (p15)

### Grafton Road, Grafton Underwood
* Flooding occurred at the junctions on Grafton Road (south at Warkton Road and north at Geddington Road). Water backed up at the junction with Geddington Road, causing road junction flooding; reverse flow flooded the rear garden and patio of a property (did not reach dwelling threshold); culvert blockages and inability of run-off to enter Alledge Brook led to manhole cover overtopping on the highway. (p10)
* Highway sections on Grafton Road were flooded at junctions with Geddington Road and with Warkton Road (junction with Geddington Road flooded to an unspecified depth; junction with Warkton Road flooded to an unknown depth). (p11)
* The highway drainage system and the culverts beneath the highway serving Alledge Brook at the junction of Grafton Road and Geddington Road did not operate satisfactorily, causing the road junction to flood. (p15)

### Grafton Underwood Catchment
* Agricultural fields on higher ground in the catchment shed surface water (overland flow) onto lower-level properties, contributing to internal flooding in the village. (p8)
* {1982, 1989, 04 Nov 2012, 21 Nov 2012, 25 Nov 2012, 20 Dec 2012, 26 Jan 2013, 27 Jan 2013}: Surface water runoff from agricultural land to the east of the village flowed into the village, entering rear gardens and causing internal flooding of properties and flooding of roads. People and property were affected during the event. (p9) [MUL]
* {1982, 1989, 04 Nov 2012, 21 Nov 2012, 25 Nov 2012, 20 Dec 2012, 26 Jan 2013, 27 Jan 2013}: Quant: 3 properties internally flooded (25/11/2012); at least 2 residential properties had rear gardens flooded; 4 properties internally flooded (1982); 4 properties internally flooded (1989); 1 property internally flooded (04/11/2012); roads flooded but not properties on 21/11/2012, 20/12/2012 and 26/01/2013–27/01/2013. (p9) [MUL]

### Anglian Water
* {general}: The sewer systems managed by Anglian Water Services can become overwhelmed in extreme weather and result in sewer flooding, putting properties served by those sewers at risk. (p13)

### Alledge Brook
* Surface water route from agricultural land to Alledge Brook passes through properties; in considerable rainfall this route can result in surface water or groundwater emerging within dwellings, contributing to property flooding. (p15)

### Northamptonshire County Council
* Residents in the village experienced surface water runoff from neighbouring land and the emergence of groundwater that created flood risk to properties. Recommended responses in the report (to reduce or manage that flooding) include identifying routes of land drainage from surrounding fields, informing owners of drainage systems of maintenance responsibilities, reinstating historic drainage (re‑excavating ditches to a calculated cross‑sectional area), repairing or replacing piped/culvert sections, and dredging/trimming watercourses; LLFA and NCC Emergency Planning were to provide property‑level protection advice and update the community flood plan. (p16)

### Environment Agency
* The report identifies the need for the Agency to inspect and maintain the main river to ensure its capacity and operation are maintained to reduce flood risk to the village. (p16)

### Appletree Cottage
