

# Shireoaks flooding 20 Oct 2023

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Shireoaks – Storm Babet, October 2023<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/shireoaks-s19-storm-babet-oct-2023.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Shireoaks
* The village suffered a flood event during Storm Babet caused by sustained heavy rainfall. (p1)
* Flooding centred on the corridor through the village adjacent to the River Ryton. The highlighted route on the map (through Shireoaks) shows residential and agricultural areas were within the flood-affected zone. (p3) [IMG]
* The River Ryton burst its banks through Shireoaks on 20th October 2023, directly causing flooding to many properties in the village. (p4) [PAG]
* Flooding occurred when the River Ryton came out of bank and caused flooding on Shireoaks Road; this led to internal flooding of at least one residential property. Overall flooding broadly followed the fluvial flood extents shown in the Figure 4 map (with a slightly enlarged area). (p5) [IMG]
* A Flood Warning was issued for the River Ryton at Worksop Town Centre including Shireoaks; the community was warned and informed by the Environment Agency. (p7)
* Communities in Worksop and Shireoaks were affected by flooding associated with Storm Babet; a multi‑agency drop-in event is to be organised for these settlements. (p8)
* Quant: at least one residential property (p5) [IMG]

### River Ryton
* The River Ryton rose quickly during the main event and was the source of flooding affecting the Shireoaks corridor. It reached a recorded peak level at the Worksop gauge, remaining high overnight before falling the next morning. (p3) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Rose rapidly during sustained heavy rainfall on 20th October 2023, reached a peak level of 2.11m at 11pm on 20th October at the Worksop gauge and subsequently burst its banks, causing fluvial flooding through Shireoaks. (p4) [IMG, PAG]
* Flooding on the River Ryton prompted a Flood Alert for the Ryton Oldcotes catchment and a Flood Warning for the River Ryton (covering Worksop Town Centre and Shireoaks). The Environment Agency carried out flood patrols and cleared blockages before and after peak flows. (p7)
* Interactions between the fishing lakes and the River Ryton were observed by residents and contributed to local flooding during the event. (p8)
* Quant: 2.11m peak level at Worksop gauge (recorded 11pm on 20th; 3rd highest on record) (p3) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Quant: peak level 2.11m; 3rd highest level on record (p4) [IMG, PAG]

### Shireoaks Road, Shireoaks
* Flooding centred on the corridor along Shireoaks Road; the annotated map shows the main route through the flood-affected area following Shireoaks Road, with residential and agricultural areas adjacent to the road likely inundated or cut off due to high River Ryton levels. (p3) [IMG]
* Floodwater from the River Ryton overtopped its banks and flowed onto Shireoaks Road, causing inundation of the road and resulting in internal flooding of at least one residential property. (p5)
* Quant: at least one residential property (p5)

### Bethel Terrace, Shireoaks
* Bethel Terrace is shown on the annotated map as being on the highlighted route through the flood-affected corridor, indicating it was within the area impacted by flooding from the River Ryton. (p3) [IMG]
* Properties on Bethel Terrace were affected during the flooding and were reported to have been aware of pumping interaction from local fishing lakes to the River Ryton. (p4) [PAG]

### Spring Lane, Shireoaks
* Highlighted on the annotated map as part of the main route through the flood-affected area, indicating Spring Lane was within the inundation corridor and likely experienced flooding and access disruption related to high River Ryton levels. (p3) [IMG]
* Floodwater on Spring Lane was deep enough that cars attempting to drive through became stranded in the floodwater. (p4) [PAG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Severn Trent
* Described as the water and sewerage company responsible for managing risk from surface water, foul and combined sewers and for managing the impact of flooding on their networks; provided emergency response and support to the management of the flooding event as a Category 2 responder and engaged with other RMAs on sewer-related flood risk. (p6)
* Referenced as investigating sewer flooding issues related to the flood event. (p7)

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Played a key role in responding to the Major Incident across the county: initiated a Community Flood Recovery Grant to support individuals affected by internal flooding; attended sites to verify internal flooding and offer support, guidance and advice to affected residents and businesses; collected and published verified flood impact data; visited affected properties to establish flood sources and severity; chaired/attended coordination groups and provided 24-hour emergency planning support. (p6)

### Bassetlaw District Council
* Provided emergency flood response support: deployed sandbags and aquasacs to vulnerable premises and residents; opened a rest centre at Retford Leisure Centre; provided temporary accommodation; supplied skips/caged vans to dispose of flood‑damaged property; cleansed detritus from highways and car parks; rehoused affected council tenants and repaired affected council housing; promoted/administered recovery grant schemes. (p7)

### Ryton Oldcotes Catchment
* A Flood Alert was issued for the Ryton Oldcotes catchment; the Environment Agency instigated flood patrols and cleared blockages before and after peak flows. (p7)
