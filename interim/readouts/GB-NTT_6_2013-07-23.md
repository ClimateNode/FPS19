

# Lowdham flooding 23 Jul 2013

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Lowdham July 23rd 2013<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/lowdham-section-19-report.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Nottinghamshire
* Major flooding occurred in parts of the county during the intense rainfall on 23 July 2013; properties across the county were affected. (p1)

### Lowdham
* Approximately 70 properties in Lowdham suffered internal flooding. Contributing sources cited include overland flow (surface water), surcharged public sewers and highway drains (sewer flooding), and flooded watercourses (river flood). (p1) [PAG]
* Up to 70 properties were recorded as being internally flooded during the event; flooding in the village came from three sources (fluvial from the Cocker Beck, rapid surface water run-off (pluvial) from surrounding fields/roads, and sewer flooding when public sewers exceeded capacity). Floodwater moved through Main Street and onto Southwell Road, affecting properties in both northern and southern sections of the village. (p2) [IMG]
* Quant: Approximately 70 properties (p1) [PAG]
* Quant: up to 70 properties (p2) [IMG]

### Southwell Road, Lowdham
* Properties along Southwell Road were flooded when surface-water run-off was channelled via Main Street from the upper catchment and bypass. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Rapid surface water run-off used Main Street as a channel and flooded properties along Southwell Road; highway drainage was overwhelmed and could not convey the flow. (p3) [PAG]
* Quant: up to 70 properties internally flooded (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: up to 70 properties internally flooded (village-wide) (p3) [PAG]

### Main Street, Lowdham
* Main Street acted as a conveyance/channel for surface-water run-off from the upper catchment, transmitting flood water through the village and contributing to flooding of properties along its route and down towards Southwell Road. (p2) [IMG]
* Quant: up to 70 properties internally flooded (p2) [IMG]

### Epperstone by-Pass
* The Epperstone By-Pass was flooded by rapid surface water run-off from fields in the upper catchment during the intense cloudburst; significant surface water ran from the fields and flooded the bypass. (p2) [IMG]

### Cocker Beck
* The Cocker Beck (river) broke its bank in isolated locations during the event, causing some localised flooding. (p3) [PAG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Severn Trent
* Public sewers were unable to cope with the volume of water flowing into them during the intense rainfall, contributing to internal flooding of properties in the village. (p3)
* Quant: up to 70 properties internally flooded (village-wide) (p3)

### Environment Agency
* Some isolated instances of Cocker Beck breaking its bank caused localised flooding during the event. (p3)
* Provided emergency response crews during the flood event; actively engaged with the Lowdham community after the event; funded and facilitated a feasibility study into potential flood alleviation schemes for Lowdham and continue to investigate a Flood Alleviation Scheme. (p4)

### Highway Authority
* The standard design capacity of the highway drainage was insufficient to cope with the extreme surface water run-off; roads (including Epperstone By-Pass, Main Street and connecting routes) were flooded and highway drainage was overwhelmed. Maintenance issues with riparian ditches and culverts were also highlighted. (p3)
