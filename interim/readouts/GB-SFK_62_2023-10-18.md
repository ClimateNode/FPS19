## Place names with coordinates

### Framlingham
* One of the most severely impacted locations: intense rainfall overwhelmed natural flow paths and the design capacity of drainage infrastructure, and the convergence of surface water and fluvial flow paths led to rapid increases in flood water, causing internal flooding of properties, and disruption to infrastructure and services. (p4)
* One of the most significantly impacted communities during Storm Babet (20 Oct 2023). The town experienced major flooding from fluvial and pluvial sources (often combined); the majority of property flooding included a fluvial element as the River Ore and Cherry Brook exceeded capacity and overtopped their banks. Water levels rose rapidly and a Flood Warning was issued. (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* The central area of Framlingham (including the town spine) was affected by flooding during the event as rising River Ore / Mere levels and heavy rainfall caused inundation of central streets and public areas. (p8)
* The town has been impacted by multiple historical flood events (fluvial and pluvial) and experienced notable impacts during Storm Babet when several streets were among the worst affected. (p9) [PAG]
* Central and low-lying parts of Framlingham experienced surface-water (pluvial) and river (fluvial) flooding. During the October 2023 Storm Babet event, parts of the town (including central areas and locations along the main river channel and tributaries) were among the worst affected — roads and pavements flooded and drainage systems overflowed. The town has a history of pluvial flood incidents since 2012. (p10) [IMG, PAG]
* Central and low-lying parts of Framlingham experienced converging overland pluvial flows from surrounding higher ground and fluvial influence from the Cherry Brook and the River Ore during the event, causing surface water accumulation and fluvial inundation; the valley acted as a storage area retaining excess flood water which discharged slowly into the river and overwhelmed local infrastructure and watercourses. (p12) [IMG, PAG]
* Multiple overland pluvial flow paths from higher ground converged in the town; the valley acted as a storage area during intense rainfall causing excess flood water to be retained and slowly discharge into the river and down the catchment. Infrastructure and watercourses were overwhelmed during the event. Low-lying streets identified as some of the worst affected were Brook Lane, Albert Place, Station Road and Fairfield Road. (p13) [IMG, PAG]
* The town's low-lying position on Lowestoft Formation 'Diamicton' (boulder clay) and surrounding loamy, clayey soils with reduced infiltration led to overland flow and accumulation of surface water during the extreme rainfall (Storm Babet following a wet week). These ground and topographic conditions contributed to surface water/overland flow flooding of the town. (p14) [IMG, PAG]
* Overtopping of the River Ore and the Mere combined with pluvial runoff from surrounding roads and catchment flow paths caused rapid expansion of flood water across north Framlingham. Flood water inundated The Elms car park and flowed through to flood the Elms residential area; River overtopping also flooded residential, commercial properties and Bridge Street. Flood water propagated south and west towards Riverside and Well Close Square. (p15) [PAG]
* Framlingham Mere and the River Ore exceeded capacity and overtopped onto roads. Pluvial flows from multiple pathways added to floodwater and highway drainage assets were overwhelmed. Several locations north of Framlingham reported localised surface-water flooding and overwhelmed highway drainage. (p16) [IMG]
* High rainfall caused excess floodwater from the upper catchment to flow into Framlingham Mere and River Ore; both exceeded capacity and overtopped onto roads in and around Framlingham. Pluvial flows from multiple pathways added to the floodwater and highway drainage assets were overwhelmed beyond their design capacity, contributing to localised road and property flooding. (p17) [PAG]
* Parts of Framlingham (notably West Framlingham and Brook Lane) experienced fluvial overtopping from Cherry Brook combined with rapid surface water runoff from surrounding fields, producing flow routes through the urban area and causing flooding of roads, gardens and properties. (p18) [IMG]
* High rainfall led to Cherry Brook and the River Ore exceeding capacity, causing overtopping and combining with surface-water flows across Framlingham. Multiple roads (including Brook Lane, Station Road and Fairfield Road) and highway drainage assets were overwhelmed, resulting in road and property flooding across the town. (p19) [PAG]
* Central Framlingham acted as a basin into which floodwater collected. Flood flows converged along roads (notably around Albert Place and Station Road), with pluvial inflows from Fore Street, Albert Road and gardens adding to the inundation and highway drainage being overwhelmed. (p20) [IMG]
* River Ore overtopped both eastern and western banks, flooding public open space and commercial buildings west of the river. Surface water and runoff from surrounding fields and roads converged through central corridors producing extensive flood depths and flow routes through the town. (p21) [IMG]
* South Framlingham experienced pluvial flooding from adjacent fields which combined with floodwater on roads. The River Ore overtopped in places, causing external rear flooding to properties. Multiple commercial properties on the north end of Woodbridge Road flooded from the front due to surface water; residential properties on Kettleburgh Road were flooded from pluvial flows and overwhelmed ditches. Assets associated with the sewage works reportedly backed up, with water emerging from manhole covers and causing internal flooding (backed-up toilet) in one property. (p23)
* High rainfall generated large pluvial flows from multiple directions that converged on the central area; flood routes show water concentrating towards the main road and green space, overwhelming drains and causing local flooding (eg. car park and riverside areas shown in images). (p24) [IMG]

* Fairfield Road has recorded historical incidents of blocked and overflowing drains and of flooded roads and pavements. It was also listed among the worst affected locations during Storm Babet (Oct 2023). (p10) [PAG]
* Fairfield Road is listed among the lowest-lying locations and was identified as one of the worst-affected areas following Storm Babet, indicating it experienced surface water accumulation and local inundation. (p12) [PAG]
* Floodwater flowed along the road and could not discharge into the River Ore because river levels were exceedingly high; highway drainage assets were overwhelmed, causing road flooding and contributing to floodwater buildup on Fairfield Road. (p19)
* Culvert capacity was exceeded and flows backed up, causing flood water to flow along Fairfield Road in a southerly direction. The road became described as 'a river' within minutes; extensive flood water accumulated on the road and flooded commercial and residential buildings from the front. Highway drainage was overwhelmed, preventing discharge to the river and creating a large expanse of standing water around properties. (p21) [PAG]

Simultaneously the River Ore overtopped its eastern bank, flooding properties from the rear. Highway drainage was overwhelmed so water could not discharge to the river, creating a large expanse of standing and fast-flowing water around properties. Floodwater velocity damaged front fencing at one property; a brick wall in front of another property was knocked over. The Environment Agency recorded up to 110 cm of internal flooding in one residential property. (p22) [PAG, MUL]

* Floodwaters inundated Fairfield Road and adjacent properties, covering roads and entering residential and commercial buildings; access disrupted and infrastructure affected. (p28) [IMG, PAG]

* Following the storm the Environment Agency met a resident about concerns along Fairfield Road and removed multiple channel/blockage obstructions that had arisen after Storm Babet to reduce local flooding risk. (p31) [TAB]
* {01 Feb 2024 - 29 Feb 2024}: Following a February 2024 meeting between the EA and a resident about flooding concerns, blockages along the watercourse near Fairfield Road were removed to address those concerns. (p32)
* Blockages were observed within the channel along Fairfield Road (including a large willow tree within the channel). Removal actions have been started because these blockages increased flood risk and were linked to the flooding during Storm Babet. (p33) [TAB, PAG]
* Channel blockages and surface water flow affected the road during the event; identified as one of the four worst-affected roads requiring removal of blockages, investigation of highway drainage capacity and potential reconnection of surface water flows back into the river. A large willow in the channel (see Willow) was identified for removal to reduce risk. (p34) [PAG]
* Surface water was flowing on Fairfield Road; investigations proposed to route flows back to the main river and to examine/enlarge culvert connections to the river to reduce local flooding. (p35) [PAG]
* Culverts beneath Fairfield Road that connect local watercourses to the main river were identified as capacity‑constrained and contributing to flooding; investigation actions propose enlarging these culverts and improving highway drainage to better manage surface water flows. (p36)

### River Ore
* Exceeded capacity and overtopped its banks during the event, contributing to the majority of property flooding in Framlingham; river level gauge upstream of the Mere was used to monitor rising levels and a Flood Warning was issued. (p7) [PAG]
* Rapid rises in the River Ore caused the Environment Agency to issue an automatic Flood Warning for Framlingham. The river gauge (located upstream of the Mere) recorded a fast increase in level that produced little forewarning to residents. (p8)
* {1999}: 1999 fluvial flooding from the River Ore caused the Mere to overflow into the present-day area of Tanyard's Court. (p9)
* The River Ore overtopped its banks and the Mere, producing fluvial flows that inundated adjacent areas in Framlingham and fed flood routes into The Elms car park and residential areas. Upstream river levels at the gauge above the Mere reached their highest on record since installation in 2000. (p15)
* The River Ore overtopped between the road bridge and the Mere, causing flooding of Bridge Street (road), and flooding of nearby residential and commercial properties. Blockages (likely woody debris) under bridges were reported and may have contributed to overtopping. (p16)
* The River Ore overtopped and breached its western bank at multiple locations (junction of Albert Place/Fore Street; south of Bridge Street/Riverside; downstream of the B1119 bridge), causing downstream flooding of Riverside, Albert Place and gardens behind properties on Station Road. High river levels submerged drainage outfalls, preventing road drainage and contributing to further inundation. (p19)
* The Ore exceeded capacity and broke its western bank downstream of the B1119 road bridge, overtopping along Riverside and south of Fore Street and flooding rear gardens of properties on Station Road. The river's overtopping combined with road-borne flows to flood properties. (p20)
* The River Ore exceeded its banks: eastern bank overtopping sent fluvial water toward properties along Fairfield Road from the rear, and western bank overtopping flooded public open space and commercial buildings to the west of the river. (p21) [PAG]
* The Ore exceeded its eastern bank, causing fluvial floodwater to flow into the rear gardens of properties on Fairfield Road, and exceeded its western bank, flooding the public open space and commercial buildings west of the river. River high flows combined with overwhelmed highway drainage prevented road water discharging to the channel, creating a large expanse of water around properties. (p22) [PAG]
* The River Ore reached capacity and overtopped its banks. Fluvial floodwater rapidly filled rear gardens, leaving properties surrounded by water on both sides. The Environment Agency measured internal flooding depths of up to 110cm in one residential property. (p23) [PAG]

### Suffolk
* Storm Babet caused significant flooding-related disruption to communities across the county between 18–21 October 2023. (p4) [PAG]
* {11 Oct 2023 - 13 Oct 2023, 18 Oct 2023 - 21 Oct 2023}: October 2023 (including Storm Babet) brought unusually heavy rainfall leading to saturated land, river flows close to or exceeding records, and significant flooding of roads and properties across the county.
