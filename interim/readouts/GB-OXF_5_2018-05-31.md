

# Didcot flooding 31 May 2018

## Place names with coordinates

### Didcot Parkway Station
* The rail station was the subject of a flood incident investigation (document title identifies Didcot Rail Station as the location of the flood incident). (p1)
* {16 Sep 2016, 31 May 2018}: Flooding at Didcot Railway Station on 31 May 2018 led to disruption at this transport interchange and required assistance to commuters and staff; the event prompted a formal investigation. (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* Internal flooding of the station property (the Parkway/Didcot Railway Station building). Surface water flooding affected the front of the station and Station Road; the site is at a local low point and drainage from gullies and surface water sewers (which discharge to a downstream Network Rail ditch) was a focus of the investigation. (p7)
* Flooding affected the main station building, the subway and the station car park; operations at the busy transport interchange were disrupted. Flooding was attributed to surface water sewer exceedance after severe rainfall, with several blockages in pipework (including road cones wedged in the sewer), debris at critical points and a grade 5 sewer defect contributing. Network Rail removed blockages in the forecourt drainage, improved sewer connectivity to under-car-park surface water storage tanks, cleared downstream ditch and repaired the sewer defect. (p9)
* Site was the subject of a flood incident investigation indicating the station was affected by flooding. (p19) [PAG]
* Floodwater entered a building on the station frontage adjacent to Didcot Parkway (map annotation: 'Flooding entered building here'). (p21) [PAG]

### Didcot
* Didcot is named as the location of the flood incident report (the flood incident occurred in Didcot, the town containing the affected rail station). (p1)
* Flooding affected Didcot Railway Station on 31 May 2018, inundating station premises and prompting a formal Section 19 flood investigation by the Lead Local Flood Authority. (p3) [PAG]
* {16 Sep 2016, 31 May 2018}: The flood at The Parkway/Didcot Railway Station on 31 May 2018 impacted the town's rail interchange, disrupting commuters and requiring emergency assistance. (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* The area around Didcot Railway Station experienced drainage system surcharge due to sewer blockages and pipe restrictions (including a blocked sewer near the Cow Lane underpass and debris near surface water storage tanks), contributing to flooding at the station/forecourt and associated underpass drainage problems. (p8) [PAG]
* Flooding at Didcot Railway Station and surrounding area was attributed to surface water sewer exceedance during severe rainfall; blockages in pipework, a grade 5 sewer defect and interceptor maintenance issues were identified as contributing factors. (p10) [PAG]
* Surface water and sewer-related flooding affected Didcot Railway Station and its immediate surroundings: floodwater entered the station entrance and the adjacent car park drainage was compromised. Blocked or inadequate surface water drains, gullies, highway culverts and a sewer syphon were identified as contributing issues and are the focus of recommended inspections and maintenance. (p14) [PAG]
* Didcot (specifically Didcot Railway Station) experienced flooding on 31 May 2018, triggering a multi‑agency investigation and recommendations to address watercourse ownership/maintenance, drainage capacity and planning controls to reduce future flood risk. (p16) [PAG]

### Cow Lane, Ladygrove
* A large blockage was identified in a sewer near the Cow Lane underpass (traffic cones wedged in the pipe) causing severe flow restriction; the underpass drainage sewer was also recorded as having a Grade 5 defect, contributing to surcharge risk. (p8) [PAG]
* Location where Network Rail broke out a sewer to remove traffic cones that had become wedged and were contributing to sewer blockages; an access chamber was installed and the sewer reconnected as part of the flood response. (p9)

### Station Road, Didcot
* Station Road and the area in front of the Parkway rail station are identified as being at high risk of surface water flooding; the road is a local low point and its gullies/channels connect to the surface water sewer network implicated in the incident. (p7)

### Marsh Lane, Didcot
* Floodwater entered a building on the station frontage adjacent to Marsh Court (sic), causing internal water ingress and demonstrating vulnerability of that building to station-area flooding. (p21) [IMG]

### Orchard Centre Car Park
* Trash screen and banks adjacent to the car park had debris cleared by South Oxfordshire District Council and Network Rail as part of the post‑flood clearance operations. (p9)


## Topics without coordinates

### Network Rail
* {16 Sep 2016, 31 May 2018}: An obstructed outfall from Thames Water into Network Rail's ditch contributed to flooding of the drainage system and flooding at Didcot Railway Station; the ditch required maintenance which was identified as a cause in a previous (16 Sept 2016) incident and Network Rail infrastructure was implicated in station flooding. (p6) [MUL]
* Network Rail-owned sewer and ditch assets were affected by blockages and defects that contributed to drainage surcharge. A large blockage (traffic cones wedged in the sewer) was found downstream of Network Rail's ditch near the Cow Lane underpass; debris collected in two pipe sections near surface water storage tanks causing serious flow restrictions and surcharging; a Network Rail sewer draining the underpass had a grade 5 defect requiring repair. Network Rail subsequently broke out the sewer, removed the cones, installed an access chamber and reconnected the sewer. (p8) [PAG]
* Undertook physical response and repairs after the flood: broke out the sewer near Cow Lane to remove lodged traffic cones and installed an access chamber and reconnected the sewer; placed a replacement sewer in an open ditch to reduce ingress of debris/cones; removed blockages in the forecourt drainage system; improved connectivity of sewers to surface water storage tanks under the station car park; cleared a downstream ditch; removed debris from banks and a trash screen adjacent to Orchard Centre Car Park; repaired the identified grade 5 sewer defect. (p9)
* Responded to the Didcot Railway Station flood on 31 May 2018: removed blockages (including road cones) from sewer pipework, cleared debris and silt from the main outfall ditch, updated drainage records and made improvements to reduce the risk of recurrence; committed to ongoing maintenance and review of maintenance schedules. (p10)
* Surface water flooding impacted Network Rail assets at Didcot Railway Station: blocked/compromised surface water drains and floodwater ingress at the station entrance. Network Rail was advised to inspect surface water drains as part of regular maintenance and consider entrance modifications to restrict floodwater access. (p14)

### Thames Water
* {16 Sep 2016}: Thames Water's outfall into Network Rail's ditch was obstructed (maintenance required), which contributed to drainage failure and flooding at Didcot Railway Station (noted in the 16 Sept 2016 incident). (p6)
* A section where Network Rail ditch base levels were raised slightly above the main outfall sewer invert from Thames Water and Network Rail sewers was identified, creating an outfall mismatch and potential restriction that contributed to surcharging of the drainage system. (p8)
* Updated drainage records following the Didcot flooding incident; drainage records were considered as part of the investigation into sewer exceedance causing the flood. (p10)
* Sewer/syphon issues were implicated in the flood incident at Didcot Railway Station. Thames Water was recommended to include maintenance of the syphon in their regular programme and inspect sewer pipework at regular intervals to reduce sewer-related flooding risk. (p14)

### Oxfordshire County Council
* {31 May 2018, 01 Jun 2018 - 30 Jun 2018}: Established a flood investigation working group in June 2018 (with South Oxfordshire District Council, Network Rail, Great Western Railway, Thames Water, Thames Flood Advisors and the Environment Agency) to investigate the 31 May 2018 station flood. (p6) [MUL]
* Cleared a downstream ditch and programmed further downstream clearance work as part of the post‑flood response. (p9)
* As the Lead Local Flood Authority, investigated the incident and arranged to clear silt from the watercourse further downstream; agreed ongoing maintenance and to review/update maintenance schedules and asset records. (p10) [PAG]
* Following the flooding at Didcot Railway Station, the county council (as LLFA) was advised to review drainage plans and consider adding a section of ditch and culvert to the critical drainage register to manage surface water flood risk. (p14)

### South Oxfordshire District Council
* South Oxfordshire District Council assisted in the investigation: supplied historical sewer records, surveyed downstream ditches (assessing silt levels and potential flow restrictions) and inspected downstream manhole chambers as part of the response to the flood incident. (p8)
* Took part in inspection of the surface water drainage system and cleared debris from the banks of the watercourse and from the trash screen adjacent to the Orchard Centre Car Park as part of the flood response. (p9)
* Flooding affected the district council's adjacent car park drainage at Didcot Railway Station; the council was advised to ensure the car park drainage systems are cleansed and functioning to prevent further flooding. (p14)

### Great Western Railway
* Flooding at The Parkway (Didcot Railway Station) on 31 May 2018 caused disruption at the transport interchange affecting commuters and staff; emergency crews (Oxfordshire Fire and Rescue Service) attended and GWR were engaged in the investigation (GWR were unable to provide photographs of the event). (p6)
* Flooding at Didcot Railway Station affected Great Western Railway assets and drainage controls (flow control, foul water backflow valve and drainage interceptor). GWR was advised to include maintenance of these items and inspect surface water drains within their remit to prevent recurrence. (p14)

### Oxfordshire Fire and Rescue Service
* Attended Didcot Railway Station during the flood on 31 May 2018 and assisted commuters and station staff as part of the immediate response. (p6)

### Highway Authority
* Blocked or insufficiently cleared highway gullies and downstream highway culverts contributed to surface water flooding at a critical location near Didcot Railway Station, affecting drainage and potentially access. The Highway Authority was advised to review gully emptying frequency and monitor downstream ditches and culverts. (p14)
