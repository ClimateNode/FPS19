

# Rossendale flooding 01 Dec 2015 - 31 Dec 2015

## Place names with coordinates

### River Irwell
* Although the River Irwell did not breach its banks at this location on 26 December 2015, the culvert at Rockliffe Road restricted river flow and put pressure on the drainage network, contributing to a manhole being lifted and subsequent property flooding. (p12)
* The River Irwell overtopped its banks and was identified as the primary source of flooding at Hareholme (MSFW131), causing internal flooding to residential properties. (p15)
* Backed up at Lumb Bridge, came out of channel and flooded the main village and Meadow Park; contributed to pump inundation and property flooding. (p17) [PAG]
* High flows in the River Irwell on 26 December 2015 contributed to multiple flooding mechanisms: backing up a surface water drain that caused Lambert's Mill to flood; undermining the foundations of New Hall Hey Business Park which contributed to a channel and culvert blockage and a subsequent river breach; floodwaters then inundated the adjacent cricket pitch and flowed east and west, causing properties to flood at cellar and ground levels. (p18)
* Became blocked by unconsented gabion defences that collapsed into the channel and breached through a scoured wall at a cricket pitch, causing inundation and overland flow into nearby streets and parkland. High levels and overtopping also caused inundation of Recreation Gardens, exceeded bunds and led to overtopping near North Street in Strongstry and Chatterton. The river undermined the central pier of Bridgeway Bridge, causing its collapse. (p20) [PAG]
* High river levels overtopped banks (left bank into Recreation Gardens and later the right bank), and undermined the central pier of Bridgeway Bridge leading to its collapse. (p21)
* Cellar flooding along Bacup Road (over footbridges of the River Irwell) was believed to be caused by a groundwater issue associated with proximity to the River Irwell. (p23)

* Quant: 16 properties; 24 properties (p20) [PAG]

### Lancashire
* Significant flooding occurred across Lancashire in December 2015; the event exposed shortcomings in communication to affected communities and led to the development of district-level reports and follow-up actions by risk management authorities. (p3) [PAG]
* Lancashire experienced major flooding during December 2015 (the highest recorded river flows and the wettest December), resulting in widespread impacts on people, homes and businesses across the county. (p8) [TAB]
* {December 2015, 01 Dec 2015 - 31 Dec 2015}: Extreme storms and rainfall in November and December 2015 caused widespread flooding across Lancashire during December 2015, damaging private gardens, garages and out-buildings, business premises, agricultural property and critical public infrastructure including roads, bridges and retaining walls, water treatment plants, power and communications installations, and community buildings such as schools, village and town halls. (p9)
* Communities across the county experienced flooding as a result of two major storm events in December 2015. The extent and geographic distribution of flooding led to a countywide investigation to identify affected communities and to track and prioritise remedial actions via stage two reports. (p10) [PAG]
* Quant: over 2,500 properties flooded (p8) [TAB]
* {December 2015, 01 Dec 2015 - 31 Dec 2015}: Quant: over 2,500 properties (p9)

### Rossendale
* The borough was affected as part of the county-wide December 2015 floods; recovery support activities were held in Rossendale (Irwell Vale drop-in on 10 Feb 2016) for residents impacted by the floods. (p9)
* In the Bacup area (within Rossendale) on 26 December 2015, drains along Burnley Road became overwhelmed and blocked, causing surface water to enter properties. Surface water flowed down Burnley Road and ponded at a low spot on Yorkshire Street. A culvert at Rockliffe Road restricted river flow and put pressure on the drainage network; a manhole cover was lifted under the pressure, leading to internal flooding of residential and commercial properties along Rockliffe Road, Market Street and River Street. Vehicle bow waves on the highway also forced water into houses. (p11) [TAB]
* Quant: 17 properties (p11) [TAB]

### Irwell Vale
* Community experienced flooding during December 2015; a multi-agency drop-in event was held in Irwell Vale on 10 Feb 2016 to provide flood recovery support, gather eyewitness reports and advise affected residents. (p9)
* Village flooded when both the River Ogden and the River Irwell came out of channel; floodwater flowed into the main village and Meadow Park causing internal flooding to properties. Over-pumping was used to remove surface water but pumps were later inundated by river peak flows. (p17)
* Quant: 62 properties (internal flooding) (p17)

### St Michael's-On-Wyre

* Identified as a critical area affected by the December 2015 floods where the Environment Agency carried out pumping to manage floodwater. (p9)

### Shawforth
* River Spodden overtopped its banks and caused internal flooding to properties in Shawforth. Nine properties are recorded as having suffered internal flooding on 26 December 2015; at least one property on Market Street experienced ground-floor flooding due to a bow wave from vehicles driving through deep water in the highway. (p19)
* Quant: 9 properties; 1 property on Market Street (p19)

### Stacksteads
* Internal flooding to residential and commercial properties across the community. 16 properties suffered internal flooding on 26 December 2015. Floodwaters from a River Irwell blockage and breach inundated a cricket pitch, flowed across Brandwood Road and Waterbarn Lane and onto Brandwood Park. Several properties experienced cellar flooding. Groundwater and surface water flooded businesses in a mill on Blackwood Road and entered cellars of properties around Railway Street, flooding the ground floor of a commercial property. (p20)
* Quant: 16 properties (p20)

### Strongstry

### Lancashire County Council
* Following significant flooding in December 2015 the Lead Local Flood Authority (Lancashire County Council) coordinated local flood risk activities and developed district-level reports to provide affected communities with information on risk management actions, to identify opportunities for further investigation and investment, and to encourage property-level flood resilience measures. Reports are compiled and published by the LLFA based on data from risk management authorities and updated quarterly. (p3)
* As the Lead Local Flood Authority, Lancashire County Council recorded details of the December 2015 flood event, established a flood timeline, concluded relevant RMAs exercised their functions, and improved communications and multi-agency response arrangements following the floods. (p9)
* Experienced and responded to widespread flooding during the two major storm events in December 2015. Improved its communications around flood events; led and commissioned a countywide Section 19 flood investigation, recorded and verified significant flood data, published investigation findings and appendices, and is implementing follow-on (stage two) reports and actions to address identified issues and adapt multi-agency response. (p10) [PAG]
* {01 Dec 2015 - 31 Dec 2015, December 2015}: On 26 December 2015 surface water flooding overwhelmed drains on Burnley Road in Bacup, allowing water to enter several properties. Ponding occurred in the low spot on Yorkshire Street. Vehicle bow waves contributed to water entering houses. In Cowpe, one property suffered internal flooding on 26 December 2015. The Lead Local Flood Authority (LCC as LLFA) reviewed impacts and discussed further investigations and actions following the event. (p12) [TAB]
