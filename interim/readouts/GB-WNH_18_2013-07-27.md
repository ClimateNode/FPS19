

# Hollowell flooding 27 Jul 2013

## Place names with coordinates

### Hollowell
* Intense short-duration rainfall caused existing drainage to be unable to cope; excess surface water flowed along natural contours into low points in the village, resulting in internal flooding of properties. (p6) [PAG]
* Intense short-duration rainfall exceeded drainage capacity causing excess surface water to flow overland to low points in the village, resulting in internal flooding of properties. (p7) [PAG, MUL]
* Intense rainfall on 27 July 2013 exceeded drainage capacity causing flooding. Internal flooding of residential properties occurred; at least two properties experienced internal flooding and at least two other properties were at high risk and residents manually prevented ingress. (p9)
* An intense rainfall event on the evening of 27/07/13 led to various incidents of flooding that affected people and property in the locality. (p11)
* Flood incidents occurred in the village associated with surface runoff, groundwater and/or ordinary (non‑Main River) watercourses; NCC can investigate, suggest causes and make recommendations to mitigate risk but cannot compel or fund all actions. (p14)
* Intense short-duration rainfall produced surface water flooding across the village. Drainage systems were unable to cope, causing surface water run‑off from private areas to flow onto the public highway and vice versa; surface water from higher parts of the village reached the lowest points (up to 700 metres). Physical barriers (kerbs) were compromised and ponding and high velocity flows occurred at low points, causing overtopping towards properties. (p18)
* {general}: Surface water catchments across the village do not follow historic natural contours; surface water from different catchments is directed to a single watercourse discharge location, increasing risk of surface water flooding and concentration of runoff at one discharge point. (p19)
* Anglian Water Services is to assess sources of water entering the public sewerage system; excess surface water or inappropriate inflows to the sewer network are identified as contributing to flood risk (risk of sewer surcharge/flooding), so assessment and reduction of these inflows is recommended. (p20)

* Quant: internal flooding of at least two properties; more than one occasion in the last 5 years (p7) [PAG, MUL]
* Quant: at least two properties; at least two other properties (p9)

### Creaton Road, Hollowell
* {general}: Run-off from the South catchment flows east to Creaton Road. A surface water public sewer exists at the lowest area of Creaton Road that begins on Creaton Road, flows to the rear of properties and discharges to Stowe Brook; the sewer collects discharge from properties and road gullies. (p10)
* Internal flooding to properties on Creaton Road; ponding in the highway of Creaton Road; on 27/07/13 surface water was manually prevented from entering two further properties on Creaton Road. (p11) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* Surface water from higher ground settled as a large area of ponding at low points on Creaton Road, spanning the entire carriageway and unable to drain sufficiently to Stowe Brook; road gullies surcharged and ponded water entered properties (including flowing through doorways and air bricks). Residents undertook actions (sweeping water to gullies, re‑excavating a verge grip drain) to allow partial drainage to Stowe Brook. (p12)
* Drainage systems became fully surcharged and water began to flow out of road gullies and manhole chambers. The area of Creaton Road immediately west of Stowe Brook formed a large, deep pond that was unable to drain down sufficiently into road gullies and drainage systems. (p13)
* {06 Sep 2013}: Surface water overtopped the kerbs on Creaton Road, producing flooding/ponding on the road/verge area. Residents re-excavated a verge grip drain during the flood incident. (p39) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface water overtopped the kerbs and caused local ponding/flooding on the carriageway and verges. (p40) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 2 properties (internal flooding) on 06/06/10; 2 properties (internal flooding) on 27/07/13; 2 properties (surface water prevented) on 27/07/13 (p11) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* Quant: 1 property had surface water flow at over 300mm to low points and internal ground floor flooding to a depth of 25mm; kitchen of 1 second property was flooded; residents of 2 further properties took action to prevent ingress. (p12)

### Guilsborough Hill, Hollowell
* Runoff from the North catchment is allowed to run off onto the public highway of Guilsborough Hill and into the road gullies; during the intense rainfall event of 27 July 2013 the village drainage capacity was exceeded and runoff flowed onto the highway. (p9)
* A heavy flow of surface water came from higher areas along the carriageway and footway, flowing off farmland and private driveways onto the public highway and contributing to downstream ponding and flow around The Green onto Creaton Road and into properties. (p12)
* Carriageway and road gullies on Guilsborough Hill were heavily silted by runoff, indicating deposition of sediment during the flood event and likely impairment of drainage capacity contributing to localised flooding. (p41) [IMG]
* Surface water run-off from adjacent sloping fields flows down a worn channel onto the road (Guilsborough Hill), creating a surface-water flooding hazard and potential water accumulation on the road during heavy rainfall. (p43) [IMG]

### Church Hill, Hollowell
* {general}: Run-off from the South catchment flows onto the public highway of Church Hill; Church Hill is a source/collector of surface water from adjacent residential areas. (p10)
* Heavy surface water flowed along the carriageway and footway from higher areas, contributing to downstream surface water routing around The Green and onto Creaton Road and into properties. (p12)
* {general}: Surface water runoff from adjacent fields flows onto the carriageway; the carriageway is designed/positioned to accept this runoff, causing localised inundation of the road surface and contributing to road wear and edge erosion. (p44) [IMG]

### The Green, Hollowell
* Sewage overtopped a manhole cover at The Green (reported as part of the 27/07/13 incident). (p11) [TAB]
* Surface water flowed around The Green and onto Creaton Road; sewage flowed out of a manhole cover at The Green. (p12)
* Foul water effluent flowed from a manhole on The Green during the flood incident; visible staining/residue around the manhole indicates overflow of wastewater onto the carriageway. (p41)

### Orchard Close, Hollowell
* The local watercourse sinks into a culvert at the rear of Orchard Close; this culvert conveyed runoff during the intense rainfall event of 27 July 2013 when drainage capacity was exceeded and was part of the flow route associated with the flooding. (p9)

### Home Farm Yard, Hollowell
* The culvert flows underground to Home Farm Yard and continues east as open watercourse and culvert sections to Stowe Brook; during the intense rainfall on 27 July 2013 this route conveyed runoff when drainage was exceeded and was part of the flooding pathway. (p9)


## Topics without coordinates

### Northamptonshire County Council
* Highways assets are affected by surface water run-off from private properties (run-off reaching the public highway). NCC Highways are instructed to undertake regular drainage cleansing, assess asset capacity, review gully positions and kerb/camber levels to prevent highway run-off reaching private properties and to consider improvement works where asset capacity is insufficient. (p20) [PAG]
* {general}: Highway drainage issues and insufficient highway drainage capacity in areas are contributing to flood risk to properties; NCC Highways is asked to undertake regular highway drainage cleansing, assess asset capacity, review gully positions and kerb/camber levels and develop an asset plan to share with the LLFA and community to reduce surface water runoff from highways to private properties. (p21) [PAG]

### Daventry District Council
* {general}: Daventry District Council is involved in managing flood risk via planning consultations with the Environment Agency and by using enforcement powers under Section 25 of the Land Drainage Act 1991 where riparian owners fail to maintain watercourses — addressing poorly maintained watercourses that contribute to surface water flooding. (p21)
