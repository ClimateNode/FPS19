## Place names with coordinates

### Earby
* Earby experienced the most significant impacts. Earby Beck burst its banks, flooding the road and leaving silt and debris; blocked gullies and drains led to residents' properties being inundated; roads and footways were inundated and police closed roads. (p3)
* Intense rainfall caused Earby Beck to burst its banks, producing significant flash flooding in the town. Roads were closed by police. Firefighters rescued people stranded in a restaurant. Local highways crews attended to surcharging gullies, conducted jetting and cleaned roads to enable reopening. (p12) [PAG]
* Urban flash-flooding occurred in Earby village with gullies surcharging; response crews cleared gullies and jetting/clean-up was required to make the road passable. Firefighters were mobilised to evacuate members of the public and to clear floodwater from affected areas. (p13)
* Reported flooding affected homes and public highways in the locality during the event; the LLFA intends to contact Earby to establish a more complete picture of extent and impact. (p14)
* Quant: up to 150mm (6 inches) of water (inundation reported in properties) (p3)
* Quant: 9 properties; 10 people (p12) [PAG]

### Lancashire
* Heavy overnight rainfall on the night of 13 September 2016 caused surface water flooding across the county; local urban drainage networks were overwhelmed and did not have capacity to accommodate the run-off, resulting in widespread flooding of homes, roads and footways. (p3) [PAG]
* Across Lancashire 42 properties were reported flooded as a result of the intense rainfall on 13 September 2016; flooding was reported across the listed districts (Burnley, Chorley, Pendle, Rossendale, South Ribble, Wyre). (p12)
* The 13 September 2016 rainfall event overwhelmed natural and man-made drainage networks across locations in Lancashire, producing particularly damaging flooding in parts of the county (notably Pendle and Lancaster districts). (p14) [PAG]
* The extreme rainfall event of 13 September 2016 overwhelmed natural and man-made drainage networks across locations in Lancashire, producing surface water/flash flooding and particularly damaging flooding in some districts (notably Pendle and Lancaster). Reported effects of the intense rainfall included traffic driving through standing water producing bow waves that washed onto private property, vehicles abandoned in flooded roads impeding access, and water ingress at low‑lying properties. (p15)
* Quant: 33 properties flooded; 11 homes internally flooded (p3) [PAG]
* Quant: 42 properties (p12)

## Topics without coordinates

### Lancashire County Council
* Lancashire County Council Highways deployed resources (gully wagon and crew) to Earby where gullies were surcharging; crews ensured gullies were free-flowing, conducted jetting and hose-down of residual silt to assist remedial clean-up so the road could be reopened. (p13)

### Highways
* Highways crew sent a gully wagon to Earby where gullies were surcharging; they jetted gullies and hosed down residual silt until ~0230hrs to clear drainage and assist road reopening. (p13)

### Salterforth IDB
* The LLFA will contact the Salterforth IDB to establish a more complete picture of the extent and impact of flooding from the 13 September 2016 event, indicating the IDB's area was implicated in flooding that affected properties and highways. (p14)
