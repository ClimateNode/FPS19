

# Bournemouth flooding 18 Aug 2011

## Place names with coordinates

### Bournemouth
* Heavy surface-water flooding inundated urban streets and a public park, with water covering road surfaces and reaching up to the top of a park fence. Traffic was disrupted with buses and other vehicles driving through deep water; street furniture and shopfront frontages were partially submerged and debris was floating in the floodwater. (p1)
* Intense convective rainfall on 18 August 2011 caused widespread surface water/urban flooding across the borough: significant property damage in localised areas, considerable disruption to the local transport system, and some residents were unable to return to their properties for at least six months while homes dried out. A major clean-up operation was required after the event. (p3) [PAG]
* A significant, highly localised storm centred on the Town Centre produced intense rainfall (epicentre moved N/NE over Winton and Charminster) that caused surface water/urban flooding across Bournemouth; the borough was affected more severely than neighbouring Poole and Weymouth. (p5)
* Widespread heavy rain produced surface-water/urban flooding across Bournemouth: roads were gridlocked or impassable (e.g. Wallisdown Road A3049 between Boundary Road and the University; Castle Lane East and West; access to Bournemouth Hospital via the Cooper Dean Roundabout), and emergency services and the council received very large numbers of flood-related calls. (p12) [PAG]
* Rapid-onset surface water/urban flooding across the town: major roads (e.g. Castle Lane A3060 east and west, Wessex Way A338, Pier Approach Flyover) were closed, impassable or gridlocked (access to/from Bournemouth Hospital via Cooper Dean Roundabout affected). The sewerage system was overwhelmed causing manholes to surcharge foul sewage, manhole covers to be blown off, and road surfaces to lift; highway gullies blocked with debris. A small retaining wall collapsed in Exeter Road. Flooding to properties occurred within about 15 minutes of the heavy rainfall and subsided quickly. (p13) [PAG]
* Very localised but intense rainfall on 18th August 2011 caused significant local surface water flooding across Bournemouth; the town's urban nature and topography increased its vulnerability to this type of flooding. (p17)
* Multiple urban areas within Bournemouth were flooded during the storm; the council identified five significant localised areas for further study and assessed its own properties to identify high-risk properties for potential protection measures. (p18)
* The coastal fringe shows clusters of external flood points, indicating the coastline and immediate coastal urban area experienced flooding on 18th August 2011. (p26) [IMG]
* Widespread flooding on 18th August 2011 concentrated in low-lying coastal and estuarine areas and along river valleys. Flooded locations clustered near the coast and river mouths, with events scattered across multiple administrative areas. (p27) [IMG]
* The intense, short-lived storm on 18 August 2011 caused widespread surface water/urban flooding: lower gardens, Boscombe Chine (emergency access for the Air Show) and other areas were affected; roads were closed (including Copper Dean Roundabout); contaminated public open spaces required fencing off; large clean-up effort enabled the Air Show to open the next day; Ask Bournemouth received nearly 1,000 calls in a short period. (p31)
* On 18 August 2011 intense surface-water/urban runoff flooding caused internal flooding of properties across Bournemouth. Council records cited about 250 properties flooded internally during the event; localised flooding also led to road closures (for example Castle Lane West), restricting access to the hospital. (p37) [TAB]
* On 18 August 2011 the area experienced localised surface-water/urban flooding that caused internal flooding of domestic properties and disrupted access (Castle Lane West was closed restricting access to the hospital). Local response and cleanup were required. (p38)

### Bournemouth Borough Council
* As the local authority, BBC recorded and responded to the severe flooding on 18 August 2011: collecting reports of incidents, coordinating and undertaking a major clean-up operation into the night and early morning with partners, and establishing a Task and Finish Group to review the event and plan future actions. (p3) [PAG]
* Council employees and partner agencies manned pumps and carried out rapid clean-up operations across beaches, the Air Festival site and large areas of the town; the Council convened a Task and Finish Group to investigate the event and recommend improvements. (p4)
* Council staff were on site in the severely affected Air Festival area; the council received a very large number of flood-related calls and logged over 80 incidents and was extremely stretched in recording and responding to calls during the event. (p12)
* Widespread surface water and sewer flooding across the town required the Council to follow up reported incidents and carry out site investigations. Flooding involved sewage contamination, surcharging manholes (some covers blown off or road surfaces lifted), blocked highway gullies from debris, and localised structural damage (small retaining wall collapse in Exeter Road). There was significant short-term transport disruption with multiple roads impassable or closed. (p14)
* The Council led and coordinated the immediate response and clean-up on 18th August 2011, mobilising staff and management in the affected areas, managing communications (with some difficulties), and deciding not to escalate to a major incident; it also identified the need for longer-term flood resilience work. (p17)
* The 18 August 2011 extreme event (approaching a 1-in-100-year event) produced significant surface-water/urban flooding that overwhelmed the piped drainage/sewer system. This prompted the Council to work with Wessex Water to install sewer monitors (to observe system response), update sewer models for Bournemouth, combine those models with surface water modelling, and to develop highly localised solutions including a temporary flood storage solution in Kinson using parkland to store water from surface water sewers and release it slowly back to the sewer. (p19)
* Coordinated response to flooding on 18 August 2011: provided advice to residents affected by flooding; worked jointly with Wessex Water on a mitigation project at the Kinson flood ‘hot spot’ (using parkland to temporarily store surface water sewer outflows and return it slowly to the sewer); initiated work on five areas described as severely affected; and recommended improvements to communications and information collection for future flood events. (p20)
* The local Council team responded to the 18 August 2011 flash flooding, convened a Task and Finish Group to review what happened, improve reporting/communication and coordination with neighbouring councils and partner organisations, and are pursuing their legal obligation to investigate significant flooding. (p29) [PAG]
* Council staff were mobilised onto clean-up duties; almost all available operatives were switched to recovery work after intense rainfall. Council services dealt with sewage-contaminated public grassed areas (fenced off for four days on Environmental Health advice), managed road closures and access issues (including emergency access), and handled insurance/repair arrangements for council-owned housing and damage to covers/tarmac. The Council's Ask Bournemouth contact team was overwhelmed by a surge of flood-related calls and advised members of the public to contact the sewerage company for sewage flooding reports. (p30) [TAB, PAG]
* Council resources and staff led and carried out large-scale clean-up after extreme rainfall and surface-water/sewer flooding; council-owned land drainage and maintenance were implicated in run-off causing local flooding; roads were closed by police (including Copper Dean Roundabout) affecting emergency vehicle access to the hospital for a short period; Environmental Health advised fencing off sewage-contaminated grassed areas. (p31) [PAG]
* The Ask Bournemouth contact centre was temporarily overwhelmed by flood-related calls; staff advised people reporting sewage flooding to contact Wessex Water directly, raising concerns about the adequacy of advice given. (p32) [PAG]
* Council operatives worked alongside Wessex Water on clean-up and prioritisation of hot-spots (including Castle Lane East). Flooding in Castle Lane East restricted access to the hospital and required response actions and liaison on site. Council staff also used and maintained a hot-spot database and surface water modelling to identify high-risk areas following the event. (p33) [TAB]

### Wessex Water
* Participated as a partner in the major clean-up operation following the flooding, working into the night and early morning to support recovery and enable event continuity. (p3)
* Named as a key partner that 'literally manned the pumps' during the immediate response and helped with rapid clean-up of the Air Festival, beaches and large areas of town following the storm. (p4)
* During the 18th August 2011 event Wessex Water handled large numbers of public enquiries about drains and flooding. Their call centre used detailed questionnaires but, because of call volumes and limited information from callers, staff were frequently unable to record accurate locations of flooding; this hindered subsequent investigation and inclusion of affected properties on formal registers (DG5). (p15)
* Sewer network was identified as a key failure mechanism during the extreme 18 Aug 2011 surface-water event; Wessex Water has been commissioned to update sewer models and install monitors to observe sewer response to intense rainfall so as to verify model predictions and support mitigation. (p19) [PAG]
* Acknowledged for providing an excellent response during and after the 18 August 2011 flash flooding, working alongside the Council team in the operational response. (p29)
* Wessex Water staff assisted the council with the cleanup and response after the storm on 18th August 2011. Combined/foul sewers surcharged in the lower gardens, contributing to heavily contaminated surface runoff; Wessex Water were commended for their response and identified as a party to engage on reducing local flood 'hot spots'. (p30) [TAB, PAG]
* Wessex Water staff assisted the clean-up and response. Combined/foul sewers surcharged in the lower gardens during the storm, causing sewage contamination of grassed areas which were fenced off and advised to remain out of bounds. (p31)
* Received a high rate of emergency calls on 18 August 2011 (first call ~09:45). Mobilised operations staff and contractors to assist clean-up and emergency response across the town, concentrating on identified hot-spots (including Lower Gardens and Boscombe seafront). Worked on the ground with Bournemouth Borough Council operatives. Incurred extra contractor costs met by Wessex Water. Confirmed sewer system capacity was exceeded by the event (return periods well in excess of design standard), limiting what could be done to prevent some highway and internal flooding; were unable to provide significant physical assistance at Castle Lane East where flooding restricted access to the hospital. Explained customer remedies for sewer-related internal flooding (rebates) and described obligations and modelling/AMP-led investment and planned schemes for future mitigation (e.g. Priestly Road works, Queens Park pumping station, intended future schemes in Wood Lane, Kinson Road, Exeter Road/Lower Gardens and Meon Road). (p33) [TAB]
* Mobilised operations staff and contractors to respond to flooding on 18 August 2011; concentrated cleanup on identified hot‑spots (including Lower Gardens and Boscombe seafront); sewer/drainage network was exceeded during the event so WW could only deal with flows entering their pipe system and could not prevent road flooding; WW met the extra contractor costs and worked closely with Bournemouth Borough Council operatives during cleanup. (p34) [PAG]
* Responded to flooding caused by the extreme rainfall on 18 August 2011. Funding and works targeted at resolving internal sewer flooding (AMP allocation for the WW region), completed a small pumping station in Queens Park, and carried out works in Priestly Road (approx £1 million). Policy: internal flooding from the sewerage system entitles affected properties to a 100PCSYM rebate of sewerage charges (50PCSYM rebate if external flooding); flood-resolution funding prioritises internal flooding and requires incidents to be recorded on the DG5 register to trigger works. (p35) [PAG]
