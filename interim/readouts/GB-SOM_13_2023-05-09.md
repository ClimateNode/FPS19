

# Yeovilton flooding 09 May 2023

## Place names with coordinates

### Podimore
* Severe flooding on 9 May 2023 affecting the locality: drains filled rapidly and inadequate attenuation exacerbated impacts; roads became impassable; properties were partially submerged and homes rendered unliveable for an extended period; vehicles were written off and some residents required temporary relocation. Part of the total of 29 reported properties affected in the event were in this locality. (p5)
* On 9 May 2023, 24 residential properties in Podimore were internally flooded. Flooding followed heavy rainfall and a combination of pluvial and fluvial processes investigated in the report. (p7) [TAB]
* On 2023-05-09 water began flowing into the village from fields east of Church Street (around midnight), carrying waste material from corn/maize fields. The most severe flooding occurred between 03:00 and 04:00 on 2023-05-09, with water entering properties, following road and open-space pathways, and converging southeast through the village. (p31) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* Overnight runoff from adjacent maize fields and overflow from the road and surrounding fields led to rapid inundation beginning around midnight and peaking between 03:00 and 04:00 on 2023-05-09. Water entered houses around Lower Farm Meadows at both front and rear, encircling properties. Several properties were partially submerged to depths of around 3 feet, with some remaining inundated for days. Vehicular movement through the village was severely restricted; numerous vehicles were written off. Many residents (including elderly people) were temporarily displaced during home renovations. Floodwater carried waste material from corn fields and bow waves from vehicles worsened property flooding. (p32) [IMG, MUL]
* Experienced a sudden rush of water passing down the catchment on 9 May; standing water was present on 10 May such that officers could not visit all houses. Community reported feeling abandoned by responders; local actions and a point of contact were established following the visit. (p33)
* Exceptional rainfall on 2023-05-09 overwhelmed drainage and attenuation, inundating properties in Podimore. Drains filled rapidly, roads became impassable, homes were rendered unliveable for an extended period, properties were partially submerged, some vehicles were written off, and residents required temporary relocation. (p38)
* Experienced unusually severe internal property flooding on 9 May 2023 at levels not previously recorded. After the event residents reported the village has flooded more frequently since May 2023, possibly due to increased silt in drains and changes to A303 runoff; roads and properties required clean-up following the flood; a reservoir at the base of the A303 bridge filled and flowed back toward a T-junction. (p39) [MUL]
* Blocked/choked drainage (reported as the ditch/drainage from the A303) contributed to surface water flooding and impeded drainage of runoff. (p41) [MUL]
* 24 properties reported flooded in Podimore (24 of 29 properties reported flooded across the three communities). (p42)

### Somerset
* The heavy rainfall event on 2023-05-09 affected communities in the county, including the parish of Yeovilton where 29 properties were reported internally flooded (24 in Podimore and 5 in Bridgehampton). Flooding comprised a combination of pluvial (surface water) and fluvial (river) flooding across parts of the county in this locality. (p7) [IMG]
* On 2023-05-09 a rapid wave of water moved down the River Cam catchment (Cam Valley), sweeping through Yarlington, North Cadbury, South Cadbury, Blackford, Sparkford, Queen Camel, West Camel, Bridgehampton and Podimore. Several properties in the affected villages were partially submerged to at least 3 feet, some remained inundated for days. Numerous vehicles were written off and many residents (including elderly) had to temporarily relocate during home repairs. Church Farm flooded for the first time in 150 years; residents described a sudden 'gush' or 'tidal wave' of water passing down the catchment. Shepton Montague (Cattle Hill) recorded a sudden surge described as a 'tsunami.' (p33)
* Widespread heavy rainfall across Somerset on 9 May 2023 stretched Risk Management Authorities county-wide, resulting in multiple major incidents and thinly spread response resources. (p34) [PAG]
* The investigation area within Somerset experienced flooding on 9 May 2023. A combination of river flooding and surface-water flooding led to 29 properties reported flooded across the three communities in the report, with 24 of those properties in Podimore. (p41) [IMG, MUL]

## Topics without coordinates

### A303
* Map annotations show flood-impacted areas distributed along and near the A303 in the Yeovilton area on 2023-05-09, indicating localized surface-water and fluvial flooding adjacent to the road corridor in places (exact effects on carriageway or closures are not specified). (p7) [IMG]
* Drainage from the A303 was reported as a contributing factor directing water toward Podimore during the 2023-05-09 event; community concerns about the A303 drainage system prompted requests for design, attenuation and maintenance information to assess its role in the flooding. (p32)
* Drainage associated with the A303 was overwhelmed during the 2023-05-09 heavy rainfall, contributing to road drainage failures and residents' concerns. Surface water flooding led to access issues and sections of road becoming impassable, exacerbating impacts to nearby properties. (p38) [PAG]
* Roadside drainage changes associated with A303 development reduced the number of drainage points (from five to three), concentrating runoff. Residents report runoff from the A303/its attenuation pond entering local drains and ditches, contributing to more frequent village flooding since May 2023; a reservoir at the base of an A303 bridge was observed to fill and backflow toward a nearby T-junction. (p39) [PAG]
* The ditch and drainage system from the A303 were reported as blocked and severely choked; this lack of maintenance contributed to surface water/runoff problems that exacerbated local flooding. (p41)

### Somerset County Council
* Somerset Council officers visited Podimore on 2023-05-10 in direct response to the flooding; they could not access all houses because of standing water, set up a local point of contact and compiled a list of actions to support residents affected by the 9 May flooding. (p33)
* Widespread heavy rainfall across Somerset on 9 May 2023 spread Risk Management Authorities thinly; the recent unification of district and county councils left emergency procedures undefined, reducing the council's capacity to coordinate response. Intelligence gathering was ad-hoc, and public under-reporting meant it took several days for a full picture of the incident to emerge. (p34) [MUL]
* Had previously identified ditch-clearance works at Podimore which were underway; the May 2023 weather event led to the clearing of an additional 800 metres of ditch as part of the response to flooding. (p39)

### Cam Valley
* A rapid flood wave travelled down the Cam Valley on 9 May 2023, producing sudden surges ('gushes'/'tidal wave') that passed through downstream communities and contributed to widespread inundation and access problems. (p33) [PAG]
* Major flood incidents were raised early in the day in some areas of the Cam Valley on 9 May 2023, requiring Risk Management Authorities to prioritise resources toward those areas and contributing to a stretched county-wide response. (p34)

### Urgashay Farm
* On 2023-05-09 water flowed from Urgashay in a north-to-south direction and the river overflowed from a bend, sending flow east–west toward Bridgehampton; ditches were reported full of rainwater, contributing to downstream overtopping of attenuation features. (p30)

### Lower Farm Meadows
* Houses around Lower Farm Meadows experienced water entering both the front and back of properties and were encircled by floodwater; flooding was most severe between 03:00 and 04:00 on 2023-05-09. The floodwater was reported to have come from adjacent roads and overflowing surrounding fields. (p31)

### Environment Agency
* Residents reported that the Environment Agency's response to the flooding on 9 May was slow; the EA was one of the Risk Management Authorities involved in the event and was criticised by the community for the perceived pace of its response during the Podimore visit on 10 May. (p33)

### District
