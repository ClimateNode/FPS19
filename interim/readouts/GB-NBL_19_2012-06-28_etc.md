

# Halton Lea Gate flooding 28 Jun 2012 and Halton Lea Gate flooding 04 Aug 2012

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Halton Lea Gate<br>091\_Northumberland/Halton-Lea-Gate-FIR-v2.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Halton Lea Gate
* {28 Jun 2012, 04 Aug 2012}: Flooding concentrated in two clusters along the main road through the village. Residential properties experienced internal/external flooding and three roads through the village were affected. Surface water flow from higher ground converged toward the central village area. No businesses, other buildings or critical infrastructure were reported as impacted. (p1) [IMG, PAG]
* {28 Jun 2012, 04 Aug 2012}: On 28 June flooding affected two main areas of the village. In the Leaside area the inlet to a culvert under the A689 became blocked, causing the ordinary watercourse to rise, go out of bank and flow through gardens and onto the road; surface water runoff from fields at the rear of northern properties of Leaside also flooded gardens and contributed to road flooding. A green area in Leaside is frequently very wet due to groundwater flooding (possibly linked to historical mining). At the eastern end of Pennine Road (A689) water funnelled down the cycle-path access and into gardens and properties; five properties on Pennine Road were flooded. (p2)
* {28 Jun 2012, 04 Aug 2012}: Surface water flow directions on the map show runoff from the south/southwest converging toward the settlement and the A689, indicating areas of water accumulation and flood risk across the village area. (p3) [IMG]
* {28 Jun 2012, 04 Aug 2012}: Quant: 7 residential properties; 3 roads (p1) [IMG, PAG]
* {28 Jun 2012, 04 Aug 2012}: Quant: 5 properties; two main areas of the village (p2)

### Pennine Road, Halton Lea Gate
* {28 Jun 2012, 04 Aug 2012}: Surface water was funnelled down the access road to the cycle path and into gardens and properties at the eastern end of Pennine Road, causing internal flooding of properties. (p2)
* {28 Jun 2012, 04 Aug 2012}: Surface water flow arrows on the map show runoff moving across fields toward Pennine Road, implying overland flow onto the road and potential for surface-water accumulation or temporary flooding of the carriageway. (p3) [IMG]
* {28 Jun 2012, 04 Aug 2012}: Quant: 5 properties (p2)

### Leaside, Halton Lea Gate
* {28 Jun 2012, 04 Aug 2012}: Blocked inlet to a culvert on the ordinary watercourse caused water levels to rise and the watercourse to come out of bank; flood waters passed through some gardens in Leaside and onto the road. Surface water runoff from fields at the rear of northern properties led to flooding of gardens and contributed to flooding within the roads. A green area in Leaside is frequently very wet due to groundwater flooding (possibly linked to historical mining), contributing to local wetness. (p2)
* {28 Jun 2012, 04 Aug 2012}: A blocked inlet immediately west of Leaside is documented with pooling visible at the obstruction, restricting flow and causing localised water accumulation adjacent to fencing and nearby outbuildings. (p3) [IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### A689
* {28 Jun 2012, 04 Aug 2012}: A culvert running under the A689 had its inlet blocked, causing flood waters to rise and come out of bank; floodwaters reached the road. Surface water routing from the cycle path/access also directed water towards properties on the A689 (Pennine Road). (p2)
* {28 Jun 2012, 04 Aug 2012}: Map arrows indicate surface water flowing from surrounding fields toward the A689, suggesting that the main road is a focus for overland flow and potential surface-water accumulation affecting the carriageway and nearby roadside areas. (p3) [IMG]

### Glendue Fell
* {28 Jun 2012, 04 Aug 2012}: Rainfall on Glendue Fell (part of the village catchment) contributed runoff that fed the ordinary watercourse and played a role in the flood event that led to culvert blockage, out-of-bank flow and flooding in Leaside and Pennine Road on 28 June. (p2)
