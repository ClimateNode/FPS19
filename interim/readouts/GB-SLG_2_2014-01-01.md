### Colnbrook West Lake
* The west (main) penstock on Colnbrook West Lake was open for an unknown period, allowing continuous flow into Colnbrook via Hawthorn Ditch and County Ditch; at the peak the lake overtopped, bypassed the penstocks and flowed into Colnbrook village, worsening local flooding and keeping groundwater levels topped up. (p4)
* Colnbrook West Lake overtopped its embankment; water bypassed the penstocks and flowed into Colnbrook village. The embankment level was 9 cm below its designed level, increasing overtopping risk. (p5) [TAB, PAG]
* Embankment level recorded 9 cm below the designed level, increasing the risk of premature overtopping during the Jan–Feb 2014 flood; recommendation made to raise the embankment (permanently or temporarily, e.g. sandbags or concrete kerbs). (p6) [PAG]
* During the January–February 2014 event the Colne Brook overtopped the low embankment and flowed into the West Lakes (including Colnbrook West Lake); the Lakes provided flood storage which reduced flows conveyed downstream. Site visits after the floods noted Colne Brook levels higher than the Lakes. (p7)
* Colnbrook West Lake (part of the West Lakes) was identified in post‑flood site visits after the Jan–Feb 2014 event; the report notes the relationship between Colne Brook and the West Lakes during the 2014 floods and their role in flood storage dynamics. (p8) [PAG, IMG]
* Penstocks at Colnbrook West Lake were central to controlling flows into County Ditch during the flood event; at the time there was uncertainty over which authority should operate the asset. Temporary operating conditions were established after the flood, the main penstock was closed, and two other penstocks (HW1 and HW3) are controlled to prevent backflow to the road. A flap valve is to be fitted upstream of the outfalls to stop backflow. (p11) [IMG]
* Colnbrook West Lake threatened to backflow towards the adjacent road during the winter floods; penstocks at the lake were a focus for preventing lake water reaching the highway (controls and flap‑valve measures were specified). (p12) [IMG]
* Penstocks on the West Lake outlets were observed partially closed or fully open and submerged during the event; obstructed seating required tarpaulin and bagging to reduce flow. The main penstock being open allowed continued flow from the lake, and closure by the council on 12 February corresponded with significant drops in local water levels. The report also notes that even with the penstock shut, high flows in Colne Brook risked earlier overtopping of the West Lake embankment and continued flow towards Colnbrook. (p13) [PAG]
* Main penstock to the lake was open on 11/02/2014 allowing a sustained flow into Colnbrook; penstock seating obstructed and needed tarpaulin/sandbagging to reduce flow. All flood storage in the West Lakes was used up and the West Lake embankment overtopped, forcing flood water over the downstream embankment towards Colnbrook. The embankment crest was 9 cm below design, causing earlier overtopping. Closure of penstocks on 12/02/2014 led to observed falls in water levels. (p14) [MUL]
* {general}: The status of the Colnbrook West Lake penstock is discussed as influencing flood outcomes: had the penstock been shut the local area might have experienced less flooding initially, but shutting it earlier could have led to overtopping of the Colnbrook West Lake embankment and still resulted in floodwaters reaching the area. Penstock operation therefore affected flood timing and extent. (p16) [PAG]
* Penstocks at West Lake were confirmed as open during the incident; the council sought to confirm responsibility and ensure they were closed to manage high water levels. (p20) [TAB]
* Penstocks at West Lake were confirmed open during the event; authorities sought to confirm responsibility and to close them to control flows. (p21)
* The main penstock was located, shut and sealed with tarpaulin and sandbagging by Slough Borough Council staff who entered deep water during the flood response. (p22) [TAB]
* {general}: Environment Agency modelling reviewed after the event indicated Colnbrook West Lake would overtop for an event with a return period of less than 1 in 50 years, identifying the lake as a potential source/contributor to surface water flooding and prompting further monitoring and consideration. (p23) [TAB]
* West Lake operations were altered during the response: the main West Lake penstock was kept closed under a temporary winter agreement to control levels, there were concerns about backflow from the lake to the road (leading to a proposed flap valve in chamber CP110), and a formal penstock operation manual was proposed to manage future flood risk. (p25) [TAB]
* High water levels at West Lake prompted operational changes to the main penstock (temporary gate closure over winter under agreement), actions to make the penstock safe to operate when levels are high, and marking of the penstock location with a permanent bollard; these measures were taken to reduce future flood risk. (p26)
* Backflow from West Lake was identified as a source of potential flooding to the adjacent road; this prompted the proposal to fit a flap valve to the West Lake penstock chamber (CP110) to prevent lake-to-road backflow. (p27)
* The west (main) penstock on Colnbrook West Lake was open for an unknown period, allowing continuous flow into Colnbrook via Hawthorn Ditch and County Ditch; at peak the lake overtopped, bypassed the penstocks and flowed into Colnbrook village, and the open penstock helped keep groundwater levels topped up. (p28) [TAB]

### Colne Brook
* Fluvial flooding from the Colne Brook caused flooding along Mill Street; Colne Brook-driven high river levels contributed to local flooding during the event. (p4)
* Fallen trees and other debris in the Colne Brook created a head differential that prevented water passing back from the West Lakes, exacerbating the volume of water routed through the penstock and contributing to downstream flooding. (p5) [TAB, PAG]
* During the Jan–Feb 2014 floods Colne Brook exhibited elevated river levels observed on post‑flood site visits. Its high levels influenced flow interactions with the West Lakes and downstream drains and contributed to flood conditions in the Colnbrook investigation area. (p7)
* Experienced elevated river levels during the January–February 2014 floods. Site visits after the 2014 floods noted Colne Brook levels were higher than adjacent West Lakes; the brook conveyed higher flows that interacted with embankments, penstocks and the Albany Park barrage (affecting flow routing). (p8) [PAG, IMG]
* {general}: Colne Brook forms the detailed river network mapped for fluvial flood risk (Floodzone 3 follows its course) and is identified as the watercourse whose capacity exceedance and overtopping would cause fluvial flooding in the Colnbrook/Poyle area. Areas adjacent to the river were inundated in January–February 2014, but the report does not explicitly attribute the causal role of the Colne Brook in those specific events. (p9) [PAG]
* Flows from the Colne Brook contributed to flooding risk by feeding County Ditch during flood conditions; operation of penstocks at Colnbrook West Lake was used to prevent flow from the Colne Brook entering County Ditch and exacerbating inundation and groundwater rise. (p11) [IMG]
* High river levels during the January–February 2014 rainfall event contributed to widespread property flooding in the catchment and created a risk of backflow into adjacent channels (managed by local assets such as the Albany Park Barrage). (p12)
* High flows in the Colne Brook spilled into the West Lakes. Trees and debris in the Colne Brook are reported to have obstructed reverse flow, creating a head differential that prevented water returning to the brook and instead forced water over the West Lake embankment into Colnbrook. (p14) [PAG, MUL]
* Colne Brook is implicated in the local flood processes: water routed into West Lakes did not flow back into the Colne Brook (partly attributed to low West Lake embankment height and fallen trees/debris in the Colne Brook), and river proximity relates to the mapped flood areas through the village. (p15)
* {general}: Colne Brook's capacity to receive flow from the West Lakes was limited during the event; inability for water to pass back into the Colne Brook likely increased flooding extent in the downstream park and residential area. (p16)
* River levels were reported to be high and contributed flow that flooded Mill Street (flow reported in bank at Mill Street on 1 February 2014). (p18)
* Post‑event actions included surveying low points between the Colne Brook and adjacent lakes/areas to better understand flood pathways. The river and connected channels were investigated as sources/pathways for floodwater and in relation to gully connectivity and local ditch management. (p23) [TAB]
* Trees and other debris were removed from the Colne Brook between Orlitts Lake and the A4 Colnbrook by‑pass bridge by the Environment Agency to clear the channel following the flood event, as a priority to restore flow and reduce flood risk. (p25) [TAB]
* Fluvial flooding from the Colne Brook was identified as the predominant source of flooding along Mill Street; debris and head differentials also prevented flows returning to the Colne Brook, exacerbating flooding. (p28) [TAB]
* Trees and other debris were removed by the Environment Agency in a key area to reduce the risk of channel overtopping following the flood; connectivity between the Colne Brook and the West Lakes was identified for investigation because raised flows downstream worsened drainage from Colnbrook. (p29) [TAB]

### West Lakes

* Connectivity between the Colne Brook and the West Lakes (Old Slade Lake, Orlitts Lake, Colnbrook West Lake) was identified for investigation because it may have contributed to overtopping and local flood impacts during the Jan–Feb 2014 event. (p6) [PAG]
* The West Lakes form part of the floodplain storage adjacent to the Colne Brook. During the Jan–Feb 2014 event they were a key feature of the flood system (post‑flood site visits noted the Colne Brook was higher than the lakes), affecting flood storage and flow routing via the embankment and penstock connections. ### County Ditch
* County Ditch fed Cottesbrook Ditch and contributed to fluvial flooding affecting The Albany Park and Colnbrook area. (p4)
* Fluvial flood levels in County Ditch needed control during the event; control relied on preventing inflow from the Colne Brook by closing the Colnbrook West Lake penstocks so that flows in County Ditch matched local rainfall and groundwater baseflow. (p11) [IMG]
* Because the main penstock was open, County Ditch (and Cottesbrook ditch) drained a larger upstream area into the West Lake system and through the penstocks into Colnbrook, increasing flows and contributing to groundwater being kept topped up. Engineers estimated flows through the penstocks at about 500 l/s rising to 1000 l/s at peak overflow. (p14) [PAG, MUL]
* An open main penstock (noted open on 11 February 2014) allowed a constant flow of water from County Ditch into Colnbrook; this inflow may have maintained high groundwater levels in Colnbrook and contributed to local flooding. (p15)
* {general}: County Ditch (identified as a source alongside Cottesbrook Ditch) was observed delivering water into the park/road area; flow from the ditch contributed to flooding in Albany Park and the adjacent residential roads and was a focus for pumping to lower levels. (p16) [PAG]
