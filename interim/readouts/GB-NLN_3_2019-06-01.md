## Place names with coordinates

### Wainfleet All Saints
* Floodwaters inundated roads, yards, agricultural fields and parts of the residential settlement. Water reached up to the bottom of building windows and partially submerged fences and entrances, disrupting transportation and access. A levee/embankment breach allowed forceful flow into adjacent land; sandbags were deployed in attempts to control the breach. (p1)
* Wainfleet was flooded during June 2019. The investigation references a breach in the relief channel that contributed to flooding of the town and led to recommendations for embankment assessment and other mitigation measures. (p5)
* Wainfleet was flooded in June 2019 as a result of high flows in the Steeping River and its relief channel, triggering a Section 19 investigation and subsequent recommendations to RMAs. (p6) [PAG]
* Wainfleet experienced a flood incident in June 2019 that formed part of the catchment-wide flooding response; properties suffered internal flooding and Risk Management Authorities mounted a collective response to the incident. (p9)
* Multiple properties in Wainfleet experienced internal flooding from fluvial sources; multi‑agency response included evacuation coordination, Fire and Rescue pumping properties out, RAF dropping sandbags to seal a breach, and Environment Agency repair/reinforcement of the breached bank. (p10) [TAB, PAG]
* Concentration of internal property flooding in the town; properties reported internal flooding caused by main river overtopping and surface-water runoff, with multiple-source flooding incidents in the lower catchment near the town. (p12) [IMG]

* Multiple streets in Wainfleet experienced internal flooding from fluvial (river) sources and some surface-water/other sources. Fire and Rescue Service responded: pumped out properties, visited affected residents to offer advice and gathered information, and carried out measures to minimise flood impacts. A multi‑agency group (Environment Agency, Lincolnshire County Council, East Lindsey District Council, Lincolnshire Police) coordinated evacuations of Wainfleet. The RAF dropped sandbags to seal a breach. The Environment Agency repaired and reinforced the bank at the breach site. ## Topics without coordinates

### River Steeping
* The Steeping River system experienced extensive overtopping of its relief channel culminating in a breach of the relief channel bank on 12 June 2019; this breach substantially increased the flood impacts to communities, contributing to internal flooding of properties and large-scale evacuations. Military aid (MACA) was used to plug and seal the breach. (p3)
* Overtopping of the relief channel embankment during heavy rainfall (notably 10–11 June 2019) led to erosion on the rear face and the formation of a breach. The breach exacerbated the scale and impact of flooding across the catchment by allowing additional flow to escape the channel; contributing factors noted include embankment construction from excavated arisings (offering limited protection), siltation, constrictions in the river (syphons and sluice gates), raised summer water levels for abstraction, and bank damage from cattle grazing. Maintenance works earlier in 2019 (March and May) that removed vegetation and filled ruts are noted as contextual factors at the breach location. (p4)
* Raised embankments along the Steeping River and its relief channel were implicated in the June 2019 event; a breach location on the relief channel is referenced in the investigation. The event prompted recommendations to assess embankment condition, consider replacement, and manage silt/vegetation to reduce future flood risk. (p5) [PAG]
* High flows in the Steeping River and its relief channel during June 2019 contributed to flooding in Wainfleet; issues identified include the condition of raised embankments and reduced channel capacity due to silt and vegetation. (p6) [PAG]
* Flooding of the Steeping River caused internal flooding of residential properties, rendered critical services inoperable, flooded agricultural land for extended periods and made roads impassable, with disruption to transport timetables. (p7) [PAG]
* Main-river flooding from the Steeping River contributed to internal property flooding on the river's lower reaches (areas downstream towards Wainfleet All Saints). (p12) [IMG]
* {1947, 1968}: The Steeping River was the reported source of flooding in historic incidents (notably in 1968 and 1947). Flood-reduction works were later implemented (raising localised low spots in banks, improving sluice gate operation at the outfall, and removal of silt upstream of Wainfleet). (p15) [TAB, MUL]
* The Steeping River acted as the main river source of flooding that led to internal property flooding in Wainfleet All Saints; flood impacts concentrated near the river and along adjacent roads. (p16) [IMG]
* The Steeping River (and associated relief channel/banks) is shown as the main-river source causing internal flooding of properties; recommendations identify embankments and banks along the Steeping River for assessment and possible replacement to reduce future risk. (p17) [IMG]
* The Steeping River is identified on the map as the main river source for flooded properties (main-river flood markers). The map analysis highlights properties flooded by the main river and recommends embankment inspection and mitigation measures along this channel. (p19) [IMG]
* Quant: Breach first partially filled by helicopter (40 tonnes); breach sealed by midnight 14 June with a total of 270 tonnes of material; internal flooding attributed to main river sources: 61 properties; a further 290 properties evacuated as a precaution after renewed overtopping. (p3)

### Steeping Basin
* Lower reaches experienced overtopping of the relief channel which culminated in a bank breach on 12 June 2019; the breach significantly worsened flooding across the catchment. Military and helicopter assistance was used to fill/seal the breach (initial helicopter fill ~40 tonnes; breach sealed with a total of 270 tonnes by midnight 14 June 2019). Further overtopping led to a second assistance request and additional precautionary evacuations. (p3) [MUL]
* A breach in the relief channel/embankment led to river/relief-channel flooding that affected Wainfleet during the June 2019 event. The breach and elevated silt/vegetation levels prompted recommendations to assess and potentially replace or reinforce raised embankments, monitor and manage silt and vegetation, consider dredging or reconfiguring the relief channel, and review maintenance regimes and pumping station resilience. (p5)
* Land management within the Steeping River catchment (sediment runoff, livestock access, burrowing animals) was identified as contributing to increased sediment in drains and watercourses, reducing conveyance and contributing to the June 2019 flooding risk for Wainfleet. (p6) [PAG]
* Flooding in the Steeping River catchment caused multiple residential properties to be internally flooded, rendered critical services inoperable, flooded agricultural land for more than two days, made roads impassable and adversely impacted normal timetables. (p8)
* Flooding from fluvial and coastal sources affected the catchment during the June 2019 event. The investigation assessed 88 incidents of internal property flooding within the catchment and identified numbers of properties at risk from river and sea flooding for design events. (p9)
* Multiple sources of flooding across the catchment caused internal property flooding concentrated in the southeastern catchment (notably Wainfleet All Saints, Thorpe St Peter and Great Steeping); both main river and surface-water sources are shown as contributing. (p12) [IMG]
* Exceptional intense rainfall in June 2019 (notably 10–11 June) within the Steeping catchment led to flooding originating in the catchment and contributing to the June 2019 flood event. (p13) [TAB]
* Fluvial (river) flooding across the Steeping River catchment between 11–16 June 2019 caused internal flooding of residential properties on multiple streets, breaches of river/bank requiring repair, and multi‑agency evacuation and flood‑response actions (pumping, sandbagging, bank reinforcement, and pumping by IDBs). (p14) [PAG, IMG]

Subsequent mitigation works were carried out (raising low bank spots, improving sluice gate operation at the outfall, and removing silt upstream of Wainfleet). (p15) [MUL]
