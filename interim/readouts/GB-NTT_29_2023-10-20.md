

# Epperstone flooding 20 Oct 2023

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Epperstone – Storm Babet, October 2023<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/epperstone-s19-storm-babet-oct-2023.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Nottinghamshire
* The document states a Major Incident had significant impacts across the county; County officers played a key role in the response and recovery (site visits, verification of internal flooding, grants, coordination). (p4)
* Widespread surface water flooding that progressed to river flood risk (Storm Babet) across the county. County actions and impacts recorded include delivery of sandbags/aqua-sacs to affected communities, 24-hour emergency planning support, closure of roads to protect public safety, initiation and leadership of a Section 19 flood investigation, liaison with parish councils and community groups, and coordination of Property Flood Resilience grant procurement and delivery. (p5) [PAG]
* Quant: 77 roads (p5) [PAG]

### Newark and Sherwood
* Surface water flooding that then progressed to a continued risk of river flooding (notably River Trent) in the district. Impacts and response included visits to impacted communities and vulnerable individuals, many aqua-sacs delivered, assistance with evacuations and provision of emergency accommodation where necessary, coordinated clean-up of roads and paths, removal of bulky waste from flooded homes, and direct contact with affected individuals to identify humanitarian needs. (p5)

### Epperstone
* Areas of the village were flooded following sustained heavy rainfall; 6 residential properties experienced internal flooding and roads were reported flooded due to saturated ground. (p1)
* {general}: Map analysis identifies the area in and around Epperstone as having the highest surface water flood risk (flood extent concentrated along the river corridor), indicating exposure to surface-water flooding. (p3) [IMG]
* Referenced in post‑event activity: a community flood signage scheme is being developed with NCC support and officers are helping the community develop a local flood action group; the community is included in consideration for Property Flood Resilience measures following Storm Babet. (p6)
* Quant: 6 residential properties (p1)

### Main Street, Epperstone
* Intense, prolonged rainfall caused overland flow across Main Street from the north which overwhelmed drainage and led to internal flooding of residential properties. A separate surface water flowpath from Hagg Lane to Main Street also contributed to the flooding. (p2) [PAG]
* Surface water / overland flow from agricultural land overwhelmed drainage and flowed across Main Street (north to south and also from Hagg Lane), causing internal flooding to residential properties. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 6 residential properties (p2) [PAG]
* Quant: 6 residential properties (p3) [IMG, PAG]

### Hagg Lane, Epperstone
* A surface water flowpath from Hagg Lane into Main Street contributed overland flow during the event, increasing flooding on Main Street. (p2)

### River Trent
* Predicted extreme levels of the River Trent during the event created a continued risk of river flooding. This prompted the council to coordinate in-person visits to high-risk communities to reinforce the need to prepare for imminent flooding, supported evacuations and provision of emergency accommodation where necessary, and led to recovery actions once the risk passed (clean-up of roads/paths and removal of bulky waste from flooded homes). (p5)


## Topics without coordinates

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Responded to a Major Incident with significant county‑wide flood impacts: initiated a Community Flood Recovery Grant to support people affected by internal (property) flooding; attended sites after flooding to verify scale of internal flooding and offer support, guidance and advice to affected residents and businesses; fed site information back to relevant risk management authorities and partners; chaired and attended Strategic/Tactical/Recovery Coordination Group meetings. (p4)
* As Lead Local Flood Authority, the council witnessed and responded to flooding: ran an internal and multi‑agency debrief, worked with parish and town councils to restock stores of aqua sacs, is developing Flood Warden / Road Closure training, supporting community flood signage (e.g. in Epperstone) and evaluating Property Flood Resilience support for communities affected by Storm Babet; will work with partners on riparian enforcement and asset inspection. (p6)
