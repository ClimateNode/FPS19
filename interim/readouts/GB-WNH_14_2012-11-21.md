

# Grimscote flooding 21 Nov 2012

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Manor Road, Grimscote, 21st November 2012<br>090\_West Northamptonshire/Grimscote - November 2012.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Manor Road, Grimscote
* Intense short-duration rainfall onto a saturated catchment caused excess surface water to flow over ground to low points in the village and be drained via highway drainage. At least one property on Manor Road experienced internal flooding. (p5)
* Runoff from high ground during significant rainfall on 21 November 2012 flowed through properties toward and onto Manor Road, with at least one property on Manor Road flooded internally and other properties placed at high risk. (p8) [PAG]
* {general}: An outlet pipe from the north-side ditch of Litchborough Road carries water north through grassland to an inspection chamber in the grounds of a dwelling on Manor Road; the pipework continues north and the system of road gullies on Manor Road drains to the historic pipe culvert. The culvert was reportedly diverted/replaced in places during recent property construction, altering the drainage path that conveys surface water toward the downstream watercourse. (p9)
* A large volume and high-velocity surface water flowed south across agricultural fields toward properties on the south of Manor Road, ponded at the rear of two residential properties, crossed boundaries and resulted in one residential property on Manor Road being flooded internally. (p11)
* A diverted historic culvert around a residential property on Manor Road includes multiple changes of direction and restricted access that restrict flow and has historically been a source of flooding. A large pond formed at the rear of two residential properties, deepened, overtopped property boundaries, flowed through the rear garden of one property and into the building. Temporary pipework and slab defences were installed and the gravel driveway was re-levelled to try to divert/protect the affected property. Ponding was also reported on the Manor Road carriageway which drained via the highway drainage system. (p12) [PAG]
* Temporary flood defences and pipework were installed behind a residential property on Manor Road. (p35) [IMG, PAG]
* Temporary flood defences (panels) and pipework were installed directly behind a residential property to prevent water ingress; barriers placed at ground level against the fence with an integrated pipe outlet to channel water away. (p36) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 property (p5)
* Quant: at least one property (p8) [PAG]
* Quant: one residential property (p11)
* Quant: two residential properties; one residential property (p12) [PAG]

### Litchborough Road, Grimscote
* Runoff from agricultural land and from Litchborough Road's riparian ditch system (one ditch had been blocked by driveways to four dwellings) was carried north via pipes and gullies into the Manor Road drainage system, contributing to floodwater reaching properties on Manor Road. This runoff was a causal pathway in the event of 21 November 2012 when at least one property experienced internal flooding. (p8) [PAG]
* {general}: The riparian-owned ditch on the south side of Litchborough Road that previously flowed eastwards to an ordinary watercourse has been blocked by the driveways to four residential properties; at the low point a pipe conveys water north beneath the carriageway to the north-side ditch. These drainage obstructions/alterations form part of the surface water drainage route described in the report. (p9) [PAG]
* Surface water emanated from agricultural land south of Litchborough Road and from Litchborough Road itself; the riparian-owned ditch system and associated pipework on Litchborough Road had insufficient capacity and overtopped, producing overland flows. An agricultural access track on Litchborough Road produced considerable overland flow that ran east down Litchborough Road and discharged into the riparian ditch at the low point. (p11) [PAG]
* Blockage/closure of a historic riparian-owned ditch on the south side of Litchborough Road redirected a greater quantity of water to flow eastwards through the village of Grimscote, altering local flow routes and increasing flood risk through the village. (p12)
* {22 Apr 2013}: Standing water was present in a riparian-owned roadside ditch on the south side of Litchborough Road, adjacent to residential properties; the condition was documented during a flood incident investigation (photographs show the standing water and the ditch outlet/inspection cover). (p32) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Standing water is present in riparian-owned ditches on the south side of Litchborough Road adjacent to residential properties. The outlet on the north side has a diameter-reducing fitting and a metal grate with water pooled at the base. Ditches are described as overgrown and silted up, indicating reduced conveyance and potential for local ponding. (p33) [IMG, PAG]
* Riparian-owned ditches on Litchborough Road are described as overgrown and silted-up, reducing drainage capacity and implicated in flooding impacts to nearby properties on the south side. (p34) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: at least one property; four dwellings (p8) [PAG]
* {general}: Quant: four residential properties (p9) [PAG]

### Grimscote
* Intense short-duration rainfall on a saturated catchment caused excess surface water to flow over ground to low points in the village; this resulted in internal flooding of property/properties and was aggravated by ineffective historic drainage systems. (p6) [PAG]
* A greater quantity of water was made to flow through the village (where it did not previously), increasing flood risk within Grimscote; ponding and drainage issues are described on Manor Road within the village. (p12)
* Ponding was reported on the carriageway of Manor Road; the surface water ponding drained via the highway drainage system. (p13)
* Intense rainfall on a saturated catchment caused existing drainage systems and watercourses to be unable to cope. Excessive flows overtopped systems and flowed to the low point in the village, with residential properties on the flow path being affected by the resulting surface water/flash flooding. (p16)
* Flows of water from higher ground overtopped drainage systems and flowed to the low point in the village, affecting residential properties; overspill of land drainage from watercourses impacted the public highway and private property. (p17)
* Quant: at least one property (p6) [PAG]

### Litchborough
* Surface water originating from agricultural land south of Litchborough Road and from Litchborough Road overtopped the riparian-owned ditch system, producing high-velocity overland flows that changed flow routes through the area and routed greater volumes of water through the village/Grimscote area (where this had not previously occurred), contributing to downstream ponding and property flooding. (p11) [PAG]
* Quant: one residential property; two residential properties (p11) [PAG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Grimscote Catchment
* Significant rainfall on 21 November 2012 across the Grimscote catchment produced runoff from high ground that flowed through properties toward Manor Road, leading to at least one internal property flood and putting several properties at high risk. (p8)
* Significant rainfall on 21 November 2012 over the Grimscote catchment produced saturated ground and generated runoff that led to surface water flooding incidents affecting people and property in the catchment area. (p11)
* Quant: at least one property (p8)

### Manor Farm
* {general}: The culverted pipework flows past Manor Farm and discharges to an existing watercourse to the northeast of the village, placing Manor Farm on the downstream flow path of the drainage system described in the report. (p9)

### Highway Authority
* Highway drainage assets in the village catchment are at risk of being overwhelmed by surface water in extreme events, leading to overtopping of the public highway and contributing to flood risk to nearby properties. The report recommends regular highway drainage cleansing, development of a detailed asset plan, assessment of asset capacity and, where capacity is insufficient and poses flood risk to properties, consideration of highway drainage improvement works. (p18)

### Northamptonshire County Council
* NCC, acting as LLFA and through Emergency Planning, is implicated in managing local surface water flood risk: providing advice to residents on property-level protection, establishing communication lines with riparian owners and the Highway Authority, instating and liaising with a Flood Warden, and producing a village community flood plan. (p18)

### South Northamptonshire Council
* South Northants Council is identified as needing to continue consultation with the Environment Agency regarding planning applications to ensure new developments reduce flood risk, and to engage in establishing ownership/maintenance responsibilities for piped culverts and historic drainage reinstatement to reduce surface water flood risk in the village. (p18)

### Anglian Water
* Anglian Water is asked to assess sources of water entering the public sewerage system (including streams, springs, land drainage and other surface water connections) that may be overloading sewers and contributing to flooding, and to consider disconnecting these connections to reduce sewer-related flood risk. (p18)
