

# Misterton flooding 06 Feb 2016

Reference document:<br>Middle Street, Knowle Lane, Silver Street, Newbery Lane, Misterton, Somerset. Flood Event of 6th February 2016<br>113\_Somerset County/Flood Investigation Report Misterton.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Misterton
* Six properties were internally flooded (water depth up to 300mm) and the highway flooded to a depth up to 1200mm; large volumes of surface run-off flowed onto the highway. (p4)
* The civil parish (Misterton CP), including the village of Misterton and surrounding farmland, was the focus of the flood investigation and experienced flooding associated with flows in the Misterton Stream that affected the central village area and adjacent fields. (p8) [IMG]
* Flooding associated with the Misterton Stream flowing eastwards through the village. Community flood incident reports and the LLFA investigation identify overwhelmed highway drains (by agricultural surface run‑off) and blocked watercourses as contributors to flooding in the Misterton area. (p9) [PAG, IMG]
* Multiple properties flooded — six properties reported flooded during the event on 2016-02-06. The report also records previous community flood incidents in the village (May 2008, December 2008, December 2013). (p10) [MUL]
* Heavy rainfall and agricultural surface run-off caused the Misterton Stream to be heavily silt-laden; surface run-off from fields adjacent to Cathole Bridge Road flowed onto Middle Street and A356 Middle Street; runoff from Knowle Lane and Newbery Lane channelled into the stream, contributed to blockages and siltation, and affected residents (resident reports of being affected by flooding). (p13)
* During the 2016-02-06 flood event highway drainage in Misterton was overwhelmed by excessive runoff and/or watercourse flows: highway drains exceeded capacity, silt and detritus were washed in reducing capacity, and there were obstructions and debris at the outfall of the culvert running under the railway line. Local gullies and a silt-trap on Knowle Lane were affected (silt trap later cleared and a collapsed gully cover repaired). (p14) [PAG]
* Misterton experienced flooding associated with the 2016-02-06 event; the flooding aligned with Environment Agency flood risk data, properties and local drainage were affected, and a likelihood of repeated flooding was noted. (p15)
* The village experienced flooding on 2016-02-06; the event corresponded with Environment Agency flood risk data, affected properties/residents, and prompted this investigation which identified contributory factors and proposed measures (community Flood Action Plan support, property-level protection advice, consideration of additional silt-trap on Knowle Lane and use of Mill Farm land as flood storage). (p16) [PAG]

### Knowle Lane, Misterton
* Large volumes of surface water run-off onto the highway causing the highway to flood; highway flood depth reported up to 1200 mm during the event. (p4)
* During the 2016-02-06 event a significant volume of surface runoff travelled via Knowle Lane, overwhelming highway drainage. The highway was reported flooded (overall highway depths 100 mm to 1200 mm) and this overland flow contributed to six properties flooding internally (water entered properties at about 18:00–18:30 to depths of 100–300 mm). (p11)
* Middle Street (A356) received surface-water run-off from adjacent fields (via Cathole Bridge Road) and from Knowle Lane, causing surface water flooding of the highway and contributing to local flooding impacts in Misterton during the event. (p13)
* A silt-trap gully on Knowle Lane (100m south of the junction with Middle Street) was affected by silt and detritus from the flood; the silt trap and downstream pipe were cleared and a collapsed gully cover at the silt trap was logged and repaired on 30/05/2016. (p14)
* Following the 2016-02-06 flood event, Knowle Lane experienced runoff-related deposition and drainage blockage. A silt trap and the downstream pipe were found to contain silt and were cleared on 2016-05-30; one gully cover on the silt trap was found collapsed, logged for a 24-hour repair and repaired the same day. Run-off was identified as originating from agricultural land at the top of Knowle Lane, and an additional silt-trap was later proposed. (p15) [PAG]
* The watercourse flowing parallel to Knowle Lane and associated sediment transport contributed to the flooding observed on 2016-02-06; sedimentation/silt in this channel was identified as a contributory factor and an additional silt-trap on Knowle Lane was proposed to reduce silt, improve flow and mitigate future flooding. (p16) [PAG]
* Identified as affected by siltation/sediment issues related to the 2016-02-06 flood; an additional silt-trap on Knowle Lane was proposed to reduce future silt deposition and improve flow, with potential works planned for the 2017/18 financial year. (p17) [PAG]

### Cathole Bridge Road, Misterton
* During the 2016-02-06 event a significant volume of surface runoff travelled via Cathol Bridge Road (sic), overwhelming highway drainage. The highway was reported flooded (overall highway depths 100 mm to 1200 mm) and this overland flow contributed to six properties flooding internally (water entered properties at about 18:00–18:30 to depths of 100–300 mm). (p11) [MUL]
* Surface run-off from fields adjacent to Cathole Bridge Road flowed onto Middle Street during the heavy rainfall, contributing to surface water flooding on Middle Street. (p13)
* Identified as part of the larger catchment contributing to the 2016-02-06 flooding; the LLFA/FWRM team proposed inspecting the catchment (including Cathole Bridge Road) to identify flow improvements to reduce future flood risk. (p17) [MUL]

### South Somerset
* The district contains Misterton where six properties were internally flooded up to 300mm and the highway flooded up to 1200mm; South Somerset's Area Highways engaged with SCC to discuss surface water on Knowle Lane and possible installation of an additional silt-trap. (p4)

### Silver Street, Misterton
* During the 2016-02-06 event a significant volume of surface runoff travelled via Silver Street, overwhelming highway drainage. The highway was reported flooded (overall highway depths 100 mm to 1200 mm) and this overland flow contributed to six properties flooding internally (water entered properties at about 18:00–18:30 to depths of 100–300 mm). (p11)
* The Misterton Stream was observed brown and heavily silt-laden with active bed movement and gravels reducing channel capacity. Meandering sections and washed-in bank material created pinch points and, together with debris and obstructions (including at culvert outfalls), reduced conveyance and contributed to flooding downstream. (p13)

### Newbery Lane, Misterton
* During the 2016-02-06 event a significant volume of surface runoff travelled via Newbery Lane, overwhelming highway drainage. The highway was reported flooded (overall highway depths 100 mm to 1200 mm) and this overland flow contributed to six properties flooding internally (water entered properties at about 18:00–18:30 to depths of 100–300 mm). (p11)
* During heavy rain, water flows down Newbery Lane (from Silver Street) directly into the Misterton Stream. The Irish Bridge railings collect debris causing blockages, the culvert under the highway is considered too small, an upstream sump/sediment trap has not been maintained, and there are obstructions at the culvert outfall under the railway—factors that reduced conveyance and contributed to localised flooding on 2016-02-06. (p13)

### Middle Street, Misterton
* Location of the confluence (OS Grid Ref ST455081 345548) where the stream flowing from Sheepwash Farm joined the system; this confluence is identified in the report as part of the flow route that contributed to the 2016-02-06 river flooding affecting Misterton. (p17)


## Topics without coordinates

### Irish Bridge
* Newbery Lane acted as a flow path for heavy surface-water runoff into the Misterton Stream. The section around the Irish Bridge (on Newbery Lane) contains a culvert considered too small and was a pinch point, contributing to restricted conveyance and local flooding downstream. (p13)

### Misterton Stream
* The Misterton Stream (historically called the Paddocks Lake), which flows eastwards through the village and discharges to the River Parrett, was the primary watercourse investigated for the flood incident. Flood flows conveyed by the stream affected the village and surrounding farmland; the stream's 97 m, 1.5 m diameter tunnel beneath the Exeter–London Waterloo railway was a key hydraulic feature during the event. (p8)
* The watercourse (historically called Paddocks Lake / Misterton Stream) has a large catchment and received excessive surface run‑off contributing to flooding; local reports note highway drains being overwhelmed by agricultural run‑off and blocked watercourse sections feeding flooding from this stream. (p9)
* {general}: The stream was brown and heavily silt-laden; a meandering reach acted as a pinch point (≈650–750m upstream of the Irish Bridge) with active bed movement and gravels reducing flow capacity. Bank erosion and debris (including material caught on Irish Bridge railings and obstructions at culvert outfalls) reduced conveyance and contributed to flooding of adjacent roads and properties during the event. (p13)

### Highways
* The Highways Area Office had carried out improvements to the drainage system (including installing a silt-trap on Knowle Lane) and, following the flood, undertook maintenance: cleared the Knowle Lane silt trap and downstream pipe and logged/repaired a collapsed gully cover (repaired same day). (p14)
* Area Highways engaged in direct post-flood response and planning: attended a site meeting on 2016-07-14 to discuss highway drainage improvement possibilities for flood-affected locations (including Knowle Lane), advised that upgrade options were limited and suggested consideration of an additional silt-trap. (p15)

### Somerset County Council
* As the Lead Local Flood Authority SCC carried out a Section 19 investigation into the 2016-02-06 flood: the Flood and Water Management Team met residents (25/02/2016 and 14/04/2016), explained LLFA roles and funding/PLP, and oversaw/arranged inspections and highway drainage maintenance (including clearance of the Knowle Lane silt trap and repair of a collapsed gully cover on 30/05/2016). SCC also hold permissive powers under the Land Drainage Act 1991 to require removal of obstructions on ordinary watercourses. (p14)
* Highways maintenance crews cleared a silt trap on Knowle Lane, the downstream pipe and a short distance up the lane; one gully cover on the silt trap was found collapsed, logged for a 24-hour repair and repaired on the same day. (p15)

### Mill Farm
* Land at Mill Farm was identified during the investigation of the 2016-02-06 event as possible floodplain/storage to improve flow and reduce flood risk to Misterton residents; it lies within the flood flow-path and was considered for temporary flood storage to benefit affected properties. (p16)

### Sheepwash Farm
* Source of a stream that was part of the catchment influencing the 2016-02-06 flood; the stream originating at Sheepwash Farm flowed NE parallel to Knowle Lane and joined at Middle Street, contributing to elevated flows during the event. (p17)
