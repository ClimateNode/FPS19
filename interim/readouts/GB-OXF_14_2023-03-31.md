

# Sunningwell flooding 31 Mar 2023 - 01 Apr 2023

The inspection found no capacity concerns with the culvert, but the reported blockage indicates it contributed to local flow restriction during the flood. (p16)

(p26)

### Sunningwell
* {31 Mar 2023 - 01 Apr 2023, 13 Oct 2023}: Widespread flooding on 31 Mar–1 Apr 2023 (and highway flooding on 13 Oct 2023): the village watercourse was overwhelmed by overland flow from surrounding hillsides; water passed through gardens, garages and properties toward the watercourse; surface water overloaded foul sewers with reports of foul flooding; grips and gullies were full of silt and sections of culverted watercourse were restricting flow. (p2) [TAB, MUL]
* {general}: Much of the village is shown as being at high risk from surface water flooding. Surface water flood maps indicate areas of the village have a greater than 3.3PCSYM chance of flooding each year (shown as dark blue on the EA surface water maps). The village is in Flood Zone 1 for fluvial/tidal risk but smaller local catchments have not been modelled by the Environment Agency, so surface water flood risk affects the settlement. (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* Environment Agency surface-water flood maps show much of the village is at high risk (>3.3PCSYM annual chance). A small watercourse runs through the village centre (culverted under some property accesses); two main carrier drains convey flows through the centre to a small pond on land owned by the Parish Council. Springs where Lower Greensand sandstone meets clay feed these watercourses, channelling runoff towards the Bayworth / Sunningwell area. (p8) [IMG, PAG]
* {2001, 2007, 2012}: Flooding affected properties in the village. A small watercourse runs through the centre of the village (culverted in places) with two main carrier drains transferring water through the centre to a small pond on land owned by the Parish Council; these watercourses/drainage features are implicated in local flooding of properties. (p9) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* {2001, 2007, 2012}: Flooding affected property in the village in previous events. (p10) [IMG, MUL]
* The village experienced river and overland flooding when the brook burst its banks and the village pond overflowed on 31 March–1 April 2023. Floodwater flowed along Sunningwell Road, through gardens and garages, and surrounded several properties. Foul drains were overwhelmed in parts of the village, causing foul-water contamination of floodwater and hydraulic overload of sewers in some locations. (p11)
* Overland flow paths and ordinary watercourses converge toward the village and the village pond catchment, directing runoff into low-lying parts of Sunningwell and increasing local surface water flood risk. The existing watercourse through the village was surveyed as part of the investigation. (p14) [IMG]
* The catchment draining to Sunningwell Brook received the event rainfall used in the hydrology modelling. This produced an estimated total rainfall volume of 65,243 m3 across the catchment and a modeled peak flow in the watercourse of around 0.83 m3/s during the 31 Mar 2023–01 Apr 2023 event. (p15) [IMG, PAG]
* Sunningwell experienced multiple flood mechanisms during 31 Mar 2023 - 01 Apr 2023. Open channel reaches and culverts through the village were heavily silted, reducing conveyance capacity and contributing to overbanking and garden/property flooding. Oxfordshire Highways inspected and cleared silt from culverts under Sunningwell Road. A 900mm diameter culvert running to the rear gardens of Sunningwell Road was approximately 50PCSYM silted, reducing its capacity and restricting flow. Significant overland flow from the surrounding hillside passed through and around several properties, increasing peak flows. Overloaded foul sewers in the village (Thames Water's network) caused internal flooding, notably in the Penn Lane / Sunningwell Road and Bullsmead areas. Thames Water are investigating and identified a non-return valve near the Penn Lane / Sunningwell Road junction that was installed in the wrong chamber and does not provide intended protection. (p18) [PAG]
* On 31 Mar–1 Apr 2023 exceptionally heavy rainfall and saturated soils produced significant overland flow from surrounding hillsides, which overwhelmed the watercourse through the centre of the village and caused water to pass through gardens, garages and properties. The event also overloaded foul sewers, producing reports of foul (sewer) flooding. Local gullies and grips were observed filled with silt and some culverted sections of the watercourse were restricting flow, exacerbating flood impacts. (p19) [PAG]
* The village experienced surface water flooding during the event, prompting a Section 19 investigation and recommendations for property-level protection, highway drainage assessment and community flood planning. (p28)
* Quant: 12 properties affected by internal flooding; 6 properties with ground-floor internal flooding; 2 cellars flooded; 5 properties with internal garage flooding; up to 30cm water depth in some properties (p11)

### Oxfordshire County Council

### Culvert

These obstructions contributed to flooding through the village; desilting and a CCTV survey of the outfall were recommended to check for root ingress/damage. (p2) [TAB, PAG]
* Culvert outfall at the village green pond was noted to have high silt levels impairing flow; culverts (including a 375mm diameter culvert under the main road) conveyed high flows and contributed to runoff/connectivity under the road. Recommendations include clearing silt, carrying out CCTV to check for root ingress/damage and restoring conveyance. (p25) [TAB]

### Sunningwell Brook
* The watercourse drains the catchment north of the village and is implicated in the village's flooding history; it is likely to have been involved in flood processes during the 31 Mar – 1 Apr 2023 event (investigation context). (p10) [IMG]
* During the 31 Mar 2023–01 Apr 2023 event the watercourse experienced high flows that filled the valley channel and overtopped/fully filled structures. The top timber check dam was likely full and water had scoured around the side of the top structure; flow was restricted downstream by a 225mm pipe causing a lake to form behind it; the attenuation area contains variable silt which reduced conveyance/storage and influenced discharge through the restriction. (p16)
