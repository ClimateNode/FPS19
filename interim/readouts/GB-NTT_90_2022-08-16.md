### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Initiated and led the Section 19 flood investigation; administered the Flooding Hardship Fund; held a public drop-in session on 8 September to support affected residents and businesses; started recruiting Flood Signage Wardens and establishing a Road Closure Scheme for parts of Worksop; initiated and coordinated emergency planning procedures including vulnerable people checks for streets identified for potential evacuation. (p12)
* Led the Section 19 Flood Investigation, administered the Flooding Hardship Fund, held a public drop-in to support impacted residents and businesses after the flood, and recruited flood signage wardens as part of local response/mitigation following the event. (p13)
* As Lead Local Flood Authority, continuing investigations into the causes of the incident; administering/supporting a Flood Warden scheme and local sandbag stores; recruiting and facilitating a Community Flood Signage Scheme in Worksop; prioritising support to vulnerable communities post‑flood. (p14)

### Severn Trent
* Following reports of flooding, Severn Trent attended sites across Worksop, supplied tankers to assist with flood management and carried out post‑flood investigations including CCTV inspection of the local sewer network to check for defects or blockages. (p13) [PAG]
* Attended sites following reports of flooding and supplied tankering support; completed CCTV investigations of the local sewer network to check for defects or blockages; engaged with residents post‑event; undertaking hydraulic modelling and evaluating rainfall data as part of the flood investigation. (p14) [PAG]

### Doncaster-Lincoln Line
* Surface water flooding occurred along the railway corridor adjacent to Queensway; the higher topography of the railway impounded runoff from the north of the catchment and contributed to water ponding south of the line, which led to internal flooding of nearby properties on Queensway. (p7) [IMG]
* Quant: 3 residential properties (p7) [IMG]

### Environment Agency
* Provided an emergency response crew and operated local flood-alleviation assets during the event; coordinated with other risk management authorities to determine the flooding was not fluvial from the River Ryton, advised that emergency evacuation due to fluvial risk was unlikely, and issued a Flood Alert for the River Ryton and Tributaries on 16th August 2022 at 18:27 (river warning thresholds were not reached). They also assisted the Canal & Rivers Trust in operating their assets. (p13)
* Participated as a Risk Management Authority in the incident investigations and jointly administers local Flood Warden scheme alongside Nottinghamshire County Council. (p14)

### Highway Authority
* Responded to the flood by providing sandbags to affected streets (Central Avenue and Priorswell Road), dispatched a gully-cleansing unit the following day to the most affected areas, and carried out cleansing and investigation of highway drainage assets on roads where properties or carriageway had been flooded; subsequent remedial works were scheduled/completed by end of October. (p13)

### Via East Midlands
* As VIA East Midlands Ltd (contractor for the highway authority) supported the response by providing sandbags to affected areas, attending with gully-cleaning equipment the following day, and undertaking cleansing and investigation of highway drainage assets where flooding had affected properties or the highway. (p13)

### Bassetlaw District Council
* Provided emergency response support during the flood event including access to sandbags/aqua sacs, provision of skips and staffed assistance to dispose of flood-damaged property, cleansing of detritus from roads/car parks/paths, relocation of affected tenants from council properties and repairs to affected council housing properties; actively engaged in the Section 19 investigation and supported residents/businesses post-flood. (p13)
