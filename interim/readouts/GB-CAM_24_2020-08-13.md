

# St Neots flooding 13 Aug 2020 - 16 Aug 2020

## Place names with coordinates

### Eynesbury
* {1947, 1998}: As one of the historic settlements forming modern St Neots, Eynesbury lies within the valley of the River Great Ouse and is therefore part of the area described as being at significant risk from fluvial (river) flooding and surface water flooding. Flooding is exacerbated when the River Great Ouse remains high and contributory watercourses and drainage cannot discharge efficiently. Associated major flood events occurred in 1947 and 1998. (p4) [MUL]
* {general}: Eynesbury is shown within Flood Zone 3 and Flood Zone 2 along the River Great Ouse and tributaries, placing residential and urban areas at risk of fluvial (river) flooding and surface water flooding; limited areas benefit from adjacent flood defences. (p5) [PAG, IMG]
* {general}: Residential and commercial areas in Eynesbury are at significant risk from fluvial (river) flooding and from surface water flooding. Flood risk is concentrated along the river corridors and tributaries; prolonged high levels in the River Great Ouse can prevent efficient discharge from contributory watercourses and interconnected gullies/sewers, exacerbating flooding in built-up areas. (p6) [PAG, IMG]
* Internal property flooding was reported within Eynesbury (Howitts Lane is named as a location where internal flooding occurred). (p9)
* One property on Howitts Lane reported internal flooding. External flooding was reported at Bishops Road, The Broad Walk and at Ridgeway. One property on Ridgeway reported no internal flooding itself but that neighbouring properties had flooded. (p10) [PAG, IMG]
* Gardens, outbuildings (including garages) and nearby gardens were flooded; some reports also note flooding inside other nearby homes. (p11) [TAB]
* Internal flooding of properties (water entered houses and gardens or outbuildings) (p12) [TAB, PAG]
* Internal property flooding was investigated after an extreme rainfall event. The LLFA liaised with the Highway Authority to arrange cleansing and surveying of the highway surface water drainage system and wrote to riparian landowners requesting ditch maintenance. Ditch clearance was carried out (April 2021) to gain access to a culvert; cleansing of the culvert was scheduled for 2021-22. The Highway Authority included gullies in a gully-jetting programme (April–June 2021) and increased gully maintenance frequency. (p14) [IMG]
* Internal flooding to residents investigated; contributory cause identified as a blind outfall for a surface water sewer in a resident's back garden that drained highway surface water. Highways Projects arranged rerouting of highway drainage into the Anglian Water surface water sewer to alleviate the issue. (p15) [TAB]
* Quant: Part of 7 properties reported flooded internally (p9)
* Quant: 1 property internal; 3 properties external (p10) [PAG, IMG]

### Eaton Socon
* {1947, 1998}: As components of St Neots located either side of the River Great Ouse, Eaton Socon and Eaton Ford lie within the area at significant risk of fluvial (river) flooding and surface water flooding. Flooding can be worsened when the River Great Ouse remains high, impeding discharge from contributory watercourses and drainage systems. The area has recorded major flood events in 1947 and 1998. (p4) [MUL]
* {general}: Eaton Socon is shown to lie within Flood Zone 3 and Flood Zone 2 adjacent to the River Great Ouse and tributaries, meaning properties and infrastructure are vulnerable to fluvial (river) flooding and surface water flooding; flood defences provide limited local benefit. (p5) [PAG, IMG]
* {1947, 1998}: As one of the settlements comprising St Neots and shown on the surface-water risk map, Eaton Socon is located adjacent to river corridors and is subject to surface water flood risk (high/medium/low bands along watercourses) and to fluvial flooding linked to the River Great Ouse and its contributory watercourses. (p6) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* Multiple properties within Eaton Socon experienced internal flooding as surface water drainage systems were unable to convey intense rainfall and water followed local topography into properties. (p9)
* Internal flooding of residential properties reported at Drake Road and Monarch Road, and three properties on Marchioness Way. External flooding was reported at Ackerman Street (two properties). (p10) [PAG, IMG]
* Multiple residential locations in Eaton Socon experienced flooding of inside houses, gardens and outbuildings (including garages) as recorded in the Central Eaton and North West Eaton sections. (p11) [TAB]
* Internal flooding of properties in Central Eaton Socon; surface water drainage system surcharged during the storm event and surface water manholes were damaged by surcharge. (p12) [TAB]
* Internal flooding was investigated on site; Anglian Water jet-cleaned the drainage system along the B1428 and the route of the surface water drainage from Drake Road was cleared. A blockage on the outfall from the highways surface water drainage system to Duloe Brook was investigated and cleared. Highway cleansing and gully-jetting works were scheduled/increased as part of the response. (p13)
* Quant: 3 properties (collective for the three Eaton Socon streets); part of 7 properties reported flooded internally (p9)
* Quant: 5 properties internal; 2 properties external (p10) [PAG, IMG]

### St Neots
* {1947, 1998}: Large areas of St Neots are at significant risk from fluvial (river) flooding and from surface water flooding. Flooding can be exacerbated when the River Great Ouse remains high for long periods, preventing contributory watercourses and interconnected highway gullies and surface water sewers from discharging efficiently into the river. The town has experienced major flood events in 1947 and 1998. (p4) [MUL]
* {1947, 1998}: Large areas of the town are at significant risk from fluvial flooding from the River Great Ouse and its six contributory watercourses; surface water flooding risk is also significant. Flood Zone 3 closely follows the river and tributaries and extends into residential and urban areas (including Eaton Ford, Eaton Socon and Eynesbury). Flood defences are limited and primarily protect central parts only. Historical major flood events affected the town in 1947 and 1998. (p5) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* {1947, 1998}: Large areas of the town are at significant risk from fluvial flooding (from the River Great Ouse and its main- and ordinary‑watercourse tributaries) and from surface water flooding. The highest surface-water flood risk is concentrated along the main river and its tributaries; flooding is exacerbated when the River Great Ouse remains high for prolonged periods and contributory watercourses, highway gullies and surface water sewers cannot discharge efficiently into the river. (p6) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* Experienced intense rainfall on 13 and 16 August 2020 that put extreme pressure on local drainage systems and produced high water volumes in watercourses and drainage systems, increasing flood risk across the settlement. Hardened, dry ground preceding the events reduced soakage and worsened surface water runoff. (p7) [MUL]
* Between 13 and 16 August 2020 intense rainfall overwhelmed drainage networks in the town. Surface water sewers and road drainage were inundated, causing flooding from highways gullies, surface water on roads and surcharging of sewers. Floodwaters followed local topography and entered properties in areas at risk of surface water flooding. (p8)
* Across the town seven properties were reported to have flooded internally (Drake Road; Monarch Road; Marchioness Way — 3 properties; Howitts Lane (Eynesbury); Hempsals (Eaton Ford)). Six further properties reported external flooding (Ackerman Street — 2 properties; Bishops Road (Eynesbury); The Broad Walk (Eynesbury); Ridgeway (Eynesbury)). (p10) [PAG, IMG]
* St Neots area recorded multiple instances of residential flooding across suburbs (Eaton Socon, Eynesbury and Eaton Ford) with houses, gardens and outbuildings affected. (p11) [TAB]
* Two consecutive extreme rainfall events on 13 and 16 August 2020 produced very intense storms that overwhelmed local drainage, causing surface water flooding across St Neots. Highways drainage and sewer systems came under extreme pressure; investigation and maintenance actions were initiated and a residents Flood Liaison Action Group (FLAG) was set up to work with risk management authorities. (p19)
* {1947, 1998}: Quant: six contributory watercourses; two historically recorded major flood events (1947 and 1998) (p6) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* Quant: 7 properties (p8)
* Quant: 7 properties internal; 6 properties external (p10) [PAG, IMG]

## Topics without coordinates

### Anglian Water
* During 13–16 August 2020 intense rainfall caused surcharging of Anglian Water sewers, contributing to local flooding where sewer systems discharged or were unable to convey the volume of surface water. (p8)
* Anglian Water's surface water drainage system in the area was affected by the storm: the system was jetted and the route of the surface water drainage from Drake Road was cleared after surcharge-related problems. (p12) [TAB]
* Owner of the surface water sewer whose blind outfall in a back garden contributed to internal flooding; liaised with the LLFA and Highways Authority Project Team regarding the surface water scheme to address the flooding. (p15) [TAB]
* Anglian Water's foul sewers and surface water drains were put under extreme pressure during the 13 and 16 August 2020 storms; sewer blockages (commonly caused by fat, oil and grease (FOG) and unflushable items) contribute to flooding and pollution incidents. Anglian Water operates a 24-hour helpline for reporting and is undertaking maintenance and community work (Keep it Clear) to reduce blockages. (p19) [PAG]
* Quant: more than 50PCSYM reduction in blockages (average) reported from community work in high-risk areas (p19) [PAG]

### Highway Authority
* Investigated and cleared a blockage on the outfall from the highways surface water drainage system, and arranged cleansing/surveying of the highways drainage network; included gullies in a jetting programme to address highway-related surface water flooding caused by the storm. (p12)

### Huntingdonshire District Council
* Confirmed maintenance schedules for Duloe Brook (vegetation maintenance and regular inspection of grills) to manage and reduce flooding risk following the storm-related flooding. (p12) [TAB]
* Confirmed that maintenance schedules are in place for Duloe Brook (autumn vegetation maintenance; fortnightly inspection of grills and additional inspections when extreme weather is forecast). Liaised on cleansing/surveying of highway surface water drainage following the flooding. (p13)
