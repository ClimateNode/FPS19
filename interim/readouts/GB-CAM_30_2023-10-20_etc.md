

# Earith flooding 20 Oct 2023 and Earith flooding 05 Jan 2024

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Earith<br>021\_Cambridgeshire County/021\_2023\_Cambridgeshire County/Earith-October-2023-and-January-2024-Section-19-Flood-investigation-report.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Earith
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: The village of Earith experienced surface water flooding concentrated in southern and central parts, with reported impacts to properties (external premises significantly affected) and compromised sanitation/toilet use. The map and report note a history of surface water flooding in this location. (p1) [IMG, TAB, MUL]
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: Surface water flooding occurred in the Earith area during heavy rainfall (19–20 October 2023 and 3–5 January 2024). The affected area is low-lying with a distinct depression (shown in the LiDAR DTM), which led to water accumulation and higher-than-modelled incidence of flooding; residents were visited by the LLFA on 31 October 2023. The report notes that observed flooding exceeded the mapped surface-water risk and that some areas marked as high risk were less severely affected, indicating extremely localised factors influenced where flooding occurred. (p2) [PAG, MUL]
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: High-intensity rainstorms caused surface water flooding in Earith, flooding at least four properties. Foul inspection chambers were reported surcharging with ingress of surface water (foul elements present in some flooded gardens/outbuildings), indicating interaction between surface water and the foul sewer network. (p3) [MUL]
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: Intense, repeated rainfall on 20 October 2023 and 5 January 2024 overwhelmed the sewerage network and caused water to accumulate at topographic low points in Earith. Local carriageway drainage and gullies may have been compromised by silt/debris (last cleaned Sept 2023), there is a private piped drainage connection into an inspection chamber on Meadowlands Park whose outfall is unknown, and flooding is a recurring issue requiring further investigation (including non-return valve effectiveness on Bridge End). (p4) [TAB, MUL]
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: Quant: 4 flood reports (October 2023); 5 properties reported further flooding (January 2024); 1 report included internal property flooding; 3 properties deployed pumps (p1) [IMG, TAB, MUL]
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: Quant: 4 properties (p3) [MUL]

### Bridge End, Earith
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: Properties on Bridge End and Vermuyden experienced surface water flooding that significantly affected external premises. One report included internal property flooding; sanitation and ability to use toilets were compromised. Some households deployed pumps to prevent internal flooding. (p1) [MUL]
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: On 20th October 2023 surface water accumulated at the low point on Bridge End (reported depth ~230mm). Gardens and outbuildings were flooded with surface water that included foul elements (e.g. toilet paper). Residents reported the local Anglian Water pumping station threshold had an issue on that date, affecting pumping performance. (p3) [MUL]
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: Intense, repeated rainfall overwhelmed the sewerage network and accumulated at topographic low points on Bridge End. Road gullies may have been partially silted, carriageway drainage was a focus for inspection, and previous installation of non‑return valves to protect properties on Bridge End is being reviewed — indicating properties and the highway were affected by flooding during the events. (p4) [TAB, MUL]
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: Quant: 4 flood reports (October 2023); 1 report with internal property flooding; 3 properties deployed pumps; 5 properties reported further flooding (January 2024) (p1) [MUL]
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: Quant: 230mm depth (p3) [MUL]

### Vermuyden, Earith
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: Properties on Bridge End and Vermuyden experienced surface water flooding that significantly affected external premises. One report included internal property flooding; sanitation and ability to use toilets were compromised. Some households deployed pumps to prevent internal flooding. (p1) [MUL]
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: On 20th October 2023 surface water accumulated at the low point on Vermuyden (reported depth ~230mm). Foul inspection chambers on Vermuyden were overwhelmed by surface water ingress and were reported as surcharging; residents described the surcharging water as appearing “clean” (i.e. surface water ingress into foul chambers). (p3) [MUL]

### River Great Ouse
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: Reported impacted areas and the highest surface-water flood risk are concentrated adjacent to the Great Ouse; surface water flooding affected properties on Bridge End and Vermuyden with significant external flooding and compromised sanitation. (p1) [IMG, MUL]
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: Quant: 4 flood reports (October 2023); 1 internal property flooded; 3 properties deployed pumps; 5 properties contacted the LLFA in January 2024 (p1) [IMG, MUL]

### Meadowlands Park
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: A private piped drainage system from nearby properties connects to an inspection chamber on Meadowlands Park; the outfall and current configuration are unrecorded. The public open space's surface water system is to be CCTV surveyed and targeted for remedial/riparian works, implying it was implicated in the area's poor drainage during the flood events. (p4) [TAB, PAG, MUL]


## Topics without coordinates

### Anglian Water
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: Anglian Water confirmed a pumping-station threshold issue on 20th October 2023 that affected activation of the pump at the recreation ground serving the rear of the properties; the issue was resolved within two weeks. Residents reported foul inspection chambers were surcharging and receiving surface water ingress. Anglian Water previously surveyed the area (2022/23) and installed two non-return valves serving three of the affected properties and has agreed to inspect those valves and review the project. (p3) [IMG, MUL]
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: The sewerage network was overwhelmed by intense, repeated rainfall during the events, contributing to local flooding. Anglian Water is a named lead for investigating non‑return valve effectiveness and working with the LLFA on actions following the floods. (p4) [TAB, MUL]
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: Quant: two non-return valves serving three properties (p3) [IMG, MUL]

### Highways Authority
* {20 Oct 2023, 05 Jan 2024}: Cambridgeshire Highways owns a separate surface water system that drains carriageway water only. Road gullies on Bridge End were last inspected/cleaned in September 2023 and showed some silt/debris build-up that could have reduced flow. Residents reported gullies were draining on 20th October 2023. The Highways Authority was assigned to inspect gullies on Bridge End and undertake remedial works following the flooding. (p4) [TAB, MUL]
