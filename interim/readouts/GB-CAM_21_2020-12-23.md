

# Sawtry flooding 23 Dec 2020

## Place names with coordinates

### Sawtry
* {1947, 1998}: Significant areas of Sawtry are at risk from surface water flooding. Mapping shows that where IDB drains are throttled under the A1, both fluvial (river) and surface water flood risk is increased slightly upstream. The settlement previously experienced significant flood events in 1947 and 1998. (p5) [TAB, MUL]
* {general}: The main built-up area of Sawtry is largely outside the highest risk zones, but the map shows southern and eastern fringes of the town (especially toward Green End) adjacent to Flood Zone 2 and Flood Zone 3, indicating potential river-flood risk to peripheral parts of the town. (p6) [IMG]
* {general}: Residential areas show patchy surface water flood risk, with some streets and blocks falling into medium and high risk zones; roads and the nearby railway intersect flood risk zones indicating potential disruption to transport during surface water flooding events. (p7) [IMG]
* Surface water drainage systems were unable to convey runoff from intense rainfall on an already saturated catchment, resulting in excess surface water flowing over ground following local topography and affecting properties located in areas at risk of surface water flooding. (p8)
* Nine flood reports were received for Sawtry on 23 December 2020, including at least one internal property flood reported to the LLFA; Parish Council communication indicated more widespread external and internal flooding across the village and reports of foul manholes surcharging. (p10)
* Runoff from intense rainfall on 23 December 2020 caused significant overland surface water flooding and flooding from drainage systems that exceeded capacity in Sawtry; this prompted actions on riparian maintenance, highway drainage improvements and ongoing liaison with landowners and Risk Management Authorities. (p19)
* Quant: 9 flood reports; 1 internal property flooded; c.2 properties; c.4 properties (p10)

## Topics without coordinates

### Anglian Water
* Anglian Water surveyed, jetted and cleansed relevant sewer/surface-water assets at locations that flooded and is undertaking surface water investigations to reduce contributions to their systems; actions relate to addressing foul surcharges (toilets and manholes) and surface-water ingress. (p13) [TAB]
* {general}: Surface water and groundwater ingress into the sewerage system affecting the resilience of Anglian Water sewer structures; Anglian Water will investigate the resilience of existing structures in the area. (p16)
* {general}: Flooding originating from Anglian Water assets (surface water drains or foul sewers) leading to flooding and situations where flooding can prevent the proper function of toilets, showers and similar facilities; sewer blockages caused by fat, oil and grease (FOG) and unflushable items are a major cause of such flooding and associated pollution incidents. (p18) [PAG]

### Highway Authority
* Highways staff jetted, cleansed and exposed the outfall to a culvert beneath the highway at St Judith's Lane to address blocked drainage caused by surface water flooding; arranged cleansing of accessible surface water drainage gullies at flooded locations. (p13)
* Quant: External/Gardens up to a depth of 15cm (p13)

### Middle Level Catchwater Drain
* {general}: Mapped as the primary watercourse associated with the highest river flood risk in the area; the drain is linked to adjacent low-lying land that is at high risk of river flooding. (p6) [IMG]

### Fire Service Toilets
* Reported near-miss where floodwater was redirected due to Fire Service toilets surcharging; foul manholes were also surcharging. This surcharging redirected floodwater during the event. (p10)

### Cambridgeshire Highways
* {February 2021}: Highways drainage and associated assets were affected by surface water / overland flooding; Cambridgeshire Highways carried out works including jetting, cleansing and exposing an outfall to a culvert beneath the highway, surveying and cleansing relevant assets at flooded locations, and supporting excavation/exposure of a downstream watercourse to alleviate flows. (p14)

### Cambridgeshire County Council
* {February 2021}: Acting as the LLFA, the council investigated flooding, worked with riparian landowners to raise awareness of maintenance responsibilities, completed initial riparian maintenance and coordinated further maintenance and flood-alleviation actions (gully cleansing, jetting/cleansing of drainage assets, surveys) to address surface water / overland flooding. (p14)
