## Place names with coordinates

### Blenheim Close, Old Hurst
* {23 Dec 2020}: Flooding significantly affected external premises (gardens) and caused internal flooding to at least one residential property on Blenheim Close. (p10) [TAB]
* {04 Dec 2020, 23 Dec 2020}: Surface water flooding routed into this low-lying spot from the south and south‑west, causing internal flooding to one property and flooding to gardens of other properties. Water entered foundation level and exited via air bricks 8–16 cm above ground; three properties that had flooded on 4 December were pumped to prevent internal flooding on 23 December. (p14) [IMG, MUL]
* {December 2020}: Properties were flooded internally and gardens inundated; water depths reached approximately 15 cm. (p16) [TAB]
* {December 2020}: Reported areas of flooding prompted LLFA walkovers and meetings with residents and landowners known to have flooded. LLFA met the Highways Authority to investigate historical drainage changes and initiated CCTV survey works to establish the condition and extent of highway drainage; CCTV survey work was completed September 2021. (p17) [TAB]
* {04 Dec 2020, 23 Dec 2020}: The South - Blenheim Close Area is recorded in Table 3 as having groundwater flooding and fluvial (river) flooding associated with the December 2020 events (impacts linked to saturated ground and exceeded channel capacity). (p22) [TAB, MUL]
* {23 Dec 2020}: Quant: 1 property with internal property flooding (p10) [TAB]
* {04 Dec 2020, 23 Dec 2020}: Quant: one property (internal flooding); five residential properties (garden flooding to a maximum depth of c.30 cm); air bricks showing flow at 8–16 cm; one resident reported having to leave premises for 5–10 days (p14) [IMG, MUL]
* {December 2020}: Quant: c.15cm (depth) (p16) [TAB]

### Grove Park, Old Hurst
* {23 Dec 2020}: Floodwater significantly affected external premises (gardens) and caused internal flooding to residential properties in the Grove Park area. (p10) [TAB]
* {23 Dec 2020}: Surface water flooding occurred where three watercourses converge at the eastern corner of Grove Park; ditches exceeded channel capacity and contributed to overland flows toward nearby properties. A culvert under Grove Park was observed (April and September 2021) and was clear of large debris in Sept 2021 but further desilting was identified as required. One watercourse had been altered without Land Drainage Consent; three small pipes installed within that watercourse were removed after the flood. (p12) [IMG]
* {23 Dec 2020}: Internal flooding of properties, gardens and outbuildings with floodwater reaching about 30 cm depth; at least one resident was displaced from their home. (p13) [TAB]
* {December 2020}: During the winter 2020 flooding the Grove Park area experienced watercourse-related flooding. The LLFA undertook walkovers of reported flooded locations, inspected topography and relevant features, and met residents and landowners known to have flooded. The LLFA also met riparian landowners on site to confirm required watercourse management and the need for Ordinary Watercourse Land Drainage Consent and planned to write to riparian owners to clarify responsibilities under the Land Drainage Act (1991). (p17) [TAB]
* {23 Dec 2020}: Quant: 2 properties with internal property flooding (p10) [TAB]
* {23 Dec 2020}: Quant: two residential properties; maximum internal flood depth of c.30cm; residents unable to return for 5-10 days; three small pipes removed (p12) [IMG]
* {23 Dec 2020}: Quant: c.30 cm depth; 1 resident displaced for 5-10 days (p13) [TAB]

### Old Hurst
* {Feb 2014, December 2020}: Localized inundation of low points in the village (one to the north and one to the south) from surface water. To the north, the confluence of two unnamed ordinary watercourses adjacent to residential properties can flood when runoff exceeds bank capacity. To the south, runoff from arable land can overwhelm surface water ditches and flow toward residential properties. Flooding has affected buildings: Cambridge Fire and Rescue Service recorded flooding of properties to the north in February 2014, and the northern location flooded again during the Winter 2020 event. (p4) [MUL]
* {04 Dec 2020, 23 Dec 2020}: Surface-water flooding affected Old Hurst during intense heavy rainfall on 23rd December 2020. Saturated ground and a high water table following a very wet autumn and December produced very high runoff and resulted in significant surface-water flooding in and around the village. Surface-water flooding had also been reported to the LLFA for areas to the south of the village on 4th December 2020. (p7) [MUL]
* {December 2020}: An area within/adjacent to Old Hurst flooded in winter 2020. A ditch to the south and east of the flooded area had been poorly maintained for many years and is expected to have contributed to the flood risk; LLFA intervention and follow-up maintenance (including removal of a previously blocked piped access crossing) were carried out to improve conveyance. (p15) [IMG]
* {04 Dec 2020, 23 Dec 2020}: Significant overland surface water flooding across saturated farmland and from ditches which exceeded channel capacity during December 2020; groundwater and fluvial (river) flooding were also identified in village areas. The LLFA has engaged riparian owners and is taking/considering maintenance and enforcement actions to reduce future risk. (p22) [MUL]
* {Feb 2014, December 2020}: Quant: 2 domestic properties; 1 commercial property (p4) [MUL]

### East Anglia

(p7) [PAG, MUL]

### Anglian Water
* {December 2020}: Following the winter 2020 flooding Anglian Water continued to monitor the resilience of its structures in the area. Piped sections of the watercourse associated with Anglian Water were identified for periodic inspection and cleansing to manage silt and maintain drainage function in response to the flood impacts. (p17) [TAB]

### Cambridgeshire County Council
* {December 2020, February–November 2021}: As the Lead Local Flood Authority, investigated the winter 2020 flooding in Old Hurst: met with the Highways Authority to examine historical drainage changes, initiated and completed CCTV survey work to establish highway drainage condition (completed September 2021), liaised with riparian owners about maintenance and reinstatement of obstructed watercourses, and coordinated onward reporting to responsible management authorities. (p18) [MUL]

### Environment Agency
* {December 2020}: Identified as a responsible management authority expected to receive the investigation report and provide strategic oversight and leadership for flood risk management following the winter 2020 flooding in Old Hurst, per its role under the Flood and Water Management Act 2010.
