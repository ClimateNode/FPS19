

# Thurgarton flooding 18 Oct 2023 - 20 Oct 2023

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Thurgaton – Storm Babet, October 2023<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/thurgaton-s19-storm-babet-oct-2023.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Thurgarton
* Internal flooding of residential properties; gardens and outbuildings also flooded. Flooding occurred quickly because the ground was already saturated from persistent rain prior to the event. (p2) [PAG]
* {18 Oct 2023 - 20 Oct 2023, unknown}: Internal flooding occurred in two residential clusters within the village; flooding was primarily caused by Thurgaton Beck bursting its banks and over-topping, with surface water runoff contributing. Areas affected include the zones around Beck Street / Station Road and Priory Park and an area near Bleasby Road/Thurgaton Village Hall. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Mapped Flood Zone 3 (highest risk) follows the main river and extends into the village; the text and maps indicate the village experienced flooding corresponding to those flood zones during the event. (p4) [IMG]
* Quant: 17 residential properties (p2) [PAG]

### Nottinghamshire
* Major Incident with significant flood impacts across the county: verification of internal flooding at properties, delivery of sandbags, provision of 24-hour emergency planning support, collation and publication of verified flood impact data, and closure of roads to reduce risk. (p6)
* Quant: 77 roads (p6)

### Newark and Sherwood
* District experienced or prepared for flooding that required an intensive multi-departmental response: the council stood up a management team ahead of the storm and staff worked throughout the flood event to prioritise and deliver response activities. (p6)

### Nottingham Road, Thurgarton
* Surface water flooding affected the village road network (Nottingham Road/A612 runs through Thurgaton), contributing to flooding of roads and nearby properties and likely causing access issues during the event. (p1)

### Beck Street, Thurgarton
* Area around Beck Street experienced internal flooding when Thurgaton Beck overtopped; identified as part of the residential cluster affected during the event. (p3) [IMG]

### Station Road, Thurgarton
* Station Road and the surrounding residential area were flooded as a result of Thurgaton Beck overtopping during the event. (p3) [IMG]

### Priory Park, Thurgarton
* Priory Park was within the area flooded when Thurgaton Beck burst its banks; it forms part of the residential cluster affected by internal flooding in the village. (p3) [IMG]

### Bleasby Road, Thurgarton
* One of the two residential clusters affected by internal flooding during Storm Babet is located near Bleasby Road and Thurgaton Village Hall. Residential areas adjacent to Bleasby Road were highlighted as internally flooded on the map; surface water runoff and local drainage/topography contributed to this localized urban/internal flooding. (p3) [IMG]

### River Trent
* Predicted extreme river levels created a continued risk of river flooding which prompted the council to coordinate in-person visits to communities at high risk and reinforced the need for immediate preparatory actions. (p7)


## Topics without coordinates

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Responded to a major flood event that caused internal flooding to residents and businesses across the county: verified and recorded internal flooding on site, provided support, guidance and advice to affected residents and businesses, delivered sandbags to affected areas, closed roads to promote safety and avoid further flood damage, collated and published verified flood impact data, provided 24-hour emergency planning support, chaired/attended Strategic/Tactical/Recovery coordination groups, and led the Section 19 Flood Investigation and delivery of the DEFRA Property Flood Resilience Repair Grant Scheme. (p6)
* Mobilised a large multi-departmental team to prepare for and respond to the flood event; staff worked beyond normal hours, visited impacted communities and vulnerable individuals, delivered aqua sacs, assisted evacuations, provided emergency accommodation, coordinated community visits where River Trent levels were predicted to be extreme, led clean-up of roads/paths and bulky waste removal after flooding, contacted affected individuals to identify humanitarian needs, administered recovery grants and ran internal and multi‑agency debriefs. (p7)
* As Lead Local Flood Authority, the council has witnessed community-devastating flooding, is prioritising vulnerable people, supporting flood wardens and community volunteers, and has procured equipment to monitor water levels so groups can respond to rising levels in the local watercourse. (p8) [PAG]
* Quant: 77 roads closed (p6)
* Quant: £50k capital contribution; many hundreds of aqua sacs were delivered (p7)

### Isle of Axholme Internal Drainage Board
* Identified as part of the IDB districts where the regular annual maintenance regime was delivered and continued to reduce flood risk and identify potential risk areas during/after the flood event. (p7)

### A612
* The A612 (Nottingham Road) lies within the area where heavy rainfall and saturated ground resulted in flooding of roads and properties during Storm Babet. The road is therefore described as experiencing road flooding during the event in Thurgaton. (p1)

### Thurgaton Beck
* The watercourse burst its banks (overtopped) during the event, spilling out of its channel and directly flooding the areas around Beck Street, Station Road and Priory Park; it is identified as the primary cause of the flooding. (p3)

### Trent Valley
* Part of the IDB districts where regular maintenance was delivered and where TVIDB had staff on site during and after the flood event to reduce risk and identify potential risk areas. (p7)
