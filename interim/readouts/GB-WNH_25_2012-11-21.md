### Port Road, Upper Harlestone
* Internal flooding of property(ies) on Port Road. Excess surface water from intense rainfall on a saturated catchment flowed over ground following natural contours to areas around the watercourse at Port Road, causing internal flooding. (p5) [PAG]
* Excess surface water flowed over ground following natural contours to areas around the watercourse at Port Road; internal flooding of at least one property on Port Road was reported. (p6) [PAG]
* Floodwater overtopped the public highway, flowing in and around properties on Port Road and adjacent fields; at least one property experienced severe internal flooding to ground floor living areas; residents required temporary alternative accommodation. (p8) [PAG]
* The public highway became full of surface water flowing to the low point at the culvert; water was retained within the verge and kerbs, increased in depth and led to overtopping of a residential driveway. (p9)
* The public highway became full of surface water flowing to the low point at the culvert; water was retained within the verge and kerbs, gained depth as rain continued and overtopped a residential driveway. (p10)
* {20 Dec 2012}: Surface water from agricultural fields to the south and east flowed onto Port Road; strong flows bypassed many road gullies and travelled up to a low point adjacent to a culverted watercourse without entering the drainage system, contributing to highway and residential property flooding. (p15)
* Adjacent fields either side of the watercourse west of Port Road ponded during the flood incident; this ponding prompted recommendations to investigate and reinstate historic drainage and to assess highway drainage (gullies, carriageway levels and verge grips) to reduce runoff/ponding affecting the highway and nearby properties. (p16)
* Carriageway crossfall and drainage arrangement directs surface water runoff toward nearby properties, creating areas of flood risk to property. Historic road gullies and highway drainage in this location have been found buried (under approximately 1 m of carriageway verge), which may impair drainage function and maintenance and increase surface water reaching properties. The culvert inlet under Port Road has a grille arrangement that is vulnerable to debris ingress, increasing the risk of blockage and consequent surface water flooding. (p17)
* {20 Dec 2012}: Ponding of surface water occurred on the left side of the carriageway near the roadside gully; water accumulation and damp patches were visible on the road surface, indicating drainage inefficiency or blockage of the highway drainage/culvert system beneath Port Road. (p35) [IMG, PAG]
* Roadside drainage (culvert and debris screen) was observed intercepting and conveying runoff from the road; visible water flow at the culvert entrance indicates the drainage feature was actively managing surface water from the road. (p36) [IMG, PAG]

### Upper Harlestone
* Flooding occurred in the village as a result of intense rainfall on a saturated catchment; properties (including those on Port Road) experienced internal flooding and surface water followed natural contours toward the watercourse. (p6) [PAG]
* People and property were affected by flooding on 21 November 2012. Fields either side of the watercourse became ponded; surface water in the public highway deepened and a residential driveway was overtopped. A residential property experienced internal flooding of the ground-floor living area (a previous incident reported to have manifested in the same way as the 21 Nov 2012 event). (p9)
* The report discusses flooding in the locality: rear yards, access ways and gardens of properties were flooded; emergency services attended (Fire Service) and highway works were carried out on Port Way to manage flow; overland flow entered properties and later drained away into the watercourse/Fish Pond. (p11)
* Flooding occurred in the village during the Flood Incident as a result of intense rainfall. Residents were identified as being at risk of flooding and were advised to take property-level protection and community resilience measures (sign up to Flood Warning Direct, nominate flood wardens, produce flood plans, implement flood protection, move valuables to higher ground). (p14) [PAG]
* {20 Dec 2012}: Intense short-duration rainfall on a saturated catchment overwhelmed existing drainage systems and watercourses, causing ponding and overspill that led to flooding of the public highway and residential properties in the village. (p15)
* Quant: at least one property (p6) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 residential property (internal ground-floor flooding); 1 driveway overtopped (p9)


## Topics without coordinates

### Port Way
* Excavation works carried out in the south west verge adjacent to the watercourse to try to allow water from the public highway to flow directly into the watercourse (response to highway surface water/overland flow). (p11)

### Fish Pond
* Water in the watercourse slowed and was unable to flow away freely at the Fish Pond due to debris and weirs, causing water to become very deep at this location. (p10)
* Received a faster flow of water when a downstream debris collection reportedly gave way, allowing flood water to flow away into the Fish Pond. (p11)
* {20 Dec 2012}: A poorly defined channel and significant debris at the silt trap upstream of the Fish Pond caused water to slow and back up at the culvert, contributing to upstream blockage/backing-up of water and associated flooding. (p15)
* Silt trap is installed immediately upstream to intercept sediment-laden water and protect pond water quality; the presence of high turbidity and sediment upstream indicates active sediment delivery toward the pond. (p38) [IMG]

### Northamptonshire Fire and Rescue Service
* Attended while flooding was occurring; were unable to pump water away from residential properties due to constant heavy inflow from driveway entrances; opened manhole covers and the culvert access to allow drainage; left site for approximately one hour during which overland flow ceased entering the property; remained to assist clearing flood water from internal areas of a residential property (property drained via open manholes and natural flow into the watercourse/silt trap). (p11)
* Quant: one hour; one residential property (p11)
