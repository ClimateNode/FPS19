

# Lancashire flooding 01 Dec 2015 - 31 Dec 2015

Reference document:<br>December 2015 Floods In Lancashire: Section 19 Investigation<br>069\_Lancashire County/section-19-flood-investigation-report-december-2015-floods.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Lancashire
* Extreme storms and rainfall in November–December 2015 caused flooding throughout December 2015 affecting communities across the county. Approximately 2,500 homes across 229 separate communities were flooded. Floods also damaged private gardens, garages and out-buildings, business premises, agricultural property and critical public infrastructure including roads, bridges, retaining walls, water treatment plants, power and communications installations, schools, village halls and town halls. (p3)
* Widespread flooding across the county during December 2015 that required emergency response and recovery actions by Risk Management Authorities. (p7)
* Experienced widespread and extreme-magnitude flooding during December 2015 that affected infrastructure, commercial properties and the local economy, with a particular emphasis on internal flooding of residential properties. Investigations were required across many communities to account for flood mechanisms and remedial actions. (p12)
* {01 Dec 2015 - 31 Dec 2015, December 2015}: Significant and widespread flooding across the county from storm rainfall, affecting infrastructure, commercial properties and the local economy; many residential properties experienced internal flooding; highways and critical infrastructure were reported flooded in places. (p13)
* {December 2015, 01 Dec 2015 - 31 Dec 2015}: Extensive increases in river flows across the county in response to persistent heavy rainfall through November–December 2015; contributed to flood events investigated in the report and exceptional river flows recorded in the North West. (p18) [IMG]
* {December 2015, 01 Dec 2015 - 31 Dec 2015}: Experienced fluvial (river) flooding with an additional surface water element during the event on 5/6 December 2015; river levels rose following the rainfall peak, reducing the ability of surface-water systems to discharge and resulting in internal flooding to domestic properties, mainly in the north of the county. (p20) [IMG]
* {December 2015, 01 Dec 2015 - 31 Dec 2015}: Northern Lancashire experienced fluvial (river) flooding with an additional surface-water element during the December 2015 events. Rivers were at capacity and overtopped, causing out-of-bank flow and backup/surcharge of surface drainage systems producing visible surface water on roads, gardens and causing internal flooding to domestic properties. (p21) [IMG, PAG]
* Rising river levels on 25–26 December caused river channels and receiving drains to become unable to discharge. Drainage systems surcharged (backed up) against elevated stream and river levels, causing flooding of properties. The majority of affected locations were caused by fluvial flooding or a combination of fluvial and surface water runoff. (p23) [IMG]
* County-wide flooding during December 2015: 2,467 homes across 115 towns, villages and the city of Lancaster were flooded (7,500 people directly affected); 229 separate communities identified for further investigation; several major roads were flooded and not passable; bridges were closed due to concerns over structural integrity; roads closed due to collapse of retaining walls, landslips or deteriorated surfaces; disruption to rail services requiring repairs; electricity substations in Lancaster were flooded causing widespread power loss. (p25)
* During the December 2015 floods parts of Lancashire experienced widespread flooding: 2,467 homes across 115 towns, villages and the city of Lancaster were flooded (about 7,500 people directly affected); 229 communities/sub-communities were identified for further investigation; several major roads were flooded and impassable; bridges were closed over concerns about structural integrity; roads were also closed due to retaining wall collapse or landslips and required repairs before re-opening; rail disruption occurred when a landslide on the West Coast Main Line required repairs before services could resume; electricity substations were flooded, leaving 61,000 homes in Lancaster without power. (p26) [PAG]
* County-wide flooding during December 2015 that prompted multi-agency response, investigations (Stage 2) and bids for flood-reduction funding; led to increased data collection and collaborative risk-management activity by local RMAs and the LLFA. (p27)
* Widespread December 2015 flooding affected communities across the county, prompting emergency response, investigations led by the LLFA and other RMAs, and some remedial works. Early investigations were disrupted by additional flooding later in the month; recovery and coordinated programmes of further investigation and mitigation were initiated. (p28) [PAG]
* {December 2015, 01 Dec 2015 - 31 Dec 2015}: Unprecedented December 2015 rainfall exceeded drainage and river capacities so significant volumes of water flowed above ground, damaging property and public assets and limiting travel, public services and business operations for many days. (p30) [PAG]
* {December 2015, 01 Dec 2015 - 31 Dec 2015}: During December 2015 prolonged heavy rainfall and saturated ground caused main rivers to flow out-of-bank (notably on 5–6 December) and surface water drainage systems to surcharge (notably on 25–26 December). Significant volumes of water flowed above ground, damaging property and public assets, restricting travel and limiting public services and business operations for many days, and causing many households to be displaced while repairs and restoration were carried out. (p31)

### Lancashire
* County-wide impacts from December 2015 storm events (notably 5/6 Dec and 25/26 Dec) documented by EA flood extent maps and District Summary Reports: residential properties flooded internally in affected communities, and some transport and utilities disruption reported. (p14)

### Lancaster
* Clusters of internal flooding to domestic properties occurred on 5 December (north-west Lancashire), with fluvial flooding the predominant source; high river levels led to out-of-bank flow and surcharge of drainage systems producing surface water on roads and gardens. (p21) [IMG, MUL]
* The city was among places flooded during the December 2015 events; flooded electricity substations in Lancaster resulted in 61,000 homes being without power. (p25)
* Quant: 61,000 homes (p25)

### Garstang
* Garstang is identified as a major cluster of flooded properties on 5 December; flooding was predominantly fluvial with associated surface-water surcharge as rivers overtopped and drainage systems backed up, causing internal property flooding and surface water on roads/land. (p21) [IMG, MUL]

### River Lune
* Recorded the largest ever flows on an English river during the November–December 2015 events, representing an extreme river flood peak. (p18)
* Recorded exceptionally high flows in December 2015 relative to long-term averages; these elevated flows contributed to higher river levels and increased flood risk and impaired surface-water drainage capacity during the early-December storm period. (p19) [IMG]

### River Eamont
* Observed exceptionally high river flows in December 2015 compared with long-term averages; contributed to elevated river levels that reduced the capacity of surface water systems to discharge during the Storm Desmond period (early December 2015), increasing local flood risk. (p19) [IMG]

### River Wharfe
* Recorded exceptionally high flows in December 2015 versus long-term averages, contributing to elevated river levels and increased flood risk during the early-December storm period; this would have reduced the ability of surface-water systems to discharge locally. (p19) [IMG]

### River Severn
* The Upper Severn showed exceptionally high river flows in December 2015 (well above the long‑term average), indicating elevated flood risk and contributing to the high regional river levels recorded during the event. (p19) [IMG]

### West Coast Main Line
* A landslide during the December 2015 storms/flooding disrupted rail services on the West Coast Main Line; repairs were required before services could resume. (p25)


## Topics without coordinates

### Lancashire County Council
* Acted as the local highway authority responsible for surface water drainage on adopted public roads and was involved in the emergency response and recovery to the December 2015 floods in Lancashire. (p7)
* Lancashire County Council (via its framework consultant) analysed rainfall, river flow and storm events leading to December 2015 flooding (notably storms on 5/6 Dec and 25/26 Dec) and led the Stage 1 Section 19 investigation into those flood incidents; coordinated investigation and RMA responsibilities for the county response. (p14)
* {general}: As the Lead Local Flood Authority the Council carried out Section 19 investigations following the December 2015 floods, coordinated risk management authorities (RMAs), collated data from the events, and progressed detailed investigations and community engagement to address impacts and recovery across affected locations. (p31)
* {general}: Quant: 229 locations (p31)

### Local Resilience Forum
* The Local Resilience Forum coordinated multi‑agency response and major-incident management for the December 2015 floods: managed debrief and multi‑agency reviews, arranged/attended drop-in events (January–February) to support flooded residents and businesses and to gather eyewitness reports, and reviewed/exercised Responsible Managing Authorities' flood-response functions and follow-up investigations and asset repairs. (p26)
* Quant: 2,467 homes; 7,500 people; 229 separate communities and sub-communities (p26)

### Environment Agency
* Produced EA-recorded GIS flood extent maps and District Summary Reports documenting the extent of flooding during the December 2015 storms (including events on 5/6 Dec and 25/26 Dec). Records linked flooded properties (residential properties flooded internally in some communities) and identified main-river flood extents and some extents from other sources. (p14) [PAG]
