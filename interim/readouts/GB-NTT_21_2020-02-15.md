

# Cropwell Butler flooding 15 Feb 2020 - 16 Feb 2020

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Cropwell Butler – February 2020<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/cropwellbutlersection19reportfebruary2020.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Cropwell Butler
* On 16 February 2020, 15 properties on Hardigate Road, Main Street and Back Lane experienced internal flooding following the prolonged period of heavy rainfall. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Village-wide impacts in three localized clusters (Hardigate Road, Main Street, Back Lane) where converging unnamed watercourses and saturated ground after prolonged heavy rainfall led to internal flooding of properties. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Multiple local watercourses and drainage systems were overwhelmed by surface water runoff and tributary flows, causing water to exit watercourses and drainages into properties and public areas. The Grove tributary (fed by A46 balancing ponds) exceeded capacity and discharged into the rear boundaries of an estate, entering properties on Hardigate Road; convergence of several catchments and the Radcliffe Road tributary led to flooding at Back Lane including overtopping caused by a blocked trash screen; gullies and manholes on Main Street surcharged and seven properties on Main Street flooded from the front (with additional bow-wave damage from vehicles). (p4)
* Quant: 15 properties (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 15 properties (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 5 properties on Hardigate Road; 3 properties on Back Lane; 7 properties on Main Street (p4)

### Nottinghamshire
* During Storm Dennis (15–16 February 2020) there were 38 flood warnings and 16 flood alerts in place across Nottinghamshire. (p1)
* Sustained further intense rainfall on 16 February 2020 (ground already saturated) that resulted in extensive flooding across the Nottinghamshire area. (p2) [IMG]

### Rushcliffe
* Communities in Rushcliffe experienced surface water flooding when runoff exceeded the capacity of drainage systems; Rushcliffe Borough Council provided emergency response support, delivered sandbags, managed recovery and administered hardship and recovery grants and relief schemes. (p5)

### Hardigate Road, Cropwell Butler
* Internal flooding of residential properties; water entered properties from the rear after the Grove tributary exceeded capacity and exited the rear boundaries into the estate. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Floodwater exited the rear boundary of the estate where the Grove tributary passed, entering and flooding properties from the rear; flows originated from the Grove tributary which exceeded capacity and takes some flows from A46 surface water balancing ponds. (p4) [PAG]
* Quant: 5 properties (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 5 properties (p4) [PAG]

### Main Street, Cropwell Butler
* Internal flooding of properties in the Main Street area as part of the village-wide event where local watercourses and highway drainage converged in the village centre. (p3) [IMG]
* Seven properties were flooded from the front as drainage gullies and manholes surcharged and the system was overwhelmed; additional damage occurred from bow waves generated by vehicles driving through floodwaters on the road. All local watercourses converge at Main Street before flowing under the Village Hall. (p4)
* Quant: 15 properties (combined across Hardigate Road, Main Street and Back Lane) (p3) [IMG]
* Quant: 7 properties (p4)

### Back Lane, Cropwell Butler
* Internal flooding of properties in the Back Lane area as part of the broader village flooding where unnamed watercourses and surface runoff concentrated in the centre. (p3) [IMG]
* Three properties in a natural low spot were flooded when converging catchments and the Radcliffe Road tributary overtopped; at the rear of one property a blocked trash screen caused overtopping that flooded an adjacent property and flowed into Back Lane. Blocked gullies and over-capacity downstream drainage contributed to the flooding. (p4)
* Quant: 15 properties (combined across Hardigate Road, Main Street and Back Lane) (p3) [IMG]
* Quant: 3 properties (p4)
