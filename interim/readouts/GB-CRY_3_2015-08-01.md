### Croydon
* Severe surface water flooding in August 2015 affected multiple locations across the Borough, impacting commercial and residential properties and major transport routes. (p6)
* Widespread surface water flooding across the borough in August 2015; a significant number of calls were placed to emergency services by the public about risks to properties. The report identifies pump failure and surcharge of surface water sewers as contributing factors increasing flood risk in affected sites. (p7) [PAG]
* {20 Jul 2007, August 2015}: The borough has experienced high‑intensity surface water / flash flood events. On 24 August 2015 the Borough received a short, high‑intensity rainfall event across Croydon that produced a high risk of surface water flooding. Separately, the most significant recent recorded surface water flood affecting the Borough occurred on 20 July 2007 when intense rainfall caused flash floods across numerous locations in the Borough; Purley town centre experienced significant flooding to property and the transport network. (p10) [MUL]
* Short, intense rainfall on 24 August 2015 caused significant surface-water flooding across the Borough. High rainfall intensity exceeded the capacity of road gullies and drain gratings, producing runoff that ponded in low-lying areas. The road drainage network was put under pressure, resulting in lifting of sewer manhole covers. Pump failure at the Purley Cross Underpass caused flooding within the underpass and led to backing up of the sewer system. (p11) [TAB, PAG]
* Widespread surface-water and urban flooding across the borough during the August 2015 event: highway drainage was overwhelmed, drainage systems surcharged and manhole covers lifted, causing rapid onset of surface-water flooding at multiple locations (including roundabouts, underpasses and roads). (p12) [TAB, PAG]
* Intense, high‑intensity rainfall across the urbanised borough overwhelmed highway drainage and sewer networks causing widespread surface water flooding and sewer surcharge. Pump failure in a pedestrian underpass caused additional local ponding. Impacts included five commercial properties flooded at Purley Oaks Road; temporary closure of roads and pedestrian access (including access to Purley Oaks train station); nuisance flooding of roads and footpaths borough‑wide; and some external residential property flooding (garages and driveways) reported by the London Fire Brigade. (p15) [IMG, PAG]
* During the intense August 2015 rainfall the borough experienced multiple localised surface-water flooding incidents: nuisance flooding of roads and footpaths, pooling on residential streets (photographed at Harewood Gardens and Warren Road), some external residential property flooding (garages and driveways), and surcharge/backing-up of highway drainage where gullies and drains were blocked. (p16) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 5 commercial properties (p15) [IMG, PAG]

### Purley
* Experienced localised highway and surface water flooding during the torrential rain in August 2015, contributing to transport and property impacts noted in the report. (p6)
* {20 Jul 2007}: Purley town centre experienced significant flooding to property and the transport network during an intense surface water/flash flood event. (p10) [PAG]
* Multiple locations in Purley experienced flooding during August 2015: highway flooding on the northern and western sides of the roundabout; manhole surcharge on Purley Oaks Road (Station Approach) with subsequent flooding of commercial premises; residential flooding reported at Brancaster Lane (including garage flooding at 46 Brancaster Lane); highway flooding on Sunnydene Road and multiple-source flooding (manhole surcharge, sewer and highway) on Old Lodge Lane. (p12) [TAB, PAG]
* Heavy rainfall overwhelmed the highway drainage at Purley Cross roundabout causing the drainage system to surcharge and lift manhole covers. Surface water flooded across the roundabout and the pedestrian underpass beneath the roundabout became rapidly inundated. The underpass flooding was exacerbated by a pumping system failure. Flooding on surrounding roads (including northern and western sections of the roundabout) caused travel disruption. (p13) [IMG]
* Localised surface-water flooding was reported at multiple locations in Purley (examples in the report include Lower Barn Road and Warren Road), causing road and footpath inundation and instances of external residential flooding (garages/driveways) where reported. (p15) [IMG, PAG]
* Localised surface water flooding from high intensity rainfall that exceeded highway drainage capacity. Reported impacts include nuisance flooding of roads and footpaths, and some external residential property flooding (garages and driveways). Flooding was exacerbated in locations by blocked gullies and drainage bottlenecks (notably issues identified on Purley Oaks Road). (p16) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: five properties (commercial) flooded (Purley Oaks Road) (p12) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: approximate depth of 2.4m in the pedestrian underpass (p13) [IMG]

### Purley Cross
* Pedestrian underpass and approaching footpaths flooded after the highway drainage network at the roundabout was overwhelmed and surcharged; underpass flooding was exacerbated by failure of the pumping system. (p12) [TAB, PAG]
* The Purley Cross area (central intersection and surrounding roads) experienced surface water flooding during the event; area identified as a topographic depression where surface water flowed overland when drainage capacity was exceeded, contributing to widespread urban flooding and travel disruption around the junction. (p13) [IMG]
* High intensity rainfall exceeded gully and drain capacity causing overland surface water flows. The downstream sewer network backed up, surcharged and flooded to significant depths in low-lying areas. The pedestrian underpass flooded when its pump failed. Transport for London drainage assets on the A22/A23 and the pedestrian underpass were among the most affected. (p14) [PAG]
* Surface water exceeded highway drainage capacity and flowed overland causing flooding at Purley Cross including the underpass; pumps and tanker operations were used to remove floodwater, pump control panel was at risk/submerged and sandbags were placed, cleanup of silt/debris undertaken and pump repairs/upgrade works planned. (p17) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* Suffered surface-water flooding on 24 August 2015; drainage engineers removed flood water by tanker and repaired pumps as part of the immediate response. (p18)
* Drainage network upstream and downstream of the pump station did not have sufficient capacity to manage the rainfall; TfL-operated pumps at the pedestrian underpass failed due to flooding of the pump control box, exacerbating flooding at this location. (p19) [PAG]
* Quant: approximate underpass flood depth 2.4 m (reported as ~1 ft (0.3 m) below underside of bridge with 2.7 m clearance) (p12) [TAB, PAG]

### Thames Water
* The report indicates surcharge of surface water sewers and a pump failure may have contributed to inundation of commercial properties at locations such as Purley Oaks Road during August 2015, implicating TWUL assets in the event. (p7)
* During the extreme rainfall on 24 August 2015, TWUL assets and the sewer network were identified as potentially interacting with surface water drainage at Purley Oaks station and Purley Cross. The investigation recommends further engagement with TWUL to determine whether sewer condition or affiliated assets influenced local flood risk, to review soakaway outfall pipe size at Purley Oaks station, and to plan system upgrades with LBC where flood risk is greatest. (p19) [PAG]
