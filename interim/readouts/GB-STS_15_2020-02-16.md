### River Swarbourn
* During Storm Dennis on 16th February 2020 the River Swarbourn rose and was implicated in flooding of Yoxall; bank overtopping and high flows contributed to inundation of the village centre (affecting Main Street and Bond End). (p18) [PAG]
* {general}: The Swarbourn (main river through Yoxall) controls flood levels affecting the village. Small weirs, the Town Hill Bridge and the weir at Town Hill Road influence flood levels in Yoxall; the Bond End Footbridge downstream also significantly impacts flood levels. Much of Yoxall lies within Flood Zone 3 (>=1PCSYM annual probability) because of proximity to the Swarbourn. (p19)
* {01 Jul 1922 - 31 Jul 1922, 1953, 01 Aug 1987 - 31 Aug 1987, 01 Nov 2000 - 30 Nov 2000, 16 Feb 2020}: The River Swarbourn has produced notable flood events that inundated Yoxall (historic events listed include July 1922, 1953, August 1987 and November 2000). The 1987 and 2000 events caused extensive flooding to the centre of Yoxall and damage to properties and transport routes. On 16 February 2020 peak river levels corresponded with resident reports of internal property flooding in Yoxall. (p22) [PAG, MUL]
* The River Swarbourn rose and overtopped/escaped its channel onto the floodplain west of the channel, backing up at the Town Hill Bridge and causing floodwater to spill into gardens and contribute to internal flooding of properties on Main Street. (p23) [PAG]
* The River Swarbourn rose and topped its banks and was identified as the main driver of the flooding in Yoxall, contributing to internal property flooding and surcharge of the local drainage system. (p25)
* The Swarbourn catchment experienced high flows during the event; the 1.91 m stage corresponds to roughly 15–16 m3/s and produced flood conditions that affected downstream communities including Yoxall. (p27) [PAG]
* The River Swarbourn recorded its highest level on record at the Hoar Cross monitoring station during the event, with rapid rise and fall of river levels indicating a river-flood incident affecting the catchment upstream of Yoxall. (p28) [IMG, PAG]
* Recorded the highest river level since records began (1.91 m) at the Hoar Cross monitoring station on 16 February 2020; river stage and flows indicate a large flood event in the catchment and contributed to elevated river levels through Yoxall upstream/downstream influence. Modelling and rating-curve analysis give mixed estimates of event severity but document unusually high river levels on that date. (p29) [IMG]
* High River Swarbourn levels in February 2020 backed up against structures (Town Hill Bridge and Bond End Footbridge), reduced conveyance capacity and contributed to flooding in Yoxall; upstream weir likely increased upstream water levels and worsened the event. (p30) [PAG]
* Record high flows in the River Swarbourn during February 2020 caused water levels to rise and flood surrounding land, contributing to internal flooding of properties in Yoxall (including Bond End) by backing up against structures in the channel. (p32) [IMG]
* River Swarbourn was identified as the main source of flooding in Yoxall; flows larger than local drainage capacity contributed to ingress into the highway system and river flood waters were directed into gardens and properties. (p33)
* The River Swarbourn caused flooding in Yoxall on 16 February 2020. Flow was constricted by Town Hill Bridge and Bond End Footbridge which increased local flood levels and backwater; water also backed up against an upstream weir, contributing to flooding and channel issues. (p34) [TAB, PAG]
* Identified as a source of river flooding in Yoxall; water backed up against channel structures contributing to flooding of the village. River levels during the February 2020 event were approximately a 5PCSYM AEP (1 in 20-year) event and the channel at key structures was identified as having insufficient capacity to convey flows. (p38)
* Quant: 1.91 m; approximately 15–16 m3/s (p27) [PAG]
* Quant: 1.91 m river level; flow approximately 15–16 m3/s (rating curve); modelled peak discharge 8.1 m3/s at 16:15 on 16th February 2020; rating-curve equivalent 16.1 m3/s (5PCSYM AEP / 20-year) (p28) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 1.91 m river level; flow approximately between 15 and 16 m3/s (rating-curve estimate); peak discharge modelled at 8.1 m3/s at 16:15 hrs on 16 February 2020; 5PCSYM AEP (20-year) reference of 16.1 m3/s (p29) [IMG]

### Town Hill Bridge
* The bridge and roundabout at Town Hill Bridge lie at the heart of the area reported as flooded on 16th February 2020; the junction area was within the flood-affected zone and would have been impacted by floodwaters and high flows (reported/likely inundation and flow-related disruption). (p18)
* {01 Nov 2000 - 30 Nov 2000}: During the November 2000 flood one of the three arches of Town Hill Bridge became blocked by a fallen tree, causing water to back up against the structure and contributing to flooding upstream. (p22)
* The bridge acted as a significant constriction in the River Swarbourn channel; water backed up against it in the late hours of 15 February and began to spill from the channel into gardens and properties (contributing to internal flooding on Main Street) in the early hours of 16 February. (p23) [PAG]
* Acted as a constriction in the river channel that backed up floodwater, contributing to overtopping and flow into adjacent properties on Main Street. (p24) [PAG, IMG]
* During the February 2020 event floodwater backed up against Town Hill Bridge, identified as the main mechanism that led to properties on Main Street flooding; bridge constriction reduced channel conveyance. (p30)
* {16 Feb 2020, 28 Jan 2021}: River flow backed up against the bridge, creating a constriction that reduced channel capacity and contributed to flooding of properties on Main Street during the February 2020 event; the structure and upstream weir were identified as factors that can raise upstream water levels. Inspections reported no visible blockage during the February 2020 event (and a May 2019 inspection showed no blockage). (p31) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* The Town Hill bridge/structure in the channel was examined after the February 2020 event; no significant blockage was found to be a major cause, but the structure still allows flood waters to back up against it and thus exacerbates flooding during extended rainfall. (p32)
* Town Hill Bridge strongly influenced flood levels in Yoxall during the 16 February 2020 River Swarbourn flood; it can trap large debris increasing flood risk (it is also a Grade II listed structure), and special inspections after flood events were recommended. (p34) [TAB]
* Large debris became trapped at the bridge during flood events, causing blockages and leading to recommendations for special inspections after floods and assessment of upstream weir removal to reduce backing-up against the structure. (p35) [PAG]
* Water backed up against the Town Hill Bridge during the February 2020 event, contributing to localised flooding; the channel at the bridge was identified as having insufficient capacity for a ~5PCSYM AEP event. (p38)
