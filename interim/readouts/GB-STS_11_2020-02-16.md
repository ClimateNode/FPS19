### Marchington Brook
* Marchington Brook (the local watercourse flowing through Marchington and designated main river downstream of Church Lane bridge) is implicated in the flooding at Church Lane, with flooding occurring in the brook corridor and adjacent low-lying land near its confluence with a drainage ditch. (p19) [PAG, IMG]
* Marchington Brook drains the ~16 km2 catchment and conveys flows through a sequence of culverts (upstream culvert discharging into the brook, culvert beneath Church Lane opposite the Dog and Partridge public house, culverts beneath Green Lane and through a railway embankment) before joining the River Dove ~240 m downstream. The catchment is described as steep and responsive to heavy rainfall and superficial deposits can exhibit high water tables during high flows. (p20) [IMG, PAG]
* Marchington Brook has a long history of flooding; it overtopped/caused flooding affecting Church Lane and surrounding areas, with highway drainage discharging into the brook under the road bridge and no formal flood defences in place. (p21) [PAG]
* {2012}: The brook has a long history of causing flooding: culvert capacity issues under Church Lane cause flows to back up and spill onto the highway and into properties. Flood water has been observed leaving the channel upstream of Church Lane bridge and flowing across fields toward the drainage ditch at Silver Lane; downstream right-bank overtopping has sent floodwater across fields toward gardens on the northern side of Church Lane. The 2012 flood was attributed in part to water from Marchington Brook. (p22) [PAG]
* {general}: Marchington Brook is the watercourse associated with Flood Zone 3 along its banks; the brook's floodplain places adjacent low-lying areas and parts of Church Lane and Silver Lane at higher risk of river flooding. (p23) [IMG]
* Marchington Brook is described as a source/contributor to the flooding that led to floodwater entering nearby properties in Marchington on 16 February 2020. (p24) [IMG]
* The brook reached exceptionally high levels and exceeded its capacity at several points upstream and downstream of Church Lane, causing river water to spill from the right bank downstream of Church Lane bridge and flow across fields towards the Dog and Partridge pub and gardens behind properties on Church Lane. (p25)
* Elevated river levels during the February 2020 event caused the brook to exceed the capacity of the culvert beneath the road (threshold described at 1.4m), leading to water spilling onto Church Lane and contributing to local flooding and overwhelmed local drainage. (p29) [IMG]
* The brook responded rapidly to intense rainfall, rising to very high levels and causing flooding: it exceeded the culvert capacity beneath the road and spilled onto Church Lane, contributed to surcharging of the local surface water drainage which prevented discharge and led to surface water flooding of the highway, and overflowed into adjacent fields creating additional flow routes toward Silver Lane. (p30) [PAG, IMG]
* Anecdotal reports and observations described water spilling from the right bank of Marchington Brook downstream of Church Lane bridge and flowing across fields toward the Dog and Partridge and the gardens of properties on Church Lane. When Marchington Brook floods, elevated river levels also prevent highway drains from discharging, contributing to local highway and property flooding and allowing bow-waves from vehicles to push water into lower-threshold properties. (p31)
* Marchington Brook was unable to convey the high volumes of water during the prolonged rainfall on 16th February 2020. River levels rose to exceptionally high levels, causing flooding to surrounding land and contributing to flooding of residential and commercial properties in Marchington. High river levels also backed up into highway drains, depositing silt at downstream pipe ends and promoting ingress of water into the sewer network. (p32) [PAG]
* Identified as the main source of flooding in the village; the brook contributed to flooding of surrounding land and residential and commercial properties in Marchington. (p33)
