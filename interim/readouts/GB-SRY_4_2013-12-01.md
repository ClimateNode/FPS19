### Tandridge
* Internal property flooding at multiple addresses and road closures reported in the district. (p2) [TAB, PAG]
* {December 2013 - January 2014, 01 Dec 2013 - 31 Jan 2014}: The area experienced a combination of fluvial, surface water, groundwater and sewer flooding caused by unprecedented winter 2013/14 rainfall (275PCSYM of an average winter), resulting in internal property flooding and other impacts across the district. (p8)
* The report records flooding across Tandridge during winter 2013/14, with internal property flooding occurring within the area. (p11) [IMG]
* {December 2013 - January 2014, 01 Dec 2013 - 31 Jan 2014}: The district experienced local surface water flooding and fluvial flooding during the winter 2013/14 storms as a result of intense rainfall on already-saturated soils and sharp rises in river flows. (p12) [PAG, MUL]
* Residential properties experienced internal, basement and garden flooding; sewage backed up into WCs and sewage was present in gardens. Four properties were evacuated. Eight properties were without WC facilities for over six weeks. Thirteen businesses reported flooding or were impacted; three commercial premises were vacated during the flooding. Along the course of the Caterham Bourne, 1,086 homes suffered power cuts. The A22 was closed (main access) and a temporary pedestrian bridge was constructed to assist schoolchildren; the railway line into Caterham was also flooded. (p15)
* {December 2013 - January 2014, 01 Dec 2013 - 31 Jan 2014}: Flooding from the Caterham Bourne and associated surface water/sewer flooding caused internal, basement and garden flooding of residential properties; sewage backing up into WCs and sewage in gardens; evacuation of some homes; businesses and infrastructure were disrupted. Power supplies to homes failed along the Caterham Bourne corridor. The A22 (main access into Croydon) was closed and the railway line into Caterham flooded. A temporary pedestrian bridge was installed on the A22 to maintain access to school. Kenley Water Works (just outside the district) was threatened by contaminated flood water and large pumps ran continuously to reduce flood levels, posing a risk to local water supply. (p16) [PAG]
* Tandridge District was particularly affected by flooding during winter 2013/14, prompting local response and strategic coordination. (p18)
* {December 2013 - January 2014, 01 Dec 2013 - 31 Jan 2014}: Tandridge was part of the area affected by the Winter 2013/14 floods; key drainage assets in Tandridge were identified and added to the flooding asset register as part of the post-event response and asset management. (p19) [PAG]
* The Caterham Bourne rose following heavy rainfall and a rise in groundwater levels, causing flooding of roads and properties in Tandridge, including road closures and internal property flooding reported in the Smallfield sub area. (p40) [MUL]
* {December 2013 - January 2014, 01 Dec 2013 - 31 Jan 2014}: Tandridge experienced widespread flooding during winter 2013/14, including internal property flooding caused by surface water, ordinary watercourse (riparian ditches), sewer and groundwater sources. (p42)
* {December 2013 - January 2014, 01 Dec 2013 - 31 Jan 2014}: During the winter 2013/14 flood event, lack of maintenance of riparian watercourses and ditches contributed to flood impacts across the district; this prompted collection of historic flooding data, prioritisation of drainage maintenance works and recommendations for further studies and schemes. (p43)

### Surrey
* {December 2013 - January 2014, winter 2013/14}: The western part of Tandridge (within the Upper River Mole catchment in Surrey) experienced sharp rises in river flows and fluvial flooding from the River Mole and its tributaries, together with local surface water flooding during the late‑December 2013 event; fluvial flood warnings were issued across the south east. (p12) [MUL]
* {winter 2013/14, December 2013 - January 2014}: Parts of Surrey within the River Mole and upper River Eden catchments experienced unusually high river flows (peaking around a 1 in 50 annual chance in the Mole) and prolonged surface water ponding during winter 2013/14, producing flooding across affected catchment areas. (p14)
* Flooding occurred across the county during winter 2013/14, triggering strategic response and flood risk management actions by RMAs. (p18) [PAG]
* {December 2013 - January 2014, 01 Feb 2014 - 28 Feb 2014}: County-level response to the Winter 2013/14 floods: inspected and cleansed flood-affected roads, carried out ditching, cleared trees and debris, coordinated recycling and distribution of sandbags, operated a call centre, administered the Repair and Renew grant for flooded residents and businesses, and coordinated response meetings (Strategic and Tactical groups). Surrey Fire and Rescue Service pumped flood waters to protect residents, property and infrastructure; extensive carriageway damage was assessed for inclusion in the Project 400 resurfacing programme. (p19) [MUL]
* Caterham Bourne flooding (driven by heavy rainfall and raised groundwater) caused flooding of roads and properties in Surrey. (p40) [PAG, MUL]
* Surrey experienced major, widespread flooding during winter 2013/14 driven by exceptional rainfall, with flooding from surface water, ordinary watercourses, sewers and groundwater. (p42) [PAG]

### River Mole
* Fluvial flooding occurred on the River Mole following intense rainfall in late December 2013. Rapid rises in river flows from the Mole and its tributaries contributed to flood events and led to flood warnings in the area. (p12) [MUL]
* {December 2013 - January 2014, winter 2013-14}: Experienced repeated high-flow (flash) events during winter 2013–14. The river produced very sharp peaks in discharge, contributing to the flooding encountered over the period; the duration and repetition of high flows exacerbated the scale of flooding. (p13) [IMG, TAB, PAG, MUL]
* Recorded and estimated peak flows during winter 2013/14 reached around a 1 in 50 year annual chance, resulting in high river flows and river flooding on the River Mole. (p14)
* {December 2013 - January 2014, winter 2013-14}: Quant: Maximum peak flow in excess of 60 m3/sec (Horley, 23–24 Dec 2013); flow of 10 m3/sec was exceeded 26 times over the winter; water levels reported to rise at approximately 0.3 m per hour and return to regular level about 10 hours after peaks. (p13) [IMG, TAB, PAG, MUL]
