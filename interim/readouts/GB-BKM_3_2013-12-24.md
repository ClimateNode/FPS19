

# Bishopstone flooding 24 Dec 2013 - 14 Feb 2014

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Bishopstone, Aylesbury 24th December 2013 – 14th February 2014<br>018\_Buckinghamshire County/018\_2014\_Buckinghamshire County/bishopstone-aylesbury\_Feb\_2014.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Buckinghamshire
* Surface-water flooding occurred in and around Bishopstone during 24 Dec 2013 - 14 Feb 2014, with the greatest flooding in the south‑east of the village. Flood depths during the event matched the surface-water flood maps: most of the built-up area experienced shallow to moderate inundation, while deeper inundation was concentrated adjacent to streams, ponds and other low-lying watercourse corridors to the south and south‑east of the village centre. (p12) [IMG]
* Buckinghamshire County Council (the LLFA) was contacted by residents who experienced internal property flooding and referred them to AVDC; BCC coordinated local flood risk management duties during the event. (p18)

### Bishopstone
* {general}: Floodwater covered roads and property access in the village; vehicular access blocked and gated entrances submerged, causing access disruption and road closures. (p1)
* Prolonged above-average rainfall saturated surrounding land and increased surface water flows into highway drainage and main rivers. Unmaintained rivers, undersized culverts and tight river bends caused rivers to overtop; floodwaters used the highway as a secondary flow route. (p4)

### Aylesbury
* {general}: Flooding covered roads in the Aylesbury area, resulting in road closures and at least one partially submerged vehicle visible, disrupting access and transport. (p1)

### The Rookery
* The Rookery area experienced the deepest surface-water inundation in the village during the event; map predictions (which the report states reflect the observed flooding) show the highest predicted depths concentrated near The Rookery and to the east of the village centre. (p11) [PAG, IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Standall's Ditch
* Standall's Ditch (the main river flowing through the southeast of Bishopstone) was part of the village drainage network during the investigated flood incident. The channel through the village has been altered (two right‑angled bends and a culvert under the road were noted), features that were identified in the report as relevant to how water moves through the village and which may have influenced the flooding that prompted the investigation. (p7) [PAG]
* Standall's ditch (the local watercourse) reached capacity due to large quantities of surface water runoff from surrounding fields, causing excess water to flow along and across the highway. Flooding inundated verges and residential gardens, rendered a roughly 400 m length of highway impassible, and in some events led to internal flooding of properties. This behaviour is reported as the cause of the winter 2013/14 (Dec 2013–Feb 2014) flooding in Bishopstone. (p13)
* Standall's Ditch (the main river flowing through southeast Bishopstone) reached capacity during the 24 December 2013 - 14 February 2014 rainfall period. Excess runoff from saturated fields caused the ditch to overtop/use the highway as a flow path, with the culvert under the road (point B) and the highway drainage running full to capacity and unable to convey the full volume. Highway ditches were also at capacity and appeared poorly maintained, reducing conveyance and contributing to overland flow through the village. (p14) [PAG]
* The main river Standall's Ditch breached at a ninety-degree bend as it enters Bishopstone; water escaped the channel and used the highway as a secondary flow path into the village and to the lowest spot, contributing to internal flooding of properties and making the road impassable. (p15) [PAG]
* Standall's Ditch experienced high water levels from excessive rainfall; upstream overgrowth, ninety-degree bends, undersized culverts and downstream silt/fallen trees reduced conveyance, causing the watercourse to back up and contribute to flooding of the highway and properties. (p16) [MUL]
* Standalls Ditch (turning into Scotsgrove Brook) is identified as a Main River and is implicated in fluvial flooding in the Bishopstone area; responsibility for these river flood impacts lies with the Environment Agency. (p18)
* Culverts under access drives (section between A and B) were undersized, reducing the flow capacity of Standall's Ditch, causing water to back up and contributing to the flooding. (p21) [PAG]
* Standall's Ditch (downstream of the culvert) was identified as silted/obstructed and contributing to flooding in Bishopstone; recommendations included a levels survey, one‑off channel clearing, re‑grading/de‑silting, and investigation of culvert constrictions and trash‑screening. (p22) [TAB]
* Quant: six severe flood events; roughly 400 meters; three events caused internal flooding to properties (p13)
* Quant: at least two properties (p15) [PAG]

### Transport for Buckinghamshire
* TfB closed the only road through Bishopstone on 24th December 2013 and 7th February 2014 due to floodwaters; the closures remained in place for two to three days after each event while flood water subsided. (p15)
* Received reports from residents of dangerous flood water in the highway and closed the road on both severe flood incidents. (p17) [MUL]
* Transport for Buckinghamshire was identified as responsible for highway drainage and culvert issues that contributed to flooding: recommended actions included surveying the culvert to locate diameter reductions, considering increasing the culvert outfall size, cleansing gullies/highway drainage as part of a maintenance schedule, and investigating/maintaining a trash screen at the culvert inlet. (p22) [TAB]
* Quant: two road closures; two to three days (p15)
* Quant: 2 road closures (p17) [MUL]

### Scotsgrove Brook
* Scotsgrove Brook receives flow from Standall's Ditch immediately south of Bishopstone and, as the downstream main river, was part of the drainage system during the village flood incident investigated. It was therefore implicated in the context of the flooding incident affecting Bishopstone. (p7)
* Scotgrove Brook and associated ditches had high water levels due to excessive rainfall and contributed to the local flooding and to water backing up into the highway and properties. (p16) [MUL]

### Aylesbury Vale District Council
* Coordinated the Repair and Renew grant scheme for flooded properties in the Aylesbury District and has responsibilities to inspect and maintain watercourses on district land and respond to requests for assistance during flood events. (p18)
* Received reports of flooding in Bishopstone and was identified as the authority to assist affected residents (e.g. aid applications for Repair and Renewal Grant); acted as a point of contact for documenting and reporting flood incidents. (p22) [TAB]

### Buckinghamshire County Council
* As the Lead Local Flood Authority, responsible for coordinating management of local flood risk from surface water, groundwater and ordinary watercourses; involved in handling surface-water-related impacts and directing residents to grant support following internal property flooding. (p18)
* Identified as responsible for local flood risk management actions: ensuring culvert and watercourse owners are aware of responsibilities, facilitating enforcement under the Land Drainage Act, considering upstream flood alleviation/land management, and coordinating maintenance of open watercourse sections to reduce flooding impacts in Bishopstone. (p22) [TAB]

### Environment Agency
* Took a lead role in technical response actions: proposed and/or carried out surveys of Standall's Ditch and culverts, recommended one-off channel clearing/re-grading/de-silting downstream of culverts, proposed vegetation-management demonstration for landowners, distributed guidance booklets to riparian owners, and considered enforcement under the Land Drainage Act to remove obstructions contributing to flooding. (p22) [TAB]
