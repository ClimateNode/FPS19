### Top Valley
* Internal flooding to residential properties in the Bakewell Drive, Tobias Close and Tenter Close area of Top Valley as a result of the intense storm. (p3)
* The central valley area (Top Valley) experienced concentrated surface-water/urban flooding in the lowest elevation zones, with flood locations on Bakewell Drive, Tenter Close and Tobias Close leading to internal flooding of properties on 23rd July 2013. (p7)
* An intense convective storm on 23rd July 2013 (peak at 17:00) triggered surface water / urban flooding in the Top Valley area; residents recalled very heavy rain (lasting up to an hour) and the flood event started during the storm. (p10)
* The wider Top Valley area experienced saturated green open spaces and much of the storage in the surface water/sewer network was filled before the main storm, contributing to rapid overland flow and ponding during the intense rainfall on 23 July 2013. (p11) [PAG]
* Area-wide rapid surface water runoff concentrated into valley-bottom streets (e.g. Bakewell Drive) and footpaths, causing overland flows and internal flooding of properties in the neighbourhood. (p13) [PAG]
* Intense rainfall produced large volumes of surface-water runoff that overwhelmed drainage, causing ponding in low-lying areas and causing internal flooding of properties on streets in the area (Bakewell Drive, Tenter Close and Tobias Close). Downstairs of affected properties required complete renovation (flooring, kitchens, walls, doors, plaster, furniture). Evidence indicates sewers were also surcharging and contributing to surface-water volumes. (p16)
* The low-lying Top Valley area experienced internal flooding of residential properties and extensive external flooding after intense rainfall saturated the ground and filled available storage in underground drainage and sewers. (p20)

* The report states a flood occurred in the City's area; flooding is described as having devastating impacts affecting people, property, businesses, the environment and transport and the event prompted a Section 19 investigation led by the City Council. (p4)
* Surface-water flooding affected the area of Nottingham containing Bakewell Drive, Tobias Close and Tenter Close on 23 July 2013. (p8)
* An intense storm moved across the north of Nottingham City on 23rd July 2013, initiating a surface water flood event in the northern parts of the city (including Top Valley). (p10)
* Intense, repeated rainfall over the city on 22–23 July 2013 saturated ground and overwhelmed drainage networks; a final event on 23 July caused the flooding of 31 properties in Top Valley and extensive external flooding in the city. (p20) [PAG]

### Nottingham City Council
* Responsible for managing surface water flood risk and maintaining highway drainage; maintains an annual gully-cleansing regime (each road gully cleansed annually; maintenance requests responded to within three working days). During the event highway drains and gullies were likely overwhelmed and surface water could not drain away because the public sewer network was at or near capacity. (p16) [PAG]
* Surface water was unable to drain into the highway drainage system during the 23rd July 2013 event and sewers were contributing to surface water volumes. The Council secured funding to part-fund property-level protection for affected residents, placed gullies (including those on Bakewell Drive and Tobias Close) on a Targeted Gully Cleansing Regime for more frequent maintenance, and continues ongoing gully maintenance. (p17) [TAB]
* A fourth intense rainfall event on 23rd July 2013 resulted in internal flooding of 31 residential properties on Bakewell Drive, Tenter Close and Tobias Close in the Top Valley area and extensive flooding of external areas. Properties affected are in low-lying parts of the natural valley; preceding heavy rainfall saturated the ground and filled underground drainage capacity. (p19) [PAG]
* Nottingham City Council, as the local authority responsible for surface water and highway drainage, was directly involved in the response and investigation into the Top Valley flooding. The Council worked with Severn Trent Water on an action plan following the event and focused on continued maintenance of drainage systems to reduce future risk. (p20) [PAG]
* Quant: 2 properties (p16) [PAG]

### Severn Trent
* Responsible for providing drainage via the public sewer network; has a programme of sewer inspections and blockage removal. Evidence from the event indicates the sewer network was at or near maximum capacity and sewers were flooding and contributing to surface water volumes. (p16) [PAG]
* Public sewers flooded during the 23rd July 2013 event and contributed to increased surface water volumes; Severn Trent recorded the 23 July 2013 flooding, undertook inspections and remedial actions such as blockage removal, and committed to monitor future internal and external flooding incidents to identify triggers for capital investment. (p17) [TAB, PAG]
* As the public sewerage provider, Severn Trent Water was a responsible Risk Management Authority for the incident in Top Valley; the interaction between surface water and public sewers contributed to flooding that resulted in internal flooding of properties. Severn Trent investigated the event in partnership with the City Council and concluded the extreme rainfall did not trigger its capital investment programme, so agreed actions focus on maintaining existing drainage to maximise performance during storms. (p20) [PAG]
