

# Little Oakley flooding 23 Dec 2020

## Place names with coordinates

### Mill Hill, Great Oakley
* Flooding occurring on the carriageway of Mill Hill near an affected property; this was associated with internal flooding to one property and flooding of external areas of other properties nearby. (p10) [PAG]
* Carriageway flooding reported frequently near the affected property. (p11) [PAG]
* Flows in the watercourse north of Mill Hill exceeded the capacity of the culvert beneath the road, causing the watercourse to overtop and flood the road. Floodwater depth on the carriageway increased and flowed toward an adjacent property, resulting in internal flooding of that property. (p15) [PAG]
* Flows in the upstream watercourse exceeded the capacity of the culvert under Mill Hill, overtopped the watercourse and flooded the carriageway. As water depth increased on the road it flowed east toward properties causing internal flooding of one property and external flooding to an adjacent property (south) and two properties on the north side. Blocked highway gullies to the east likely increased ponding at the low spot near the watercourse. (p16) [PAG]
* Standing water collected on the carriageway to depths that impeded use and reached nearby property. Highway drainage east of the property was blocked which likely increased flood volume at the low spot; culvert capacity was exceeded causing surcharged outfall and limiting discharge into the downstream watercourse. (p17)
* Water overtopped onto Mill Hill Road (sic) during the heavy rainfall on 23rd December 2020 and flowed to low points around residential properties, causing localised surface water flooding. (p19)

### Little Oakley
* Dwellings to the east of Little Oakley experienced overland surface water flow when drainage systems were unable to cope; this led to water flowing to low points in and around properties and internal flooding of at least one building on multiple occasions. (p6)
* Internal flooding occurred to one building (this has happened on more than one occasion within the last five years). External flooding entered gardens/surrounding areas and restricted access to at least three other dwellings. (p8) [MUL]
* Flooding to the east of the village associated with a tributary of Harper's Brook; one property experienced internal flooding and external areas of other properties were flooded. (p11)
* {general}: Areas of Little Oakley adjacent to the mapped watercourse lie within higher-risk surface water flood zones; residential and built-up areas near the watercourse are shown as being at elevated risk of surface water flooding. (p12) [IMG]
* {general}: Properties, roads and other infrastructure within the highlighted (red-circled) area overlap the blue flood zone and are at direct risk of inundation from the river and associated surface-water flooding. The risk is concentrated along the river course where it crosses the road in the circled area. (p13) [IMG]
* Intense heavy rainfall on 23rd December 2020 caused the culverted watercourse beneath Mill Hill to exceed capacity, leading to overtopping and surface water flowing along natural contours to low points around residential properties in Little Oakley. (p19)
* Quant: one building; more than one occasion within the last five years (p6)
* Quant: 1 building; 3 dwellings (p8) [MUL]
* Quant: 1 property (p11)

### Harper's Brook
* An ordinary tributary of Harper's Brook that forms the western boundary of an affected property overflowed and caused internal flooding to that property. The tributary discharges to Harper's Brook through a headwall with a 300mm pipe and flap valve, which is described in connection with the flooding. (p14)
* Harper's Brook was at or exceeding bank capacity in places and caused minor flooding to rear gardens behind properties, contributing limited flooding impacts separate from the culvert overtopping at Mill Hill. (p16)
* Higher-than-normal water levels in the brook deepened downstream of the culvert, which likely closed the flap valve in the headwall, producing a surcharged outfall and limiting the culvert/watercourse's ability to discharge—contributing to local flooding. (p17)
* High water levels in Harper's Brook during the event may have restricted downstream flow and contributed to the culverted watercourse exceeding capacity, worsening flooding on Mill Hill and to nearby properties on 23rd December 2020. (p19)
* Quant: 1 property (p14)
* Quant: 2 properties experienced limited rear-garden flooding from Harper's Brook (p16)

### North Northamptonshire
* The local authority was involved in coordinating and directing responses after the surface water flooding event, including planning-team actions, highway drainage cleansing and inspections, and working with the LLFA and other parties to prepare and share drainage asset plans and public information. (p24)

### Featherbed Lane, Little Oakley
* Surface water ran down Featherbed Lane (a farm track ~300 m east) and flowed onto Mill Hill, bypassing blocked road gullies and contributing to the volume of water collecting at the low spot by the watercourse, increasing flooding on Mill Hill and toward properties. (p16)
* Quant: 300 metres (distance from affected area referenced in report) (p16)

### Wootton Brook
* Runoff within the Wootton Brook catchment contributed to increased flows during the county-wide heavy rainfall event, increasing flood risk to Little Oakley and prompting a funding bid to test natural flood management measures to reduce flows into the village. (p18)


## Topics without coordinates

### Harpers Brook Catchments
* Runoff within the Harpers Brook catchments contributed to increased flows during the intense short-duration rainfall, increasing flood risk to Little Oakley and leading to inclusion in the funded RAIN project to test measures to reduce flows to properties. (p18)

### Highway Authority
* Addressed flooding impacts on the highway network by undertaking highway drainage cleansing across the catchment, identifying and planning highway drainage assets to share with the LLFA and community, increasing inspections and maintenance in areas where flooding has occurred or are shown as at-risk on surface water mapping, assessing asset capacity where runoff causes flood risk to properties and considering improvement works, assessing third‑party drainage receiving highway discharge, and assessing options for overland flow routes to safer areas. (p24)
