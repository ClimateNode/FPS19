

# Gotham flooding 14 Nov 2019

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Gotham – November 2019<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/section19reportgothamnovember2019.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Gotham
* Subject of the Section 19 investigation into the November 2019 event. Experienced significant rainfall on 14 November 2019 and was part of the extensive flooding that affected Nottinghamshire during that period. (p1)
* Internal property flooding, flooding of gardens and curtilages, and flooding of the public highway in parts of the village. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Internal flooding of properties across the village; many gardens and curtilages flooded; public highway flooded. (p3) [IMG]
* On 14th November heavy rainfall on saturated ground produced rapid surface water run-off which overtopped ditches and watercourses and in places bypassed watercourses, running straight from land into properties and onto the highway. The foul sewer network backed up during the event, with foul sewage coming out of private drains and internally flooding properties. (p4) [IMG]
* {2012, 2016, 2019}: Village has a history of flooding (incidents in 2012 and 2016) and was affected by the 2019 flood event. Properties were internally flooded during the event; community distributed sandbags from a local flood‑resilience store and volunteers worked to check and clear culverts and watercourses; post‑event surveys and drainage/culvert clearance works were carried out. (p6) [MUL]
* Community flood resilience store containing sandbags was used; local volunteers distributed sandbags and cleared/kept culverts and watercourses clear. These community actions reduced the number of properties internally flooded during the event; some properties were internally flooded. (p7) [PAG]
* Quant: 13 reported incidents of internal property flooding (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 13 reported incidents of internal property flooding (p3) [IMG]
* Quant: 5 properties (p4) [IMG]

### Nottinghamshire
* {07 Nov 2019 - 14 Nov 2019, 07 Nov 2019}: Extensive flooding across the county during 7–14 November 2019. The response included facilitation of road closures, deployment of flood signage and distribution of sandbags; a major incident was declared in response to the severe weather. (p1) [PAG, MUL]
* {01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019}: Widespread flooding across the county during the event period; Nottinghamshire Local Resilience Forum declared a major incident and there were additional surface water, fluvial and sewer flooding incidents in the following days. (p2) [IMG]
* {07 Nov 2019 - 14 Nov 2019, 07 Nov 2019}: Quant: 66 road closures; over 750 flood signs; over 5000 sandbags (p1) [PAG, MUL]
* {01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019}: Quant: 38 flood warnings; 16 flood alerts (p2) [IMG]

### St Andrews Close, Gotham
* Properties on St Andrew's Close (and adjacent Wallace Street and Curzon Street areas) were impacted by foul sewage / sewer flooding, contributing to internal property flooding recorded in the village. (p3) [IMG]
* Grouped with Wallace Street on the maps and shown close to or within predicted surface water impact zones. It is also part of the foul sewer network that drains southeast toward the treatment facility; the village experienced foul sewer backup on 14 November, indicating this street is connected to infrastructure that experienced capacity problems during the event. (p4) [IMG]
* Quant: 13 reported incidents of internal property flooding (village-wide) (p3) [IMG]

### Nottingham Road, Gotham
* Properties on Nottingham Road experienced internal flooding from surface water runoff. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Maps and flow-path analysis (Figure 5 and the EA surface-water map) show surface water flowing from higher ground toward Nottingham Road and identify it close to or within predicted surface-water impact zones; the report states surface water run-off entered properties and the highway during the 14th November event. (p4) [IMG]
* {2019}: Properties in the vicinity of Nottingham Road were affected by the flood; following the event survey and cleansing work was carried out on the culverted system near the flooded properties and modifications/maintenance to a culvert and watercourse on private land were secured. (p6)

### Leake Road, Gotham
* Properties on Leake Road experienced internal flooding caused by surface water runoff from surrounding higher ground. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Figures 6 and the EA surface-water map highlight a large blue-shaded/predicted impact area near Leake Road and flow-path arrows show water running from open fields toward the residential area around Leake Road, indicating surface-water accumulation/run-off onto land and potentially into properties and highway during the event. (p4) [IMG]

### Curzon Street, Gotham
* Properties on Curzon Street experienced internal flooding caused by foul sewage. (p3) [IMG]
* Shown on the EA surface water map as close to blue-shaded predicted surface water impact areas. The foul sewer network map shows sewer flows from Curzon Street toward the treatment facility; the village experienced foul sewer backups on 14 November, so Curzon Street is implicated as being vulnerable to both surface water run-off and sewer capacity/backflow issues. (p4) [IMG]

### Wallace Street, Gotham
* Properties on Wallace Street experienced internal flooding caused by foul sewage. (p3) [IMG]
* Shown on the EA surface water map as close to or within blue-shaded predicted surface water impact areas. Also identified on the foul sewer network map as a contributor to the system that discharges to the treatment facility; the foul sewer system experienced backups during the 14 November event (village-wide foul sewage backing up and emerging from private drains), indicating this street is both at risk from surface water run-off and connected to the sewer network that suffered capacity/backup issues. (p4) [IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Severn Trent
* Foul sewer capacity issues (suspected surface water ingress to the foul sewer) led to sewage backing up and emerging from private drains, causing internal flooding. (p5)
* {2019}: Provided emergency response support to residents during the flooding and is continuing to investigate foul sewer issues associated with the event; actively engaged in the Section 19 Flood Investigation. (p6) [PAG]
* Quant: 5 properties (p5)

### Via East Midlands
* {07 Nov 2019 - 14 Nov 2019}: On behalf of Nottinghamshire County Council, facilitated road closures, placed flood warning/signage and delivered sandbags as part of the immediate response to the November 2019 floods. (p1)
* {07 Nov 2019 - 14 Nov 2019}: Quant: 66 road closures; over 750 flood signs; over 5000 sandbags (p1)

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* {07 Nov 2019 - 14 Nov 2019}: Acted as the local authority/lead local flood authority for the area during the November 2019 event; Via East Midlands Ltd operated on its behalf to manage road closures, signage and sandbag distribution in response to the flooding. (p1)
* {07 Nov 2019 - 14 Nov 2019}: Quant: 66 road closures; over 750 flood signs; over 5000 sandbags (p1)

### Environment Agency
* {01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019}: Monitored water levels constantly during the period and issued flood warnings and flood alerts across Nottinghamshire. (p2)
* {01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019}: Quant: 38 flood warnings; 16 flood alerts (p2)

### Rushcliffe Borough Council
* {2019}: Provided emergency response support to residents, administered Flood Hardship Fund and Central Government flood grants, delivered sandbags, worked with the parish to stock the flood resilience store, managed community recovery and engaged in the Section 19 flood investigation following the flooding.
