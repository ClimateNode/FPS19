

# Slough Unitary Authority flooding 26 Aug 2015

## Place names with coordinates

### Farnburn Avenue, Slough Unitary Authority
* External (street) flooding occurred during the heavy rainfall event; inundation was rapid and was exacerbated by the same local failures (failed pump station, collapsed storm overflow pipe, blocked drains and high runoff). (p4) [TAB, PAG]
* External flooding was recorded on 26 August 2015 as a result of the surface water flooding event. (p5) [PAG]
* External (road) flooding occurred on Farnburn Avenue during the intense rainfall/flash-flooding event; standing water was observed on the road. (p6) [PAG]
* Rapid inundation of the street during the 26th August 2015 storm; the speed and extent of flooding were exacerbated by a failed Thames Water pump station, a collapsed Thames Water storm overflow pipe, blocked highway drains/gullies, and high volumes of surface water runoff from nearby buildings and carparks. (p7)
* Water flowed into a low spot on Farnburn Avenue from a low level sewer chamber and surface water runoff from the north-east corner of the SEGRO estate; flooding was worse because the Thames Water pump station was not operational. (p9) [PAG]
* Gullies on Farnburn Avenue drain to soakaways (east and newly installed to the west) to alleviate surface water flooding; the most westerly gully had previously been connected to the Thames Water low level sewer but this connection was closed to reduce risk of sewer surcharge. A resident reported water pouring up and out from a highway drain during the event (indicative of sewer or drain surcharging), while residents on the eastern side reported soakaways worked though drained slowly. (p10)
* Blocked gullies on Farnburn Road (sic) were cleared as part of the post‑event response (Sep 2015). (p12) [TAB]
* Rapid inundation of the street caused by heavy rainfall, with the speed and extent of flooding exacerbated by a failed Thames Water pump station, a collapsed 300mm storm overflow pipe (from recent utility works), blocked highway drains/gullies, and high volumes of surface water runoff from nearby buildings and carparks. (p13) [TAB]
* Water overflowed from a low level sewer chamber onto Farnburn Avenue; gullies on the street are noted to be connected to soakaways (with anecdotal evidence that soakaways on the eastern side of Farnburn Avenue functioned). (p16) [IMG]

### Edinburgh Avenue, Slough Unitary Authority
* Roadway fully inundated from curb to curb, impeding normal traffic; emergency responders present on scene conducting response/traffic control. (p1)
* Rapid inundation of the street during heavy rainfall, causing the road to be closed. Flooding was exacerbated by a failed Thames Water pump station, a collapsed storm overflow pipe, blocked highway drains/gullies and high surface-water runoff from nearby buildings and car parks. (p4) [TAB, PAG]
* Closed due to surface water flooding on 26 August 2015, requiring traffic to be diverted. (p5)
* Flooding caused closure of the road and required traffic to be diverted; known to have flooded historically though no internal property flooding was reported at the time of writing. (p6) [PAG, IMG]
* Rapid inundation of the street during the 26th August 2015 storm; the speed and extent of flooding were exacerbated by a failed Thames Water pump station, a collapsed Thames Water storm overflow pipe, blocked highway drains/gullies, and high volumes of surface water runoff from nearby buildings and carparks. (p7)
* Street experienced flooding during the event. (p9) [PAG]
* Highway gullies were checked after the event; blockages (exacerbated by loose bark chippings washing from planted areas) reduced drainage capacity during the flood. Slough Borough Council cleared the blockages; one gully required civil works because it was filled with Tarmac. Surface water runoff from adjacent estate buildings and car parks flowed onto the highway during the event. (p10)
* Drainage assets were investigated following the flood; multiple failed assets were discovered. Blocked gullies on Edinburgh Avenue were cleared (Sep 2015). Advice was issued to adjacent landowners to avoid using chipped bark that can float during a flood. (p12) [TAB]
* Rapid inundation of the street caused by heavy rainfall, with the speed and extent of flooding exacerbated by a failed Thames Water pump station, a collapsed 300mm storm overflow pipe (from recent utility works), blocked highway drains/gullies, and high volumes of surface water runoff from nearby buildings and carparks. (p13) [TAB]

### Slough Unitary Authority
* Bursts of heavy rainfall on 26 August 2015 rapidly caused surface water flooding across the town, producing external flooding and large puddles on roads. (p5)
* Short, intense rainfall on 26 August 2015 caused flash/surface-water flooding across Slough: Edinburgh Avenue was closed and traffic diverted; external flooding occurred on Farnburn Avenue; large surface-water puddles formed on surrounding roads. (p6) [PAG, IMG]
* Short, intense rainfall on 26 August 2015 caused flash surface-water flooding across Slough, producing rapid inundation of streets (notably Edinburgh Avenue and Farnburn Avenue). (p7)
* Surface water runoff from buildings and car parks on an adjacent estate flowed onto the highway at Edinburgh Avenue, causing surface water flooding on the highway. (p11) [PAG]
* Surface water and sewer flooding affected parts of the borough. The post‑event investigation identified multiple failed drainage assets across Slough, including blocked gullies (notably on Edinburgh Avenue and Farnburn Road), tarmac‑filled gully pots and a decommissioned 300mm storm overflow pipe that impaired drainage and required remedial works. (p12) [TAB, PAG]

### Perth Avenue, Slough Unitary Authority
* {26 Aug 2015, 01 Sep 2015 - 30 Sep 2015}: A Thames Water dry‑weather flow pump station at the corner of Perth Avenue and Edinburgh Avenue was identified for repair as part of the flood response. Thames Water fixed the pump station in September 2015 to restore pumping and address the sewer/drainage condition that contributed to flooding risk. (p12) [TAB, MUL]


## Topics without coordinates

### Thames Water
* Thames Water's pump station was affected by the event: the pump station chamber was full of water and the associated 300mm storm overflow pipe was found to be blocked, contributing to drainage backup and flooding in the Edinburgh Avenue area. (p3)
* A Thames Water pump station failed during the heavy rainfall on 26th August 2015; the failure prevented removal of surface water and contributed to rapid inundation of Edinburgh Avenue and Farnburn Avenue. (p4) [TAB, PAG]
* {26 Aug 2015, 07 Sep 2015}: Thames Water asset(s) associated with the Edinburgh Avenue discharge were implicated: pump station chamber was observed full of water and a storm overflow was found blocked (location of blockage identified following the event), indicating the asset was affected by the storm and associated drainage surcharge. (p6) [IMG, MUL]
* A Thames Water low-level pump station failed during the 26th August 2015 storm (pump not working and alarm did not alert), causing the low-level sewer to fill and lose storage capacity which increased the speed and extent of surface water inundation at Edinburgh Avenue and Farnburn Avenue. A collapsed Thames Water storm overflow pipe (attributed to recent utility works) also contributed to flooding. Thames Water later repaired the pump. (p7) [PAG]
* Thames Water's pump station chamber was observed full of water during the 26 August 2015 event; water discharged from a low‑level sewer chamber adjacent to Farnburn Avenue and, combined with surface water runoff from the SEGRO estate, contributed to flooding on Farnburn Avenue and Edinburgh Avenue. Thames Water operations confirmed the pump was reported operational at the time (call reference provided). (p8) [PAG]
* Thames Water pump station chamber was full of water and the pump station was not operational during the event, which increased the volume of water flowing onto Farnburn Avenue. A 300mm storm overflow pipe connected to the high level sewer was found to have collapsed after the event, causing an obstruction to flow. (p9)
* The Thames Water low-level sewer connection on the most westerly gully was identified as a pathway that could surcharge flood water onto Farnburn Road; the connection has since been closed to reduce the risk of sewer surcharging during the event. (p10)
* Attended to assets related to the event: fixed the dry‑weather flow pump station at the corner of Perth Avenue and Edinburgh Avenue (Sep 2015) and was to investigate the dry‑weather flow pump alarm system. (p12) [TAB]
* Failure of a Thames Water pump station exacerbated surface water flooding (contributed to rapid inundation of Edinburgh Avenue and Farnburn Avenue). (p13) [TAB]
* A Thames Water pump station failed, preventing the low level sewer from acting as a tank sewer and contributing to sewer overflow onto the public highway (Farnburn Avenue). (p16)

### Slough Borough Council
* {26 Aug 2015, 07 Sep 2015}: Slough Borough Council (SBC) carried out on-site inspections of the Thames Water pump station and the storm overflow following the 26 August 2015 flood. SBC documented a pump station chamber full of water and inspected the storm overflow and the location of a blockage, supporting immediate response and investigation activities. (p3) [MUL]
* {26 Aug 2015, 07 Sep 2015}: Slough Borough Council (Highways/inspectors) attended and inspected Thames Water pump station and the storm overflow (inspection noted and blockage located); involvement was part of the immediate response and post-event inspection. (p6) [IMG, MUL]
* {08 Sep 2015}: Visited Edinburgh Avenue on 8th September 2015 to undertake a detailed inspection following the 26th August 2015 flood event and found that the Thames Water pump station was not working; this inspection established that pump failure contributed to the rapid inundation on 26th August 2015. (p7)
* Slough Borough Council officers visited and inspected the Thames Water pump station and the storm overflow as part of the immediate response to the 26 August 2015 flooding, documenting a chamber full of water and measuring depth with a rod. (p8)
* Responded to the flood by commissioning emergency works to restore the storm overflow pipe; deployed gully crews to inspect and clear highway gullies; cleared blockages found on Edinburgh Avenue; will undertake civil works to remove Tarmac from a gully and will check gully connections on Farnburn Avenue to ensure connection to soakaways only. Council staff also observed surface water runoff from adjacent estate buildings and car parks during the event. (p10)
* Council staff observed surface water runoff flowing from buildings and car parks adjacent to Edinburgh Avenue during the event; the council acted as the highways authority and LLFA with responsibilities for managing highway flooding. (p11) [PAG]
* Coordinated emergency support and carried out post‑event actions: investigated drainage assets on Edinburgh Avenue and Farnburn Avenue (7–8 Sep 2015), cleared blocked gullies on Edinburgh Avenue and Farnburn Road (Sep 2015). Identified multiple failed drainage assets and planned further remedial works (decommission tarmac‑filled gully pot; re‑commission 300mm storm overflow pipe; check gullies on Farnburn Avenue). (p12) [TAB, PAG]
* Responded to the surface water flooding by clearing most blocked highway drains/gullies and issuing a commission order to reinstate a collapsed 300mm storm overflow pipe to address a pathway contributing to rapid inundation. (p13) [TAB]

### Highways
* {26 Aug 2015, 07 Sep 2015}: The Highways Department carried out response and investigation actions associated with the road closure (traffic diversion) on 26 August 2015 and performed subsequent inspections (including an inspection of a storm overflow and a Thames Water pump station visit documented on 7 September 2015). (p6) [PAG, IMG, MUL]

### Salthill Catchment
* Storm flows that would have been conveyed into the Salthill catchment were impeded by the blocked 300mm storm overflow pipe, reducing conveyance into the catchment and increasing the extent of flooding elsewhere. (p10)
