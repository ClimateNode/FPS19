* {general}: Dŵr Cymru Welsh Water is responsible for managing the risk of flooding from its surface water, foul and combined sewer systems where flooding is caused wholly or partly by increased rainwater; sewer capacity can be exceeded in severe weather causing localised surcharging and/or flooding. (p12)
* {22 Aug 2015, 02 Sep 2015}: Quant: 452 reports of flooding across the two events; 7 confirmed internal property floods on 22 August 2015 and 73 on 2 September 2015 (p9) [MUL]

### Spital Dam
* {22 Aug 2015}: Flooding affected Spital Dam (a critical transport route in Bromborough). (p16)
* {22 Aug 2015, 02 Sep 2015}: Floodwater ponded in the low point on the road and fluvial floodwater from the Dibbinsdale Brook inundated the road, preventing drainage discharge; caused significant disruption to vehicular traffic and required rescue of a motorist from their car. (p26) [MUL]
* {22 Aug 2015, 02 Sep 2015}: Quant: 1.2m depth; 1 motorist rescued (p26) [MUL]

### Central Greasby
* {02 Sep 2015}: Converging surface water flows from surrounding slopes, ordinary watercourses running full and insufficient hydraulic capacity in the UU sewer network caused overland flows, surface water ponding and sewer-related surface flooding across central Greasby. Floodwater travelled along streets and through gardens (including Rigby Drive, Arrowe Road and Rylands Hey), with traffic-generated bow waves on Arrowe Road increasing damage to adjacent properties. (p39) [IMG]
* {general}: Identified as a flooding hotspot (Central Greasby flowpath) requiring further appraisal and modelling (recommendation refers to following Greasby Surface Water Modelling Study) to understand and mitigate repeated surface water/urban flooding at this location. (p83) [TAB]

### East Greasby
* {02 Sep 2015}: Multiple reports of property and highway flooding during the 2nd September 2015 event. Flooding mechanisms were linked to surface water runoff, exceedance of sewer capacity and connections with ordinary watercourses flowing through the village. (p27)
* {02 Sep 2015}: Surface water flowpaths converged on Coronation Park during extreme rainfall, producing standing surface water and routing runoff from surrounding residential areas and the playing field into the park; insufficient capacity in the UU Network exacerbated standing water and localised flooding of adjacent streets. (p31) [IMG]

### West Greasby
* {02 Sep 2015}: Surface water flooding during extreme rainfall: limited infiltration in Coronation Park produced large volumes of runoff that, combined with wet antecedent conditions and sloping topography, routed water northwards toward residential streets (e.g. Brookdale Close, Joan Avenue). Standing surface water was exacerbated by insufficient hydraulic capacity in the UU drainage network, producing localised flooding. (p31) [IMG]
* {22 Aug 2015, 02 Sep 2015}: Identified as the 'Central Greasby flooding hotspot' on the Greasby Central flowpath; experienced surface-water/urban flooding during the investigated events, prompting recommendation for further appraisal and modelling. (p84) [MUL]

### Town Meadow Lane Road Bridge
* {02 Sep 2015}: A resident indicated the bridge may have constricted flows during the flood, producing a local backwater effect that impacted properties upstream. (p50)
* {02 Sep 2015}: Hydraulic modelling showed Town Meadow Lane Bridge forms a constriction to flow and results in increased water levels at the structure during modelled return periods, contributing to higher local water levels during the flood event. (p56)

### North West Ambulance Service
* {02 Sep 2015}: Responded to flood-related emergencies; provided a summary of alerts and call-outs associated with the flooding on 2nd September 2015. (p21) [TAB]

### Coronation Park Car Park
* {general}: Extensive pooling of surface water and standing water observed within the car park; no formal drainage apparent at this location. (p29)

### Bidston Great Culvert
* {02 Sep 2015}: During the 2 September 2015 Moreton flooding the EA reported an alarm for the Great Culvert trash screen downstream of Moreton. The culvert/pumping location was a focus of operational response because high tides can prevent River Birket discharging to the West Float and pumping was used to manage elevated water levels and tide-locking. (p43)

### Tern Way Flats
* {02 Sep 2015}: Multi-storey residential building partially submerged by floodwater; water reached up to the bottom of ground-floor windows and surrounded the building, threatening ground-floor units and likely causing internal/external property damage. (p47) [IMG]

### Mersey Estuary
* {02 Sep 2015}: The constrained outfall to the Mersey Estuary at the Great Culvert contributed to a backwater effect that restricted downstream discharge from the River Birket, exacerbating flooding in the catchment during the 2 September 2015 event. (p53)

### Wallasey Float
* {02 Sep 2015}: Rising downstream water levels near the Wallasey Float restricted the ability of the River Birket to discharge, contributing to a backwater effect and flooding in Moreton. (p53)

### Tern Way Bridge
* {02 Sep 2015}: The Tern Way Bridge acted as a partial throttle on Arrowe Brook flows, producing a localised backwater that contributed to fluvial and surface water flooding within Moreton. (p53)

### Moreton Brook
* {02 Sep 2015}: Fluvial flooding from Arrowe Brook affected areas of Moreton during the 2 September 2015 event; these affected areas are covered by the Environment Agency Flood Warning Area for Moreton Brook (ME08). (p56)

### Durley Drive Access Bridge
* {general}: The access bridge to Durley Drive has a relatively low soffit compared to the road deck and could restrict conveyance during high flows, producing a backwater effect and causing lateral spilling over low banks; blockage of the structure could exacerbate flood risk (Environment Agency operations found no evidence of a blockage during the 2nd September 2015 event). (p62) [IMG, PAG]

### Highways Authority
* {22 Aug 2015, 02 Sep 2015}: As Highways Authority, WBC deployed additional Highway Maintenance Contractor resources on 2 September 2015 to manage and remove flood water from public highways; dispatched Highways inspectors to assess sites and arrange road closures; carried out post‑event investigations following 22 Aug and 2 Sep 2015 to identify operational problems (including root cutting schedule, gully checks and rectification, road sweeping at Moreton) and liaised with householders. (p80) [MUL]
