### Ramsey
* {1850s, 1912, 1979, 1998}: Large areas (to the north and south of Ramsey) are at risk of fluvial flooding from High Lode; when High Lode levels remain high for prolonged periods ordinary watercourses, highway gullies and surface water sewers cannot discharge efficiently, exacerbating flooding and causing surface water ponding. Local geology (sand and gravel over clay) can produce a perched water table, leading to springs, surface water ponding and high groundwater issues that compound flood impacts. (p6) [MUL]
* {1850s, 1912, 1979, 1998}: Large areas to the north and south of Ramsey are at risk from fluvial flooding (notably from High Lode); many areas of Ramsey are also at risk from surface water flooding. Central urban Ramsey appears relatively protected by engineered raised embankment flood defences between the Old River Nene and Ramsey Basin. (p7) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* Large areas of Ramsey (north and south) are at risk from fluvial flooding from High Lode; surface water flooding also affects many areas of the town. During the extreme rainfall event on 23 December 2020 the already-saturated catchment caused flooding in and upstream of the Ramsey area, placing significant pressure on drainage systems and reducing the ability of watercourses, gullies and sewers to discharge. (p8) [PAG, IMG]
* Extreme rainfall on 23 December 2020 on an already saturated catchment put significant pressure on drainage systems in and upstream of the Ramsey area. Surface water drainage systems were unable to convey water, causing excess surface water to flow over ground and flood properties in areas at risk of surface water flooding; high groundwater also entered property basements. Some flooding occurred within the nearby fluvial flood plain where channel capacity was exceeded. (p9)
* Local community reported multiple flood incidents to the LLFA: internal and external property flooding across the town, including internal property flooding of residences and flooding of gardens, garages and outbuildings. (p11)

### High Lode

High Lode lies within Flood Zone Three; when river levels remain high for prolonged periods, ordinary watercourses (e.g. Slade Dyke), highway gullies and surface water sewers cannot discharge efficiently into the river network, exacerbating flooding. Flood defences protect parts of the urban area but adjacent rural land remains at risk. (p7) [PAG, IMG]
* Some flooding occurred within the fluvial flood plain of High Lode when the channel exceeded capacity, contributing to local inundation. (p9)
* High Lode experienced prolonged high river levels that caused fluvial flooding and prevented ordinary watercourses, highway gullies and surface water sewers from discharging efficiently into the river, exacerbating surface water flooding in areas north and south of Ramsey. (p17) [PAG, IMG]
* {general}: High water levels in High Lode have reduced discharge efficiency through the culverted section between Field Road and High Lode. Highway drainage works (resurfacing and replacement of gullies in Field Road) are being taken to improve drainage as a response to these issues. (p20) [TAB]

### Ramsey Basin
* {1850s, 1912, 1979, 1998}: Ramsey Basin is affected by fluvial (river) flooding from High Lode which can keep water levels high and exacerbate flooding when connected ordinary watercourses (for example Slade Dyke), highway gullies and surface water sewers cannot discharge efficiently into the river network. The geology (sand and gravel over clay) produces a perched water table causing springs, surface water ponding and high groundwater which contributes to surface water and groundwater flooding. Engineered raised embankment flood defences are present between the confluence of the Old River Nene and Ramsey Basin. Many areas of Ramsey (including the basin) are also at risk from surface water flooding. Historical significant flood events affecting Ramsey Basin occurred in the 1850s, 1912, 1979 and 1998. (p6) [MUL]

### Slade Dyke Culvert
* {general}: The Slade Dyke culvert runs through a densely built area and, as part of the Slade Dyke system, may be unable to discharge efficiently when river levels are high, which can contribute to surface water ponding and urban flooding across streets and properties it crosses. (p6) [IMG]

### Environment Agency
* Riparian/watercourse issues affecting discharge capacity (Slade Dyke and the culverted section between Field Road and High Lode) with high water levels reducing discharge efficiency; actions include liaison on riparian maintenance, managing the Slade Dyke trash screen at the Anglian Water culvert, and addressing vegetation, debris and siltation to reduce flooding risk. (p21)

### Cambridgeshire County Council
* {winter 2020/21}: Coordinated and delivered post‑flood response actions: undertook walkovers of reported flooded areas, promoted and arranged highway gully cleansing and maintenance, updated guidance on riparian responsibilities and encouraged residents to take preparedness actions. (p22)
