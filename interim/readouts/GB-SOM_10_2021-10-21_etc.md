

# Somerset flooding 21 Oct 2021 - 22 Oct 2021

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Chard & Tatworth, 21-22nd October 2021<br>113\_Somerset County/Flood investigation Report Chard - October 2021.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Chard
* Following heavy rainfall on 21–22 October 2021 the town experienced flooding: streets and urban areas were inundated and properties were affected. The event was assessed as 1-in-38-year intensity, so extent and depths were less than those shown for a 1-in-100-year event. (p5) [IMG]
* Flooding occurred around Catchgate Lane (northwest), Beeching Close (northeast) and Glynswood. Several properties and businesses were flooded, with water reported up to two feet (approximately 600 mm) deep; some properties were described as quite badly flooded though the water cleared without significant lasting damage. The Guildhall was opened as a Place of Safety and sandbags were distributed from there. (p12) [PAG]
* Two properties in the Glynswood area were internally flooded. Part of this flooding was due to a stream that backs up across the open ground behind the secondary school; the report notes possible contribution from a blocked debris grid on a culvert and/or insufficient culvert capacity. (p14) [TAB, MUL]
* The town experienced the 1-in-38-year rainfall event which produced primary flow paths across the town, causing surface water to run through open spaces and watercourses and leading to localised flooding of properties and areas (photos from Chard referenced). Flood mitigation measures are being developed in response, including pilot projects and wider modelling. (p20) [TAB]
* {general}: Chard has experienced flooding; flood mitigation measures are being developed (including a pilot with Wessex Water to distribute water butts to affected areas in late 2023) and modelling/mitigation studies for the town will be expanded to other settlements to address surface-water/urban flood impacts. (p21)
* Flood water flowed along and across a residential street, inundating the carriageway adjacent to houses and parked cars. Water was orange-brown and carrying silt and debris, indicating sediment-laden surface runoff affecting the street and nearby properties. (p25) [PAG, IMG]
* Thorndon Park Drive: two houses flooded. (p26) [PAG, MUL]
* {general}: Combe Street: one house flooded. (p27) [PAG, TAB, MUL]

### Glynswood, Chard
* The Glynswood neighbourhood was impacted by flooding during the heavy rainfall event of 21–22 October 2021. The report includes resident-submitted photographs of flooding in progress and characterises the rainfall as roughly a 1-in-38-year event (less severe than a 1-in-100-year event). (p5) [IMG]
* Flooding reported around Glynswood with internal property flooding affecting properties in the area. (p11) [TAB]
* Two properties in Glynswood were internally flooded. Part of the flooding was due to the local stream (running across open ground behind the secondary school) backing up; blockage at the debris grid to the culvert or lack of culvert capacity was suspected to have contributed. Surface water also flowed from nearby pinch points (Catch Gate Lane, Thorndon Park) onto Glynswood. (p13) [PAG]
* Two properties internally flooded in Glynswood. Flooding was caused in part by a stream running across open ground behind the secondary school backing up; contributors may have included a blocked debris grid at a culvert and/or insufficient culvert capacity, in combination with overland flow of rainwater. (p14) [TAB]
* Area was the focus of public concern about a sluice; Wessex Water attended the Glynswood area to investigate sewer flooding and surface water entering the sewer system during the event. (p16) [TAB]
* Wessex Water attended the Glynswood area to investigate sewer flooding and surface water entering the sewer system; residents reported concerns about a sluice. There were reports of residents lifting sewer manhole covers to release surface water, which caused sewer surcharging elsewhere. (p17)
* Surface water ran into a brook that conveyed flows through open space in Glynswood and entered a culverted section beneath properties at the eastern end; the culvert acted as a bottleneck that restricted flows and increased local flooding risk during the event. (p19) [IMG]
* Photographic evidence in the report (Figure 9) shows flooding occurring on Glynswood in Chard; the street was inundated by floodwater. (p22)
* Street flooding covered a significant portion of the roadway and extended to the verge/entrances of adjacent residential properties; multiple parked cars were partially in standing water; residents observed/stood near houses suggesting risk to homes and the need for immediate attention. Water depth visually shallow (a few centimetres) but sufficient to inundate the road surface and approach doorways. (p23) [IMG]

## Topics without coordinates

### Somerset Fire and Rescue Service
* On the night of 2021-10-21 Fire and Rescue were not significantly present in Chard because they were over-capacity dealing with other, higher‑priority incidents elsewhere; they were stretched across the region and had limited ability to respond locally. They provided some support but capacity constraints affected response. They also reported operational concerns about council officers not joining rescue efforts unless requested. (p13)
* Fire and Rescue resources were reported as being busy elsewhere during the 1-in-38-year surface water flooding incident, contributing to poorly defined incident response and gaps in local emergency response coordination. (p20) [TAB]

### Chard Catchment
* Most flooding around Chard and surrounding villages during the event was pluvial (surface-water) flooding: heavy rainfall produced overland flow that moved downhill, entering houses where topography and street/house arrangements created flow bottlenecks. The event was assessed as approximately a 1-in-38-year surface-water event (less extensive than the EA 1-in-100-year map). (p16)
* Heavy rainfall produced overland flow across the catchment which increased the severity of flooding. Soil moisture deficit dropped (from 70mm at end of September to about 12mm after the 20 October rainfall), indicating the rainfall soaked into the ground and that springs were not activated during this event. (p17)

### Somerset County Council
* As Lead Local Flood Authority, Somerset County Council commissioned the Section 19 investigation and gathered information from residents and other RMAs about when, where and how flooding occurred. As Highways Authority they had teams ready to close roads if requested (they were not called), and carried out minimal road repairs soon after the event. (p16) [TAB]
* The incident exposed poorly defined emergency flood response roles and plans within Somerset Council; the report recommends an in‑depth review of the council's emergency flooding response system to define roles and multi‑event plans. (p20) [TAB]

### Highways Authority
* Teams were stood ready to close roads during the event; minimal road repairs were required after the flooding and were quickly followed up. (p16) [TAB]

### Wessex Water
* Wessex Water had teams on site investigating sewer flooding and surface water entering the sewer system; they also responded to public calls about the sluice in the Glynswood area. (p16) [TAB]

### Local Authority
* Somerset Council's emergency flooding response was shown to be poorly defined during the event (roles and incident response activities unclear when multiple events occurred), prompting recommendation for an in-depth review of the council's emergency flooding response and clearer role definitions; advice from the Environment Agency was recommended. (p20) [TAB]

### Mount Hindrance Farm
* {general}: The local area has a history of flooding and residents provided evidence of flooding; the proposed 295‑dwelling development must submit an approved drainage strategy to ensure surface water is controlled so the development does not increase downstream flooding or worsen flood risk to adjacent sites. (p21)
