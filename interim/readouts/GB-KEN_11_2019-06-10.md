

# West Kingsdown flooding 10 Jun 2019

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: West Kingsdown<br>063\_Kent/Section-19-Flood-Investigation-West-Kingsdown.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### West Kingsdown
* Around a dozen properties in the West Kingsdown area were flooded by surface water; many of those properties were affected internally. (p1)
* Multiple locations across the town experienced overland/surface water flooding (overland flow from adjacent farmland and runoff from a business park). Impacts included internal flooding to residential and at least one commercial property, external flooding, a conservatory wall collapse, and at least one evacuation. (p2) [IMG]
* {general}: Many locations within the village lie within dry valley features or low points where surface water runoff collects and is channelled during extreme weather, resulting in surface water flooding affecting parts of the built-up area (flows noted crossing the A20 and running along Neal Road, Ash Tree Close, Southfields Road and Hever Wood Road). (p3) [IMG]
* A heavy, approximately 1-in-100-year storm overwhelmed drainage, producing overland flows from the rural catchment and urban area that collected at local low points and flow paths and caused flooding to properties in the vicinity. Highway drainage was overwhelmed but drained once rainfall ceased. (p7)
* Quant: around a dozen properties (p1)
* Quant: A20 London Road: 1 residential property flooded; 1 commercial property internally flooded; Ash Tree Close/Drive: 5 properties internally flooded; Southfields Road: 1 property; Neal Road: 2 properties internally flooded and 1 property externally flooded; Heaver Wood Road: 1 property; Phelps Close: 1 property; Botsom Lane: 1 property; Primmett Close: 1 property; Warland Road: 1 property; Evacuations: 1 resident; Conservatory wall collapse: 1 (p2) [IMG]
* Quant: 1 report of flooding received during the event (p7)

### London Road, West Kingsdown
* Overland flow of muddy water from adjacent farmland affected the A20 (London Road), flowed along the carriageway and towards nearby residential streets; a commercial property and a residential property on the road were internally flooded. (p2) [TAB]
* Muddy greenfield run-off and overland flows flooded the A20 London Road; the run-off flowed through Ash Tree Close and towards Southfields Road and caused extensive damage where it travelled through properties along this corridor. (p6) [IMG]
* Quant: 1 property flooded; 1 commercial property internally flooded (p2) [TAB]

### Ash Tree Close, West Kingsdown
* {20 Dec 2019}: An event on 20 December 2019 affected Ash Tree Close (off the A20) following heavy rain on saturated ground; KCC Highways deployed sandbags between the A20 and Ash Tree Close to manage flood risk to properties. (p1)
* Flood water from fields south of the A20 crossed into Ash Tree Close causing internal flooding to multiple properties and structural damage (part of a conservatory wall collapsed). (p2) [TAB]
* {general}: Overland flow collected in nearby fields is channelled along the dry valley and flows through Ash Tree Close in extreme weather, causing surface water flooding at low points within the street. (p3)
* Overland run-off flowed through Ash Tree Close (originating from fields and the A20 corridor) toward Southfields Road, with run-off travelling through properties and causing extensive damage where it passed through them. (p6) [IMG]
* Quant: 5 properties internally flooded; 1 conservatory wall collapsed (p2) [TAB]

### Neal Road, West Kingsdown
* Internal flooding affected properties on Neal Road (waist‑high reported in at least one case); one resident required evacuation by KFRS; one other property on the road suffered external flooding. (p2) [TAB]
* {general}: The dry valley travels along Neal Road after crossing the A20, meaning runoff is channelled along this road in extreme weather and locations here lie within medium–high surface water flood risk zones. (p3)
* Road flooded early in the event (first report at 20:41) to a depth of 10 inches and later suffered very deep surface water flooding when water was trapped in the topographical low by the noise bund at the edge of the Brands Hatch Circuit. An out-of-hours officer attended and closed the road within one hour. Tankers were deployed to assist with controlling trapped flood water. (p6)
* Quant: 2 properties internally flooded; 1 property externally flooded; 1 resident evacuated (p2) [TAB]
* Quant: 10 inches; closed within one hour (p6)

### Kent
* Very heavy rainfall on 10 June 2019 led to surface water flooding across the county; areas such as West Kingsdown, Vigo, Snodland and Swanley were particularly badly affected. In the West Kingsdown area around a dozen properties are reported to have been flooded by surface water, many affected internally. (p1)
* Overland flows from farmland south of the A20 London Road caused muddy surface-water runoff onto the A20 and into residential streets in West Kingsdown. Floodwater flowed from fields across the A20 into Ash Tree Close and then north towards Southfields Road. Multiple properties experienced internal and external flooding (including kitchens), part of a conservatory wall collapsed, waist‑high flooding was reported on Neal Road and one resident was evacuated by KFRS. A commercial property was internally flooded and local roads were affected by significant muddy runoff. (p2) [IMG]
* Quant: around a dozen properties (p1)
* Quant: 15 properties; 1 commercial property; 5 properties in Ash Tree Close/Ash Tree Drive; 2 properties internally flooded in Neal Road; 1 property externally flooded in Neal Road; 1 resident evacuated; 1 part of a conservatory wall collapsed; 1 property flooded on A20 London Road; 1 property flooded in Southfields Road; 1 property in Heaver Wood Road; 1 property in Phelps Close; 1 property in Botsom Lane; 1 property in Primmett Close; 1 property in Warland Road (p2) [IMG]

### School Lane, West Kingsdown
* {general}: The eastern dry valley feature starts in the area around School Lane; overland flow originates here and is routed downstream, placing the area within the mapped medium–high surface water flood risk and at risk of surface water flooding. (p3)

### Brands Hatch Circuit
* {general}: The two dry valley drainage features cross the Brands Hatch Circuit and converge nearby; surface water from these valleys can be channelled across the circuit in extreme weather, so parts of the circuit are at risk of surface water flooding or flow across the track (inferred from valley crossing and map flood-risk patterns). (p3)
* A noise bund at the edge of the Brands Hatch Circuit contributed to creating a topographical low where water became trapped, which exacerbated very deep surface water flooding on Neal Road. (p6)
* Deep water collected behind the noise bund at Brands Hatch contributed to build-up and overland flow paths that increased water at local low points, contributing to surface water flooding of properties in West Kingsdown. (p7)

### Knatts Valley
* {general}: Knatts Valley is a dry valley feature forming a natural drainage path; runoff is channelled along it and contributes to surface water flooding in adjacent low points and built-up areas. (p3) [IMG]

### West Kingsdown Industrial Estate
* {general}: Overland flow collects in the field corner adjacent to the Industrial Estate before crossing the A20; the estate and immediate vicinity are therefore located on a flow path and lie within medium–high surface water flood risk, exposing it to surface water flooding during extreme rainfall. (p3)

### Kent County Council
* KCC HTW is recorded as the reporting/responding authority for multiple investigated flood locations, indicating involvement in managing/reporting the surface water/overland-flow incidents originating from farmland south of the A20. (p2) [TAB]
* Received the earliest report of flooding on Neal Road at 20:41 and an out-of-hours officer attended and closed the road within one hour; recorded no other reports at that time. (p6)

KCC Highways responded to emergency calls (including road closures and arranging tankers) and KCC's Flood and Water Management Team commissioned surface water modelling to assess risk and potential improvements.
