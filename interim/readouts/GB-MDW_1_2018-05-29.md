

# Medway flooding 29 May 2018

## Place names with coordinates

### Nelson Terrace, Chatham
* The most extensive flooding occurred at Nelson Terrace, causing internal flooding to properties and resulting in displacement of residents from their homes. (p10)
* Nelson Terrace experienced flooding during the event; gullies were reported blocked (inspected 27 Feb 2018 but could not all be cleaned due to parked vehicles) and flooding at Nelson Terrace was driven by overland flow from Capstone Road. Subsequent cleaning of two out of four gullies was carried out in August 2018. (p12)
* Flooding at Nelson Terrace was caused by overland flow from Capstone Road; the historic valley watercourses are now part of an underground sewerage system that was not designed to convey surrounding land drainage, contributing to flooding and surcharge of the foul system. (p13) [PAG]
* A major surface water flood incident occurred at Nelson Terrace: council officers visited to establish causes and extent; a Community Impact Assessment was undertaken; follow-up site meetings with impacted residents were delayed because residents were unable to return to the properties for approximately six months. (p14) [PAG]
* Major surface water flood incident: properties were flooded, officers visited to establish causes and extent, a Community Impact Assessment was undertaken, residents were unable to return to properties for approximately 6 months; sandbags were provided and a rest centre put on standby; follow-up site meetings and advice on property-level protection measures were provided during recovery. (p15) [PAG]
* A flood incident at Nelson Terrace escalated to emergency status; communication failures meant the council was not immediately informed and partners responded according to their own internal procedures rather than a coordinated response. (p17)
* Highway flooding causing inundation of Nelson Terrace carriageway. (p22) [TAB]
* Quant: approximately 6 months (residents unable to return) (p14) [PAG]

### Medway
* On 29 May 2018 heavy rainfall across Medway caused rapid overland and surface water flow in topographically susceptible areas, leading to multiple reports of internal and external property flooding and highway flooding. (p4) [PAG]
* Widespread surface water flooding reported across the Medway administrative area during the rainfall event; reports included highway flooding incidents and some property flooding (property specifics not published). (p9) [PAG]
* Widespread surface water flooding occurred across the Medway administrative area on 29 May 2018; multiple flood incidents were reported to the council and observed during site inspections, and the area has a history of localised flooding and drainage problems during heavy/prolonged rainfall. (p10)
* On 29 May 2018 Medway experienced widespread flooding largely attributed to excessive overland flow and sewer capacity exceedance; impermeable urban surfaces increased runoff and drainage systems were overwhelmed beyond design capacity. (p12)
* {general}: Multiple sites within Medway have experienced regular flooding and drainage issues under high-risk events; actions are planned (gully and soakaway cleansing, enlarged gully grates, additional soakaways) to reduce risk and consequences of surface water flooding. (p17)

### Capstone Road, Chatham
* Overland flow from Capstone Road was observed to cause flooding at lower-lying Nelson Terrace. Hydraulic overloading occurred in the Capstone Road area causing water to be released laterally through the highway construction which lifted the road; ingress of surface water into the foul system caused the foul system to surcharge. (p12) [PAG]
* Hydraulic overloading of the sewer system in Capstone Road caused water pressure to be released laterally through the highway construction, lifting the road. Ingress of surface water into the foul sewer contributed to surcharge of the system. (p13) [PAG]
* Emergency survey of highways drainage infrastructure was carried out at Capstone Road after the flood event. A carriageway repair was undertaken. Southern Water CCTV surveys at Capstone Road identified a lining repair requirement at the junction with Hopewell Drive (works completed Oct–Nov 2018). Gully inspections were also carried out following the event. (p15) [TAB, PAG]
* Emergency survey of highways drainage infrastructure following the flooding event; carriageway repair carried out at Capstone Road; CCTV survey of several lengths of sewer identified a need for lining repair at the junction of Capstone Road and Hopewell Drive (another pipe constructed too close to the soffit, not causing obstruction to flow); lining works completed Oct–Nov 2018; less serious defects (e.g. cracking on pipe crown) recorded in Southern Water's asset database for future AMP planning. (p16) [PAG]
* Internal and external property flooding affecting properties on Capstone Road. (p22) [TAB]

## Topics without coordinates

### Medway Council
* As the Lead Local Flood Authority for the area, the council's area experienced heavy rainfall on 29 May 2018 that produced overland and surface water flows resulting in reported internal and external property flooding and highway flooding; this prompted the council to undertake a Section 19 flood investigation to identify causes and remedial actions. (p4) [PAG]
* Heavy, prolonged rainfall on 29 May 2018 caused surface water flooding throughout the Medway Council administrative area and beyond; the extent and impact prompted the Lead Local Flood Authority (Medway Council) to undertake a formal Section 19 investigation to determine flood mechanisms and responsibilities. (p5) [PAG]
* Highway drainage (road gullies and highway soakaways) maintained by Medway Council were overwhelmed or ineffective during the event; blocked gullies were reported in areas that experienced flooding and highway drainage capacity was exceeded. (p12)
* Council operations were affected by a major surface-water flood at Nelson Terrace: there was a delay before the council became aware (up to an hour); the council set up a Silver Command Group to manage the response; officers visited flooded sites including Nelson Terrace; sandbags were provided to the public on request; a rest centre was placed on standby; a Community Impact Assessment was undertaken; prioritised highways cleaning and follow-up recovery/site meetings were undertaken (follow-up with Nelson Terrace residents was delayed because residents could not return for a period). (p14)
* A flood incident at Nelson Terrace escalated to emergency status; Medway Council was not immediately informed which affected coordinated response and prompted changes to procedures (operational procedure to inform emergency partners; improved notification from KFRS; purchase of Hydrocast to better predict and share likely local impacts). (p17)
* Surface water entered the foul sewer network causing surcharging of the foul (sewer) system. (p18) [PAG]

### Southern Water
* Combined and separate surface water sewers maintained by Southern Water experienced capacity exceedance during the event, contributing to sewer surcharge and to surface water/urban flooding in Medway when rainfall exceeded sewer design capacity. (p12)
* The underground sewerage system (historical watercourses now culverted into sewers) did not have capacity to convey the valley land drainage; ingress of surface water caused the foul system to surcharge. Southern Water provided briefings on drain maintenance and site improvements. (p13)
* Southern Water carried out CCTV surveys of several lengths of sewer at Capstone Road after the flood; the survey identified a need for lining repair at the junction of Capstone Road and Hopewell Drive (described as not causing an obstruction to flow). Works were completed Oct–Nov 2018. A limited number of less serious defects (e.g. cracking on pipe crowns) were recorded into Southern Water's asset database for future review. (p15)

### Highways
* Highway flooding affected the local road network, prompting highways actions (gully cleansing, repairs, CCTV surveys and gully/grate upgrades) to address flood impacts on carriageways. (p22) [TAB]
