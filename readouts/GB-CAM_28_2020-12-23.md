

# Woodwalton flooding 23 Dec 2020

### New Road, Woodwalton
* Surface water and overland flow routed into a low spot; overflowing highway gullies and an overgrown partially culverted ditch/culverts. Three residential properties, outbuildings and gardens were internally flooded to a maximum depth of c.460mm. Some residents were unable to return to their properties for over one month. (p9) [PAG]
* Surface water routed to this low spot, with a partially culverted ditch to the west overgrown and highway gullies overflowing; ditch/culverts overtopped. Floodwater inundated residential properties, outbuildings and gardens to a maximum depth of about 460 mm; some residents were unable to return to their properties for over one month. (p10) [TAB]
* Localized flooding affected New Road; residents reported flooding and maintenance issues with ditches and drainage which prompted LLFA walkovers and follow-up works (ditch maintenance and gully/culvert cleansing were arranged). (p13) [TAB]
* Surface water flooding affected New Road. Riparian maintenance issues were identified and RMA operational issues were recorded, requiring ongoing liaison between the LLFA and relevant Risk Management Authorities. (p18) [TAB]

### The Cross, Woodwalton
* Internal flooding of residential properties was reported at this location (listed as one of three key risk areas where internal property flooding occurred). Additional local roads and gardens in the village were also identified as flooded. (p9)
* Surface water flooding occurred; highway gullies and the ditch at The Cross overflowed. Outbuildings and gardens attached to one residential property were inundated. When floodwater leaves the ditch channel it flows toward the centre of the village, worsening flooding. A tree was observed blocking the village-green ditch, contributing to conveyance problems. (p10) [PAG]
* At the Cross and village green, outbuildings and gardens attached to one residential property were inundated with flood water; residents did not have to leave the property. A ditch at the village green was obstructed by a tree trunk and roots, causing water to pool and, when the ditch overtopped, floodwater flowed towards the centre of the village and exacerbated local flooding. In the Braefield area (within Woodwalton), surface water routed from fields to the north flooded one residential property to a depth of about 60 mm internally and flooded the outbuildings of that property to about 180 mm; anecdotal evidence indicates that this floodwater dissipated relatively quickly after the event. (p11) [TAB]
* Flooding was reported at The Cross; investigations identified issues with ditches and highway drainage leading to planned and carried-out maintenance actions to alleviate future flooding. (p13) [TAB]
* Quant: 1 residential property (p10) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 residential property; internal flooding c.60 mm; outbuildings flooded c.180 mm (p11) [TAB]

### Woodwalton
* {general}: Parts of the village (particularly along the highway) are at risk of surface water flooding. The centre of the village, where The Green and Raveley Road meet, experiences relatively regular inundation from surface water that is not conveyed away effectively. Flooding is exacerbated during sustained rainfall and saturated ground, increasing runoff volume and velocity which collects at the village centre. The LLFA reports no known historic internal property flooding. (p4)
* Significant flooding occurred in Woodwalton as a result of very high runoff from the intense rainfall on 23rd December 2020. (p6)
* Six LLFA flood reports for Woodwalton describing internal flooding to residential properties, plus flooding to some roads and gardens. Three local areas were highlighted where internal property flooding was reported: New Road; The Cross and village green; and Braefield. (p8) [PAG]
* Following the 23 December 2020 flood, drainage systems in Woodwalton required maintenance. Actions taken or arranged included cleansing of highway gullies by Cambridgeshire Highways and riparian maintenance of drainage ditches (Huntingdonshire District Council visited the site and maintenance work is understood to have been carried out). The LLFA offered support to residents to set up a local flood plan and will continue to liaise with Risk Management Authorities until outstanding actions are completed. (p17)
* Quant: six flood reports; five residential properties; three key risk areas (p8) [PAG]

### East Anglia
* Prolonged higher-than-average rainfall produced saturated ground conditions and, together with intense rainfall on 23rd December 2020, resulted in very high runoff and significant surface water flooding across East Anglia. (p6) [PAG]

### Braefield, Woodwalton
* Internal flooding of residential properties was reported at this location (listed as one of three key risk areas where internal property flooding occurred). The village-level reports also note additional flooding to roads and gardens. (p9)
* Field runoff routed from fields to the north caused surface water to flood one residential property to a depth of about 60mm; outbuildings associated with the property flooded to about 180mm. Anecdotal evidence indicates the flood water dissipated relatively quickly and the resident did not have to leave the property. (p12) [TAB, PAG]
* Braefield experienced flooding reports tied to blocked/poorly maintained ditches and drainage; this prompted meetings with residents, LLFA site visits and subsequent ditch maintenance and gully/culvert cleansing. (p13) [TAB]
* Quant: 1 residential property (p12) [TAB, PAG]

### Raveley Road, Woodwalton
* {general}: The junction/area where The Green and Raveley Road meet at the centre of the village sees relatively regular inundation from surface water that is not conveyed away effectively, contributing to highway/centre flooding during sustained rainfall and saturated ground. (p4)
* A ditch runs along Raveley Road that was reported to regularly back up and overflow during the event; the culvert conveying water away from the Raveley Road ditch required maintenance and Cambridgeshire Highways ordered maintenance on that culvert to restore conveyance. (p10)

## Topics without coordinates

### Cambridgeshire County Council
* The Cross (village area) was subject of walkover investigations into reported flooding; residents gave local insight about blocked ditches. Actions by responsible authorities included planning and carrying out gully cleansing and ditch maintenance to address the surface-water flooding issues. (p13)
* Cambridgeshire Highways (part of the County Council) carried out cleansing of highway gullies and the LLFA (County Council) is offering support to residents (Community Flood Action Programme) and providing reporting portals to address surface water flooding issues in Woodwalton. (p17)

### Huntingdonshire District Council
* Made a site visit and carried out riparian maintenance of drainage ditches in Woodwalton following the flood, as part of actions to maintain drainage systems. (p17)
