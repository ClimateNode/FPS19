

# Whitecross flooding 04 Jan 2024 and Whitecross flooding 18 Feb 2024

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Flood Investigation Report: Whitecross, Boars Hill, Abingdon<br>095\_Oxfordshire County/S19-Flood-Investigation-Whitecross-FINAL.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Whitecross
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Flooding affected residential properties and gardens along Whitecross. On 4 January 2024 seven private residential properties and four gardens were flooded as surface water cascaded along the Whitecross highway and was unable to discharge through the 900 mm culvert; water entered properties via lower accessways off Whitecross. Garden flooding was partly attributed to the watercourse running along the rear of the properties. Two of the properties flooded internally again on 18 February 2024 following further heavy rainfall. (p2) [PAG, MUL]
* {January - February 2024}: Residential properties and gardens were flooded; internal flooding in properties with peak internal flood levels averaging 0.25 m. Reports indicate the watercourse at the rear may have surcharged causing garden flooding. (p3) [PAG]
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Residential properties and gardens along Whitecross were flooded; the highway junction between Fox Lane and Whitecross was inundated causing highway and property flooding. (p4) [TAB, MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024}: Internal flooding of residential properties leading to a formal investigation. (p8) [PAG]
* {04 Jan 2024, 05 Jan 2024}: Internal flooding of residential properties within Whitecross that triggered a formal investigation. (p9) [MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024}: Surface water running off the Whitecross highway entered lower accessways and flooded properties and gardens; temporary defences (sand bag dams) were breached by the volume and velocity of runoff; garden flooding was also reported from a surcharged rear watercourse though internal flooding from that source did not occur. Inspections and photographs showed internal flooding in properties. (p13) [PAG]
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: A watercourse north of Fox Lane was silted and blocked with woody debris causing water to cascade through the adjacent woodland, overwhelm a culvert adjacent to Fox Lane and flood the highway, property gardens and properties in Whitecross. (p14) [PAG, MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Exceptional heavy rainfall on 4th January 2024 and 18th February 2024 caused highway runoff to be unable to drain to a 900 mm culvert, resulting in surcharging and flooding of the highway, adjacent land and properties. A watercourse at the rear of properties breached its banks causing garden flooding. (p15) [MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Highway drainage system and associated artificial watercourse along Whitecross were implicated by flooding during the reported events; recommendation to monitor, maintain and carry out remedial works to the highway drainage and artificial watercourse to reduce internal flooding of properties and businesses. (p21) [TAB, MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Quant: 7 private residential properties; 4 gardens; 2 properties (p2) [PAG, MUL]
* {January - February 2024}: Quant: 0.25 m peak internal flood level (p3) [PAG]
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024}: Quant: more than 5 properties (p8) [PAG]
* {04 Jan 2024, 05 Jan 2024}: Quant: more than 5 properties (internal flooding) (p9) [MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024}: Quant: 7 properties; 0.25 m peak flood level within properties (p13) [PAG]

### Fox Lane, Wootton
* {04 Jan 2024}: Flooding of the highway junction between Fox Lane and Whitecross on 4 January 2024. Highly waterlogged soils and an unmaintained ordinary watercourse about 190 m north of the junction led to the channel surcharging, water cascading through adjacent woodland and discharging over an embankment into the receiving watercourse on Fox Lane, which was unable to cope and further surcharged, flooding the junction. (p2)
* {January - February 2024}: The highway junction between Fox Lane and Whitecross experienced flooding; peak flood level reached approximately 0.1 m. The junction remained passable to vehicles. Obstructions in the adjacent unmaintained land drainage/watercourse were identified and the obstructed section was cleared by the landowner following a site review. (p3) [PAG]
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: The highway junction between Fox Lane and Whitecross experienced flooding; the trash screen and piped watercourse at the Fox Lane junction were identified for monitoring, maintenance and remedial works. (p4) [TAB, MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024}: The highway junction between Fox Lane and Whitecross was flooded when a culvert entrance was overwhelmed by cascading water from an adjacent watercourse; the culvert capacity was exceeded and the culvert became overwhelmed by water and debris from a silted/blocked upstream channel, causing water to cascade across adjacent land and through woodland onto the junction. (p13)
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: A watercourse to the north, silted and partially blocked with woody debris, caused water to cascade through the adjacent woodland and overwhelm the culvert adjacent to Fox Lane. The overwhelmed culvert resulted in water flowing across the woodland and contributed to local flooding; heavy catchment rainfall and flows along the Fox Lane watercourse were noted as possible contributing factors. (p14) [PAG, MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: A watercourse and culvert entrance adjacent to Fox Lane was overwhelmed during the heavy rainfall, contributing to flooding; post-event the landowner removed obstructions from an obstructed section of watercourse north of Fox Lane. (p15) [PAG, MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: The report recommends monitoring, maintenance and remedial works to the trash screen and piped watercourse at the Fox Lane junction because these assets were implicated in the surface water flooding that affected the junction and contributed to local property flooding during the investigated events. (p21) [TAB, MUL]
* {January - February 2024}: Quant: 0.1 m peak flood level at junction (p3) [PAG]

### Oxfordshire
* {04 Jan 2024}: Wet weather from October 2023 and especially heavy rainfall on 3–4 January 2024 caused flooding across parts of the county (notably the Vale of White Horse and South Oxfordshire). This included internal flooding to residential properties in Whitecross that triggered a formal investigation. (p9) [IMG, PAG]
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024}: Oxfordshire experienced sustained high rainfall in the weeks leading up to the flood event of 04–05 January 2024; this heavy precipitation was associated with the flooding that occurred on 04–05 January 2024. (p10)
* {04 Jan 2024}: Quant: more than 5 properties (p9) [IMG, PAG]

### Vale of the White Horse
* {04 Jan 2024}: Areas across the district were affected by flooding associated with prolonged wet weather culminating in heavy rainfall on 03–04 January 2024. (p9)

### South Oxfordshire
* {04 Jan 2024}: Parts of the district were affected by flooding during the prolonged wet period and especially heavy rainfall on 03–04 January 2024. (p9)

### Abingdon
* {04 Jan 2024, 05 Jan 2024, 06 Jan 2024}: Heavy, persistent rainfall across the area and very high River Ock flows in early January 2024 contributed to flooding in and around Abingdon; these elevated flows are linked to the flooding experienced on 4 January 2024. (p12) [IMG, PAG, MUL]

### Wootton Road, Abingdon
* {04 Jan 2024}: The fluvial/pluvial flood risk area shown on the planning maps runs across the central road corridor (labelled likely Wootton Road). Given the formal investigation recorded internal flooding of more than 5 properties in Whitecross on 2024-01-04 to 2024-01-05, it is likely the road corridor was inundated or experienced surface-water overland flow that disrupted access during that event (not explicitly itemised in the text). (p8) [IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Oxfordshire County Council
* {January - February 2024}: As the Highways Authority, OCC implemented remedial measures to address the flooding impacts to the highway and drainage system (see Highways Authority entry): upgrades, clearance and construction works to increase drainage capacity. (p3)
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: The Lead Local Flood Authority (OCC) investigated flooding of properties, reviewed the land drainage system and made recommendations; OCC LLFA led the site review and coordinated with the landowner and other stakeholders following the flood events. (p4) [TAB, MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: The council (Highways Authority) implemented highway drainage and watercourse remedial works after the floods to increase drainage capacity and reduce repeat flooding risk. (p14) [MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: A 900 mm culvert owned by Oxfordshire County Council at Whitecross could not accept highway runoff during the heavy rainfall events, resulting in surcharging and flooding of adjacent land and properties. (p15) [MUL]

### Highway Authority
* {January - February 2024}: Undertook remedial works after the flooding to the highway and associated piped gravity highway drainage and watercourse: road planing; raising and resetting highway drainage off-lets; upsizing piped connections to a 900 mm culvert; clearance of an artificial watercourse; construction of an additional highway drainage gulley; increased capacity of drainage conduits and ongoing monitoring with willingness to add further highway drainage if required. (p3)
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Flooding occurred at the highway junction between Fox Lane and Whitecross, inundating the carriageway and overwhelming the highway drainage and trash screen; OCC Highways were identified as lead stakeholder for monitoring, maintenance and remedial works to the highway drainage, trash screen and associated piped/artificial watercourse. (p4) [TAB, MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Following the floods the Highways Authority undertook remedial works to the highway and associated piped gravity drainage and watercourse to increase conduit capacity (road planing; raising and resetting drainage off-lets; upsizing piped connections to a 900 mm culvert; clearance of an artificial watercourse; construction of an additional highway drainage gulley). (p14) [MUL]

### Vale of the White Horse Council
* {January - February 2024}: A representative met with the landowner of the rear field (riparian owner) after reports that the rear watercourse may have surcharged and flooded gardens; minor obstructions were noted and the landowner is arranging removal of obstructions and regular inspections of the watercourse to reduce future surcharging and garden flooding. (p3)
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: VWHDC are recorded as a consulting stakeholder for remedial works and for monitoring and maintenance responsibilities relating to the land drainage and ordinary watercourse at the rear of Whitecross; they were consulted on actions following the flooding. (p4) [TAB, MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: A council representative met the landowner/riparian owner of the watercourse that exceeded capacity and flooded some property gardens; minor obstructions were noted and the landowner arranged removal and regular inspection of the watercourse. The landowner removed obstructions from an obstructed section along the north-eastern boundary of the woodland to the north of Fox Lane within one week. (p14) [MUL]

### Ock Catchment
* {04 Jan 2024, 05 Jan 2024, 06 Jan 2024}: Persistent, sometimes heavy rainfall across the catchment, with particularly heavy rainfall on 03–04 January 2024, produced runoff and high river flows that caused flooding on 4 January 2024 and produced very high gauge flows in early January. (p12) [MUL]
