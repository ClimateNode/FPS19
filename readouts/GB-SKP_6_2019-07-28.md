### A555 Manchester Airport Relief Road
* Post-flood investigations identified development control issues and actions on the A555 related to the flood event, indicating the road corridor was affected by flooding. (p7)
* {27 Jul 2019 - 31 Jul 2019}: Shortcomings in the A555 drainage design and construction reduced the operational resilience of the highway; the road is vulnerable to flooding during prolonged or frequent heavy rainfall. Ongoing rectification of construction defects is expected to reduce the incidence of road flooding. The review concluded the A555 drainage shortcomings would have had little effect on downstream flooding of Lady Brook and Micker Brook. (p12)
* {2016, 01 Jul 2019 - 31 Jul 2019}: The construction and subsequent performance of the A555 (Manchester Airport Relief Road) are being investigated for whether they have worsened, or could worsen, flood risk; concerns were renewed about the road's water-management capabilities following the July 2019 flooding (and it was examined previously during 2016 construction). (p14) [MUL]
* {2019}: Location at the A555 is within the upstream extent of the Micker Brook catchment that conveyed runoff during the 2019 flood events; the catchment area up to the A555 contributed runoff into downstream reaches of Micker Brook. (p20) [TAB]
* Attenuation ponds serving the A555 were still draining from earlier rainfall, reducing available storage for the 31 July rainfall and so reducing the road corridor's capacity to store/runoff. This reduced storage, combined with the intense rainfall, meant surface water drainage standards were likely exceeded and increased the risk of surface water flooding affecting the A555 during the event. (p28)
* Heavy rainfall led to closures of the A555 due to flooding: the road was closed on 28 July 2019 and again on 31 July 2019. On 31 July the A555 was closed due to flooding at three locations (two of those locations within the Stockport borough). During the period drainage of A555 catchment E was maintained by temporary pumping at Hall Moss Lane to manage floodwater. (p31) [PAG, MUL]
* {16 Mar 2019 - 18 Mar 2019, 28 Jul 2019 - 31 Jul 2019}: Flooding of the carriageway causing road closures and transport disruption. The A555 was closed due to flooding on 28 July 2019 and was reported closed due to flooding at three locations on 31 July 2019 (two of those locations within the SMBC area). The route had also experienced earlier flooding incidents, including closure from 20:00 on 16 March 2019 to 20:00 on 18 March 2019. (p32) [PAG, MUL]
* {16 Mar 2019 - 18 Mar 2019, 28 Jul 2019 - 31 Jul 2019}: Carriageway flooded on multiple occasions due to heavy rainfall and pump failures, causing repeated full closures between A523 and A34 (including closures on 16–18 Mar 2019 and 28–31 Jul 2019). Sections reopened progressively in early August; one lane remained shut for pump installation and tank desilting until 14 Aug. (p33) [PAG, MUL]
* A555 carriageway flooded during the event. Carriageway was cleared after pumps at Network C were re-started on 29 Jul; later the A555 became completely flooded at the Network C pump station when the pond overflowed on 31 Jul. Overflow from attenuation ponds E1/E2 also flowed onto and along the A555 in a westerly direction. (p34) [PAG]
* The A555 dual carriageway was completely covered by floodwater, rendering the road impassable and preventing vehicle passage; vehicles were stopped at the far side and response/maintenance activity was present. (p35) [IMG]
* Significant council effort was directed at controlling flooding on the A555 including inspections, implementing road closures and mobilising pump maintenance to manage floodwater. (p36)
* Repeated surface-water flooding during the July 2019 event caused pumping station failures; attenuation ponds overflowed due to design/construction defects and the road became completely impassable when the original A555 pump station no.4 at Hall Moss Lane was overloaded. (p45) [TAB]
* Drainage provision associated with the A555 was found to be deficient (networks designed to limited return periods and Network E only to 30-year), meaning the drainage did not provide sufficient capacity and contributed to flooding/exceedance flows during the event. (p48) [PAG]
* The A555 drainage scheme (networks A, B and C and other elements) exhibited post-flood problems: attenuation and routing arrangements did not adequately manage exceedance flows, there is evidence of potential non-filling of attenuation Pond B, blockages and construction defects, and unattenuated runoff contributed to local flooding during the 28 Jul 2019 - 31 Jul 2019 event. (p49) [PAG]
* {general}: Flows that cannot be pumped are temporarily stored on or below the A555 carriageway; the expanded drainage catchment and likely inadequate original pump/storage capacity increase the likelihood of carriageway flooding and prolonged inundation due to slow drain-down of attenuation ponds. (p51) [PAG]

Pond E2 has a storage volume of 981 m3 and a Hydrobrake pass-forward flow of 14.6 l/s. The combined E1/E2 arrangement has an extended drain-down time (estimated between 4.5 days with no inflow and up to 2 weeks with steady inflow of 10 l/s), leaving the highway vulnerable to long-duration and follow-on rainfall events. The original pump station is likely inadequate to drain the combined new and original road to a reasonable standard, increasing flood risk to the carriageway and requiring potential enlargement of ponds or additional mitigation. (p52) [PAG]

Some construction deficiencies have been or are being rectified, though the investigation concluded A555 drainage deficiencies were not a significant contributor to downstream flooding in the borough. (p54)

Inundation depths during the event had potential to affect power supplies, pumping equipment and controls serving the drainage installation. (p56) [PAG]

### Micker Brook
* {2019}: Micker Brook is identified in the report as the watercourse affecting local flood levels and as a cause of flooding local to Queens Road and Wilmslow Road. (p7) [TAB]
* Flood extents on Micker Brook during the July 2019 event were inconsistent with modelled/mapped extents; a relatively recent housing development that had been mapped outside the 1-in-1000-year outline was flooded, and other properties alongside Micker Brook were also flooded. (p11) [MUL]
* {27 Jul 2019 - 31 Jul 2019}: Experienced downstream flooding associated with the 2019 event; the A555 drainage review considered whether the road contributed to flooding on Micker Brook and concluded the road would have had little effect on the downstream flooding. (p12)
* {2019}: The Micker Brook catchment is described in detail (breakdown to A555, Bramhall Green, Demmings and River Mersey) and was identified in mapping and tables as contributing upstream runoff into the borough during the 2019 flood events, contributing to the flooding impacts and local hot spots investigated. (p20) [TAB]
* EA carried out clear-up operations and issued flood alerts for the Micker Brook catchment on 28 and 31 July 2019. The report notes the formal warning for Micker Brook is driven by River Mersey trigger levels and was insensitive to localised intense rainfall in the Micker Brook catchment. (p37)
* Heaviest rainfall was centred on Micker Brook producing more significant fluvial flooding during the July 2019 event; recorded flood extents in locations (Sandringham Road, Queens Road/The Demmings, Broadway Avenue) were inconsistent with mapped EA extents, prompting recommendation to review modelling and mapping and extend the review upstream (including Bramhall Green). (p44)
* Micker Brook (main river) produced July 2019 flood extents that exceeded the mapped 1 in 1000 extents and contributed to property flooding at multiple locations (Sandringham Road, Queens Road, Wilmslow Road). Debris and potential blockages were reported in the channel and culverts. (p45) [TAB]
* Micker Brook (main river) produced flows in July 2019 that exceeded current 1 in 1,000 flood extents in multiple reaches (including Sandringham Road, Queens Road and Wilmslow Road reaches), leading to property flooding (including Broadway Avenue and areas around Brook House) and where debris and possible culvert blockages were observed; modelling and mapping for this reach are recommended to be updated and validated against the 2019 event. (p46) [PAG]
* July 2019 flood outline exceeded current 1 in 1000 flood extent for this reach; 2019 extent may have been increased by an upstream blockage release at Brook House. (p47)
* Micker Brook produced deep and fast-flowing floodwaters that caused dangerous flooding and 'Risk to Life' conditions at adjacent locations (e.g. Bramhall Green, Queens Road, Twinning Brook Road). (p53)
* Catchment flooding on Micker Brook contributed to downstream flood impacts across Bramhall Green and other downstream locations; catchment-scale controls and a local flood warning system were recommended following the 28–31 July 2019 event. (p76) [PAG]
* Micker Brook carried the July 2019 flood flow that contributed to local flooding (including Sandringham Road). The EA pluvial map reproduces the July 2019 flood flow in Micker Brook, and the brook's hydraulic behaviour dominated local flood water levels; obstructions or weirs on the channel may have elevated levels. (p81)
* Fluvial flooding from the Micker Brook / Lady Brook system caused localised flooding at Queens Road and adjacent streets (including Pickmere Gardens and the Demmings Road Industrial Estate) on the evening of 31 July 2019. Micker Brook passes under Queens Road in a walled channel and culvert, contributing to flooding at Brook House and nearby properties. Sandringham Road flooding in July 2019 was also driven by fluvial flooding from the Lady/Micker Brook system. (p83) [PAG]
* Micker Brook overtopped/ponded as it passed under Queens Road and alongside Old Wool Lane, flowed into a culvert under Brook House and contributed directly to localised flooding and internal inundation of nearby residential and commercial properties. A wall adjacent to Brook House collapsed, releasing ponded water into the brook downstream and exacerbating flooding. (p84) [PAG]
* Micker Brook overflowed / generated overland flow across Wilmslow Road on 31 Jul 2019, producing flow across the carriageway toward Broadway and contributing to a rapid downstream rise in water levels (linked to a release of ponded water at The Demmings Industrial Estate). (p91) [IMG, PAG]
* Main river that overtopped and generated out-of-channel flow. Broke its banks downstream of Wilmslow Road, producing overland flow that flooded Wilmslow Road, returned via properties on Wilmslow Road and Broadway, and led to raised flood levels affecting gardens and basements along Broadway Avenue. (p92)
* Micker Brook generated fluvial flooding consistent with the mapped extents and the flooding reported on 31 July 2019, contributing floodwater to adjacent streets and properties (including Wilmslow Road, Brookfield Crescent and Broadway Avenue). Culverted tributaries feeding the brook contributed to overland flow paths noted in the pluvial mapping. (p93) [PAG]
* Micker Brook is identified as the primary driver of the fluvial flooding that affected the Wilmslow Road area in late July 2019. The report notes possible debris accumulations upstream of Wilmslow Road bridge, siltation downstream, and blockages at culverted tributary entrances, and that secondary flooding occurred from two local (partly culverted) tributaries feeding Micker Brook. (p94) [PAG]
* Micker Brook produced a rapid fluvial response to intense local rainfall and was the source of flooding that affected downstream areas (including the Wilmslow Road area). The catchment (including Norbury, Poynton and Lady Brook) experienced localised flooding that was not well-captured by warning levels tied to the River Mersey. (p95) [PAG]
