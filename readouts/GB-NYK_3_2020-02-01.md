### River Aire
* Flooding from the main River Aire caused inundation of the lower Aire and its washlands; the event was widespread across multiple authority areas and has prompted an Environment Agency review of flows and washland capacity. (p2) [PAG]
* The River Aire and its associated washlands were involved in the February 2020 flood events; this has prompted the Environment Agency to undertake a review and further assessment of flows and washland capacity (work noted as already in progress since the February 2020 events). (p3)
* Consecutive storms and saturated floodplain caused washlands around Hirst Courtney to reach capacity; River Aire overtopped into the communities of West Bank and Hirst Courtney, and high river levels prevented Black Drain from draining to the river, causing flooding along the Hirst Road corridor between the A19 and Carlton. (p8)
* The Lower Aire area was identified as an exception to otherwise isolated incidents and described as a worst-affected area: flooding concentrated along the Hirst Road corridor from the A19 at Chapel Haddlesey to Carlton Bridge in Selby District. (p11)
* Fluvial flooding associated with the River Aire affected low-lying land to the south of Chapel Haddlesey and contributed to flood risk/observed flow paths for Chapel Haddlesey and nearby settlements (Hirst Courtney, West Bank, Carlton Chapel Haddlesey). The presence of the weir/old lock influences upstream headwater levels and the flood behaviour at the village. (p18)
* {general}: The River Aire is identified as the source of flood risk for the area adjacent to Hirst Road: properties on the south side of Hirst Road are at high and medium risk of flooding. Hirst Road is higher than the surrounding land and acts as a barrier protecting properties to the north. (p19) [IMG, PAG]
* The Aire catchment experienced extreme rainfall in February 2020, producing very high river flows and causing river flooding across the catchment during the period. (p23) [PAG]

Persistent rainfall occurred throughout the month and during named storms. (p24) [PAG]
* The Aire catchment (Thornton Moor and Upper Aire locations) experienced persistent rainfall and elevated river flows during February, with sustained runoff particularly around 21 February that increased catchment saturation and contributed to flow peaks. (p26) [IMG]
* Multiple monitoring sites on the Aire recorded significant and, at some sites, record-high peaks during February storm and rainfall events (levels rose during Storm Ciara, rose further during Storm Dennis, and reached monthly/record peaks on 25 Feb). (p28) [PAG]

The most notable modeled increase was at Thwaite's Mill; downstream water level increases reduced with distance and washlands overtopped at Chapel Haddlesey producing flat-topped gauge peaks. (p44)

### Masham
* Following the flood event, authorities have continued monitoring of Swinney Beck in Masham and provided responsive services, indicating local flood impacts and ongoing risk management in the town. (p2)
* Flooding of properties in the Masham area caused by exceptionally high river levels during the February 2020 storms. (p8)
* Recorded river flooding during February 2020; identified by NYCC as one of the worst-affected settlements in Harrogate Borough. (p11)
* {general}: Parts of Masham experienced flood risk during recent events: highest pluvial risk areas follow highways and observed flow paths, while fluvial risk zones run along the main rivers with isolated pockets of inundation within residential gardens (notably upstream of Westholme Road). The town centre is not shown to be at risk from reservoir flooding on the map. (p17) [IMG, PAG]
* The River Ure at Masham recorded a monthly peak during the storms, reaching 3.5 m on 9 Feb. (p27)
* Masham was the focus of repeated flood warnings during February 2020: Swinney Beck at Masham had flood warnings on 9 February, 15 February, 21–24 February and again at the end of February (28–29 Feb); the River Ure at Masham was also the subject of warnings on 8 February, 16 February and 21–24 February, indicating repeated river flooding risk to Masham during 01 Feb 2020 - 29 Feb 2020. (p30)
* Property flooding reported; extent of flooding documented in contemporaneous reports of Storm Ciara impacts. (p32)
* Swinney Beck breached through a resident's garden and flowed down Red Lane, Park Square and Church Street to the low point at Silver Street; flows overtopped the beck and tracked along roads, overwhelming gullies and requiring NYCC/brigade intervention (fire brigade pumped water through to the Shooting Holme). NYCC committed to clearing highway drains on the flow route and inspected the Oaks box culvert. (p40)

* Swinney Beck capacity was exceeded due to high river flows; NYCC-owned structures were inspected and no significant defects or obstructions were found. The exceeded capacity led to flooding in Masham. (p49)
* The town experienced flooding associated with Swinney Beck during February 2020, prompting monitoring and responsive actions by authorities. (p50)

* Quant: 170mm silt in The Oaks box culvert; box culvert depth 1200mm (p40)
