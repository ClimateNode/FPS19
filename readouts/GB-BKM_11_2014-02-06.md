Residents reported floodwater contaminated with sewage and surcharging of the sewer in the field between Kingsmead and the railway. Local mitigation (bailing, sandbags, trenches) reduced more extensive internal flooding. (p18)

The culvert beneath the railway in Crowbrook Road was reported blocked during the flood and the refurbished/nearby culvert was considered unlikely to have capacity for the additional road-derived flow, concentrating water on the road. ### Monks Risborough
* Following a prolonged period of above-average rainfall groundwater levels rose and surrounding land became saturated. Subsequent rainfall caused flood flows that were unable to pass through restricted culverts and drains and caused direct runoff from land with no defined watercourse, resulting in flooding across Monks Risborough and affecting local watercourses, culverts and the piped drainage system. (p4)
* Prolonged above‑average rainfall raised groundwater levels and saturated land. Subsequent rainfall caused flooding where flow could not pass through restricted culverts and drains and from direct runoff from land with no defined watercourse. A transport link (Class C highway) was rendered totally impassable for 10 hours or more. (p5) [PAG]
* A local Class C highway (transport link) was rendered totally impassable for 10 hours or more, prompting a formal flood investigation. (p6) [PAG]
* Surface water flooding affected the village area (report highlights large areas prone to flooding on the surface water flood map). Local springs feed small, deeply incised ordinary watercourses that are constrained to pass beneath the railway at two locations (Mill Lane and the railway station); these flow restrictions are identified in the report as a mechanism contributing to localised flooding in and around the village. (p9) [IMG, PAG]
* Areas of the village experienced surface water flooding during the February 2014 event; flood extents on surface water mapping closely match the flooding that occurred. Springs and small watercourses in the village produced flow paths constrained beneath the railway (notably at Mill Lane and the railway station), concentrating flows through the settlement and contributing to inundation. (p10) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface water flood flow path from Askett southwards along Crowbrook Road to Mill Lane was observed / mapped; surface water flood extent on the EA map closely matches the flooding experienced in the village during the February 2014 event. Residential streets and properties along the central and southern corridors were intersected by mapped surface water flood zones. (p11) [PAG]
* The village experienced flooding consistent with the February 2014 event: surface water flooding extent closely matched the February 2014 flood and groundwater emerged at the surface via springs at the Chalk/Upper Greensand junction, contributing to local flooding and drainage problems. (p12) [PAG, IMG]
* {general}: Exceptionally high winter rainfall in Dec 2013–Feb 2014 caused Chalk groundwater levels to rise during the February 2014 flood event, leading to groundwater flooding and infiltration of groundwater into the foul sewer system that caused poor sewer performance and likely sewer flooding in the village. (p13)
* Flooding arose from the rainfall on 6 February and persisted for approximately three weeks; local watercourses were affected (notably Crow Brook and the Mill Stream), causing reduced drainage capacity and local inundation across parts of the village. (p15)
* Village experienced widespread flooding driven by high groundwater, springs and surface water runoff after heavy rain (start taken as 6 Feb). Roads were closed (road closed on 7 Feb, reopened 17 Feb). Areas west of the railway (Kingsmead and Molins sports / adjacent fields) and streets (Crowbrook Road, Mill Lane) were waterlogged and internally flooded; gardens and roads were inundated. (p17) [PAG]
* Exceptional January–February groundwater and saturated soils caused springs, widespread seepage and surface runoff. Restrictions at culverts beneath the railway and under the Mill Road bridge caused excess water to back up, producing extensive flooding of Mill Lane and Crowbrook Road (flood depths on Crowbrook Road reported as 'knee deep' and considerably deeper under the railway). Runoff from Molins sports ground flooded rear gardens and garages of properties in Mill Lane (east of the railway) and Crowbrook Road; internal flooding to at least floorboard height occurred in Kingsmead and Mill Lane without mitigation. Residents reported sewer surcharging and sewage-contaminated floodwater in Kingsmead. The Buckinghamshire Fire & Rescue Service assisted in removing a vehicle from floodwaters on 7 Feb. At least one property lost power; the local church was reported close to internal flooding. (p18) [IMG]
* Surface water flow paths (including flows originating in Askett) crossed the railway and reached Crowbrook Road and Mill Lane; surface water drained across land into the Kingsmead area, flowed around and under properties in Kingsmead and continued onto Mill Lane, contributing to road flooding, property inundation routes and the need for local response actions. (p20)
* Prolonged flooding in the village in February 2014 driven by above-average rainfall and exceptionally high groundwater; high water levels in local watercourses, infiltration into foul sewers causing surcharging and widespread drainage failure. (p24) [TAB, PAG]
* Standing floodwater covered grassland and extended toward residential properties; water reached close to houses, indicating residential areas were threatened and water encroached on property curtilages. (p32) [IMG]
* Quant: 3“ (0.07 m) to 5” (0.13 m) flood depths in rear gardens at the junction of Crowbrook Road and Mill Lane; one vehicle removed from floodwater (7 Feb); one property lost power (p18) [IMG]

(p17)

Local mitigation (bailing, sandbags, trenches) reduced more extensive internal flooding. (p18)

Flooding in Kingsmead rose and fell very rapidly (within an hour). Surface water flows towards Kingsmead with no defined drainage, causing water to flow in and around properties and along roads. ### Crowbrook Road, Monks Risborough
* Surface water flow path ran southwards along Crowbrook Road to Mill Lane during the February 2014 event, forming a key route for floodwater into the village; mapping suggests this route was prone to inundation and may be more extensive than shown due to railway representation. (p10) [PAG]
* Crow Brook culvert and drainage under the railway at Crowbrook Road had recent works and were identified as having reduced capacity/obstructions; the Crow Brook watercourse running parallel to the railway was an exception to freely flowing channels and contributed to local flood impacts around the railway and Crowbrook Road. (p14)
* Crowbrook Road was extensively flooded when excess water (unable to pass the railway culvert) flowed down the road to Mill Lane; flood depths were reported as substantial (noted as 'knee deep' in accounts) and conditions were worse beneath the railway bridge. (p17) [PAG]
* Runoff and groundwater flooded into the rear gardens and garages of properties on Crowbrook Road; water levels came close to causing internal flooding of residential properties. Residents reported rear garden flood depths at the junction with Mill Lane. (p18)
* Surface water flowed onto Crowbrook Road and then down to the junction with Mill Lane; the culvert beneath the railway in Crowbrook Road was reported blocked at the time of the flood and the refurbished culvert is unlikely to have had capacity for the additional flow, causing road flooding and flows into nearby properties. (p19) [PAG]
* Surface water flowing from Askett and surrounding land reached Crowbrook Road; once on the road there was little opportunity for it to re-enter the adjacent watercourse. The refurbished railway culvert under the railway near the station was unlikely to have capacity for the additional flow, so water conveyed along Crowbrook Road down to the junction with Mill Lane. (p20)
* Surface water that could not pass beneath the railway flowed down Crowbrook Road and accumulated at the lowest point beneath the railway bridge, causing local flooding of the road. (p24) [TAB]
* Road flooding related to backflow at the station culvert under the railway; recommendations include studying options to force water back into the watercourse and enlarging the culvert under the railway station to maintain design capacity. (p25)
* Quant: between 3“ (0.07m) and 5” (0.13m) in rear gardens; at least one property lost power supply (p18)

### Monks Risborough Station
* The report identifies the railway station as one of two locations where springs/flows are constrained to pass beneath the railway embankment (the other being Mill Lane). This constraint is linked to localised surface water/stream flooding at the station area and nearby stream crossing. (p9) [PAG, IMG]
* The culvert beneath the railway at the station restricted flow on the Crow Brook, causing water to back up and spill over into Crowbrook Road and Mill Lane; flood depths were reported as considerably deeper beneath the railway bridge. (p17)
* The culvert under the railway station was partially restricted and unable to accommodate runoff, contributing to surface water being forced down towards Crowbrook Road and accumulating beneath the railway bridge. (p24)

### Mill Lane Railway Bridge
* {general}: The bridge beneath the railway on Mill Road (sic) has been closed on previous occasions because of flood water. (p12) [PAG]
* The road drain beneath the Mill Lane railway bridge was blocked and had water visible at almost road‑grill level; the blockage reduced drainage capacity and contributed to local inundation around the railway bridge during the February rainfall/flooding. (p15) [PAG, IMG]

* Surface water and groundwater flows from the Molins sports ground, which lacks a defined drainage system, flowed off the site and into Crowbrook Road and Mill Lane, contributing to flooding of properties and roads. (p19)

## Topics without coordinates

### Crow Brook
* The Crow Brook watercourse running parallel to the railway appeared heavily congested with vegetation and was not fully accessible; this congestion reduced conveyance and contributed to local flooding during the February event (with limited recent maintenance near the station). (p15) [PAG, IMG]
* Flow in the Crow Brook was restricted at the culvert beneath the railway station, so excess water spilled and flowed down Crowbrook Road towards Mill Lane, contributing to extensive local flooding. (p17) [PAG]
* Crow Brook produced excess flow that probably contributed to internal flooding, garden flooding and road flooding west of the railway at Kingsmead and Mill Lane. (p18)
* A section of the Crow Brook running alongside the railway is described as poorly maintained, reducing its ability to convey flows and contributing to surface water becoming stuck on the road and flowing into adjacent areas during the flood. (p19)

### Mill Stream
* At the railway bridge the Mill Stream was heavily overgrown and had reduced capacity due to silt accumulation; railings from the footbridge were lying in the channel — obstructions that would have impeded flow and contributed to elevated water levels and local flooding during the February event. (p15) [PAG]
* {general}: Channel capacity was reduced by heavy vegetation growth and silt accumulation; railings from a footbridge were lying in the watercourse, creating an in‑channel obstruction that contributed to reduced conveyance and increased local flood risk adjacent to the railway. (p16) [IMG, PAG]
* The culvert carrying the Mill Stream beneath the railway was heavily silted with reduced capacity, contributing to reduced conveyance and increased likelihood of flooding during the event. (p19)

### Network Rail

* Crowbrook Road flooded in association with an undersized/blocked culvert beneath the railway station; Network Rail is recommended to enlarge the Crowbrook Road culvert to restore design capacity and to improve access to sand and sandbags for response. (p25)

### Thames Water

* Infiltration into the foul sewer caused surcharging in Kingsmead and Mill Lane; residents were unable to use the sewer system in the vicinity of the Mill Lane railway crossing. Thames Water was asked to work with other RMAs to address infiltration problems. (p24)

### Mill Brook Culvert
