

# Ightham flooding 25 Jun 2016

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Borough Green Road and Busty Lane, Ightham, on the 25th June 2016<br>063\_Kent/Igtham-S.19-Flood-Report-Final.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Busty Lane, Ightham
* Flooding affected Busty Lane; it is named in the report as one of the areas impacted by the flood event. (p1)
* Internal flooding to properties in the area of Busty Lane during the flood reported on 25 June 2016. (p4)
* {2000, 2003, 01 Sep 2009 - 30 Sep 2009, 01 Nov 2013 - 30 Nov 2013, 01 Jan 2014 - 31 Jan 2014, 25 Jun 2016}: KCC records show high flows in the watercourse caused flooding at the eastern end of Busty Lane in Sep 2009, Nov 2013 and Jan 2014. Anecdotal evidence from residents also links wet winters (notably 2000 and 2003) to high flows and flooding affecting external areas of properties alongside the watercourse. The lane lies within the area of flooding considered by this report. (p6) [MUL]
* Flows from the Busty Stream travelled down Busty Lane, coming out of bank near the bend by Elim Cottages and the culvert under Ezel Villas. High flows could not drain downstream through the culvert quickly enough, causing significant ponding/backing up of water in the lower part of Busty Lane and adjacent areas. (p13)
* Water flowed out of the watercourse bank through and around properties adjacent to the watercourse, through front and rear gardens and onto Borough Green Road. (p14)
* As follow-up maintenance after the flooding, the community and parish council cleared excess vegetation from the banks and removed excess silt from the bed of the watercourse along Busty Lane. (p16)
* A small build-up of silt was noted within the watercourse at the end of Busty Lane, deposited during the flooding event; the depth was limited and considered not significant. (p19)
* Flood exceedance flows passed around Busty Lane (and Borough Green Road); residents report historical flooding at the end of Busty Lane and external areas of property have flooded in wet conditions. During the 25th June 2016 event the exceedance route was close to that predicted and upstream ponding reached about 2 metres. (p20)
* A culvert at Busty Lane was implicated in the flooding (risk of blockages by debris/standing water); report recommends installing a trash screen and inspecting/maintaining the culvert to reduce blockage-related flooding. (p26) [TAB]
* Quant: maximum depth of flood water approximately 2 metres (as illustrated around a parked van) (p13)
* Quant: less than 150mm silt depth (p19)
* Quant: 2 metres deep (p20)

### Ightham
* The village of Ightham experienced flooding affecting areas including Borough Green Road and Busty Lane during the reported flood event. (p1)
* Significant flooding within the village reported on 25 June 2016 that caused internal flooding to properties in the areas of Busty Lane and Borough Green Road adjacent to the Busty Stream. (p4)
* {1968, winter 2000, winter 2003, 01 Sep 2009 - 30 Sep 2009, 01 Nov 2013 - 30 Nov 2013, 01 Jan 2014 - 31 Jan 2014}: Historical high flows in the Busty Stream caused flooding in the village: widespread flooding and damage to properties and businesses in 1968; wet winters (2000 and 2003) caused high flows and flooding to external areas of properties alongside the watercourse; KCC records show high flows causing flooding at the eastern end of Busty Lane in September 2009, November 2013 and January 2014 (those latter events did not report property flooding). (p6) [IMG, MUL]
* Flooding was reported downstream of Ightham along the River Bourne as high flows exceeded the watercourse capacity, causing inundation downstream of the village. (p15)
* Ightham experienced flooding associated with the intense, highly variable rainfall on 25 June 2016. Rain-gauge records from nearby sites were used in the post‑event analysis to assess how rainfall across the Busty Stream catchment contributed to flooding at Ightham. (p17)
* Fen Pond Road, located upstream of Ightham within the Busty Stream catchment, experienced flooding on 25 June 2016 when high flows exceeded a culvert; this was reported to KCC by Ightham Parish Council and may have resulted in flooding affecting properties in the vicinity. (p23)
* Flooding in the village caused by the Busty Stream and associated culvert issues, resulting in internal flooding of properties and accumulation of silt and debris in the watercourse. (p26)
* Residential properties and residents in the community experienced flooding; the area has been identified as having suffered flood impacts and for follow-up community resilience support. (p27)

## Topics without coordinates

### Busty Stream
* The flood affected properties adjacent to the Busty Stream; properties next to the stream experienced internal flooding during the event reported on 25 June 2016. (p4)
* {1968, 2000, 2003, 01 Sep 2009 - 30 Sep 2009, 01 Nov 2013 - 30 Nov 2013, 01 Jan 2014 - 31 Jan 2014, 25 Jun 2016}: The Busty Stream (the local watercourse) produced extremely high flows that caused widespread flooding and damage to property and businesses in 1968. Residents reported high flows in wet winters (2000 and 2003) that caused external flooding to properties alongside the watercourse. KCC records attribute high flows in the stream to flooding at the eastern end of Busty Lane in Sep 2009, Nov 2013 and Jan 2014 (no property flooding reported for those recorded events). (p6) [MUL]
* {general}: The Busty Stream is normally dry but responds rapidly to heavy rainfall and wet winter conditions because upper catchment soils are on impermeable clays; this rapid response produces high flows in the channel during intense rainfall periods, creating a flash‑flood hazard in its channel and confined reaches between properties and through culverts. (p9)
* Rapid run-off into the Busty Stream generated high, fast-flowing, sediment-laden flows through the village. The channel experienced high water levels and fast flow; where the stream crosses the A227 the flow in-channel reached approximately 1.2 metres depth. Further downstream the stream came out of bank between the bend at Busty Lane and the culvert under Ezel Villas, contributing to backwater and flooding in the lower channel and adjacent areas. (p13)
* The Busty Stream experienced peak flows (a rapid rise in water levels) approximately 2–3 hours after the localized heavy rainfall, as runoff from the catchment collected in the watercourse at Ightham. (p18)
* Heavy localised rainfall on 25 June 2016 generated flash flooding in the Busty Stream; the flattening of local topography reduced conveyance capacity causing pooling of flood water upstream of the culvert at the end of Busty Lane and contributed to downstream flooding risk. (p22)
* High flows in the Busty Stream during the 25th June 2016 event overtopped the watercourse culvert at Fen Pond Road, causing water to flow over the road; there are unconfirmed reports that adjacent properties may have been flooded by the high flows. (p23)
* Quant: approximately 1.2 metres; maximum depth backed up downstream approx 2 metres (p13)

### Kent County Council
* Deployed an out-of-hours Duty Officer (Highways) to assist with implementing a road closure and traffic routing during the flooding; as Lead Local Flood Authority, carried out follow-up investigation and data collection including rainfall and culvert information. (p16)
* KCC officers (Flood and Water Management Team and Highways Drainage Team) carried out a walkover survey after the 25 June 2016 flood, noted debris likely deposited by the event, recorded a small silt build-up, arranged CCTV culvert surveys and actions to address a localised culvert failure. (p19)
* KCC Highways duty officers attended numerous highway flooding reports on 25 June 2016. KCC Flood and Water Management Team inspected the Fen Pond Road site on 28 October following reports that high flows had exceeded culvert capacity. (p23)
* KCC Highways received reports of the flooding from multiple parties and was engaged in subsequent discussions (as the Lead Local Flood Authority) with the developer regarding surface water disposal for the adjacent site. (p24)
* KCC Highways responded to the surface water/run-off flooding on Mote Road and surrounding areas by arranging clearance of debris and undertaking maintenance and repair of the highway drainage system (works conducted under a road closure and completed by November 2016). (p25)
* Quant: one small localised collapse (repaired) (p19)

### Orchard House
* Flows from the adjacent watercourse flowed out of bank and into the grounds via the driveway entrance, causing internal flooding of the property. (p14)
* Debris-trapping and a change of culvert shape restricted flow causing water to pond upstream of the culvert; during the 25th June 2016 event flood depths upstream of the culvert reached about 2 metres and this led to internal flooding of property. (p20)
* The watercourse was found to have come out of bank and flowed downstream over land, flooding Orchard House. The culvert at Orchard House was smaller in capacity and was found to be blocked by debris after the flood. (p21)
* Debris was found trapped in the culvert at Orchard House; this blockage would have greater impact at and downstream of the culvert and places downstream land and property at risk of flooding during high flows. (p22)
* Quant: 2 metres deep (p20)

### The Willows
* Some flow re-entered the watercourse via driveway entrances adjacent to No.2 The Willows where the culverted section re-emerged; discharge from the culvert during the event had sufficient force to cause a small part of a driveway above the watercourse to collapse. (p14)
* Culvert sections at The Willows were surveyed after the flood and showed minor debris and silt deposits likely deposited during the event; the survey recorded square and arch culvert sections at The Willows. (p19) [TAB]
* Quant: 1.8 x 1.8 m culvert (length 9.56 m); 1.8 x 1.2 m arch (length 1.00 m) (p19) [TAB]

### Busty Stream Catchment
* Localized intense rainfall over the Busty Stream catchment produced rapid runoff that entered the stream and generated high/peak flows; applying the Sevenoaks rainfall to the catchment gives an estimated 5.34PCSYM AEP (1 in 18.7 year) for that rainfall intensity. (p18)
* Intense rainfall within the Busty Stream catchment contributed to the 25 June 2016 flooding; the watercourse carried and deposited debris (wood and rubbish) along its channel during the event. (p19)
* Heavy localised rainfall on 25 June 2016 generated flash flooding within the Busty Stream catchment. The flooding reduced conveyance through local culverts, contributing to pooling upstream of culverts and increasing downstream flood risk where debris was found trapped in the Orchard House culvert. (p22)
* Quant: 5.34PCSYM AEP (1 in 18.7 year return period) (p18)
