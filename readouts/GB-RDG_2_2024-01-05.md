### Lower Caversham
* {general}: Lower Caversham has experienced rising River Thames water levels such that artificially raised ground has been created to provide informal protection from those rising levels. The area is identified as being close to the main watercourse and thus at risk of property flooding if river/channel capacity is exceeded. (p8)
* {01 Jan 2024 - 31 Jan 2024}: Residents reported flooding of basement areas that may be indicative of groundwater flooding through river gravels; this flooding occurred concurrently with the January 2024 event, though the direct contribution of groundwater is uncertain. (p10)
* Mapped flood extents show inundation of low-lying areas adjacent to the river in Lower Caversham during the January 2024 event; public consultation was held for affected residents indicating the area experienced flood impacts. (p12) [IMG, PAG]
* Lower Caversham received floodwater flowing northwards from Christchurch Ditch, resulting in inundation of the low-lying area and impacts to nearby properties as reported by residents. (p22)
* Surface water flooding affected roads and pavements in Lower Caversham (notably Queens Road at the entrance opposite Coldicutt Street), impeding vehicular and pedestrian movement in the residential area. (p32) [IMG, PAG]

### River Kennet
* {01 Mar 1947 - 31 Mar 1947}: The River Kennet is cited alongside the Thames as having very high water levels during the March 1947 flood, contributing to extensive flood damage in Reading and the Thames Valley. Parts of the Kennet within the RBC area form navigable sections of the Kennet and Avon Canal (maintenance responsibility noted). (p7)
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024}: High antecedent rainfall and the heavy rainfall from Storm Henk caused saturated soils and large volumes of surface-water runoff into the River Kennet, leading to river flows and levels rising rapidly and exceeding channel capacity, increasing flows downstream and the risk of inundation of adjacent floodplain and urban areas south of Reading. (p8)
* {01 Jan 2024 - 31 Jan 2024}: Rapid runoff and high groundwater/baseflow increased flows in the Kennet and its associated streams, with ephemeral streams flowing when groundwater rose; these processes likely contributed to the January 2024 flood event in the Kennet/Upper Thames area. (p9)
* River Kennet levels rose above the normal range and produced sustained high flows through January, contributing to elevated river levels upstream of Reading. (p11) [PAG]
* River levels at the Theale gauge exceeded the normal range and rose to a notable peak, indicating the Kennet was in flood and contributed to elevated river levels and mapped flood extents through Reading. (p12) [PAG, IMG]
* Water accumulating in the River Kennet floodplain flowed back northwards towards Circuit Lane (via under the railway bridge), causing backflows that contributed to internal property flooding; the River Kennet area was also the subject of Flood Alerts/Warnings during the period. (p16) [PAG]
* Flooding from the River Kennet inundated its floodplain and flowed northwards beneath Briscoe's Bridge. (p29)
* Backflow floodwater was present on the River Kennet floodplain north of Briscoe’s Bridge, inundating wooded floodplain areas with standing/backwater and causing saturated ground and fallen tree debris. (p39) [IMG]
* Quant: 1.78m AD peak on 06 Jan 2024; normal range top 1.05m AD; levels remained above normal from 01 Jan - 27 Jan 2024 (p11) [PAG]
* Quant: Maximum recorded level 1.78m AD on 6th January 2024; levels remained above the normal range from 1st - 27th January 2024. (p12) [PAG, IMG]

(p6) [PAG]
* {01 Mar 1947 - 31 Mar 1947, winter 2002/03, 01 Jan 2014 - 28 Feb 2014}: Reading experienced significant fluvial flooding historically and in recent decades: the March 1947 event caused extensive flood damage across Reading and the Thames Valley; more recent winter events (2002/03 and the 2013/14 double peak event) resulted in properties being internally flooded in Reading. (p7) [MUL]
* River levels in Reading reached their highest level since 1947 in early January 2024, indicating significant river flooding of the borough during the event. (p10)
* River flooding from the River Thames (and high flows on the River Kennet) caused inundation of low-lying land in Reading and created conditions for property flooding; elevated river levels exceeded established thresholds and produced an extended period of above-normal river levels through January 2024. (p12) [PAG, IMG]
* Modelled fluvial inundation affected low-lying riverbanks and meadows in Lower Caversham (notably Christchurch Meadows and King's Meadow) and extends into adjacent urban areas. The modelled extents indicate schools and urban infrastructure in parts of Reading lie within the inundation envelopes for 1 in 20 to 1 in 1000 year events; the map suggests these areas were at risk during the reported flood event. It is unclear from this page alone which specific properties were observed flooded during 05–07 Jan 2024, as the figure shows modelled extents rather than confirmed property-level observations. (p49) [IMG, PAG]

* Quant: River Thames at Reading: exceeded property-flood threshold of 7.25m AD on 5 Jan 2024 (recorded 7.26m AD) and peaked at 7.54m AD on 7 Jan 2024; returned to consistently below 7.00m AD on 11 Jan 2024. River Kennet at Theale: exceeded normal range (1.05m AD) on 1 Jan 2024 and peaked at 1.78m AD on 6 Jan 2024; Kennet remained above normal from 1–27 Jan 2024. Event estimated at between a 1 in 30 and 1 in 50 year (2PCSYM–3.3PCSYM annual probability). (p12) [PAG, IMG]
