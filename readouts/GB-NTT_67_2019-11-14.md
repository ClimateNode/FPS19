

# Tollerton flooding 14 Nov 2019

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Tollerton – November 2019<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/section19reporttollertonnovember2019.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Nottinghamshire
* {07 Nov 2019 - 14 Nov 2019, 14 Nov 2019}: Following exceptional rainfall and saturated ground, flooding occurred across the Nottinghamshire area (including Tollerton). A major incident was declared on 7 November and county-wide flood response actions were carried out (road closures, signage, sandbag distribution). (p1) [PAG, MUL]
* {01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019, 14 Nov 2019}: Prolonged heavy rainfall caused flooding across the Nottinghamshire area; Nottinghamshire Local Resilience Forum declared a major incident and the Environment Agency monitored levels and issued warnings/alerts for the county. (p2) [IMG, MUL]
* {07 Nov 2019 - 14 Nov 2019, 14 Nov 2019}: Quant: 66 road closures; over 750 flood signs; over 5000 sandbags (p1) [PAG, MUL]

### Rushcliffe
* Communities in Rushcliffe were affected by flooding and are in recovery; residents received sandbags and financial support (flood hardship and recovery grants); recovery is being managed by the borough council. (p8)

### Tollerton
* Significant flood event with internal property flooding, gardens and curtilages inundated, public highways affected (Tollerton Lane closed); properties on Tollerton Lane were evacuated. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Prolonged heavy rainfall led to internal property flooding across the village; 14 reported incidents of internal property flooding. Gardens, curtilages and public highways were also affected. (p3) [IMG]
* Quant: 14 reported incidents of internal property flooding (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 14 incidents of internal property flooding (p3) [IMG]

### Tollerton Lane, Tollerton
* Severe flooding caused closure of Tollerton Lane and led to evacuation of properties located on the lane. (p3) [IMG]
* Surface-water drainage system (highway drainage, culverts and ditches) were overwhelmed during prolonged rainfall, with runoff from surrounding saturated fields exacerbating flows. This led to internal flooding of properties located low in the topography adjacent to the watercourses and flow paths shown on the map. (p4) [IMG]
* Shown within the Environment Agency's mapped flood‑risk shading (both river and surface‑water maps). As the figure notes the predicted flooding matches areas actually affected, Tollerton Lane was likely within the inundated/at‑risk area and may have experienced road surface inundation and local access disruption. (p6) [IMG]

### Cotgrave Lane, Tollerton
* Map and description identify two clusters of internal flooding associated with Cotgrave Lane (one at its junction with Tollerton Lane and another further east), indicating internal property flooding and associated impacts to nearby gardens/curtilages and highway. (p3) [IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Coordinated emergency planning and response to the flooding: delivered sandbags to affected residents; facilitated emergency road closures; carried out investigations on culverts and watercourses in the vicinity of flooded properties and checked highway gully connectivity/condition; liaised with Parish Council to offer future support and promote local flood risk awareness; administered the Flood Hardship Fund. (p7)
* As Lead Local Flood Authority, NCC is involved in flood investigation and response: administers Flood Warden scheme, supports provision of local sandbag stores and a Community Flood Signage Scheme, and is continuing work to reduce flood risk and support vulnerable residents. (p8)

### Rushcliffe Borough Council
* Provided emergency response support during the flooding: delivered sandbags to affected residents; actively engaged in the Section 19 Flood Investigation and ongoing management/recovery actions following the event. (p7) [PAG]
* Responded to a flooding event affecting Rushcliffe communities: provided emergency response support, delivered sandbags to affected residents, managed and continues to manage recovery, and administered the flood hardship fund, community recovery grants, and council tax/business rate relief. (p8)

### A38
* Closure of the public highway (Tollerton Lane) due to severe flooding; properties on Tollerton Lane were evacuated. (p2) [IMG]

### Highway Authority
* Provided site-based presence and investigations immediately following the flood event; responsible for maintenance of public highways including highway drainage assets and checking gully connectivity/condition in response to the flooding. (p7)

### East Midlands Railway
* As VIA East Midlands Ltd (Highway Authority contractor) provided site-based presence and investigations immediately following the flooding and was involved in highway drainage/asset actions post‑event. (p7)

### Environment Agency
* Co‑administered flood response measures in the area: works with NCC to administer Flood Warden schemes and a Community Flood Signage Scheme and supports local flood risk management activities related to the flooding event. (p8)
