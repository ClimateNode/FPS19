

# Egmanton flooding 18 Oct 2023 - 20 Oct 2023

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Egmanton – Storm Babet, October 2023<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/egmanton-s19-storm-babet-oct-2023.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Egmanton
* Heavy rainfall (Storm Babet, 18–20 October 2023) on saturated ground led to flooding in the village, with roads and properties being flooded; local watercourses converge in the village centre and flow out along Weston Road, contributing to flooding behavior. (p1)
* During the event, properties in the village experienced surface water flooding: 11 residential properties suffered internal flooding; additional properties experienced flooding to gardens and outbuildings. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Internal flooding across the village driven primarily by watercourses bursting their banks; additional surface water flows entered the village from roads to the west, concentrating flooding in the central/west area (Main St, Kirton Rd) with a smaller separate cluster to the east (around Burnmoor Ln). (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Flood Zone 3 (highest risk) and Flood Zone 2 are mapped along the main river that runs west–east through Egmanton; predicted surface water extents also place high/medium risk along the river and tributaries. These mapped extents correlate with the majority of flooding experienced during Storm Babet in the village. (p4) [IMG]
* Quant: 11 residential properties (p2) [IMG, PAG]

### Nottinghamshire
* The Major Incident described caused significant impacts across the county; figures in the report confirm the majority of flooding during Storm Babet affected areas in the county. (p5) [PAG]
* Major incident with significant impacts across the county including internal flooding of properties, delivery of sandbags to affected communities and the closure of roads to promote safety and avoid additional flood damage. (p6)
* Quant: 77 roads (p6)

### Newark and Sherwood
* Flooding in the district led to evacuations, delivery of flood protection equipment (aqua sacs), visits to impacted communities and vulnerable individuals, provision of emergency accommodation and active communications and briefings to support affected residents. (p6)

### Kirton Road, Egmanton
* Map (Figure 2) shows a watercourse flowing across/near Kirton Road and the report states Egmanton experienced internal flooding of residences during Storm Babet. The location of the watercourse crossing Kirton Road implicates the road corridor in the village-scale flooding and in the routing of floodwater that affected properties. (p2) [IMG]
* Located within the larger highlighted cluster of internal flooding in the central/west part of Egmanton. Flooding was associated with local watercourses bursting their banks during Storm Babet and was exacerbated by significant flows entering the village from roads to the west. (p3) [IMG]

### Weston Road, Egmanton
* Map (Figure 2) shows a watercourse crossing or passing near Weston Road with flow arrows toward the northeast. The village experienced internal property flooding during Storm Babet, so the watercourse location at Weston Road implicates this road corridor in the local flood routing that affected properties. (p2) [IMG]
* Mapped adjacent to the main river flood corridor (Flood Zone 3/2) and shown within/near predicted surface-water flood extents on the supplied maps. Figures 4 and 5 are reported to correlate with the majority of flooding during Storm Babet, indicating Weston Road is within the area exposed to river and surface-water flooding and therefore likely experienced or was at risk of inundation and associated disruption. (p4) [IMG]

### Burnmoor Lane, Egmanton
* Identified by the smaller highlighted oval on the map as an area affected by internal flooding (eastern cluster of Egmanton) during the event. (p3) [IMG]
* Burnmoor Lane falls within the mapped flood risk corridor (Flood Zone 3/2) and within predicted surface water extents around the main river and tributaries; it is therefore identified as part of the area that experienced flooding during Storm Babet. (p4) [IMG]

### Main Street, Egmanton
* Within the central/west cluster highlighted as affected by internal flooding. Flooding coincided with watercourses bursting their banks during Storm Babet and was worsened by flows entering from the west. (p3) [IMG]

### Holme Lane, Egmanton
* Mapped within the area of Flood Zone 3/2 adjacent to the main river corridor and shown in surface water flood extents; therefore Holme Lane is implicated in the mapped flood risk and in the areas that flooded during Storm Babet. (p4) [IMG]

### Mill Lane, Egmanton
* Mill Lane is within the mapped area of Flood Zone 3/2 along the river corridor and within surface water flood extents, indicating it was part of the area that flooded during Storm Babet. (p4) [IMG]

### River Trent
* Predicted extreme River Trent levels increased the risk of river flooding, prompting the council to coordinate in-person visits to high-risk communities and to take preparedness actions. (p7)


## Topics without coordinates

### North Nottinghamshire
* Part of the IDB districts where TVIDB had staff on site during and after the flood event and where regular maintenance regimes have been delivered to reduce risk and identify potential risk areas. (p7)

### Manor Farm
* Area around Manor Farm fell within the larger central/west cluster identified as experiencing internal flooding; impacted by watercourse bank failure and surface water flows entering the village. (p3) [IMG]

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Prepared to support evacuated residents and provided emergency accommodation; communications officers worked with other agencies to warn and inform the public and provided daily briefings to councillors and community leaders; commenced actions to clean up roads and paths and remove bulky waste from flooded homes; staff contacted affected individuals to identify humanitarian needs and offer direct support or liaised with other agencies; coordinated in-person visits to high-risk communities because of predicted extreme River Trent levels; led recovery activities including delivering grant funding to eligible people; worked with parish and town councils to restock stores of aqua sacs; developing Flood Warden / Road Closure training and reviewing Property Flood Resilience Programme for communities affected by Storm Babet and Henk. (p7)
