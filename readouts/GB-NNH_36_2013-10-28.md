

# Oundle flooding 28 Oct 2013

## Place names with coordinates

### Lyveden Brook
* Overtopping of Lyveden Brook during the incident caused high risk of internal flooding to residential properties; damage and loss occurred due to the depth and velocity of the overtopping water. (p9) [PAG]
* Inspection during the flood investigation found a severe narrowing of the watercourse upstream of Stoke Doyle Road with a section of barbed wire across the channel and a build-up of debris, indicating obstruction and elevated flow conditions; downstream reaches were described as narrow and shallow and the brook flows to the River Nene. (p10) [PAG]
* Depth and flow of water from Lyveden Brook caused flooding that affected people and property during the event; listed as the cause of various incidents of flooding on 28/10/2013. (p11) [PAG]
* Banks overtopped; reported to be over one metre higher than normal and flowing strongly. High water from the brook caused high-velocity overland flow across garden boundaries at Warren Bridge, led to significant damage to outbuildings, came close to internally flooding a residential property, and high levels upstream affected sports facilities (closure). (p12)
* An excessive flow in Lyveden Brook caused raised water levels and strong flows that overtopped banks and affected adjoining property; reductions in the cross-sectional area of the watercourse are identified as a factor in the high velocity and depth of flow. (p17)
* Reductions to the cross-sectional area of the watercourse and man-made flow controls upstream and downstream of Warren Bridge increased flow velocity and water depth, contributing to flooding at this location; these factors were identified for further investigation by the responsible owners. (p18)
* Overtopping / insufficient channel capacity from Lyveden Brook contributed to flooding in the town catchment, prompting recommendations to reinstate historic drainage (re‑excavate ditches, repair/replace piped/culvert sections, dredge and trim watercourses, replace structures and clear vegetation) to reduce future flood risk. (p19) [PAG]
* Lyveden Brook overtopped its banks and discharged muddy floodwater into adjacent private gardens and yards, submerging vegetation and enabling water to reach house bases. (p44) [IMG, PAG]
* Lyveden Brook was swollen and overflowed its banks, producing muddy, high-velocity flow that submerged the bases of trees and shrubs and inundated the rear of a nearby property and gardens. (p45) [IMG, PAG]
* Lyveden Brook overflowed its banks, inundating adjacent vegetation and land; trees and shrubs were partially submerged and water encroached onto the embankment. (p47) [IMG]
* High water levels observed along Lyveden Brook at the rear of Warren Bridge. Evidence of elevated flows and deposits of debris at a public sewer outfall opposite Warren Bridge. (p49) [IMG]
* High water levels were observed at a public sewer outfall opposite Warren Bridge, with deposits of debris indicating the brook reached elevated levels and overtopped or reached infrastructure margins. (p51) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: over one metre higher than normal; approximately two days (p12)

### Warren Bridge, Oundle
* {spring 1998, 04 Nov 2012, 22 Dec 2012, 28 Oct 2013}: Flooding of gardens at Warren Bridge and significant damage to outbuildings at a residential property (impacts recorded for the 28/10/2013 event). The location also experienced garden flooding on 04/11/2012 and 22/12/2012, and internal flooding of at least one residential property in Spring 1998. (p11) [TAB, MUL]
* Rear gardens became flooded; varying risk to dwellings depending on floor level. High-velocity flow across garden boundaries caused significant damage to outbuildings and nearly internally flooded a residential property. Land opposite the rear gardens was also flooded. (p12)
* Man-made controls of the natural flow upstream and downstream of Warren Bridge, and reductions in the watercourse cross-sectional area in that area, are identified as contributing factors to the high velocity and depth of water during the flood. (p17)
* Muddy floodwater pooled up to the house wall at the property by Warren Bridge and partially entered the adjoining garage, affecting access and property safety. (p42) [IMG, PAG]
* Rear garden and patio inundated by floodwater (Lyveden Brook adjacent); outdoor furniture and potted plants partially submerged; a summer house was moved/rotated on its footings and the patio area was submerged by muddy water. (p43) [IMG, PAG]
* Floodwater from Lyveden Brook overtopped nearby banks and inundated rear gardens adjacent to Warren Bridge; water reached up to the base of houses and garden areas, moving/rotating a summer house on its footings and submerging garden structures. (p44) [IMG, PAG]
* Area at the rear of a property adjacent to Warren Bridge was inundated when Lyveden Brook overflowed; high-velocity floodwater flowed from a neighbouring garden into the property's rear/passageway, threatening structures and submerging vegetation. (p45) [IMG, PAG]
* High‑velocity, turbulent muddy water flowed from a neighbouring property's garden into the rear of a property at Warren Bridge, passing forcefully through a wooden fence and inundating the rear area and narrow passage. (p46) [PAG]
* High-velocity flow of floodwater entered the rear garden from neighbouring property; garden and grounds were inundated and floodwater reached and surrounded a small outbuilding at the rear of the property. (p47) [IMG, PAG]
* High water levels recorded at the rear of Warren Bridge; nearby public sewer outfall opposite the bridge showed evidence of high water and deposits of debris. (p49) [IMG, PAG]
* Public sewer outfall opposite Warren Bridge was nearly submerged (water level near top of brick structure) with deposits of debris; Lyveden Brook at the rear of Warren Bridge also showed evidence of high water levels and debris deposits. (p50) [IMG, PAG]
* {spring 1998, 04 Nov 2012, 22 Dec 2012, 28 Oct 2013}: Quant: 1 residential property (damage to outbuildings on 28/10/2013); 1+ residential property (internal flooding in Spring 1998) (p11) [TAB, MUL]

### Oundle
* High water levels and strong flows in an Ordinary Watercourse caused the watercourse banks to overtop, with overflow affecting private property in Oundle. (p6) [PAG]
* High water levels and strong flows in an ordinary watercourse caused the watercourse banks to overtop and overflow into private property; private properties were affected by floodwater. (p7) [PAG]
* Residential properties suffered internal flooding; damage and loss were caused by the depth and velocity of water overtopping the Ordinary Watercourse (Lyveden Brook). (p9)
* On 28th October 2013 depth and flow from Lyveden Brook caused flooding in Oundle: gardens at Warren Bridge were flooded and there was significant damage to outbuildings at a residential property. Road drainage (gullies/kerb offlets) on Benefield Road north of Lyveden Brook were observed blocked with silt and debris, contributing to local surface-water issues. (p11)
* Various incidents of flooding on 28 October 2013 affected people and property as a result of depth and flow from Lyveden Brook. Rear gardens and land were flooded; sports facilities were affected and closed for approximately two days. (p12) [PAG]
* High water levels in Lyveden Brook affected local sports facilities in Oundle, causing the facilities to close. (p13) [PAG]
* Excessive flow in Lyveden Brook caused water levels to rise and strong flows to overtop the brook banks, affecting adjoining property in Oundle. (p17)
* Flooding in the town on 28th October 2013 was driven by high-velocity, deep flows in Lyveden Brook associated with reductions to the watercourse cross-sectional area and man-made controls near Warren Bridge, causing river flooding of parts of Oundle. (p18)
* Quant: 1 residential property (outbuildings significantly damaged) (p11)
* Quant: approximately two days (p12) [PAG]
* Quant: closure of sports facilities for approximately two days (p13) [PAG]

### Biggin Fish Pond
* During the flood investigation the inflow to the silt pond was observed to be at a high level and there was evidence that water levels had been higher, with debris deposited around the pond banks; the main lake discharges via a weir and spillway when levels exceed the weir capacity and some historic outlet structures east of the lake were found disused or non‑operational. (p10)
* Identified as a feature to inspect after the 28 October 2013 flood incident: the Environment Agency was asked to review inspection and supervision under the Reservoirs Act 1975 and to consider an immediate inspection to assess current flow controls, water and silt levels, and any recent changes to sluice/overflow arrangements that could have affected downstream flows. (p20)
* The pond spilled over its grassy spillway/overflow and discharged water via the main dam/weir into Lyveden Brook. Photographs show the spillway overtopping but the embankment appeared vegetated and without obvious erosion. (p53) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Water levels at the pond were high, with water reaching close to the adjacent path and a small wooden platform/dock partially submerged at the pond edge. Vegetated depression at the base of the dam indicates a historic watercourse route and a potential outlet location, and a weir from an upstream silt pond is present. (p56) [IMG, PAG]
* Elevated water levels in the upstream silt pond caused pond water to encroach onto and partially flood a woodland/dirt path alongside the pond. Photographs show the waterline reaching the path and high water at the point where a watercourse enters the upstream silt pond (stone culvert/bridge). (p58) [IMG, PAG]

### Northamptonshire
* A formal Section 19 flood investigation was carried out by the LLFA into a flood incident in Oundle (within Northamptonshire) on 28 October 2013. The incident was caused by high flows in an Ordinary Watercourse leading to overtopping of its banks and overflow affecting private property. (p6)
* Extreme flooding occurred in the town of Oundle (within Northamptonshire). The flooding was described as extreme in nature and extent and prompted the Lead Local Flood Authority to carry out a formal Section 19 investigation despite the county thresholds for a formal investigation not being formally met. (p8)

### Benefield Road, Oundle
* {general}: Gullies/kerb offlets on Benefield Road north of Lyveden Brook appeared to be blocked with silt and debris in many cases, indicating blockage of drainage infrastructure associated with the flood event conditions. (p11)

### River Nene
* Elevated water levels in the River Nene in the vicinity of the incident were relevant to the flood event and could affect flows from other watercourses into the river, potentially contributing to downstream flooding. (p20)

### Northamptonshire County Council
* {general}: Acting as the Lead Local Flood Authority (LLFA) and report author, NCC is responsible for producing and sharing documents to assist residents affected by surface water runoff and groundwater emergence, and for assessing highway drainage assets and capacity to identify locations where insufficient capacity creates flood risk to properties. (p20)
* The report specifically recommends an immediate inspection of Biggin Fish Pond to assess the arrangement of sluice and overflow controls, and water and silt levels, and to identify any recent changes to those controls and assess any effect those changes would have on downstream flows. This indicates the pond and its control structures are being investigated for their possible contribution to increased downstream flows during the 28 October 2013 flood event. (p21)

### Anglian Water
* Public sewerage system received water from external sources (surface runoff/other watercourses), potentially causing surcharge of sewers and presenting flood risk to properties; assets were required to be assessed for insufficient capacity and improvement where needed. (p20)

### Environment Agency
* The Environment Agency was tasked to review monitoring of water levels in the River Nene in the vicinity of the flood incident (to advise if these could affect flows from other watercourses), to review inspection and supervision arrangements for Biggin Fish Pond under the Reservoirs Act 1975, and to consider an immediate inspection to assess flow controls and any changes affecting downstream flows during the 28 October 2013 event. (p20)
