

# York Unitary Authority flooding 13 Aug 2018

Reference document:<br>York 13th August 2018 Section 19 Surface Water Flood Investigation<br>151\_York/Annex 1 York 130818 Section 19 Surface Water Flood Investigation March19.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### York Unitary Authority
* Intense rainfall on 13 August 2018 produced widespread standing water and recorded flooding across the city, including flooded roads, flooded gardens and in a small number of cases flooding of property. Highway drainage and sewerage infrastructure were tested and exceeded, leading to inundation of streets and surrounding areas; some locations had identified drainage defects that likely increased the chance of flooding. (p4) [PAG]
* Intense, high‑intensity rainfall on 13 August 2018 overwhelmed drainage causing widespread standing water on the highway network; a number of properties were internally flooded and outbuildings/gardens were affected. Council officers investigated flooding at locations across the city. (p6) [PAG, IMG]
* {summer 2007, 2012, 2013}: Localised surface water flooding occurred at various locations across the city on recorded occasions, with different locations affected on each occasion. (p7) [MUL]
* Impacts of the 13th August 2018 storm were spread wide across the city, with ponding of water on roads and footways and significant impacts to people and property reported to the council, emergency services and media. (p18)
* Surface water flooded multiple roads, gardens, driveways and non-attached garages across the city (examples listed in the report include Hawthorn Avenue, North Lane, Kennedy Drive, Longcroft, Mulberry Drive, Oaken Grove, Old Orchard, Ripley Grove). Gullies were cleared; downstream Yorkshire Water sewer surcharging and backups were linked to local sewer issues (e.g. Birch Lane, Albleton Grove). (p29) [PAG]
* Surface water flooded roads in the Haxby and Wigginton area (multiple streets referenced) and a pumping station malfunction caused the beck to back up into drainage systems, leading to surcharged sewers and local inundation. (p31)
* Quant: 123 locations (p4) [PAG]
* Quant: 123 locations (p6) [PAG, IMG]

### Westfield Beck
* Majority of surface water flows from Haxby and Wigginton drain to Westfield Beck during flooding; the system requires pumping to remove these flows and is implicated in local drainage and highway flooding issues. (p12) [PAG]
* Receives runoff from the Westfield Beck catchment; during the 13 August 2018 event flows draining to the beck were managed by the Westfield Beck pumping station and the watercourse/pumping arrangement influenced the ability of local surface water and sewer systems to discharge, affecting drainage efficiency in the catchment. (p13) [IMG, PAG]
* Debris build-up on the weed screen and equipment failures on 13 August 2018 elevated upstream river levels. The automated penstock was not opening sufficiently and one pump was found not to be working, reducing the capacity of the surface water drainage network and causing concern for local residents. The IDB attended and had to use a machine to clear the screen, remaining on site until late evening. (p14)
* Malfunction at the Westfield Beck pumping station (pumps and penstock) likely caused the beck to back up into drainage systems, contributing to surcharge of Yorkshire Water's sewage system and surface water flooding of roads, gardens and properties (including ground-floor flooding and roads/back lanes flooded). (p27)
* Malfunction at Westfield Beck pumping station (penstock/pumps) caused the beck to back up into the IDB and Yorkshire Water drainage system, leading to surcharging of the Yorkshire Water sewage system downstream and backup into connected drainage. (p29)
* Malfunction at Westfield Beck pumping station (pump and penstock issues) caused the beck to back up into connected IDB and Yorkshire Water drainage systems, contributing to surface water flooding of roads and surcharge of the sewer network. (p30) [PAG]
* A malfunction at the Westfield Beck pumping station (50PCSYM pump failure and restrictive penstock operation) caused Westfield Beck to back up into adjacent drainage systems (IDB and Yorkshire Water), producing flooding and sewer surcharge. (p31)
* Malfunction at the Westfield Beck pumping station (approximately 50PCSYM failure of pumps and penstock operation) caused the beck to back up into IDB and Yorkshire Water drainage systems, leading to surface-water flooding of roads and at least one property's ground floor and sewer backflow into a business. (p32)
* Quant: 50PCSYM failure of pumps; 1 property (ground floor flooded); 1 business (toilet backflow) (p32)
