

# Didcot flooding 31 May 2018

## Place names with coordinates

### Didcot Parkway Station
* The rail station was the subject of a flood incident investigation (document title identifies Didcot Rail Station as the location of the flood incident). (p1)
* {16 Sep 2016, 31 May 2018}: Flooding at Didcot Railway Station on 31 May 2018 led to disruption at this transport interchange and required assistance to commuters and staff; the event prompted a formal investigation. (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* Internal flooding of the Parkway station building (Didcot Railway Station); water entered and flooded internal areas of the property adjacent to the rail track and station parking. (p7) [PAG]
* Flooding affected the busy transport interchange: the station main building, subway and station car park were flooded. Surface water sewer exceedance during severe rainfall, together with multiple blockages (including road cones lodged in pipework), debris at critical pipe locations and a grade 5 sewer defect, were identified as contributing factors. Network Rail removed blockages, reconnected sewer sections, improved connectivity to surface water storage tanks and cleared downstream ditches and debris. (p9)
* Site was the subject of a flood incident investigation indicating the station was affected by flooding. (p19) [PAG]
* A building on the railway station frontage experienced water ingress (map annotation ‘Flooding entered building here’), indicating the station precinct suffered flood entry at that location. (p21) [PAG]

### Didcot
* Didcot is named as the location of the flood incident report (the flood incident occurred in Didcot, the town containing the affected rail station). (p1)
* Flooding affected the premises of Didcot Railway Station during the incident; the station was the subject of the Section 19 flood investigation. (p3) [PAG]
* {16 Sep 2016, 31 May 2018}: The flood at The Parkway/Didcot Railway Station on 31 May 2018 impacted the town's rail interchange, disrupting commuters and requiring emergency assistance. (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* Flooding affected the area around Didcot Railway Station: downstream sewer blockage near the Cow Lane underpass (traffic cones wedged in sewer), debris collecting within two pipe sections causing serious flow restrictions close to surface water storage tanks and causing the drainage system to surcharge; downstream ditch base levels were raised above the main outfall sewer invert, reducing outfall capacity. (p8) [PAG]
* Flooding at Didcot Parkway station (within Didcot) disrupted a busy public transport interchange when surface water sewer exceedance during severe rainfall led to inundation of the station building, subway and car park; emergency services and station staff responded. (p9) [PAG]
* Didcot Railway Station was the subject of the flood incident: the station experienced flooding driven by surface water sewer exceedance during severe rainfall. Contributing factors cited include blockages in pipework (road cones wedged in the sewer and debris), a grade 5 sewer defect and interceptor maintenance. Network Rail removed blockages and made improvements to reduce the risk of recurrence. (p10) [PAG]
* Flooding affecting Didcot Railway Station and surrounding infrastructure on 31 May 2018. Impacts included overwhelmed surface water drainage, risk of foul-water backflow and compromised access/entrance to the station; adjacent car park drainage and downstream highway ditches/culverts were implicated, prompting recommendations for drain/sewer/culvert maintenance and entrance modifications to restrict floodwater access. (p14) [PAG]
* Flooding affected Didcot Railway Station and its immediate surroundings on 31st May 2018, with surface water and sewer runoff overwhelming drainage assets and prompting an investigation and recommendations to assess sewer capacity and highway drainage maintenance. (p16) [PAG]
* Flooding entered a building at the Didcot Parkway station frontage, demonstrating that part of Didcot was impacted by floodwater on the date shown. (p21) [IMG, PAG]

### Cow Lane, Ladygrove
* A sewer near the Cow Lane underpass contained traffic cones wedged in the centre causing a large blockage; this restriction reduced flow capacity and was linked to surcharging of the drainage system. (p8) [PAG]
* Work carried out to break out the sewer near Cow Lane to remove traffic cones that were wedged in the pipe; an access chamber was installed and the sewer reconnected to restore flow and reduce a source of blockage that contributed to the sewer exceedance. (p9)


## Topics without coordinates

### Network Rail
* {16 Sep 2016, 31 May 2018}: An obstructed outfall from Thames Water into Network Rail's ditch contributed to flooding of the drainage system and flooding at Didcot Railway Station; the ditch required maintenance which was identified as a cause in a previous (16 Sept 2016) incident and Network Rail infrastructure was implicated in station flooding. (p6) [MUL]
* Network Rail drainage assets at the station were affected: a large blockage was identified in a sewer downstream of Network Rail's ditch near Cow Lane underpass caused by traffic cones; debris in two pipe sections within the station drainage caused serious flow restrictions that reduced inflow/outflow to surface water storage tanks and caused surcharging; a Network Rail sewer draining the underpass had a grade 5 defect. Network Rail undertook remedial works, breaking out the sewer near Cow Lane to remove the cones, installing an access chamber and reconnecting the sewer. (p8) [PAG]
* Undertook inspection and remedial works to the surface water drainage and sewer network serving the station: removed blockages in the forecourt drainage, improved connectivity of sewers to surface water storage tanks under the station car park, replaced a small section of sewer in an open ditch, cleared a downstream ditch, repaired a grade 5 sewer defect and removed traffic cones wedged in the sewer (access chamber installed and sewer reconnected). (p9)
* Responded to flooding at Didcot Railway Station caused by surface water sewer exceedance; removed blockages from pipework (including road cones), cleared debris and silt from the main outfall ditch, made improvements to reduce risk of recurrence and updated drainage records. (p10)
* Surface water flooding impacted drains and the station entrance at Didcot Railway Station; Network Rail was recommended to inspect surface water drains as part of future maintenance and to adjust the entrance to restrict access to flood waters. (p14)

### Oxfordshire County Council
* {31 May 2018, 01 Jun 2018 - 30 Jun 2018}: Established a flood investigation working group in June 2018 (with South Oxfordshire District Council, Network Rail, Great Western Railway, Thames Water, Thames Flood Advisors and the Environment Agency) to investigate the 31 May 2018 station flood. (p6) [MUL]
* Historical records obtained from Oxfordshire County Council archives were used to update sewer records and statutory maps for the station area, supporting the investigation into sewer condition and blockages that contributed to the flooding incident. (p8)
* Cleared a downstream ditch and programmed further clearance work downstream as part of the response to drainage exceedance contributing to flooding at the station. (p9)
* As LLFA, investigated the incident and arranged clearance of silt from the watercourse downstream of the station; agreed actions for ongoing maintenance and to review/update maintenance schedules. (p10) [PAG]
* Local drainage features (a section of ditch and a culvert) contributing to the flood were identified for review; OCC was recommended to review drainage plans and consider adding the ditch/culvert to the critical drainage register. (p14)

### Thames Water
* {16 Sep 2016}: Thames Water's outfall into Network Rail's ditch was obstructed (maintenance required), which contributed to drainage failure and flooding at Didcot Railway Station (noted in the 16 Sept 2016 incident). (p6)
* A section of Network Rail ditch downstream had base levels slightly above the main outfall sewer invert level from Thames Water and Network Rail sewers, creating a potential flow restriction/backwater condition; Network Rail also had a sewer with a grade 5 defect affecting the underpass drainage. (p8)
* Thames Water's statutory asset records were updated following the investigation and survey of the surface water drainage and sewers implicated in the event; plans were circulated confirming which organisation had maintenance responsibility for affected assets. (p9)
* Participated in the response to sewer exceedance flooding by updating drainage records as part of the investigation and coordination to reduce recurrence. (p10)
* Sewer infrastructure (including a syphon and sewer pipework) was implicated in the incident; Thames Water was recommended to include syphon maintenance in its regular programme and to inspect sewer pipework at regular intervals. (p14)

### South Oxfordshire District Council
* South Oxfordshire District Council staff surveyed downstream ditches and inspected manhole chambers with Network Rail to assess silt levels and potential flow restrictions; historical records from the Council's archives were used to update station sewer records and statutory maps and to provide a condition assessment of surface water sewers in the forecourt and downstream system. (p8)
* Cleared debris from the banks of the watercourse and the trash screen adjacent to the Orchard Centre Car Park and took part in inspection of the surface water drainage system in the vicinity of the station to examine potential flood sources. (p9)
* Surface water flooding affected the car park adjacent to the station; South Oxfordshire District Council was asked to ensure the car park's drainage systems are cleansed and working. (p14)

### Great Western Railway
* Flooding at The Parkway (Didcot Railway Station) on 31 May 2018 caused disruption at the transport interchange affecting commuters and staff; emergency crews (Oxfordshire Fire and Rescue Service) attended and GWR were engaged in the investigation (GWR were unable to provide photographs of the event). (p6)
* Flooding affected station drainage elements under GWR remit (flow control, foul water backflow valve and drainage interceptor); GWR was recommended to include maintenance of these items and inspect surface water drains as part of its schedule. (p14)

### Oxfordshire Fire and Rescue Service
* Attended Didcot Railway Station during the flood on 31 May 2018 and assisted commuters and station staff as part of the immediate response. (p6)

### Highway Authority
* Blocked or insufficiently emptied gullies and highway culverts/ditches contributed to surface water flooding at the critical location near the station; the Highway Authority was asked to review gully emptying frequency and monitor downstream ditches and culverts. (p14)
