

# Ribble Valley flooding 01 Dec 2015 - 31 Dec 2015

Reference document:<br>Ribble Valley District Flood Report<br>069\_Lancashire County/ribble-valley-final-report.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Lancashire
* Significant flooding occurred across Lancashire in December 2015, affecting communities and exposing shortcomings in how flood risk messages were communicated to those communities. (p3)
* {December 2015}: December 2015 produced the highest river flows on record and widespread flooding across the county, causing a huge impact on people, homes and businesses. (p8) [TAB]
* Storms and heavy rainfall in November–December 2015 caused widespread flooding across Lancashire during December 2015, flooding properties and damaging private gardens, garages and outbuildings, business premises, agricultural property and critical public infrastructure including roads, bridges, retaining walls, water treatment works, power and communications installations, and community buildings such as schools, village and town halls. (p9)
* Experienced widespread flooding during two major storm events in December 2015 affecting multiple communities across the county. Consequences included countywide data collection and timelines, a countywide Section 19 investigation and follow‑up 'stage two' reporting, increased household sign‑up to Environment Agency flood warnings and the formation of new local Flood Action Groups to support communities. (p10) [PAG]
* One property in the Wiswell (Pendleton Road area) suffered internal flooding. An ordinary watercourse was identified close to the affected property but it is not clear whether this was the primary source of the flooding. (p23) [TAB]
* {December 2015}: Quant: over 2,500 properties flooded in Lancashire (p8) [TAB]
* Quant: over 2,500 properties (p9)
* Quant: 1 property (p23) [TAB]

### River Calder
* The River Calder overtopped its banks and contributed to the flooding in Billington (combined with overland surface water), leading to internal flooding of properties. (p14) [TAB]
* The River Calder overtopped its banks and was identified as the primary source of flooding that caused internal flooding at the local community referenced by MSFW140 on 26 December 2015. (p18)
* The River Calder overtopped its banks on 26/12/2015 and was identified as the primary source of internal flooding in Whalley on that date. (p20)
* Flooding from the River Calder caused fluvial flooding in Whalley (the Environment Agency's work will look into the 26 December 2015 event and its return period). (p21) [TAB]
* Quant: 50 properties (p14) [TAB]
* Quant: 3 properties (p18)
* Quant: 196 properties (p20)

### Ribble Valley
* In the Barrow (Barrow Brook) area on 26 December 2015, internal flooding occurred to 1 property and to the local primary school; a report was also made relating to commercial premises. Overflow from Barrow Lodge contributed to local roads being submerged and caused access problems. Other homes are thought likely to have been flooded but were not reported to Ribble Valley District Council at the time of the report. (p11) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 1 property; 1 primary school (p11) [TAB, PAG]

### Pendle
* Affected by the extreme storms and rainfall in November–December 2015 that caused widespread flooding across Lancashire in December 2015. Local residents and businesses in Pendle were impacted and a multi-agency drop-in event was held at New Road Community Centre (21 Jan 2016) to provide advice and support on recovery and flood resilience. (p9)

### West Lancashire
* Experienced impacts from the extreme November–December 2015 storms that produced county-wide flooding in December 2015. Residents and businesses in West Lancashire were affected; multi-agency support events were held in the district (Parbold Village Hall on 25 Jan 2016 and Fiveways Public House on 01 Feb 2016) to assist recovery, provide advice on warnings and resilience, and gather eyewitness reports. (p9)

### Rossendale
* Communities in Rossendale were affected by the December 2015 floods; recovery support was provided locally, including a drop-in event in Irwell Vale (10 Feb 2016) for affected residents. (p9)

### Barrow
* Internal flooding on 26 December 2015 affected properties and community buildings in the village. Specifically, 1 property and the local primary school suffered internal flooding, and there was a reported incident relating to commercial premises. The Cockerill Terrace area was also affected (see separate entry) with properties internally flooded. Local roads were submerged, causing access problems. (p12) [PAG]
* Residential properties in the Cockerill Terrace area of Barrow experienced internal flooding during the December 2015 event. (p13)
* Quant: 1 property; 1 local primary school; 2 properties (Cockerill Terrace) (p12) [PAG]
* Quant: 2 properties (p13)

### Whalley
* 210 properties experienced internal flooding during December 2015: 11 properties internally flooded on 05/12/2015 (primary source: ordinary watercourse adjacent to Brookside Close overtopping), 3 properties internally flooded on 12/12/2015, and 196 properties internally flooded on 26/12/2015 (primary source: River Calder overtopping). (p20)
* Whalley experienced flooding during the December 2015 event (including the 26 December 2015 event). Flooding was caused by fluvial overflow from the River Calder combined with intense rainfall and affected properties and infrastructure; the Lead Local Flood Authority completed an investigation and assessed the surface water drainage network and options for managing flood risk. (p21) [TAB]
* Surface water flooding occurred in December 2015; the LLFA investigated the key surface water drainage network in the village, identified opportunities for short-, medium- and long-term flood risk management, and recommended further assessments and appraisal to define mitigation works (including consideration of culvert replacement). (p22) [PAG]
* Quant: 210 properties; 11 properties; 3 properties; 196 properties (p20)

### Billington
* Internal flooding of properties in the village; primary sources were a combination of overland surface water flows and the River Calder overtopping its banks. (p14) [TAB]
* Flooding from the River Calder affected Billington (notably the 26 December 2015 event is being investigated). The event prompted a Strategic Outline Case to assess properties and infrastructure at risk and the economic damages to inform potential government-funded defences. (p15) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 50 properties (p14) [TAB]

### Brookside Close, Whalley
* An ordinary watercourse adjacent to Brookside Close overtopped its banks and was identified as the primary source of internal flooding on 05/12/2015 affecting local properties. The trash screen at the junction with King Street was inspected and maintained after the event and placed on a weekly inspection list. (p20) [TAB]
* Quant: 11 properties (p20) [TAB]

### King Street, Whalley
* The trash screen at the junction of King Street and Brookside Close was inspected and maintained by Lancashire Highways following the December 2015 flooding and is now on a weekly inspection schedule (response to flooding impacts). (p20) [TAB]
* The King Street culvert was identified during post-December 2015 investigations as a potential failure point contributing to flooding; options considered included replacing large sections of the culvert or repairing it if detailed inspection showed it was required. Related highways/drainage works were programmed to address issues near the junction with Brookside Close. (p22) [PAG]

### Whalley Road, Barrow
* Local roads including Whalley Road were submerged causing access problems. Highway gullies on Whalley Road were found in January 2016 to need significant cleaning as a result of the event; the cleaning work has since been completed. (p12) [TAB]

## Topics without coordinates

### Lancashire County Council
* As Lead Local Flood Authority, recorded and verified data from the December 2015 floods, established a flood timeline, and initiated a countywide Section 19 investigation covering communities affected by the December 2015 storm events. Adopted a two‑stage investigation approach (stage one: investigated the two major December 2015 storm events and identified affected communities; stage two: reporting progress on recommended actions). Published the investigation findings and appendices, improved council flood communications and is adapting the multi‑agency emergency response based on lessons learned from the December 2015 floods. (p10) [PAG]
* {general}: As Lead Local Flood Authority and Lancashire Highways, LCC programmed and carried out investigations of the surface water drainage network in the area affected by flooding. The LLFA completed an initial investigation, shared findings with the Whalley and Billington Flood Action Group, and planned an expanded investigation and further updates (first stage planned 2016/17; further update due June 2017). (p15) [TAB]
* Whalley experienced flooding associated with the River Calder on 26 December 2015; the Lead Local Flood Authority concluded the magnitude and intensity of the rainfall was likely the primary cause of the flooding at this location. (p21) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* As the Lead Local Flood Authority, the council led post-December 2015 investigations of the surface water drainage network in Whalley, carried out a high-level review of mitigation options (including works on King Street culvert), and submitted bids for FCERM grant in aid (including an initial £150,000 bid) to fund detailed assessment, appraisal and delivery of medium- to long-term mitigation. (p22)

* Boyce's Brook, Duddel Brook and the River Ribble overtopped their banks and caused internal flooding to residential properties in Ribchester. (p19) [TAB]
* {general}: The Environment Agency is programming and will deliver a modelling and appraisal study to identify options to reduce fluvial (river) flood risk at this location in response to the December 2015 flooding; the scope and delivery targets are yet to be determined. (p20) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 17 properties (p19) [TAB]
