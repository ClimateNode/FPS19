* Wetheringsett-cum-Brockford was severely impacted: intense rainfall and converging overland flow paths overwhelmed watercourses and drainage capacity, causing internal flooding of properties and disruption to infrastructure and services. (p4)
* Wetheringsett was a main affected area during the event with significant internal flooding to properties and local infrastructure; flooding sources reported for the parish included surface water (pluvial), overtopping of watercourses (fluvial) and overwhelmed drainage/sewage systems. (p8) [IMG, PAG]
* Internal flooding reported in the village of Wetheringsett (village core and surroundings); localised flow paths and overtopping/drainage failure contributed to internal property flooding. (p9) [PAG, IMG]
* Significant internal flooding to properties and local infrastructure across the parish during Storm Babet; floodwater sources included surface water runoff, overtopping watercourses and overwhelmed sewage/drainage systems. (p10)
* Central Wetheringsett is mapped as being at significant fluvial flood risk from local watercourses (which discharge to the River Dove) and at risk from pluvial flow paths; specific streets identified at risk include Station Road and Wetherup Street. The wider catchment flood alert was in force (issued 20 Oct 2023). (p13) [PAG, IMG]
* During the Oct 18–21, 2023 event (reports collected during Storm Babet) the area experienced significant surface water runoff and limited infiltration. Mapping and the investigation identify central Wetheringsett as a low-lying convergence point for flow paths and at risk of flooding from both pluvial (surface water) and fluvial sources; this contributed to flooding impacts in central village locations and along the local watercourse before it discharges to the River Dove. The report notes water is likely to enter properties via airbricks or gaps under floors where flooding occurs. (p14) [PAG, IMG]

* The village experienced overtopping of the local watercourse and extensive surface water runoff from a large rural catchment. The watercourse was quickly overwhelmed (exacerbated by constrictions — a weir, two small brick-arch bridges, a culvert under the old mill, and a sharp engineered turn), causing water to force around/over these restrictions and enter the highway and surrounding properties. Torrents of water travelled down steep southern and western access roads, inundating the central low point in the village. A culvert at the entrance to the primary school overtopped and contributed flows onto the highway. Sections of the watercourse leaving the village were reported to require maintenance, which may have reduced channel capacity during the event. (p23)
* The village centre (junction serving routes from south, west and north) experienced converging torrents of fast-moving surface water from steep access roads and surrounding fields; an eastern watercourse overtopped at a culvert leading to highway inundation and flows several metres up the road. Flood waters pooled in the low spot in the centre of the village, and properties on Hockey Hill, Church Street and Cedars Hill had flood water entering from the highway. A blocked/collapsed drainage pipe on Church Street caused rapid highway backup and overtopping into houses. (p24) [IMG]
* Surface water from a large surrounding catchment overwhelmed the village watercourse and highway drainage; the foul sewer system and village pumping station were overwhelmed (the pumping station was submerged during the event), causing sewer overflows that discharged raw sewage into gardens and highways; internal property flooding occurred across the village. (p25)
* East Wetheringsett experienced flooding during the event, including inundation of Church Street by the parish church; community movement was disrupted and small boats were used to traverse flooded streets. (p29) [IMG, PAG]
* Residential gardens experienced sewage escape following Storm Babet; drainage issues within the village were identified (including repairs to drainage features on Church Street) and increased watercourse maintenance/inspection was recommended across the parish. (p34) [TAB, PAG]
* Drainage and watercourse capacity issues in Wetheringsett prompted investigations into clearance, raising-capacity works and natural flood management measures to reduce flood risk following the event. (p35)

### Church Street, Wetheringsett
* Houses on Church Street (north) had flood water entering from the highway caused by a combination of surface water runoff and overtopping from the nearby watercourse. A severe blockage or collapse of a connecting drainage pipe on the west side of the carriageway prevented highway surface water reaching the watercourse, causing rapid backup, pooling on the western side, overtopping of the kerb and flow into adjacent houses. The low spot in the centre of the village also caused attenuation and pooling of flood waters. (p24)
* Area close to the church on Church Street was inundated by floodwater during the event, flooding the carriageway and affecting access along the street. (p30)
* The junction was inundated with muddy floodwater that partially submerged a vehicle and street signage, preventing normal vehicular movement and threatening nearby properties. (p32) [IMG]
* Surface water from a nearby watercourse affected Church Street; diversion measures were installed in Manor Drive to route surface water away from Church Street and the watercourse alongside Church Street was cleared by the riparian owner. Sewage escapes into gardens in the local area since Storm Babet were reported to Anglian Water and Mid Suffolk District Council for investigation. (p33) [TAB, PAG]
* Drainage features on Church Street required repair due to flooding/drainage problems; SCC Highways scheduled repairs within the planned drainage programme. (p34) [TAB, PAG]
* Blockages of drainage features on Church Street requiring riparian owners or the relevant highways authority to carry out repairs; drainage failures contributed to localised flooding and triggered maintenance works. (p35)
* Quant: 1 vehicle partially submerged (p32) [IMG]

### Suffolk
* Significant disruption to communities across the county during the 18–21 October 2023 event as intense rainfall and overwhelmed drainage/watercourse capacity produced widespread fluvial and pluvial flooding. (p4) [PAG]
* During Storm Babet (18–21 October 2023) Suffolk experienced the heaviest rainfall across East Anglia, leading to saturated land, rapid rises in river systems and significant flooding of roads and properties across the county; the situation led to a major incident being declared on 20 October 2023. (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* Central Wetheringsett and Brockford Street experienced fluvial (river) and pluvial (surface water) flooding during the 18–21 October 2023 event. Low-lying ground and converging flow paths caused inundation through Brockford Street and central Wetheringsett; floodwaters were reported entering properties via airbricks and gaps beneath floors. Station Road and Wetherup Street were identified as surface-water flow paths contributing to local flooding. ### East Anglia
* During Storm Babet (18–21 October 2023) heavy rainfall caused rivers to rise rapidly across whole catchments and resulted in significant flooding of roads and properties across central and northern East Anglia, with widespread disruption to road and rail networks. October 2023 was recorded as the joint wettest on record in the east of England. (p6)

### Wetheringsett
* Flooding affected the central village area (village core), contributing to internal property flooding and localised impacts to streets and infrastructure within central Wetheringsett. (p9) [IMG]
* Surface water runoff from surrounding fields combined with an overtopping eastern watercourse to produce fast-moving torrents down steep approaches (Hockey Hill and Cedars Hill) into the village centre. Flood water reached several metres up the road from the watercourse in both directions and entered properties on Hockey Hill (south/east), Church Street (north) and Cedars Hill (west). A collapsed/severely blocked highways drainage pipe on the west side of Church Street caused rapid backing up and kerb-overtopping into adjacent houses. The culvert at the primary school on Hockey Hill overtopped into the highway. Large vehicle movements through floodwater exacerbated the swell and impacts. (p24) [IMG]
* Natural ground, vegetation and open areas within East Wetheringsett were submerged by muddy floodwater; flooding was reported close to the village church. (p28)
* Quant: over 20 properties (parish-wide) (p9) [IMG]

### River Dove
* {general}: Designated main river running through Brockford Street area; identified on mapping as the primary watercourse giving rise to fluvial flood risk to central Wetheringsett and Brockford Street (highest risk concentrated along the river channel). (p11)
* The river Dove overtopped and breached its banks at several points on both the east and west sides of the A140, causing significant flooding of surrounding properties that border the river, inundation of main transport routes (the A140 was closed for several hours) and preventing emergency access for several hours due to impassable roads and traffic congestion. (p17) [IMG]
* Described as overtopping and contributing floodwater that passed under/over the A140 bridge location and flowed into adjacent roads and properties; community reports also suggested debris in the river may have impeded flow and exacerbated overtopping. (p18)
* At the A140 crossing immediately south of the Griffin Lane junction the River Dove's flow exceeded the capacity of the single-span masonry arch bridge. During the event water flowed out of the channel upstream and alongside the bridge abutments and inundated the A140 / Brockford Street and surrounding properties on the west side of the A140. Local reports suggested bank works and rocks from nearby gabion baskets reduced capacity, though highway inspections noted gabions and bed material used for scour protection and did not record a blockage in the most recent inspection. (p19) [IMG, PAG]
* Grazing land adjacent to the River Dove (between Buces Hill and the caravan park at Brockford Road) flooded during Storm Babet; the floodplain continued to function. Inspections noted gabion rocks and material laid in the river bed for scour protection near a bridge; these rocks were considered possibly the material observed by residents but unlikely to have significantly reduced flow capacity. (p20)
* Quant: approximately 13 properties experienced internal flooding in the Brockford Street area (area impacted by overtopping including breaches of the River Dove) (p17) [IMG]
