

# Dudley flooding 08 Jun 2016 - 16 Jun 2016

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report for June 2016<br>038\_Dudley/038\_2016\_Dudley/dudley-metropolitan-borough-council-flood-investigation-report-for-june-2016-public-version-mar-15.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Dudley
* Widespread flooding occurred across parts of Dudley borough due to extreme rainfall, causing surface water to inundate streets and residential areas and generating large numbers of support requests to the Council; emergency response measures (road closures/vehicle blockades) were used to protect public safety. (p1) [IMG]
* Dudley experienced exceptionally intense rainfall (notably on 16 June 2016) that overloaded the local drainage network and caused significant flooding across the town on 8 and 16 June 2016; impacts described as of a magnitude unseen in recent times. (p4)
* Dudley Council activated its Pre Flood Action Plan in response to ongoing poor weather and an AMBER forecast for 14 June indicating a medium risk of significant localised surface water flooding; the regional 16 June rainfall map also shows Dudley received moderate to high 24-hour rainfall accumulations, consistent with conditions that produced localised surface water/urban flooding during the June events. (p11) [IMG, PAG]
* Areas across the borough experienced surface water flooding during the incident (scattered but far reaching). Flooding incidents included occurrences at individual properties and clusters of properties; investigations found a concentration of incidents in the Brierley Hill area. (p13)
* Unprecedented intense rainfall on 16 June 2016 caused widespread flooding across the borough. Local drainage and sewer networks were overwhelmed, producing widespread surface-water/urban flooding, some river/watercourse incidents and internal property flooding. (p16) [PAG]
* Quant: more than 300 requests for support; in the order of 75 internal flooding incidents of residential properties (p1) [IMG]
* Quant: more than 300 requests for support; 75 internal residential properties flooded (p16) [PAG]

### Brierley Hill
* A concentration of flood incidents occurred here during the event, affecting individual properties and clusters of properties. Incidents generated requests for sandbags, gully cleaning and support to prevent flood water entering properties. (p13)

### Lye Station
* Temporary closure of the railway station due to flooding. (p14)
* Quant: 1 railway station closed (p14)


## Topics without coordinates

### Dudley Council
* The Council led the local response and recovery to extreme rainfall and widespread surface water flooding across the borough; received and coordinated support for hundreds of reports of flooding and worked with partner organisations (Environment Agency and Severn Trent Water) to assist the worst affected communities. (p1)
* Received daily flood alerts in the period around the event; carried out a Section 19 flood investigation, emergency response activities, pre-flood action planning, liaison and urgent highway drainage works, post‑flood works and enforcement of riparian responsibilities in response to flooding. (p8) [TAB, PAG]
* Dudley Council received daily flood guidance alerts between 6 June and 16 June 2016 (mostly Yellow, a few Green). The 16 June Yellow alert indicated a medium likelihood of minor surface-water flooding in parts of England; the Council therefore faced the prospect of localised travel disruption and isolated property flooding within its area and was operating on heightened surface-water flood awareness. (p9)
* Activated the Council's Pre Flood Action Plan (senior officer used discretionary powers) in response to ongoing poor weather and the AMBER forecast to manage medium‑risk localised surface water flooding; coordinated actions leading up to, during and after the June 2016 events. (p11)
* Responded to storm-related surface water/urban flooding: received over 300 calls (24-hr period on 16 Jun 2016) including 29 requests for sand bags and 80 reports of blocked highway gullies; services were stretched and some callers could not register requests at times; increased staffing, assigned store person to control sand bag distribution; closed roads and carried out clearance and support tasks; delivered sandbags and cleansed gullies. (p12)
* Served as the primary initial contact point for flood reports during the event; collected the majority of initial flooding incident enquiries and transferred them to Environmental Management's highway asset database. Enquiries included requests for sandbags, gully cleaning and support to prevent internal property flooding. (p13)
* Coordinated response, recorded and mapped all known internal property flood incidents, investigated almost all internal property incidents, carried out highway gully cleaning/blockage attendance, and provided assistance following widespread surface-water and sewer-overload flooding. (p14)
* Widespread flooding across the borough including internal property flooding, flooding of garages/gardens/highways, road and rail closures and requests for sandbags; the council handled incident reports and post-storm investigations and supported evacuations and school/transport responses. (p15)
* Dudley Council Plus (the Council's contact/response service) was overwhelmed during part of the incident, with some callers unable to register requests for assistance. (p16)
* The Dudley Council Plus reporting process was changed in response to the flood event: operator scripts were revised so call handlers ask specific questions about flood incidents (in response to many calls reporting blocked gullies that were actually discharging surface water due to sewer capacity issues), improving capture and triage of surface water/sewer-related reports. (p17)
* Quant: more than 300 requests for support; in the order of 75 internal flooding incidents of residential properties (p1)
* Quant: over 300 calls; 29 requests for sand bags; 80 reports of blocked highway gullies; closed 14 roads; delivered 700 sand bags to 29 locations; checked and cleansed 61 highway gullies (p12)
* Quant: 100 residential properties with internal flooding; 15 council-owned residential properties with internal flooding; 29 requests for sand bags; 14 roads closed; 12 schools reporting flooding; 75 reports of garage, garden and highway flooding; 1 care home evacuated; 1 railway station closed (p14)
* Quant: 100 houses; 15 council-owned residential properties; 1 care home (evacuated); Lye Railway Station (closed); 75 reports of garage, garden and highway flooding; 12 schools; 14 roads closed; 29 sand bag requests (p15)
* Quant: more than 300 requests for support; 75 internal residential properties flooded (p16) [PAG]

### Severn Trent
* Partnered with Dudley Council and the Environment Agency to help those worst affected by the June 2016 flooding; involved in the response and recovery effort following widespread surface water flooding (and where relevant, sewer-related impacts). (p1)
* Provided emergency response and maintenance of the public sewer network and carried out post‑flood surveys and investigations and information/advice following flooding. (p8) [TAB]
* Received and responded to enquiries about blocked foul and storm sewers and internal flooding events in Dudley and supplied incident data to Dudley Council. (p15) [MUL]
* Severn Trent Water assets were implicated in enquiries about blocked foul and storm sewers and internal flooding; the sewerage network capacity was exceeded in places contributing to internal and surface-water flooding incidents. (p16) [PAG]
* Quant: 36 enquiries related to Severn Trent Water assets; 18 enquiries not related to Severn Trent Water assets (p15) [MUL]
* Quant: 36 enquiries related to Severn Trent Water assets; 18 enquiries not related to Severn Trent Water assets (p16) [PAG]

### Black Country
* Widespread flooding across parts of the Black Country during June 2016. Intense rainfall on 8 and 16 June 2016 overloaded local drainage networks causing significant urban/surface-water flooding of a magnitude not seen in recent times. (p4)
* Flooding was experienced in many parts of the Black Country during the June 2016 event, driven primarily by intense surface-water runoff. (p16)

### Environment Agency
* Worked in partnership with Dudley Council and Severn Trent Water to assist communities worst affected by the surface water flooding and to support recovery activities following the extreme rainfall event. (p1)
* Provided information and advice and carried out maintenance of rivers and related assets; enforced landowner riparian responsibilities for main rivers; undertook/commissioned flood defence works and requested Section 19 report information to inform national reporting in relation to the June 2016 flooding. (p8) [TAB]

### Care Home
* Evacuation of the care home due to flooding. (p14)
* Quant: 1 care home evacuated (p14)

### Highways Authority
* As Highways Authority (Dudley Council) carried out highway drainage maintenance and emergency response measures: Section 19 flood investigation, Pre-Flood Action Plan actions, liaison on highway drainage, urgent and short-term works, post-flood works and enforcement of riparian responsibilities for ordinary watercourses in response to the June 2016 floods. (p8) [TAB]
