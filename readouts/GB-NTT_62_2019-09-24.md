

# Kimberley flooding 24 Sep 2019

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Kimberley – September 2019<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/section19reportkimberleyseptember2019.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Kimberley
* Following a prolonged period of heavy rainfall on 24 September 2019 (around 10:00am) parts of Kimberley were subject to significant floodwater. Flooding affected properties along Main Street and areas adjacent to the low point on Eastwood Road. Seven businesses suffered internal flooding and additional businesses struggled to keep floodwater out of their premises. (p1) [IMG, PAG]
* Parts of Kimberley were subject to significant floodwater following an intense 3-hour downpour; 7 businesses were internally flooded and additional businesses struggled to keep floodwater out. Flooding was concentrated along the Main Street and adjacent to the low point on Eastwood Road. (p2)
* An intense three‑hour downpour over the built-up urban area produced rapid overland flow across impermeable surfaces, with flows running down side streets into the valley floor and converging at the town centre (notably the intersection of High Street and Main Street). Consequences across the town included drainage capacity exceedance (e.g. Sainsbury's car park and rear building flooding), and manholes lifting in rear gardens and on side roads as smaller drains and combined sewers struggled to cope. (p3) [IMG]
* Widespread surface water from heavy rainfall overwhelmed local drainage: the rear car park of Sainsbury's reached capacity and flooded into the rear of the building; manholes were observed lifting in rear gardens and on side roads as smaller connecting drains struggled; rapid surface runoff along Main Street bypassed gullies and ran onto footpaths via low/drop kerbs, flooding low-threshold business entrances; ponding at a low point on Eastwood Road left vehicles stranded and caused flooding of the adjacent Broxtowe Borough Council Depot; the low point junction of Church Hill was also inundated with vehicles partially submerged. (p4) [IMG]
* Businesses in Kimberley were affected by the flood event; local economic support/advice may be offered to affected businesses by the county council's Growth and Economic Development Team and The Growth Hub. (p6)
* Quant: 7 businesses (p1) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 7 businesses (p2)

### Main Street, Kimberley
* Main Street acted as a conduit for floodwaters with flow from northwest to southeast; three zones along and near Main Street experienced internal flooding and businesses along this street were among those internally flooded. (p2) [IMG]
* Located at the bottom of the valley, Main Street acted as a channel conveying rapid overland flow from side streets and valley sides toward the low point on Eastwood Road; experienced concentrated surface water flow and associated accumulation along the street. (p3) [IMG]
* Main Street acted as a channel for rapid surface water flow; shallow/low kerbs and drop kerbs allowed water to run onto footpaths and into business entrances with low thresholds. (p4) [IMG]
* Area affected by flooding attributed to a complex interaction between the highway drainage system and the public sewer system; being investigated by Nottinghamshire County Council with Severn Trent Water to identify actions to lower future risk. (p6)
* Quant: 7 businesses (p2) [IMG]

### Eastwood Road, Kimberley
* Businesses adjacent to the low point on Eastwood Road were affected by floodwater (some of the businesses internally flooded were situated adjacent to this low point). (p2)
* Identified as the low point receiving water routed down Main Street; therefore experienced accumulation/pooling of surface water as flows were eased toward this location. (p3)
* Surface water collected and ponded at a low point on Eastwood Road; ponding was deep enough to leave vehicles stranded. (p4)
* Flooding on/around the highway led to diversion of traffic around Eastwood Road by the Highways response team. (p5) [PAG]
* Area affected by flooding attributed to a complex interaction between the highway drainage system and the public sewer system; being investigated by Nottinghamshire County Council with Severn Trent Water to identify actions to lower future risk. (p6)

### Newdigate Street, Kimberley
* {general}: Surface water flowed along Newdigate Street and it is shown as part of the mapped surface-water flow and accumulation network converging on the central area. (p3) [IMG]
* {2013}: Following a 2013 flooding event, the public surface water and combined sewer system was surveyed and a section of the combined sewer was relined between Newdigate Street and Nine Corners. (p6)

### High Street, Kimberley
* {general}: Surface water flowed along High Street as part of the main runoff routes toward the central intersection/Main Street; the street was within the surface-water flow and accumulation zone shown on the map. (p3) [IMG]

### Brewery Street, Kimberley
* {general}: Surface water flowed along Brewery Street as part of the urban runoff network; the street is identified on the map within the surface-water flow/accumulation area. (p3) [IMG]

### Church Hill, Kimberley
* Low-point junction of Church Hill was heavily flooded with water deep enough to partially submerge vehicles and impede traffic. (p4) [IMG]

### Sainsbury's
* Surface water drainage for the car park at the rear of Sainsbury's reached capacity due to the volume of runoff (including flow from uphill areas), which led to flooding into the rear of the building. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Drainage for the car park at the rear reached capacity due to surface water and flow from higher ground, which led to flooding into the rear of the building. (p4)


## Topics without coordinates

### Severn Trent
* {2013, 24 Sep 2019}: Provided site-based presence and investigations following the flood event; instigated on-site CCTV work to survey sewer condition; actively engaged in the Section 19 Flood Investigation to examine the interaction between the public sewer system and highway drainage. Following a similar (less severe) 2013 event, commissioned survey work that led to relining a section of combined sewer between Newdigate Street and Nine Corners. (p5) [MUL]
* {2013}: Following a similar (less severe) flooding event in 2013, commissioned a survey of the public surface water and combined sewer system which led to a section of the combined sewer being relined between Newdigate Street and Nine Corners. (p6)

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Responded to a flood event affecting the Eastwood Road/Main Street area: provided Highways response to divert traffic around Eastwood Road; initiated and coordinated emergency planning procedures; led the Section 19 flood investigation and worked with Severn Trent Water to survey the surface water sewer network and to investigate interaction between highway drainage and the public sewer system. (p5) [PAG]
* Provided emergency response support in management of the flooding event and actively engaged in the Section 19 flood investigation; acted as the Lead Local Flood Authority and committed to continuing investigations and measures to reduce future flood risk and protect vulnerable community members. (p6)

### Council Depot
* Adjacent depot was flooded by the ponding/overflow of surface water at the low point next to Eastwood Road. (p4)

### Broxtowe Borough Council
* Provided emergency response support in management of the flooding event and actively engaged in the Section 19 Flood Investigation. (p5)

### Highway Authority
* Responsible for maintenance of public highways and highway drainage assets; provided site-based presence and investigations immediately following the event and supported diversion of traffic around Eastwood Road. (p5)

### Via East Midlands
* As part of the Highway Authority arrangement, involved in maintenance of highways/highway drainage and providing site-based presence and investigations following the flood event affecting the Eastwood Road area. (p5)
