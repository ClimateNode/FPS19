

# Stapleford flooding 18 Oct 2023 - 20 Oct 2023

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report –Stapleford – Storm Babet, October 2023<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/stapleford-s19-storn-babet-oct-2023.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Stapleford
* Stapleford suffered a flood event caused by sustained heavy rainfall; saturated ground led to flooding of roads and properties. (p1)
* Internal property flooding across the town following intense rainfall: the River Erewash overtopped its banks downstream, flooding the road network and causing water to enter properties from both the front and the back. (p2)
* Residential neighbourhood flooding from both a surcharged ordinary watercourse (culvert under Mill Road) and overtopping of the River Erewash downstream; the combined flooding affected the highway and caused internal flooding of properties across the area. (p3) [IMG]
* Quant: 130 properties (p2)
* Quant: 110 properties; 5 properties (on Mill Road) (p3) [IMG]

### Wellington Street, Stapleford
* Internal property flooding. 52 properties suffered internal flooding after the River Erewash overtopped downstream, flooding the road network which then followed local topography and the highway to flood properties from both the front and the back. (p2)
* Floodwater from the River Erewash overtopping downstream reached Wellington Street via the road network and local topography; properties were flooded from both the front and back, resulting in internal flooding. (p4) [IMG, PAG]
* Street experienced flooding driven by overland flow from the River Erewash combined with surface water. Local businesses reported many blocked gullies which slowed drainage and meant gullies could not discharge effectively while river levels were high. (p5) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 52 properties (p2)
* Quant: 110 properties (total across affected area including listed streets) (p4) [IMG, PAG]

### Nottinghamshire
* Led and delivered the emergency response to flooding across the county: visited flood-affected residents and businesses to establish sources and mechanisms of flooding; provided 24-hour Emergency Planning support; initiated and led the Section 19 Flood Investigation; delivered sandbags to affected areas and communities; closed roads to promote safety and avoid further flood damage; supported District and Borough partners and communicated key messages to the public and elected members. (p8)
* Quant: 77 roads (p8)

### Broxtowe
* Communities in Broxtowe were affected by the flooding and have required recovery actions, distribution of sandbags and administration of hardship and relief grants. (p8)

### Mill Road, Stapleford
* An ordinary watercourse running behind properties surcharged at the culvert under Mill Road; the surcharged water followed the highway and caused internal flooding of properties. Residents also reported the River Erewash overtopping its banks downstream, which flooded the road network and allowed water to follow topography and highways to flood properties. (p2)
* An ordinary (culverted) watercourse behind properties surcharged at the point it is culverted under Mill Road during heavy rain; the surcharged water then followed the highway, flooding properties. In residents' accounts the River Erewash also overtopped downstream, flooded the road network and subsequently flooded properties from front and back. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 5 properties; 110 properties (p2)
* Quant: 5 properties; 110 properties (p3) [IMG, PAG]

### River Erewash
* The River Erewash overtopped its banks downstream; the overtopping flooded the road network and contributed to properties being flooded from both the front and the back, leading to widespread internal flooding in the residential area. (p3)
* High river levels caused overland flows into the surrounding area and prevented local gullies from discharging effectively, and therefore was a main contributing source of flooding to nearby streets. (p6)
* Quant: 110 properties (p3)


## Topics without coordinates

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Responded to a Major Incident with significant county-wide flood impacts: initiated a Community Flood Recovery Grant for individuals affected by internal flooding; attended sites to verify scale of internal (property) flooding and offered support, guidance and advice to affected residents and businesses; collated and published verified flood impact data; visited affected residents/businesses to establish flood sources/mechanisms and severity; provided 24-hour support via the Emergency Planning team; carried out actions requested by strategic/tactical/recovery coordination. (p7)
* Nottinghamshire County Council (as Lead Local Flood Authority) coordinated response to flooding across the county: provided 24-hour emergency planning support, visited flood-affected residents and businesses, delivered sandbags, closed roads to protect at-risk areas, initiated and led the Section 19 Flood Investigation, and led procurement/delivery of DEFRA Property Flood Resilience repair grant activity. (p8)
* Quant: 77 roads (p8)

### Broxtowe Borough Council
* Provided emergency response support for the flooding event in Broxtowe: delivered and distributed sandbags to affected residents, managed and continue to manage recovery in affected Broxtowe communities, and administered a flood hardship fund plus council tax and business rate relief grant scheme. (p8)
