

# Wooburn Green flooding 06 Feb 2014 - 12 Feb 2014

Reference document:<br>Wooburn Green February 2014<br>018\_Buckinghamshire County/018\_2014\_Buckinghamshire County/section-19-final-report-wooburn-green-31118.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Windsor Hill, Wooburn Green
* Flooding forced full closure of the road; closure disrupted access and transport through the area. (p4)
* Windsor Hill road was closed due to flooding, causing loss of highway access over the period of the event. (p5)
* Floodwater from the River Wye flowed across fields toward the houses on Windsor Hill, and additional rapid overland flow ran down the steep Windsor Hill, increasing flood risk to properties on the hill. (p14)
* Floodwater from the River Wye flowed across the field and down Windsor Hill. The field filled with water up to waist depth; horses had to be removed. Residents moved cars to safety and pumped water out of front gardens. Water approached houses from the road and the rear; properties had water up to their floorboards and gardens and drives were flooded. The Queen and Albert public house had its cellar flooded. (p15) [PAG]
* Floodwater from the River Wye broke its banks and flowed down into the field towards Windsor Hill. The field was inundated to about waist depth, horses were removed for safety, residents moved cars to safety, water was pumped from front gardens, and properties experienced flooding to floorboards, gardens and drives. The road was closed (reports show closures from 07 Feb, remaining closed through 11 Feb, reopened 12 Feb). (p16) [PAG, TAB]
* Overtopping of the River Wye's right bank caused floodwater to flow into the adjacent field, across the road and up to the house on the north side of Windsor Hill; the road experienced deep water and was closed by TfB/BCC to prevent vehicles pushing water towards houses; residents undertook protective measures and sought assistance from the EA. (p17)
* Windsor Hill road and adjacent properties were flooded: the road was closed between 6 and 12 February 2014; water surrounded properties on the north side of Windsor Hill, residents pumped to try to prevent ingress, and water rose up to the floorboards in a number of properties. (p21)
* Quant: 2 houses (p4)

### Wooburn Green
* Flooding at Windsor Hill within Wooburn Green caused the Windsor Hill road to be closed for several days; residential properties experienced internal flooding to floorboard level and the public house cellar flooded. (p4)
* Flooding in the Wooburn Green area caused the closure of Windsor Hill (a Class A/B highway) and prompted a formal section 19 investigation by the county authority. (p5) [PAG]
* Flooding from the River Wye affected the Wooburn Green area. The River Wye, which runs through Wooburn Green, splits under Windsor Hill; one channel flows toward the A4094 and the other flows around the eastern boundary then along the southern boundary of the Wooburn Manor Park residential area before rejoining. These channel routes were implicated in the local inundation of the Wooburn Green locality during the event. (p6) [PAG]
* The region of Wooburn Green was flooded, with inundation affecting areas around Wooburn Manor Park and other low-lying areas adjacent to the River Wye's channels. (p7) [PAG]
* Floodwater from the River Wye breached its banks and flowed across the lower-lying fields between the river and the property Salan, inundating those fields and flowing onto Windsor Hill road toward houses on Windsor Hill. Runoff down the steep Windsor Hill road also contributed to flood routing into the settlement. Photographs and resident records document historic inundation along Windsor Hill and the intervening fields. (p13)
* Area of Wooburn Green (particularly Windsor Hill) was affected when the River Wye breached its banks and flooded adjoining fields and properties — causing inundation to gardens, drives and floorboards, horse evacuation, vehicle moves and road closures. (p16) [PAG, TAB]
* The fluvial event (caused by prolonged rainfall and worsened by a tree blocking the River Wye channel upstream of Windsor Hill Bridge) led to river overtopping that produced deep water on roads and flow into fields and up to houses in the Wooburn Green area; residents called the Environment Agency, took protective measures for their properties and requested assistance; highways authorities closed the road to reduce the risk of vehicles pushing water into houses. (p17)
* Quant: road closed for 5 to 6 days; 2 houses with water up to floorboards; 1 public house cellar flooded (p4)

### River Wye
* The River Wye's channel through the Wooburn Green area split as it passed under Windsor Hill, with flows directed west toward the A4094 and SW around the eastern boundary. During the event these river channels contributed to flooding in the Wooburn Green area (including the area around Wooburn Manor Park). (p7)
* The River Wye burst its banks and flowed across the right bank, across fields and toward the houses on Windsor Hill, delivering floodwater into the built-up area. A fallen tree was mapped on the east bank upstream of the main bridge and may have obstructed flow, exacerbating localized flooding. (p14)
* The River Wye burst its banks when high winter rainfall combined with a channel blockage (a fallen tree) prevented flow. The river broke its banks on the right bank and flowed across the adjacent field towards Windsor Hill, filling the field and causing downstream flooding of properties. (p15) [PAG]
* River Wye levels were very high and, combined with a tree blocking the channel upstream of Windsor Hill bridge, the river breached its right bank upstream of the blockage. Floodwater flowed into the adjacent field and towards Windsor Hill, inundating properties and infrastructure. (p16) [PAG, TAB]
* A tree in the channel upstream of Windsor Hill bridge blocked the free flow of water during high flows, causing the river to overtop the right bank and flow into the adjacent field, across the road and up to a house on the north side of Windsor Hill. (p17)

### Buckinghamshire
* Buckinghamshire County Council (BCC) implemented the closure of Windsor Hill (with TfB) between 06 Feb 2014 and 12 Feb 2014 as part of the immediate response; the flooding was caused by River Wye overtopping its right bank after a tree blocked the channel, leading to water on the road and surrounding properties on the north side of Windsor Hill. (p21) [TAB]

### Wooburn
* Wooburn Green (part of Wooburn) was flooded; the flood triggered a section 19 investigation and was associated with local transport disruption (Windsor Hill road closure). (p5) [PAG]
* The two River Wye channels rejoin near the SW corner of Wooburn Manor Park and then follow a SW route along the A4094 through Wooburn, causing river flooding along that route through Wooburn. (p7) [PAG]

### Wooburn Manor Park, Wooburn Green
* River Wye channels split after flowing under Windsor Hill and flowed around then along the southern boundary of the Wooburn Manor Park residential area, resulting in flooding of the residential area and adjacent land. (p7)

### Thames Valley
* During the period of flooding the Environment Agency was engaged responding to flood incidents elsewhere across the Thames Valley, which prevented an immediate EA response to the incident reported in Wooburn Green. (p17)
* {general}: Heavy, persistent rain across the Thames Valley in Dec 2013 and Jan/Feb 2014 saturated the land so increased runoff entered the river network, contributing to the high flows that caused local flooding in the area. (p21)

### Windsor Hill Bridge
* A tree lodged in the channel of the River Wye upstream of Windsor Hill Bridge obstructed flow during high flows; this blockage contributed to the river overtopping its right bank and sending water into the adjacent field, road and up to the house on the north side of Windsor Hill. (p17)
* A tree in the River Wye channel upstream of Windsor Hill Bridge blocked flow; during high flows water overtopped the right bank and flowed into the adjacent field, onto the road and up to a house on the north side of Windsor Hill. Removal of the tree allowed water levels to recede. (p21)

### Queen and Albert (former)
* Cellar of the Queen and Albert public house was flooded during the event; nearby properties experienced flooding to floorboards, gardens and drives. (p16) [PAG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Transport for Buckinghamshire
* TfB (with BCC) implemented road closures at Windsor Hill during the flood (diary records closures and later reopening) as part of the immediate response to the flooding and inundated road conditions. (p16) [PAG, TAB]
* Windsor Hill road was submerged by flood water and was closed by Transport for Buckinghamshire (TfB) and BCC between 06 Feb 2014 and 12 Feb 2014 to prevent vehicles driving through deep flood water and to avoid wash from vehicles into properties at risk. (p21) [TAB]

### Buckinghamshire County Council
* Responded to the incident by closing the road to prevent vehicles passing through deep water and pushing water into houses. (p17)
* Closed Windsor Hill road between 6 and 12 February 2014 to prevent vehicles driving through deep flood water and to prevent wash into properties; residents on the north side of Windsor Hill pumped to prevent flood water entering properties and properties were surrounded by water, with water reaching floorboards in a number of properties. The immediate cause was overtopping of the right bank of the River Wye where a tree in the channel upstream of Windsor Hill bridge impeded flow. (p21) [TAB]
