

# Linton flooding 20 Jul 2021

## Place names with coordinates

### Linton
* {2014, 2017, 2019, 20 Jul 2021}: Linton has a floodplain associated with the River Granta and large areas are at significant risk of surface water flooding. The 20 July 2021 flooding occurred largely in areas already identified as at risk; some properties around the village centre are located within a flood zone. The village has also experienced previous flooding events in 2014, 2017 and 2019. (p5) [MUL]
* On 20 July 2021 the village experienced flooding largely from surface water; inundation occurred in parts of the village already identified as at risk from surface water, including areas around the village centre and properties located within mapped flood zones. (p6) [PAG, IMG]
* Significant surface-water flooding occurred across parts of the village on 20 July 2021 after an intense rainfall event; flooding affected large areas already identified as at risk from surface water, with inundation extending into built-up parts of Linton. (p7) [PAG, IMG]
* Significant flooding occurred on 20 July 2021 from intense, convective rainfall. The event produced localised, intense surface water/flash flooding that resulted in internal and external flooding of properties; local flood reports were submitted to the Lead Local Flood Authority (LLFA) and Linton Parish Council documenting affected properties. (p8) [PAG]
* On 20 July 2021 the LLFA received 23 flood reports from Linton. Properties experienced rapid ('flash') water entry with water levels receding relatively quickly. Overall 17 properties were reported to have flooded internally and a further 6 properties experienced external flooding of gardens, outbuildings and roads. At least 5 properties were likely to be uninhabitable for 1–12 months. Three localised risk areas were identified (High Street; Balsham Road/Lamb Fair Court; Lonsdale/Bakers Lane/Bartlow Road/Finchams Close). (p9)
* Village-wide reports of property flooding: internal and external flooding of residential properties and gardens/outbuildings; multiple rapid-onset (flash) surface-water flood incidents with water entering properties and receding relatively quickly. At least some properties rendered uninhabitable. (p10) [PAG, IMG]
* Surface water flooding affected the town, with reports that residents had to leave their properties for periods of between 1 and 8 months. The LLFA and the highway authority attended to investigate reports, inspected highway drainage, and raised works orders to clean gullies. Further work was initiated with Highways and landowners to reduce surface water runoff from fields and highway and to investigate introducing additional attenuation capacity. (p13)
* On 20 July 2021 an intense convective storm produced surface water flooding across Linton. Rainfall exceeded drainage capacity so surface water flowed along roads and across fields toward the village low points; drainage networks were overwhelmed. A partially constructed development (stripped of vegetation and without a completed surface water network) increased runoff and contributed to the flooding. (p20)
* Quant: 23 flood reports (p8) [PAG]
* Quant: 23 flood reports; 17 properties flooded internally; 6 properties with external flooding; at least 5 properties uninhabitable (p9)
* Quant: 23 flood reports; 17 properties flooded internally; 6 properties with external flooding; at least 5 properties likely uninhabitable for 1-12 months (p10) [PAG, IMG]

### Lonsdale, Linton
* {July 2017}: Properties on Balsham Road and on Lonsdale flooded during the July 2017 flooding event. (p5)
* Reported internal property flooding as part of the south‑east residential cluster (grouped with Bakers Lane, Bartlow Road and Finchams Close); flooding was rapid ('flash') with properties affected internally. (p10) [PAG, IMG]
* Surface water flowing west along Horseheath Road entered Lonsdale; most was captured by the highway drainage system, but there was an initial report of minor flooding at the bottom of Lonsdale where water entered a garage. (p11) [PAG]
* A retaining wall situated between Lonsdale and Bakers Lane initially held back an inrush of water but then overtopped/failed, releasing a large volume of surface water that flowed towards and into properties in the adjoining streets and exacerbated flooding. (p12)
* Surface water flowing across a construction site at Lonsdale prompted meetings and immediate mitigation by the developer (tankers on standby to reduce water in a partly constructed basin, rearranging stored materials to avoid mobilising debris into the basin) to reduce the risk of further flooding. (p14)
* On 20 July 2021 surface water ran along Lonsdale into low points causing surface water flooding. The report also records misconnections/ingress of rainwater into the foul network or sewer at this location, exacerbating sewer-related flooding risks; riparian maintenance issues were noted as a contributing factor. (p20) [TAB]
* Quant: one garage (p11) [PAG]
* Quant: 15-45cm; 15-20cm (p12)

### High Street, Linton
* {July 2017}: Properties on High Street flooded during the July 2017 flooding event. (p5)
* Identified as one of three key risk areas where internal property flooding was reported on 20 July 2021; properties experienced rapid ingress of water with levels receding relatively quickly. (p9) [PAG]
* Surface water flooding occurred at a topographic low where high surface water flows were directed toward properties; limited highway drainage and low kerbs contributed to inundation. Internal property flooding was reported in this area and water entered properties rapidly before receding. (p10) [PAG, IMG]
* Surface water pooled in a topographic low and was directed toward properties. Properties were flooded internally and gardens and outbuildings were affected. The area has low kerbs and limited highway drainage which contributed to the flooding. No reports of residents leaving their premises. (p11) [TAB]
* Reports of flooding prompted on-site attendance and investigations by the LLFA and Highway Authority; highway drainage investigations were funded/confirmed and gully maintenance frequency was increased. South Cambridgeshire District Council provided sandbags to properties in the area. (p14) [TAB]
* During the intense convective storm on 20 July 2021 surface water followed local topography and flowed along High Street. Rainfall rates exceeded drainage capacity, causing surface water flooding of the road and low-lying points. (p20) [TAB]

### Balsham Road, Linton
* {July 2017}: Properties on Balsham Road and on Lonsdale flooded during the July 2017 flooding event. (p5)
* Part of the Balsham Road/Lamb Fair Court risk area where internal property flooding was reported on 20 July 2021; flooding involved rapid entry of water into properties. (p9) [PAG]
* Reported location of internal property flooding as one of three key risk areas in the village; properties experienced rapid-onset flooding with water ingress to buildings. (p10) [PAG, IMG]
* Surface water flowed down Balsham Road from the highway and surrounding fields; the highways drainage system was inundated. Water flowed over dropped kerbs towards properties on the south side of the road, flooding properties internally and gardens/outbuildings to an approximate depth of 15 cm. Water receded within about 12 hours. Some highway drains were found partially or fully blocked. There are reports of residents having to leave their property for over one month. (p11) [TAB]
* Surface water runoff from the fields and highway above these roads contributed to flooding; the LLFA is working with Highways and landowners to investigate measures to reduce runoff and whether additional attenuation capacity can be introduced. Highway drainage (gullies) were inspected and works orders raised for cleaning. (p13)
* During the 20 July 2021 storm surface water flowed along Balsham Road; the drainage network capacity was exceeded. Riparian maintenance issues were also identified as a contributing factor to flooding on this road. (p20) [TAB]

### Cambridgeshire
* Areas within the county experienced surface water flooding that prompted the LLFA to undertake walkovers and inspections. In response, works orders were raised to clean gullies in affected areas, a new recording system was introduced to monitor all drainage works, and a cyclic gully-cleaning programme (every other year) was established. (p13)
* County-wide response included increasing the frequency of gully maintenance to every second year and undertaking highway drainage investigations to reduce surface water runoff after flooding; these policy/maintenance changes were made following the flooding. (p14) [TAB]
* Following flooding in the area, gully cleaning was increased and the frequency of gully maintenance for gullies across Cambridgeshire was changed (actioned from July 2021); highway gullies were cleaned in areas where flooding was experienced to improve surface water removal. (p15) [TAB]

### Horseheath Road, Linton
* Large volumes of surface water from surrounding arable fields and carriageways flowed onto and along Horseheath Road. Highway drainage was minimal and inundated, causing water to enter a construction site through the temporary site entrance and main entrance and to continue westerly along the road into Lonsdale. (p11) [PAG]
* Large volumes of surface water flowed westerly down Horseheath Road due to a large upstream surface water catchment, absence of crops and minimal highway drainage. Surface water entered the construction site south of Horseheath Road via the temporary entrance, and other flows continued along the road into the development site and further into Lonsdale. (p12) [PAG]
* Surface water runoff from the fields and highway above these roads contributed to flooding; the LLFA is working with Highways and landowners to investigate measures to reduce runoff and whether additional attenuation capacity can be introduced. Highway drainage (gullies) were inspected and works orders raised for cleaning. (p13)
* Surface water from the 20 July 2021 event flooded the highway; highway gullies were blocked/required cleaning and highway drainage on Horseheath Road was reviewed. The road was added to a grip‑cutting programme to improve surface water removal. (p15) [TAB]

### Granta
* {general}: There is a floodplain associated with the River Granta throughout Linton, creating flood risk to adjacent land and properties in the village. (p5)
* A floodplain associated with the River Granta runs through Linton and high surface-water flood risk zones are concentrated along the river. During the intense rainfall on 20 July 2021 significant flooding occurred in Linton; flood extent and surface-water flooding were closely associated with the River Granta corridor and extended into nearby low-lying built-up areas. (p7) [PAG, IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### South Cambridgeshire District Council
* Acted in immediate flood response by providing sandbags to properties affected by flooding (completed July 2021) and attended on-site meetings to provide local insight and help determine next steps. (p14) [TAB]
* Engaged with the developer and local residents regarding a planning discharge for surface water drainage at the development site south of Horseheath Road following flooding associated with the 20 July 2021 event. (p15) [TAB]

### Highway Authority
* Attended flood investigations, raised work orders to cleanse gullies, increased gully maintenance frequency county-wide, requested downstream system checks and cleaning, and identified a collapsed pipe downstream with works planned to address it. (p14)
* Responded to highway flooding by requesting and carrying out cleaning of highway gullies where flooding occurred, added Horseheath Road to a 'grip cutting' programme to improve surface water removal, and monitored the surface water system and raised works orders as required after the 20 July 2021 event. (p15)

### Cambridgeshire County Council
* As the LLFA attended and investigated reports of flooding on 20 July 2021, they coordinated actions with RMAs, engaged with landowners, the parish council and the developer, contacted Anglian Water about the foul network, and progressed options to create additional storage capacity and reinstate ditches/verges to manage surface water runoff. (p15)
* A partially constructed development site within Linton, stripped of vegetation and lacking a completed surface water network, contributed to increased runoff and the severity of surface water flooding during the storm. (p19)

### Anglian Water
* Contacted by the LLFA about reported flooding of the foul (sewer) drainage network during the event; Anglian Water reported that it had not received any confirmed reports of problems with their systems during the flood. (p15) [TAB]
