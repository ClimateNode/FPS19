

# West Sussex flooding 10 Jun 2012 - 12 Jun 2012

Reference document:<br>West Sussex County Council Report On June 2012 Flood Event.<br>142\_West Sussex County/142\_2012\_West Sussex County/final\_report.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### West Sussex
* An extreme rainfall event on 10–12 June 2012 caused widespread flooding across parts of the county. Almost 800 properties across West Sussex were affected by flooding from a variety of sources; the worst affected areas included the Manhood Peninsula, Bognor Regis, Littlehampton and Worthing. The Bognor Regis conurbation (including Middleton on Sea, Elmer and Felpham) had reports of properties flooded primarily by surface water. (p3)
* The county experienced unprecedented summer 2012 rainfall (notably 10–12 June 2012) that overloaded local drainage networks across the coastal plain. Flood water generally dissipated over 2–3 days with no significant infrastructure damage, but the event caused widespread surface water flooding. (p5)
* {01 Jun 2012 - 30 Jun 2012}: Significant internal and external flooding occurred at multiple locations across West Sussex; some locations experienced flooding on more than one occasion. (p10) [PAG]
* {01 Jun 2012 - 30 Jun 2012}: Significant internal and external flooding occurred at multiple locations across West Sussex; some locations experienced flooding on more than one occasion. This prompted West Sussex County Council to conduct a Section 19 investigation. (p11) [PAG]
* Widespread surface water flooding during the June 2012 event; flooding patterns closely matched the county's Areas Susceptible to Surface Water Flooding maps. Prolonged heavy rainfall (some locations received large totals in 12–24 hours) generated excess runoff and caused inundation across parts of the county. (p13)
* West Sussex experienced very high short‑duration rainfall that generated excess runoff and led to surface water flooding in the county. (p14) [PAG]
* Numerous extreme rainfall observations were recorded across West Sussex during the event (Met Office and other networks). The report notes multiple very large local totals in the county and describes highly localised convective cells, indicating the county experienced intense rainfall that increased the likelihood of significant disruption and potential flooding in parts of West Sussex. (p15)
* Extremely high, localised rainfall across West Sussex produced widespread surface water flooding (the majority of property flooding on 10–12 June) and some river-linked flooding; the Environment Agency issued Flood Alerts and Flood Warnings for multiple rivers and watercourses in the county. (p16) [PAG]
* Widespread extreme rainfall in June 2012 caused extensive surface water flooding across the county (main event 10–12 June 2012). Some property flooding occurred; river levels in parts of the county (notably Aldingbourne Rife and the Manhood Peninsula rife network) remained high for days after the rain. (p17) [PAG, IMG]
* Quant: almost 800 properties; up to 370 properties (p3)
* Quant: 780 properties (p5)

### Bognor Regis
* Bognor Regis conurbation was among the worst affected in the 10–12 June 2012 event; properties flooded and the conurbation is thought to have been flooded primarily by surface water. (p3)
* The Bognor to Chichester area recorded the highest rainfall during the 10–12 June 2012 event; the intense rainfall overloaded local drainage leading to surface water/urban flooding in low-lying parts of the area. (p5)
* Extreme, highly localised rainfall was recorded in Bognor Regis during the event. The Environment Agency rain gauge at Bognor Regis recorded 114mm over the whole event — the highest 24‑hour total recorded at Bognor since EA records began in 1974 — and the report characterises this as approximately a 1-in-200-year (0.5PCSYM annual chance) rainfall event. A separate town council gauge and other local gauges reported substantially higher totals at nearby locations, indicating an exceptionally intense, localised storm over the Bognor area and implying a high potential for disruption. (p15) [PAG]
* Extremely intense, localized rainfall during the June 2012 event (recorded as one of the highest totals in the EA network) contributed to surface water flooding; properties in the area were flooded during the main event. (p17) [PAG, IMG]
* Quant: up to 370 properties (Bognor Regis conurbation incl. Middleton on Sea, Elmer and Felpham) (p3)

### Hampshire
* Prolonged heavy rain over 12–24 hours during the 10–11 June 2012 event generated excess runoff and led to flooding across Hampshire; the report lists Hampshire as one of the worst affected areas. (p13) [PAG]
* {10 Jun 2026}: Persistent heavy rain beginning on 10 June tracked into East Hampshire and continued without interruption in worst affected parts, generating excess runoff that led to flooding; very heavy bursts were expected in eastern parts of the county and were likely to bring significant disruption. (p14)
* Eastern parts of Hampshire were highlighted by the FGS as likely to receive very heavy bursts of rain on 10 June that could bring significant disruption; EA/other gauge coverage also recorded extreme rainfall across Sussex and eastern Hampshire that raised flood risk. (p15) [PAG]
