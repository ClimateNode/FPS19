### Bristol

* Areas of the city adjacent to the River Avon experienced tidal flooding during the high tides of 11–12 March 2020, with multiple streets, basins and a footpath inundated along the river corridor. (p6) [IMG]
* Central Bristol was affected by a tidal flood event; flood alerts and warnings were issued and the Environment Agency operated sluices and closed flood gates (e.g. Eastville sluice gates to allow River Frome flows to discharge to the River Avon downstream of Black Rocks, and gates at Shirehampton) with pre/post-tide checks and clearances to manage tide locking. (p19)
* On 11 March 2020 (and into 12 March 2020) Bristol experienced tidal/coastal flooding caused by an astronomical high tide combined with a storm surge and westerly winds; the event reached a peak of 8.81mAOD and affected several areas across the city, including the Portway and Cattle Market Road. (p21) [PAG]
* The city experienced a tidal flood caused by an astronomical high tide combined with a storm surge, reaching a peak of 8.81mAOD and affecting residential and highway infrastructure across multiple areas. (p22)

### River Bristol Avon
* The tidal River Avon caused flooding at multiple adjacent locations during the high tides of 11–12 March 2020; the river’s tidal surge is identified as the source of inundation along the mapped sites. (p6) [IMG]
* During the high tides on 11–12 Mar 2020 the river experienced higher-than-predicted peak high tide levels at Bedminster Bridge as surge pushed water up the channel; predicted peaks were underestimated, increasing local tidal flood hazard in the city centre. (p9) [PAG]
* A tidal surge propagated up the River Avon raising upstream water levels and contributing to higher tide levels in the city. The council applied a +0.15 m adjustment from Avonmouth to predict city centre levels, but observed increases from Avonmouth to Bedminster Bridge were 0.18 m (11 Mar 2020) and 0.23 m (12 Mar 2020), meaning the adjustment underestimated peak tide heights. Channel geometry (V-shaped at low flows vs rectangular at high flows) was noted as amplifying height differences at lower peak tides. (p11) [PAG]
* Tidal floodwater entered the Floating Harbour (overtopping/flowing in at Bathurst Basin and Junction Lock) and caused tidal flooding of low-lying areas adjacent to the river, contributing to inundation at locations such as Sea Mills, the Portway and Cattle Market Road. (p12) [PAG]
* Tidal inundation along the River Avon produced elevated high tides that propagated upstream and contributed to flooding at locations listed in Table 5; tide level varied significantly along the river (highest upstream at Netham/Bedminster and lowest at Avonmouth), complicating quantification of depths and durations. (p13) [PAG]
* Tidal high tides on the River Avon caused flooding and road closures at multiple locations along the river, and led to one internal residential flooding incident where tide levels exceeded a demountable barrier at a property on Sea Mills Lane. (p15) [TAB]
* {1981, 1999, 2014, 11 Mar 2020 - 12 Mar 2020}: High tides and associated storm surges on the River Avon caused tidal flooding of adjacent residential areas. In the March 11–12, 2020 high-tide events a property on Sea Mills Lane experienced internal flooding when tide levels exceeded its demountable flood barrier; flood gates at Shirehampton and the Cumberland Road flood wall were closed/deployed and protected properties along Avon Crescent, Cumberland Road and Commercial Road. Historical high-tide/surge events on the River Avon also caused property inundation and evacuations (e.g. 1981, 1999, 2014). (p16) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* A tidal surge combined with relatively high astronomical tides and strong south-westerly/westerly winds caused elevated water levels in the River Avon that overtopped/extended onto adjacent residential areas. Properties were inundated (including properties on Avon Crescent) and some evacuations of properties were required. (p17) [PAG]
* Footpath along the River Avon at Crew's Hole Road and St Phillips was closed as part of the response to the high tides; river/tidal levels were monitored and led to closures. (p18)
* The Environment Agency closed flood gates along the River Avon (for example at Shirehampton) and carried out pre- and post-tide checks and clearances to prevent backing up and blockage during potential tide locking associated with the tidal event. (p19)
* Elevated tidal levels and storm surge on 11–12 Mar 2020 caused high water in the River Avon that threatened flooding to the city centre and low-lying roads. Actions taken in response to the high tides included pre-lowering the floating harbour, contacting the EA to open Eastville Sluices, issuing warnings to residents and businesses along the river, monitoring tidal gauges (Bedminster Bridge) and photographing high water at vulnerable locations (Bathurst Basin, Cattle Market Road, Cumberland Basin, The Portway, Sea Mills). Several roads were identified as likely to flood and were closed or monitored. (p24) [PAG]
* Elevated tidal surge levels in the River Avon (astronomical + surge) produced high tides that threatened adjacent low-lying areas, triggered requests to open Eastville Sluices and to pre-lower the floating harbour, and prompted city-wide warnings and monitoring ahead of the high tides on 11-12 March. (p25) [PAG]

(p9) [PAG]
