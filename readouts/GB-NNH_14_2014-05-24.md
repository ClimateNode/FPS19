

# Oundle flooding 24 May 2014

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Benefield Road, Oundle, 24th May 2014<br>090\_North Northamptonshire/Benefield-Road-Flood-Investigation.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Benefield Road, Oundle
* Intense short-duration rainfall and inadequate drainage caused excess surface water to flow over ground and follow contours to low points around private property on Benefield Road, resulting in internal flooding of at least one property; local kerb/footway levels and surface features exacerbated the flow. (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* {01 Oct 2013 - 31 Oct 2013, 24 May 2014}: Internal flooding of a residential property (water crossed the threshold). Flooding was reported near the junction of Benefield Road and Lytham Park and has occurred on more than one occasion. (p9) [PAG, MUL]
* {05 Aug 2013, 22 May 2014, 24 May 2014}: Surface water from the carriageway flowed down the carriageway channel into the low point adjacent to a culverted watercourse and then over front gardens into residential property on the Benefield Road/Lytham Park development. (p11) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* Surface water flowed down the carriageway channel from east and west to a low point on the south side adjacent to a culverted watercourse; a shallow wave of surface water moved from the carriageway across the footway and toward nearby properties, entering at least one property's front doorway. The resident took measures to sweep flood water back to the door and divert flows to lower areas to the east; kerbing at the low point was laid flush with the footway which contributed to flow across the footway. (p12)
* Heavy rainfall combined with the layout and levels of the carriageway, kerb and footway, and the layout/levels of the property's front approach and steps, channelled surface water toward the property's front doorway threshold. Highway drainage effectiveness and inability to discharge satisfactorily contributed to increased accumulation of surface water at the lowest point of Benefield Road. (p17)
* A flood incident occurred at the low point of Benefield Road (adjacent to the flood incident), indicating highway drainage failure/insufficient capacity; this prompted recommendations for highway works including full height kerbing, re-levelled footway and review of developer-implemented highway works. (p20)
* Surface water ponded at a low point on Benefield Road during the flood incident; a roadside ditch was observed in‑filled with small aggregate, restricting drainage and contributing to water ponding over the carriageway. (p43) [IMG]
* Road gullies located at the low point, together with flush kerbs and a backward-sloping footway, concentrated runoff and reduced drainage effectiveness. These features were recorded as causing surface water to pond at the low point during the 24 May 2014 event. (p45) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface water ponding occurred at a low point on Benefield Road. Drainage works and excavation were carried out at the location during the investigation; sandbags were placed at the entrance to a nearby residential property to prevent water ingress. Road gullies and the flush-kerb/backward-sloping footway at the low point are recorded as features at the flood location. The start of a culverted section of a tributary of Lyveden Brook is located on the north side of Benefield Road (downstream) and was investigated in relation to the incident. (p46) [IMG, PAG]
* Flooding occurred at the north side of Benefield Road where the culvert entrance of the tributary is located; drainage works were undertaken at this spot during the flood investigation. (p47) [IMG]
* Quant: one property (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* {01 Oct 2013 - 31 Oct 2013, 24 May 2014}: Quant: one property (p9) [PAG, MUL]

### Lytham Park, Oundle
* {01 Oct 2013 - 31 Oct 2013, 24 May 2014}: Internal flooding affected a residential property near the junction of Benefield Road and Lytham Park; the area was the focus of a formal flood investigation following the incident and residents reported previous flood occurrences. (p9) [MUL]
* A property adjacent to the Lytham Park development was flooded by surface water runoff from the highway/drainage catchment; localised drainage works were installed by the developer (including a new ACO channel at the back of the footway) to collect highway runoff and reduce the risk of recurrence. (p10) [PAG]
* {05 Aug 2013, 22 May 2014, 24 May 2014}: Residential property within the Benefield Road / Lytham Park development was flooded when surface water from the Benefield Road carriageway flowed over front gardens into properties. (p11) [TAB, MUL]
* A shallow wave of surface water flowed from Benefield Road across the footway, across the front garden and steps to a property on Benefield Road/Lytham Park and into the property's front doorway and internal hallway; the resident swept and diverted water to reduce internal impact, aided by wooden laminate flooring in the hallway which eased clean-up. (p12)
* Areas immediately east of Lytham Park (the location of the Lyveden Brook tributary) were implicated in surface water/runoff problems during the event; the report recommends confirming ownership and maintenance of the watercourse there to address flooding and surface water collection. (p18)
* Localised drainage works were being carried out on the Lytham Park housing development at the time of the investigation because surface water runoff/overland flow from or around the development had contributed to flooding; ENC was asked to review whether watercourse works and other measures were satisfactorily designed and implemented during construction. (p21)
* Ditch immediately west of Lytham Park was observed in-filled with small aggregate and overgrown, restricting conveyance. The obstructed ditch and outlet arrangement contributed to localised surface water pooling at the low point during the 24 May 2014 event. (p45) [IMG]
* The route of a culverted watercourse installed as part of the Lytham Park development (south of Benefield Road, east of Lytham Park) was involved in the flood incident; drainage works were recorded along the culverted route and where the culvert discharges to open water east of Lytham Park. (p47) [IMG, PAG]
* {01 Oct 2013 - 31 Oct 2013, 24 May 2014}: Quant: one property (p9) [MUL]

### Oundle
* Benefield Road in Oundle was the subject of a Northamptonshire County Council Section 19 flood investigation, indicating flooding affected the location. (p2)
* {01 Oct 2013 - 31 Oct 2013, 24 May 2014}: Flooding in Oundle included an incident on Benefield Road where one property experienced internal flooding; residents were spoken to about the 24th May 2014 event and previous incidents. (p9) [MUL]
* {05 Aug 2013, 01 Oct 2013 - 31 Oct 2013, 22 May 2014, 24 May 2014}: Lyveden Brook experienced excessive flows that overtopped banks and affected adjoining properties in October 2013 (river flooding). Separately, surface water flowing along Benefield Road inundated front gardens and residential properties in the Oundle area on 05/08/2013, 22/05/2014 and 24/05/2014. (p11) [MUL]
* Reported routes of surface water were recorded during the event; surface water flowed along those reported routes causing flooding in Oundle during the incident. (p13)
* Parts of Oundle (specifically Benefield Road) experienced surface water flooding on 24th May 2014 due to heavy rainfall and local carriageway/kerb/footway levels that channelled water towards property thresholds; highway drainage discharge issues increased surface water collection at the lowest point. (p17)
* Surface water collected on highway and low-lying parts of the town linked to ineffective highway drainage and runoff, prompting a community flood planning response and recommendations for property-level and community-wide measures. (p18)
* Surface water run-off and inadequacies in historic drainage/watercourse capacity caused flooding in the town, prompting town-wide measures: re-excavation of ditches, repair/replacement of piped/culvert sections, dredging/trimming of watercourses, replacement of structures and vegetation clearance; community-level response actions (flood warden and village flood plan) were recommended. (p20)
* {01 Oct 2013 - 31 Oct 2013, 24 May 2014}: Quant: one property (p9) [MUL]

### Northamptonshire
* Within Northamptonshire (LLFA area) an event of intense short-duration rainfall produced surface water runoff that caused flooding at Benefield Road, Oundle, including internal flooding of a property; the event prompted a formal Section 19 investigation by the Northamptonshire LLFA. (p6)
* Surface water flooding affected Benefield Road, Oundle. Heavy rainfall combined with the layout and levels of the carriageway, kerb and footway adjacent to the property, and the layout/levels of the property's front approach and steps, channelled surface water towards the front doorway threshold causing the property to flood. (p16)
* Quant: one property (p6)

### Wakerley Close, Oundle
* Overgrown ditch systems west of Lytham Park at Wakerley Close were heavily vegetated and obstructed, limiting flow and maintenance access. These obstructions were identified as contributing to localised surface water flooding during the 24 May 2014 event. (p45) [IMG]

### Lyveden Brook
* {01 Oct 2013 - 31 Oct 2013}: Excessive flow in Lyveden Brook caused water levels to rise and overtop the banks, with strong flows affecting adjoining property (documented in the October 2013 investigation). The brook's catchment contributes to surface water impacts on Benefield Road. (p11)
* The watercourse (and its tributary immediately east of Lytham Park) was identified as contributing to surface water runoff and flooding within the local area; the report recommends confirming ownership and maintenance responsibilities for sections of this tributary to reduce future surface water collection and conveyance issues. (p18)
* The tributary of Lyveden Brook (start of a culverted section) was the location of a flood incident; drainage works were carried out at the culvert inlet on the north side of Benefield Road during the investigation and the culverted section connects downstream to open water east of Lytham Park. (p47) [IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Environment Agency
* The EA was to be consulted on planning applications and engaged as required to reduce flood risk from watercourses and surface water associated with the development and incident; their input was part of the response to flooding impacts around the development. (p21)

### Anglian Water
* Their public sewerage system received additional water from surface runoff/overland flow during the incident; the report requires Anglian Water to assess sources entering the public sewer, identify assets of insufficient capacity where that creates flood risk to properties, and develop a detailed asset plan to share with the LLFA and community. (p21)

### East Northamptonshire Council
* ENC was required to review planning permissions and building inspection records for the Lytham Park development, to review developer drainage works being carried out during the investigation, and to consider enforcement under the Land Drainage Act where riparian owners fail to maintain watercourses—actions prompted by flooding from surface runoff/overland flow that affected properties. (p21)
