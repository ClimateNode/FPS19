

# Beeston flooding 17 Jun 2020

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Beeston & Chilwell – June 2020<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/beestonchilwellsection19reportjune2020.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Beeston
* Widespread internal flooding of properties following a torrential downpour that overwhelmed drainage infrastructure; properties experienced internal flooding. (p1) [PAG]
* Internal property flooding across the town: 32 businesses and 57 residential properties suffered internal flooding; Boundary Road, Ullswater Crescent and Eastcote Avenue were identified as the worst-affected residential locations. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Internal property flooding of residential properties across the town; 57 residential properties suffered internal property flooding. (p3)
* Quant: 32 business properties; 57 residential properties (p1) [PAG]
* Quant: 32 businesses; 57 residential properties (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 57 residential properties (p3)

### Nottinghamshire
* Widespread internal property flooding in the Beeston area during the storm event: 57 residential properties internally flooded (throughout Beeston, worst affected locations Boundary Road, Ullswater Crescent and Eastcote Avenue); 32 businesses recorded by the Flood Risk Management Team suffered internal flooding (locations include High Road and Wollaton Road); properties at Chilwell Retail Park were also flooded. Highway water levels rose to threshold, causing lower-lying properties to flood. (p3) [IMG]
* Quant: 57 residential properties; 32 businesses (p3) [IMG]

### Broxtowe
* Communities in Broxtowe were affected by the flooding. Broxtowe Borough Council provided emergency response support, delivered sandbags to affected residents and managed ongoing recovery in affected communities. (p5)

### Chilwell
* Properties at Chilwell Retail Park were recorded as suffering internal flooding damage (businesses affected). (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Commercial properties and retail units within Chilwell (including Chilwell Retail Park and nearby High Road) experienced internal flooding during the storm event. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 32 businesses (total reported to the Flood Risk Management Team across affected locations including Chilwell area) (p3) [IMG, PAG]

### Eastcote Avenue, Bramcote
* Residential properties on Eastcote Avenue suffered internal property flooding; listed as one of the worst affected locations. (p3)
* Properties experienced internal flooding: some via seepage through airbricks and others through integral garages. Flooding occurred as excessive rainfall overwhelmed the drainage system, with water on the carriageway moving to lower-lying properties. (p4) [PAG]
* Quant: 57 residential properties (total across Beeston) (p4) [PAG]

### Wollaton Road, Beeston
* Cluster of businesses on Wollaton Road reported internal flooding damage (shown on Figure 2). (p2) [IMG]
* Businesses on Wollaton Road suffered internal flooding due to excessive rainfall and raised highway water levels; some lower-lying properties flooded from highway water. (p3) [PAG]
* Quant: 32 businesses (combined on High Road and Wollaton Road) (p3) [PAG]

### Boundary Road, Beeston
* Residential properties on Boundary Road suffered internal property flooding; identified as one of the worst affected locations. (p3) [IMG]
* Properties experienced internal flooding: some minor internal flooding via seepage through airbricks; others had greater internal flooding via water ingress through integral garages. Flooding occurred because excessive rainfall overwhelmed the drainage, water collected on the highway and, because the properties are lower than the carriageway, water flowed into the properties. (p4) [PAG]
* Quant: 57 residential properties (total across Beeston) (p4) [PAG]

### Ullswater Crescent, Bramcote
* Residential properties on Ullswater Crescent suffered internal property flooding; identified as one of the worst affected locations. (p3) [IMG]
* Properties experienced internal flooding: some minor internal flooding via seepage through airbricks; others had larger internal flooding through integral garages. Flooding resulted from excessive rainfall collecting on the highway after the drainage was overloaded, with water flowing to lower-lying properties. (p4) [PAG]
* Quant: 57 residential properties (total across Beeston) (p4) [PAG]

### High Road, Chilwell
* Businesses on High Road suffered internal flooding due to excessive rainfall and raised highway water levels; lower-lying premises experienced internal flooding from water within the highway. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 32 businesses (combined on High Road and Wollaton Road) (p3) [IMG, PAG]

### High Road, Beeston
* Cluster of businesses on High Road reported internal flooding damage (shown on Figure 2). (p2) [IMG]

### Chilwell Retail Park
* Properties/businesses at Chilwell Retail Park suffered internal flooding (illustrated in Figure 3). (p2)
* Properties at the retail park were flooded (internal property flooding recorded at retail units during the storm event). (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Businesses suffered internal flooding from the front of properties due to excessive runoff from the hard-standing car park. Additional flows entered the car park because water on the highway was displaced by bow waves from vehicular traffic, which is understood to have worsened the flooding near the retail units. (p4)


## Topics without coordinates

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* {general}: Initiated and co‑ordinated emergency planning procedures in response to the flood; administered the Flooding Hardship Fund for affected residents; initiated and led the Section 19 Flood Investigation; committed to ongoing work with partners and communities to reduce future flood risk and support the most vulnerable. (p5)

### Broxtowe Borough Council
* Provided emergency response support during the flood event; delivered sandbags to affected residents; managed and continue to manage recovery in affected Broxtowe communities; actively engaged in the Section 19 Flood Investigation. (p5)

### Highway Authority
* {general}: Responsible for maintenance of public highways including highway drainage assets; provided site‑based presence and carried out investigations immediately following the flood event to assess and respond to highway/drainage impacts. (p5)
