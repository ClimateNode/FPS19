

# Hounslow flooding 07 Dec 2018, Hounslow flooding 21 Dec 2018 and Hounslow flooding 10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019

Reference document:<br>Ferndale Avenue: Flood Risk Investigation<br>058\_Hounslow/flooding\_report\_section19\_Ferndale\_Avenue\_may23.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Ferndale Avenue, Hounslow
* {2013, 2019}: Multiple reported flooding incidents where surface water pooled at a topographical low and drainage was slow to clear. Flooding was exacerbated by limited surface water drainage, blockages in foul and surface water sewers, and the foul sewer acting as a combined sewer which may have contributed to flooding at the end of the road; only one privately owned gully on the road to the garages means water can take a long time to drain away. (p3) [MUL]
* {10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019}: Residents on Ferndale Avenue reported flooding affecting properties/residents; surface water drainage assets at the eastern end of the development are under investigation to determine whether surface water discharges into the foul (sewer) system and whether drainage connections/maintenance of a private road and garages contribute to the problem. (p4)
* {2013–present}: Flood incidents have been recorded on Ferndale Avenue; the LLFA database holds repeated reports of flooding at this street, with historic reporting prior to 2018 limited and largely anecdotal. (p7)
* {07 Dec 2018, 21 Dec 2018, 10 Jun 2019, 10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019, 13 Jun 2019}: Rainfall-driven surface water ponding at the end of the cul-de-sac caused flooding of the carriageway and footway at the eastern end of Ferndale Avenue; flood water extended towards and entered private garages behind the houses and in places overtopped the kerb. Infrastructure impacts include manholes and gullies surcharging associated with a filled soakaway, and reported foul sewer blockages (FOGs) and silt in the surface water sewer noted as contributing factors. (p8) [MUL]
* {08 Nov 2013, 10 Nov 2013, 31 Dec 2013, 27 Dec 2014, 17 Jan 2016}: During heavy rain the footway and carriageway flood; sewage-contaminated water affects houses at the eastern end of Ferndale Avenue. Two manholes opposite 33 Ferndale Avenue have been reported to overflow and an 'overflow tank' is reported to fill and surcharge from these manholes. (p9) [TAB, MUL]
* {general}: Identified as being within a small hydrological catchment that contributes surface water runoff. The eastern end is a topographical low where water can accumulate if a local drainage network is absent or not functioning; a small flow path begins in the south‑eastern corner of Ferndale Avenue and joins the main overland flow path on Heathdale Avenue, indicating areas at increased risk of surface water flooding. (p11) [IMG]
* {general}: The eastern end of Ferndale Avenue is a topographical low where water can accumulate; the EA RoFSW map shows the eastern end to be at high risk of surface water flooding while the remainder of the street is predicted to have a very low risk. Fluvial flood probability for the location is very low. (p12) [PAG]
* {general}: The eastern end of Ferndale Avenue is mapped at high risk of surface water flooding (annual probability >3.3PCSYM); the remainder of Ferndale Avenue is predicted to have very low surface water flood risk. Fluvial flood risk at Ferndale Avenue is assessed as very low (annual probability <0.1PCSYM). RoFSW mapping shows 1-in-30 and 1-in-100 year surface-water flood extents affecting parts of Ferndale Avenue and nearby streets. (p13) [PAG, IMG]
* {general}: Residents and TWUL reported flood incidents at Ferndale Avenue: both foul (sewer) and surface-water flooding incidents have been reported and attributed to blockages rather than capacity. Residents reported surcharging manholes with a strong odour. Two gullies on the extended section of road (which drain to a soakaway on the grass verge) surcharge during rainfall. The surface water network starts outside No. 33 and does not extend to the newer development, leaving limited visible provision for surface-water drainage. (p14) [PAG]
* {general}: Reported flood incidents on Ferndale Avenue attributed to sewer blockages and surcharge. Residents reported surcharging manholes (with a ‘bad stench’) indicating the foul sewer is acting as a combined system and unable to cope with rainfall. Two gullies on the extended eastern section of the road drain to a soakaway on the grass verge and are reported to surcharge during rainfall; one remaining highway gully is located on the bend where the old kerb was. TWUL reported flood incidents (both foul and surface water) in the area caused by blockages rather than capacity issues. (p15) [IMG, PAG]
* {07 Dec 2018, 27 Mar 2019, 10 Jun 2019, 10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019}: Manholes and gullies at Ferndale Avenue surcharged during reported flood incidents. Two gullies that drain to a soakaway became overwhelmed and surcharged. The newer development at the end of Ferndale Avenue lacks known surface water drainage so the foul sewer (allegedly acting as a combined sewer) must accept additional runoff from houses at the eastern end, increasing flows and contributing to the system becoming overwhelmed. One gully on the privately owned road leading to the garages behind Ferndale Avenue is privately owned and maintained. (p16) [PAG, MUL]
* {10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019}: Surface water and sewer flooding occurred in Ferndale Avenue: gullies and the local soakaway became overwhelmed leading to gullies surcharging; the sewer system was at risk of becoming overwhelmed by additional runoff and manholes were reported to surcharge, limiting drainage of floodwater from the street. (p17) [PAG]
* {10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019}: Soakaway at the end of Ferndale Avenue became overwhelmed causing connected gullies to surcharge; increased flow entering the drainage system at the street end risked overwhelming the system and causing manholes to surcharge. A privately owned gully at the topographical low point limits the ability for surface water to drain away. (p18) [PAG]
* {17 Jan 2016, 26 Jun 2016, 24 Oct 2017, 07 Dec 2018, 21 December 2018 / 4 January 2019, 06 Feb 2019, summer 2019, 10 Jun 2019}: Recurring flood incidents were reported at Ferndale Avenue. A foul/sewer flooding report was logged (forwarded 17 January 2016). Multiple blocked/cleared gullies were recorded on Ferndale Avenue and, following recurring incidents (including the 10 June 2019 incident), a non‑return valve was fitted in the surface water system draining to the soakaway to prevent backflow; no flood incidents have been reported to Hounslow since that installation. (p19) [MUL]
* {21 Dec 2018, 09 Feb 2019, 27 May 2019, 10 Jun 2019, 17 Jul 2019, 30 Jul 2019, 2020}: Recurring flooding incidents on Ferndale Avenue that prompted multiple attendances by the sewerage company; causes identified during visits included blockages in the foul and surface water systems (FOGs and silt), issues with road gullies and groundwater, and problems with the surface water system that leads to the soakaway. Hounslow Highways fitted a non-return valve in the surface water system draining to the soakaway in summer 2019 to prevent backflow; no further flood incidents at Ferndale Avenue have been reported to Hounslow since the valve installation. (p20) [PAG, MUL]
* {2013, 01 Jun 2019 - 30 Jun 2019}: Repeated flooding incidents at Ferndale Avenue caused by issues with the foul water sewer system; residents experienced sewer-related flooding and were advised by the water company to complete a Sewer Flooding Questionnaire and follow guidance to reduce sewer blockages. (p21) [PAG, MUL]
* {2013, 01 Dec 2018 - 31 Dec 2018, 01 Jun 2019 - 30 Jun 2019}: Repeated ponding and surface-water flooding at the eastern low point of Ferndale Avenue due to local topography and a lack of surface water drainage; water pools at the eastern end and can take a long time to drain. The soakaway at the eastern end has insufficient capacity or poor condition. Blockages in the foul sewer (attributed to disposal of FOGs) have contributed to flooding and surcharging, and there is a potential groundwater influence contributing to surcharging of gullies/soakaway. (p22) [PAG, MUL]
* {10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019}: Surface water pools in the topographical low point of Ferndale Avenue; drainage from this low point is slow because there is only one privately owned gully (located on the road leading to the garages). Gullies and the soakaway can surcharge due to groundwater ingress, and residents reported flooding since summer 2019. (p23) [PAG, MUL]
* {2013–present}: Quant: 10 reports of flooding (p7)
* {07 Dec 2018, 21 Dec 2018, 10 Jun 2019, 10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019, 13 Jun 2019}: Quant: ten reports of flooding (since 2013); photographs taken on 10 and 13 June 2019; TWUL called three times to investigate foul sewer blockages in 2020 (p8) [MUL]
* {general}: Quant: one highway gully; two gullies on the extended section of the road; one privately owned gully on the private road to the garages (p15) [IMG, PAG]
* {17 Jan 2016, 26 Jun 2016, 24 Oct 2017, 07 Dec 2018, 21 December 2018 / 4 January 2019, 06 Feb 2019, summer 2019, 10 Jun 2019}: Quant: one foul sewer flooding report (17 January 2016); two gullies cleared (26 June 2016); one blocked gully cleared (24 October 2017); one blocked gully reported (6 February 2019); one non‑return valve installed (summer 2019) (p19) [MUL]
* {21 Dec 2018, 09 Feb 2019, 27 May 2019, 10 Jun 2019, 17 Jul 2019, 30 Jul 2019, 2020}: Quant: ten call-outs since 2018; three blockages rectified in 2020 (p20) [PAG, MUL]
* {2013, 01 Dec 2018 - 31 Dec 2018, 01 Jun 2019 - 30 Jun 2019}: Quant: two incidents analysed (December 2018 and June 2019); one privately owned gully at the low point (p22) [PAG, MUL]

### Hounslow
* {2013–present}: Flood incidents within the borough have been recorded by the Hounslow LLFA; records include ten reports of flooding at Ferndale Avenue. (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* {07 Dec 2018, 21 Dec 2018, 09 Feb 2019, 14 Mar 2019, 27 Mar 2019, 10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019, 17 Jul 2019, 30 Jul 2019}: At the eastern end of Ferndale Avenue, heavy rain led to footway and carriageway flooding with sewage-contaminated water; garages were flooded; residents reported trouble accessing houses and cars and a strong foul odour. (p10) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* {general}: Report identifies fluvial, surface-water and groundwater flood risk across the borough: main rivers (River Crane, Duke of Northumberland’s River, River Thames, Longford River) run through the borough and are considered in fluvial flood risk; areas within the borough (e.g. around Ferndale Avenue) are mapped for RoFSW (surface water) 1-in-30 and 1-in-100 year extents; groundwater flood risk is noted as a general borough-wide hazard following prolonged rainfall. (p13) [PAG, IMG]
* {summer 2019}: Hounslow Highways (part of the local authority) fitted a non-return valve in the surface water system that drains to the soakaway in summer 2019 (after the 10 June 2019 incident) to prevent backflow during rainfall. Following this installation, no flood incidents at Ferndale Avenue have been reported to Hounslow. (p20) [PAG]
* {2013–present}: Quant: 10 reports of flooding at Ferndale Avenue (p7) [IMG, PAG]

### Heathdale Avenue, Hounslow
* {general}: Described as the primary overland flow path south of Ferndale Avenue; the main overland flow runs along Heathdale Avenue and the small flow path from Ferndale Avenue joins this route, indicating Heathdale Avenue is a key flow corridor and area of greatest surface water flood risk in the local analysis. (p11) [IMG]
* {general}: RoFSW mapping (Figure 3‑3) shows the 1 in 30 and 1 in 100 year surface water flood extents concentrated near Heathdale Avenue, indicating the street and adjacent residential areas are at increased risk of surface water flooding during these events. (p13) [PAG, IMG]

### Dale Avenue, Hounslow
* {general}: Located within the hydrological catchment that drains toward Ferndale Avenue; runoff from the catchment is routed through this area along the mapped flow path, placing the street in the area identified as at greatest risk of overland surface water flooding. (p11) [IMG]

### Martindale Road, Hounslow
* {general}: RoFSW mapping (Figure 3‑3) shows flood extents (1 in 30 and 1 in 100 year) concentrated along watercourses adjacent to Martindale Road, indicating the road and nearby residential areas are at risk of surface water inundation during these events. (p13) [PAG, IMG]

### River Crane
* {general}: Identified as the closest Main River to Ferndale Avenue and therefore the relevant source for fluvial flooding in the area; included in the fluvial flood risk assessment (fluvial risk at Ferndale Avenue assessed as very low). (p12)


## Topics without coordinates

### Thames Water
* {2013, 2019}: TWUL identified blockages to foul and surface water sewers as root causes contributing to flooding at Ferndale Avenue; they investigated and where necessary cleared/resolved these blockages following incidents. (p3) [MUL]
* {21 Dec 2018, 09 Feb 2019, 27 Mar 2019, 10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019, 17 Jul 2019, 30 Jul 2019}: Reported foul water line blockage(s) and silt build-up in the surface water line contributing to surface water flooding; TWUL assets were noted as free-flowing in one report and TWUL reported clearing multiple blockages. Flooding affected garages, footway and carriageway and caused a reported bad stench. (p10) [TAB, MUL]
* {10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019}: Provided reports of foul and surface-water flooding incidents at Ferndale Avenue which were attributed to sewer blockages (not to sewer capacity). TWUL's capacity mapping indicated the area was not at risk of surcharging. (p14)
* {10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019}: TWUL have reported foul and surface-water sewer flood incidents at Ferndale Avenue caused by blockages (rather than capacity issues). Their capacity mapping shows the area is not at risk of surcharging; TWUL also noted potential interaction with groundwater and the highway drainage network. (p15) [IMG, PAG]
* {27 Mar 2019, 10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019}: Blockages in TWUL sewer systems reduced pipe capacity, causing flows to back up and manholes to surcharge; this contributed to flooding at Ferndale Avenue (including the newer development at the end of Ferndale Avenue) when the sewer system was overwhelmed. (p16) [MUL]
* {21 Dec 2018, 09 Feb 2019, 27 May 2019, 17 Jul 2019, 30 Jul 2019, 2020}: TWUL were called out to flooding on Ferndale Avenue on multiple occasions and carried out operational responses: inspected assets (21 Dec 2018 found assets free-flowing), jetted the surface water system (9 Feb 2019), cleared a foul sewer blockage (27 May 2019), inspected manholes (17 Jul 2019), attended 30 Jul 2019 (found no TWUL network problems but an issue with the surface water system to the soakaway), and rectified three foul sewer blockages in 2020. TWUL identified multiple causes including FOGs in the foul sewer and silt build-up in the surface water sewer. (p20) [MUL]
* {21 Dec 2018, 09 Feb 2019, 27 May 2019, 17 Jul 2019, 30 Jul 2019, 2020}: Quant: ten call-outs since 2018; three blockages rectified in 2020 (p20) [MUL]

### Hounslow Highways
* {2013, 2019}: Blocked road gullies and limited surface water drainage (including potential soakaway capacity/condition issues) exacerbated surface water flooding and pooling at Ferndale Avenue. Hounslow Highways raised cleansing reports for gullies, installed a non-return valve on the surface water system to the soakaway, and recommended reviews of soakaway connections, capacity and groundwater monitoring to address the drainage issues. (p3) [MUL]
* {07 Dec 2018, 21 Dec 2018, 10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019}: Footway and carriageway at the eastern end of Ferndale Avenue flooded (including sewage-contaminated water); garages flooded and residents reported access problems to houses and cars; bad odour reported. (p10) [TAB, MUL]
* {17 Jan 2016, 26 Jun 2016, 24 Oct 2017, 07 Dec 2018, 21 Dec 2018, 06 Feb 2019, 10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019, 01 Apr 2021 - 30 Apr 2021}: Managed and cleared blocked highway drainage/gullies associated with flooding incidents at Ferndale Avenue; carried out reactive gully cleansing and site visits during flood events; installed a non-return valve in the surface water system draining to the soakaway in summer 2019 to prevent backflow, after which no further flood incidents were reported to Hounslow. (p19) [MUL]
* {17 Jan 2016, 26 Jun 2016, 24 Oct 2017, 07 Dec 2018, 21 Dec 2018, 06 Feb 2019, 10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019, 01 Apr 2021 - 30 Apr 2021}: Quant: two gullies; one non-return valve; one blocked gully (24 October 2017); one blocked gully reported 6 February 2019 (p19) [MUL]

### Highway Authority
* {17 Jan 2016, 26 Jun 2016, 24 Oct 2017, 07 Dec 2018, 21 December 2018 / 4 January 2019, 06 Feb 2019, summer 2019}: The highway service provider carried out flood‑related actions: reactive gully cleansing in response to reports, site visits during flood incidents, forwarding a foul sewer flooding report to Thames Water, and fitting a non‑return valve in the surface water system draining to the soakaway (summer 2019) to prevent backflow. These works were carried out in response to the recurring flood incidents at Ferndale Avenue; no further flood reports at Ferndale Avenue have been received since the valve installation. (p19) [MUL]
* {17 Jan 2016, 26 Jun 2016, 24 Oct 2017, 07 Dec 2018, 21 December 2018 / 4 January 2019, 06 Feb 2019, summer 2019}: Quant: two gullies and their connections cleared (26 June 2016); one blocked gully cleared (24 October 2017); one blocked gully reported (6 February 2019); one job raised for gully cleansing (21 December 2018 for 4 January 2019); one foul sewer flooding report forwarded to TWUL (17 January 2016); one non‑return valve installed (summer 2019) (p19) [MUL]

### Hounslow Council
* {07 Dec 2018, 21 Dec 2018, 14 Mar 2019, 10 Jun 2019 - 13 Jun 2019}: Received reports of flooding at the eastern end of Ferndale Avenue: footway and carriageway flooded with sewage-contaminated water and garages flooded; residents reported difficulty accessing houses and cars and a bad sewage odour. (p10) [TAB, MUL]
