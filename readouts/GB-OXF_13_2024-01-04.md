

# Nuneham Courtenay flooding 04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Flood Investigation Report: Nuneham Courtenay, South Oxfordshire, Oxfordshire<br>095\_Oxfordshire County/S19-Flood-Investigation-Nuneham-Courtenay-June-2024.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Nuneham Courtenay
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: The settlement experienced flooding (inundation) during the reported events. (p1) [PAG, MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Exceptionally heavy rainfall (04–05 Jan 2024 and 18 Feb 2024) caused overland flow from fields to the west to overwhelm the village surface water drainage system, causing surcharging at several locations. Surface water also overloaded foul sewers with reports of foul (sewer) flooding. The event was one of the worst in recent times (worst since 2006). (p2) [TAB, MUL]
* Surface water flows affected parts of the village. Areas to the north‑west and east of Nuneham Courtenay lie within a surface-water flow path which, during the 04/01/2024–05/01/2024 incident, allowed flow through the village. (p5) [IMG]
* {2003, 2004, 2006}: Flooding affected property within the village. (p7) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* {2003, 2004, 11 Oct 2006}: Rapid, unrestricted overland flow entered the village from the west during intense storm conditions (October 2006) when a blocked strategic highway culvert and saturated farmland combined with heavy rainfall. This caused flooding of the lower part of the village and flooding of residential properties; earlier events in 2003 and 2004 also affected property in the village. (p8) [PAG, MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Internal flooding of ground-floor areas of properties from surcharging drainage and overland flows from fields to the west; surface water entering foul drainage and overwhelming pump station capacity; gardens flooded by surface water. (p9) [PAG, MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Overland flows from fields to the west flowed onto the road leading to Nuneham Estate and into the village; a gully on that road discharged to the Victorian Culvert contributing to flood flow into the settlement. The village experienced flooding on 04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024 and again on 18 Feb 2024. On 18 Feb 2024, Oxfordshire Fire and Rescue conducted focused pumping at the north end of the village which helped to alleviate the flooding. (p10) [PAG, MUL]
* The local watercourse and drainage system conveyed high runoff into the village; the pond acted as the only natural storage and was brought into use when inflows exceeded the discharge capacity of the 300 mm outfall pipe. Downstream the culvert system bifurcates and conveys flows via a 600 mm stone culvert through the rear gardens of properties (Nos.29-40) and to the rear of No.29, with sections of the culvert running beneath or immediately adjacent to cottages. (p11) [PAG]
* The village was the subject of the Section 19 investigation for the flood event of 04–05 Jan 2024. Flooding involved the local highway drainage and the Victorian culvert network, prompting a hydraulic assessment and CCTV survey to check culvert condition and potential flow restrictions. (p12) [PAG]
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Overland flow from fields to the west overwhelmed the village surface water systems, causing surcharging at multiple locations and flooding of properties; properties with floor levels below ground level were particularly affected. (p14) [PAG, MUL]
* Observed flow paths during the event routed water from higher ground toward the main watercourses and across the village main street, indicating surface-water inundation of streets and low-lying areas where flows converged toward the watercourses. (p25) [IMG, PAG]
* CCTV survey of the local drainage network recorded multiple sections of pipe and adjacent ditches as 'under water' or 'ditch full', and survey progress was abandoned at several manholes due to submerged or blocked conditions. These observations indicate local inundation of drains/ditches within the village and blockages impairing conveyance. (p26) [IMG]
* {2003, 2004, 11 Oct 2006}: Quant: 20 houses (p8) [PAG, MUL]
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Quant: 10 properties; 2 properties (p9) [PAG, MUL]
* Quant: 42 hectares (catchment area); 225 mm culvert; 300 mm outfall pipe; 600 mm stone culvert; 12 properties (Nos.29-40) (p11) [PAG]
* Quant: 8 survey points (S1, S1A, S1B, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6) noted on the map where flow conditions or access problems were recorded (p26) [IMG]

### Oxfordshire
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: As Lead Local Flood Authority/highway authority, OCC was involved in response; its highway drainage assets in the area were overwhelmed and require increased maintenance/cleansing per the Highway Maintenance Programme. (p2) [TAB, MUL]

### South Oxfordshire
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Sections of ordinary watercourse and culverts were identified as requiring de-silting; SODC (as the land drainage authority) may use powers to ensure riparian owners carry out maintenance to reduce surface water runoff that contributed to flooding. (p2) [TAB, MUL]


## Topics without coordinates

### Victorian Culvert
* Surveyed following the January event; generally found in good condition but with sections requiring cleansing. Cleansing and removal of silt/obstructions are identified as riparian landowner responsibilities to reduce future risk. (p2)
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: The Victorian culvert east of the A4074 began to surcharge, causing surcharging of rainwater downpipes, surface water manholes and foul sewers and contributing to internal property flooding. (p9) [PAG, MUL]
* {18 Feb 2024}: A road gully discharged overland flows from the road (originating in fields to the west) into the Victorian Culvert during the observed flood on 18 Feb 2024; the culvert formed part of the flow path into Nuneham Courtenay. (p10)
* The Victorian brick-roofed stone culvert (600 mm diameter route through rear gardens) conveys drainage through the village. A CCTV survey (commissioned following the flood) found loose debris and rootmass ingress that prevented full camera runs at multiple inspection chambers and could restrict conveyance during the 04–05 Jan 2024 flood; the culvert bifurcates and outfalls at the southern end of the village. (p12) [TAB, PAG]
* The older Victorian culvert at the surveyed location contained a number of obstructions and had minimal flow during inspection. Camera surveys could not be completed in sections due to obstructions (brick/debris) and root ingress; root mass approximately 0.5 m downstream of S1 prevented a camera survey though some flow continued to pass. These obstructions reduce culvert conveyance and may impair discharge during high flows; removal of roots and further inspection was recommended. (p13)
* Water was building up where overland flow entered the culvert crossing the A4074; rootmass within the Victorian Culvert was identified as obstructing flows and should be removed to aid discharge. (p14)
* Quant: 0.5 m (p13)

### Church Road
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Overland flows from fields flowed to the south of Church Road, contributing to surface water entering the village drainage network. (p9) [MUL]

### South Oxfordshire District Council
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Surface water runoff contributed to flooding in the village; SODC is identified to assist in informing riparian owners of maintenance duties and to use powers under the Land Drainage Act 1991 to ensure watercourses and culverts are cleared of silt/obstructions following the event. (p2) [TAB, MUL]
* South Oxfordshire District Council is to assist in informing riparian landowners of land drainage duties, may use powers under the Land Drainage Act 1991 to ensure riparian maintenance, and is engaged in promoting property flood resilience and remedial actions following flood impacts. (p3) [PAG]
* The overflow system installed by SODC was surcharged for much of its length during inspection. Sections contained silt deposits reducing capacity, and the outflow culverts at the end of the open ditch were not visible. Flow could be seen entering the ordinary watercourse and build-up occurred where this entered the culvert crossing the A4074; jetting and inspection were recommended to restore discharge. (p13)
* South Oxfordshire District Council engaged in post‑flood response and enforcement actions: to inform riparian landowners of their duties and to use powers under the Land Drainage Act 1991 to ensure required maintenance to reduce future flooding. (p20) [TAB]

### Thames Water
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Foul sewer network serving the village was overloaded by surface water during the events, with reports of foul flooding; Thames Water are recommended to check for surface water connections, blockages and capacity issues and carry out remedial works. (p2) [TAB, MUL]
* Local Thames Water pump station became inundated with surface water entering the foul drainage system; although pumps remained operational they did not have capacity to cope with the additional flows, contributing to surcharging of foul sewers during the event. (p9)
* Surface water during the event overloaded foul sewers serving the village and inundated the Thames Water pump station in Nuneham Courtenay. (p14)

### Oxfordshire County Council
* Highway drainage assets were overwhelmed during the event, prompting recommendations to review and increase the frequency of highway drainage maintenance and cleansing under the Highway Maintenance Programme to reduce future surface water flooding risk. (p2) [TAB]
* OCC Highways are to review frequency of highway drainage maintenance and increase cleansing where required because highway drainage assets were implicated in the surface water flooding; they are a lead stakeholder for highway drainage actions. (p3) [PAG]

### A4074
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Surface water flows from the road entered the surface water drainage at the A4074 junction; the drainage system and culvert were surcharging and capacity was exceeded, contributing to flooding in adjacent properties. (p9) [MUL]
* Water was building up where the ordinary watercourse enters and crosses the culvert beneath the A4074. The build-up indicated restricted discharge through the culvert; jetting and inspection of the culvert were recommended to ensure flows could discharge freely. (p13)

### OCC Highways
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: High intensity rainfall and overland flow overwhelmed highway drainage running through the village, causing surcharging in multiple locations and indicating a need to increase cleansing/maintenance frequency of highway drainage assets. (p2) [TAB, MUL]

### Highway Authority
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Surface water runoff and overland flow overwhelmed highway surface water drainage through the village, causing surcharging at multiple locations and prompting recommendations to increase cleansing frequency and review highway drainage maintenance. (p2) [TAB, MUL]

### Environment Agency
* EA is identified as a stakeholder in recovery and resilience actions (e.g. property flood resilience coordination/support) following the surface water flood incident affecting the parish. (p3)

### Oxfordshire Fire and Rescue Service
* {04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024, 18 Feb 2024}: Attended the village on the night of 4 January 2024 (arrived ~23:00) deploying pumps to remove floodwater; pumps had limited success. Responded again on 18 February 2024 and focussed pumping at the north end of the village. (p9) [MUL]

### Nuneham Estate
* {18 Feb 2024}: Runoff from the fields was observed travelling down the road servicing Nuneham Estate toward Nuneham Courtenay, indicating overland flows affected the estate access/adjacent land. (p14)

### Highways
* Blocked/insufficient highway drainage contributed to surface water flooding in the village; report recommends increasing frequency of cleansing and reviewing maintenance of highway drainage assets. (p20) [TAB]
