## Place names with coordinates

### Yelvertoft
* Internal flooding of private property: excess water flowed over ground to low points around private property when local drainage and storage could not convey runoff. (p7)
* Internal flooding entered at least one property in the village; this triggered a formal Section 19 investigation. Residents reported the 9 March 2016 event and previous internal flooding incidents. (p9) [MUL]
* {general}: Large areas in the centre of the village are shown to lie within Flood Zone 3 (i.e. could be flooded from a river by an event with a 1PCSYM (1 in 100) or greater annual chance). Multiple streets/areas (Ashwells Lane, High Street, Bridgend, Swinnertons Lane, Crick Road, Elkington Road and parts of Wards Lane estate) are identified as being in areas at high risk of surface water flooding. Two main watercourses (Clay Coton Brook and Yelvertoft Brook) flow through/around the village; Yelvertoft Brook has historic culverting and was previously diverted into a small flood storage area (features relevant to local flooding behaviour). (p10)
* {Easter 1998, 06 Nov 2000, 10 Jan 2007, 09 Mar 2016}: Multiple recorded flood incidents in the Yelvertoft area. A historic overspill feature is noted for the culverted section. Easter 1998: 26 properties recorded affected. 06/11/2000: 1 property flooded on School Lane. 10/01/2007: Crick Road reported almost impassable; 1 property had nearly 2 ft of water and required pumping by the fire service; 5 properties reported water in gardens. 09/03/2016: approximately 15 properties flooded after excessive rainfall caused the FSA to fill and overtop; excess could not pass into an undersized culvert, resulting in nearby properties being affected. (p11) [PAG, MUL]
* The village experienced flooding on 9 March 2016 due to exceedance of the Yelvertoft Brook flood storage area and culverts. Yelvertoft Brook reached the top of its banks, overtopped and flowed along highways (Crick Road, High Street), and drainage/culvert capacity was exceeded causing overland flow to low points and flooding of properties (including internal flooding reported on some High Street and Crick Road properties). (p12) [TAB, PAG]
* Heavy rainfall on a saturated catchment overwhelmed local drainage. Drainage systems and culverts (including a culverted section of Yelvertoft Brook alongside Swinnertions Lane) were unable to convey and dispose of rainwater; culvert capacity and the flood storage area were exceeded and water flowed over ground following natural contours to low points around private properties. Affected properties are situated at localised low points and were vulnerable to overland flow of surface water running off the highway. (p13) [PAG]
* Flood incidents occurred in Yelvertoft and were subject to investigation by the LLFA (NCC); NCC can suggest possible causes and make recommendations to mitigate flood risk but lacks the mandate/funding to force other authorities to act. (p15)
* {general}: Identified as an area where regular highway drainage cleansing (including carriageway channels) is required to manage surface water flood risk to highways and properties; asset planning and cleansing are recommended to reduce that risk. (p19)
* Subject of a Northamptonshire County Council flood incident investigation (Incident Date: 9 March 2016). The map in the report locates the village within the Upper Avon catchment and shows nearby main watercourses that are relevant to the investigated flood event. (p27) [IMG]
* Flood Zone 3 is concentrated along the river within the village, with Flood Zone 2 extending further; this places residential properties, the primary school and several farms adjacent to the watercourse at risk of being flooded in extreme river flood events. (p29) [IMG]
* The investigation and map show surface-water flood risk concentrated along the central watercourse through Yelvertoft. Flood-risk shading overlaps built-up areas in the village centre and north‑east, placing residential properties, several farms, the primary school and the sewage works within or adjacent to flood‑risk zones and indicating they were at risk or impacted during the flood incident. ### Northamptonshire
* Widespread flooding across the county from a combination of agricultural runoff, surface water runoff and ordinary watercourse flooding; numerous properties flooded internally. (p7)
* On 9 March 2016 heavy rainfall across the county produced widespread flooding from a combination of agricultural runoff, surface water runoff and ordinary watercourse flooding, resulting in numerous internal flood incidents and other reports of flooding. (p9)
* {general}: The county contains recorded flooding 'hotspots' and is the area over which the LLFA (NCC) must keep a register of assets affecting flooding from surface runoff, groundwater and ordinary watercourses. (p15)
* {general}: As Lead Local Flood Authority (LLFA) responsible for coordinating community-level response and investigation: asked to prepare Community Flood Risk Reports, work with Emergency Planning Team and Environment Agency, support Community Flood Groups and inform drainage owners of legal responsibilities because communities in the county are at risk from surface water flooding. (p19) [PAG]

## Topics without coordinates

### River Avon Catchment
* Saturated catchment generated heavy runoff that caused flooding at Yelvertoft and elsewhere in the county. (p7)
* {general}: Two local watercourses (Clay Coton Brook and Yelvertoft Brook) drain into the Upper Avon catchment; the catchment is therefore the receptor for flows that place central Yelvertoft in Flood Zone 3 and at risk of river flooding. (p10)
* The catchment is identified on the flood map as the drainage area for the main watercourses connected to the Yelvertoft flood incident; it is the spatial context used to identify contributing watercourses for the event on 9 March 2016. (p27) [IMG]
* Quant: over 70 properties internally flooded; over 200 reports of flooding (p7)

### Clay Coton Brook
* {general}: Main River flowing to the east of Yelvertoft that drains into the River Avon; it is part of the local river network that can contribute to river flooding affecting Yelvertoft. (p10)
* Banks were overtopped at the confluence with Yelvertoft Brook, resulting in floodwater reaching driveways and gardens of nearby properties. (p12) [TAB, PAG]
* High water levels in Clay Coton Brook covered culvert outlets, preventing discharge from local drainage/culverts. This contributed to the inability of culverts to convey water and led to upstream surface water and overland flows that flooded properties in Yelvertoft. (p13)

### Flood Storage
* {general}: A small Flood Storage Area received a historic diversion of Yelvertoft Brook (pre-2005) and functions as a local flood storage feature for flows from the brook. (p10)
* The Flood Storage Area (FSA) filled to capacity and overtopped on 09/03/2016; the overtopping and the inability of an undersized culvert to pass the excess flow resulted in nearby properties being flooded. (p11) [TAB]
* Quant: Approximately 15 properties (p11) [TAB]

### Severn Trent
* {general}: Sewer and combined sewer systems can become overwhelmed in extreme weather and result in sewer flooding, creating a risk of properties being flooded. STW is responsible for managing risks from sewers and has duties (including adoption of some private sewers since 1 July 2011). (p15) [PAG]
* {general}: Severn Trent is required to assess the capacity of its sewerage assets and sources of water entering the public sewerage system because insufficient capacity can lead to flooding of properties; asked to develop a detailed plan of assets to share with the LLFA and community. (p19)

### Yelvertoft Brook
* {general}: Classed as a Main River through the village; extensively modified (historically culverted along the southern edge of Swinnertons Lane with ~180m culverted, then resurfacing, then ~40m culverted) and was diverted along the north edge of Crick Road into a small Flood Storage Area prior to its designation as a Main River in 2005. Culverting, diversion and recent removal of a brook channel through land associated with Elkins Close may impede overground flows and are directly relevant to local flood behaviour and risk. (p10)
* The brook reached the top of its banks and overtopped, sending water along Crick Road and High Street and contributing to flooding at the confluence with Clay Coton Brook; banks being overtopped resulted in floodwater on driveways and gardens. (p12) [TAB, MUL]

### Northamptonshire Fire and Rescue Service
* {10 Jan 2007}: The fire service pumped a property that had nearly 2 ft of internal flooding and attended flood reports; they reported road conditions during the incident. (p11) [TAB]

### Crick Road Flood Storage
* The flood storage area was almost full and its capacity was exceeded during the event, contributing to overtopping and subsequent flooding in the village when it could not contain the flow. (p12) [TAB, MUL]
* Designated flood storage areas adjacent to Crick Road (two green‑filled storage areas shown) were used to hold excess water from the local watercourse during the flood incident; they are highlighted as the flood mitigation/holding locations for the 9 March 2016 event. (p35) [IMG]

### Daventry District Council
* {general}: Required to undertake regular highway drainage cleansing throughout Yelvertoft (including the carriageway channel) and to identify and develop a detailed plan of their assets to share with the LLFA and the community to reduce surface water flood risk to highways and properties. (p19)

### Yelvertoft Brook Flood Storage
* Flood storage area (and culverted/below-ground drainage) were unable to collect and convey rainwater effectively, contributing to excess water flowing to low points and causing internal flooding of private property. (p7)

### River Warwickshire Avon
* {general}: River identified as the source for Flood Zone 3 exposure in the centre of the village; large central areas of Yelvertoft are shown in Flood Zone 3 and 'could be flooded from a river' (1PCSYM / 1 in 100 annual chance). (p10) [PAG]

### Highway Authority
* {general}: Highway drainage assets with insufficient capacity may lead to runoff not being drained from the highway, creating surface water flooding that poses flood risk to properties; where this occurs improvement works should be considered. (p19)
