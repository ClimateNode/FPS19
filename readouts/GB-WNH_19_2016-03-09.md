

# Moreton Pinkney flooding 09 Mar 2016

## Place names with coordinates

### Moreton Pinkney
* Site of the flood incident on 9 March 2016 where surface water and watercourse runoff led to internal flooding of at least one property. (p7)
* A flood incident occurred in the village (Banbury Lane) causing internal flooding of at least one property on 9 March 2016; the village has experienced repeat internal flooding of that property within the last five years. (p9) [PAG, MUL]
* The village experienced surface water and ordinary watercourse (fluvial) flooding that led to internal flooding of properties; areas either side of the watercourse through the village are shown as Flood Zone 3 (functional floodplain). (p10) [PAG]
* {general}: Much of the village centre is mapped as at low risk of surface water flooding, but a band of land either side of the south–north watercourse through the village is mapped at high risk of surface water flooding and fluvial (river) flooding (Flood Zone 3 — the functional flood plain). (p11) [PAG]
* Heavy rainfall on a saturated catchment caused local watercourses and drainage to be unable to convey flows. Culverts under Banbury Road had insufficient capacity causing upstream water to overtop and pond on Banbury Road (approx. 50 m west and 30 m east). Floodwater surrounded and entered a property on Banbury Road (entered front door, came up through the floor) producing internal flooding and mud throughout; external partially enclosed areas of the same property were filled to depths up to 600 mm. Five cars became stuck on Banbury Road and were written off. Surface water and agricultural run-off contributed to carriageway flooding. At the south/north watercourse at Brook Street flows rose, overtopped and extended up Brook Street, affecting surrounding land and gardens; gardens of three properties on Brook Street were affected. Floodwater subsequently receded over a few hours leaving mud and requiring professional cleaning. (p14) [PAG]
* Heavy rainfall on a saturated catchment caused drainage systems and local watercourses to be unable to convey rainwater to larger watercourses north of the village. This led to watercourses overtopping and overland flow following natural contours to low points around private properties, resulting in internal flooding. (p15) [PAG]
* The watercourse immediately north of Brook Street has variable width/depth and obstructions (footbridges, land slips, fallen trees and vegetation) that slowed conveyance, leading to channel deepening and causing flooding that affected the village and put residents at risk. (p16) [PAG]
* The village experienced flooding which prompted a Section 19 investigation by the Lead Local Flood Authority (NCC); NCC can suggest causes and make recommendations to mitigate flood risk in the village. (p18)
* A flood incident is annotated near the eastern edge of the village; the map shows the catchment boundary that contributed to that flood incident and encloses the majority of the village. (p32) [IMG]
* Schematic areas of flooding are shown within and near Moreton Pinkney associated with overland surface water flow converging on the main watercourse. Key drainage chokepoints identified include a culvert under Banbury Road (Q2/A) and a ford at Brook Street (Q3/A), with main routes of overland flow directed towards the village watercourse during the flood incident. (p33) [IMG, TAB]
* Quant: one property (p7)

### Banbury Road
* {1947, 1960's, 1997, 26 Apr 1999, 20 Jul 2007, 01 Jul 2011 - 31 Jul 2011, 09 Mar 2016}: Internal flooding of property on Banbury Road due to overtopping of the adjacent watercourse onto Banbury Road. (p12) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* Culverts under Banbury Road had insufficient capacity; upstream watercourse depth increased and spilled onto Banbury Road causing ponding extending approximately 50 metres west and 30 metres east. Floodwater surrounded and entered a property on Banbury Road (entered front door and came up through the floor), leaving internal flooding (ground floor ~100mm, deeper in some sunken rooms) and external areas up to 600mm depth. Floodwater receded over a few hours leaving mud throughout; professional cleaners were employed. Surface water and agricultural run-off flowed north within the carriageway and likely contributed. Five cars became stuck in the flood water on Banbury Road and were written off. (p14)
* Culverts beneath Banbury Road were identified as a restriction on the north/south watercourse flow; this likely contributed to the watercourse overtopping onto the road and to internal flooding of a property. (p15)
* Banbury Road is shown crossed by an identified culvert (Q2/A) and by mapped overland flow routes; the incident plan indicates surface-water/overland flooding affected Banbury Road during the 9 March 2016 event, with flood flow routes crossing the carriageway near the culvert location. (p33) [IMG]
* Road was inundated by floodwater; the incident was documented in photographs taken by the local Parish Council. (p54) [PAG]
* Floodwater covered the entire width of the road, rendering it impassable for vehicles and pedestrians and causing local transport disruption; people and a vehicle were observed at the far end assessing or held up by the flooding. (p55) [IMG, PAG]
* Road overtopped by floodwater flowing from adjacent fields across the carriageway; rendered impassable and threatening nearby houses and roadside infrastructure. (p56) [IMG, PAG]
* Floodwater entered the property, inundating exterior areas (outbuilding/garage) and multiple interior rooms including a living area and kitchen, causing substantial water intrusion and likely property damage and disruption to the household. (p57) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Interior floodwater covered the entire visible floor area and partially submerged furniture and other items, causing interior damage and the need for remediation. (p58) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Roadside ditch and verge-grip drains along Banbury Road were observed conveying and holding runoff; the ditch is partially overgrown which reduces conveyance and leads to localised pooling beside the unpaved road and verge, increasing risk of road surface runoff and erosion. (p79) [IMG, PAG]

### Brook Street, Moreton Pinkney
* {general}: The carriageway at the ford on Brook Street drains directly to the south–north watercourse and there are no recorded road gullies on Brook Street; proximity to the watercourse (which is within a band shown at high risk of surface water and fluvial flooding) and variable channel geometry upstream can increase flood interaction with the street and its drainage. (p11)
* {20 Jul 2007, 21 Nov 2012, 07 Jan 2014, 09 Mar 2016, 15 Jun 2016}: Flooding of Brook Street, surrounding land and gardens originating from the ford; ponding extended eastwards up Brook Street from the ford. (p12) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* The south/north watercourse rose, flow velocity increased and the watercourse overtopped, sending floodwater up Brook Street to the east and affecting surrounding land and gardens. (p14)
* The watercourse immediately north of Brook Street had variable width/depth and restrictions (footbridges, land slips, fallen trees/vegetation) that could slow conveyance, deepen the channel and cause flooding. (p15)
* Brook Street is shown adjacent to the ford (Q3/A) and mapped overland flow routes; the incident plan indicates surface-water/overland flooding affected Brook Street during the 9 March 2016 event, particularly at the ford crossing. (p33) [IMG]
