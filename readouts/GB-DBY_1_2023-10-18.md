

# Derbyshire flooding 18 Oct 2023 - 21 Oct 2023

Reference document:<br>Derbyshire County Council Section 19 Investigation Report – Storm Babet 18/10/23 – 21/10/23<br>034\_Derbyshire County/034\_2023\_Derbyshire County/section-1-introduction-final-storm-babet-dcc-section-19-report.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Derbyshire
* Between 18 and 21 October 2023 Derbyshire experienced extensive property flooding. Watercourse overtopping was the main cause of property flooding at most communities; surface water flooding was the main cause at some communities and a contributing cause at many; some sewer network flooding occurred where pumping stations were overwhelmed by other sources of flooding. The worst affected communities included Chesterfield, Sandiacre and Long Eaton, with higher concentrations of flooded properties in the Don and Rother, Derwent Lower, and Erewash catchments (east of the county). (p3) [PAG]
* Widespread flooding across the county during Storm Babet. A total of 1,647 properties were internally flooded across all eight districts and boroughs. Infrastructure damage recorded included bridge scouring, damage to an electricity substation, and a footbridge washed away. Several roads became impassable causing local traffic disruption. The borough of Chesterfield (within Derbyshire) was especially badly affected, with one resident killed and a large number of properties flooded. (p5) [PAG]
* Widespread internal flooding of residential properties across the county; communities investigated where five or more residential properties were internally flooded. (p6) [PAG]
* Quant: 1,647 properties internally flooded; 1,251 residential; 396 non-residential; 35 communities investigated encompassing 1,284 of the internally flooded properties (p3) [PAG]
* Quant: 1,647 properties; 1 fatality; more than 400 properties (Chesterfield) (p5) [PAG]
* Quant: 1,647 internally flooded residential properties; 35 communities identified containing five or more flooded properties; 1,284 of the flooded properties are within the investigated communities (p6) [PAG]

### UK
* Storm Babet (18–21 Oct 2023) produced extreme rainfall across the UK, causing widespread river, surface water and drainage-network (sewer) flooding in multiple regions. (p5)

### Yorkshire
* Significant flooding was observed in parts of Yorkshire during Storm Babet, driven by exceptional rainfall and elevated watercourses. (p5)

### Nottinghamshire
* Nottinghamshire experienced significant flooding associated with Storm Babet as intense rainfall fell on already saturated ground, causing river, surface-water and drainage-related flooding in parts of the county. (p5)

### Staffordshire
* Parts of Staffordshire experienced significant flooding during Storm Babet due to exceptional rainfall on saturated ground, producing river and surface-water flooding impacts. (p5)

### Suffolk
* Suffolk was among the areas reported to have significant flooding associated with Storm Babet between 18–21 October 2023. (p5)

### England
* Significant flooding occurred across multiple counties in England during 18–21 Oct 2023, driven by exceptional rainfall on already saturated ground and manifesting as river, surface water and drainage-network flooding. (p5)

### Chesterfield
* Community was among the worst-impacted in Derbyshire during Storm Babet with internal property flooding reported. Flooding was associated with watercourse overtopping (the main cause at most communities) with surface-water flooding a contributing or primary cause in some areas. (p3)
* During 18–21 Oct 2023 Chesterfield experienced severe flooding impacts: one resident died and more than 400 properties were internally flooded. (p5)
* Quant: 1 fatality; more than 400 properties (p5)

### Sandiacre
* Named as one of the worst-impacted communities in Derbyshire during Storm Babet, experiencing internal property flooding. Flooding drivers noted in the report include watercourse overtopping and surface-water flooding (either as a main or contributing cause). (p3)

### Long Eaton
* Identified among the worst-impacted communities in Derbyshire with internal property flooding during Storm Babet. The report attributes most community flooding to watercourse overtopping, with surface-water flooding either primary at some communities or a contributing factor at others. (p3)

### Ilkeston
* Ilkeston is cited as an area with higher population density within eastern Derbyshire where higher concentrations of internally flooded properties occurred during the Storm Babet event, linked to watercourse overtopping and surface water flooding (with some sewer-related incidents where infrastructure was overwhelmed). (p3)

### River Rother
* The Rother catchment was reported to have experienced higher concentrations of internally flooded properties than catchments to the west of the county, associated with the Storm Babet rainfall and resulting watercourse overtopping. (p3)

### River Erewash
* The Erewash catchment experienced higher concentrations of internally flooded properties during the Storm Babet event, driven by higher magnitude rainfall in the east of the county and local population density; watercourse overtopping and surface water were significant causes, with some sewer-related flooding where pumping stations were overwhelmed. (p3)


## Topics without coordinates

### Midlands
* The Midlands experienced particularly severe rainfall—the wettest independent three‑day period on record for the region—which led to widespread and significant flooding during the period of 18–21 October 2023. (p5)

### Derbyshire County Council
* Significant flooding occurred across the council's administrative area due to extreme rainfall associated with Storm Babet, affecting communities within Derbyshire. (p4)
* The council recorded county-wide flood impacts from 18–21 Oct 2023, including 1,647 properties internally flooded across all eight districts and infrastructure damage such as bridge scouring, damage to an electricity substation, and a footbridge washed away; multiple roads were made impassable. (p5) [PAG]
* Quant: 1,647 properties; 1 footbridge (p5) [PAG]

### Environment Agency
* Acted as a Risk Management Authority working with Derbyshire County Council, district/borough councils, water and sewerage companies, emergency services and community organisations to respond to flooding caused by Storm Babet (18–21 Oct 2023). Participated in flood investigations, recorded technical evidence and flood incident data, supported management and maintenance works, and assisted incident response where rivers, surface water and drainage networks overtopped or overwhelmed local defences. (p5)
* Quant: 1,647 properties internally flooded across Derbyshire; more than 400 properties internally flooded in Chesterfield; one resident lost their life; local infrastructure damage reported (e.g., washed-away footbridge) (p5)

### River Don
* The Don catchment experienced a higher concentration of internally flooded properties compared to catchments to the west of the county, driven by higher magnitude rainfall in the east and local population density. Flooding links to watercourse overtopping are noted in the report. (p3)

### Derwent Catchment
* The Derwent Lower catchment experienced higher concentrations of internally flooded properties relative to western catchments during Storm Babet, linked to elevated rainfall in the east and resulting watercourse overtopping. (p3)
