

# Northampton flooding 05 Aug 2013

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Bedford Road, Northampton, 5th August 2013<br>090\_West Northamptonshire/Bedford Road, Northampton - August 2013.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Bedford Road, Northampton
* Intense short‑duration rainfall caused existing drainage to back up and overtop at ground level, resulting in surface water flooding of Bedford Road; the flood affected Bedford Road, a critical arterial route for Northampton. (p6) [PAG]
* Intense short-duration rainfall overwhelmed local drainage; drainage systems backed up and overtopped at ground level, causing flooding of the carriageway on Bedford Road, disrupting this critical arterial route and prompting a formal Section 19 investigation. (p7) [PAG]
* The A428 Bedford Road was temporarily closed to traffic due to the depth of flood water and manhole covers being lifted; the closure affected a critical arterial route connecting Northampton town centre to the A45. (p9) [PAG]
* Standing water ponded in the carriageway (reaching a depth higher than the kerb lines so verges and footways were flooded), manhole covers were lifted by excessive volumes in the sewerage system, and the road was closed to through traffic causing delays, congestion and traffic diversions. (p12)
* Bedford Road was flooded by intense short-duration rainfall; existing drainage systems were unable to cope, became surcharged and ejected water back to the surface, causing surface water to flow into areas and drainage systems that could not manage the volume. (p17)
* Carriageway and road gullies on Bedford Road were liable to overflow during extreme rainfall, contributing to surface water flooding and increasing flood risk to lower-lying ground and properties; recommendation to allow controlled overflow routes to lower ground (e.g. grip drains, lowered verges). (p19)
* During the extreme rainfall on 5th August 2013 the carriageway and road gullies on Bedford Road overtopped, causing surface water to flow across the road. Measures were recommended to allow overflow of road gullies to lower ground (e.g. grip drains, lowering verges/footways) to reduce repeat overtopping. (p20) [PAG]
* Flooding occurred at the junction of Bedford Road and Cliftonville Road; Main River Nene overflowed into the adjacent flood channel south of Bedford Road. Flood pressures caused manhole covers to be lifted at the junction. (p44) [IMG, PAG]
* Flooding occurred at the junction of Bedford Road and Cliftonville Road; manhole covers were lifted (surcharging) and the junction was inundated. (p45)
* Flooding occurred at the west end of Bedford Road and at the junction with Cliftonville Road; surface water outfall to the River Nene is located at the west end south of the car park. Manhole covers were lifted, indicating sewer surcharge and local surface-water inundation. Low spots in the vertical alignment of Bedford Road contributed to ponding. (p46) [IMG, PAG]

### Northamptonshire
* Intense, short-duration rainfall overwhelmed existing drainage on Bedford Road, Northampton. Water flowed onto the road and into local drainage systems that were unable to manage the volume, causing flooding of the highway and surrounding drainage assets. (p16)

### Northampton
* Flooding from intense rainfall caused drainage systems to back up and overtop at ground level on Bedford Road, which is a critical arterial route for the town. (p6) [PAG]
* The flood incident on Bedford Road within Northampton, caused by intense short-duration rainfall and overwhelmed drainage, affected a key arterial route in the town and led to a formal investigation. (p7) [PAG]
* A primary arterial route within Northampton (Bedford Road) was temporarily closed due to flood water depth and lifted manhole covers, disrupting traffic circulation in the town. (p9)
* Intense short-duration rainfall across Northampton caused existing drainage systems to be unable to cope; systems were surcharged and ejected water to the surface. High levels in the receiving watercourse may have restricted discharge rates from drainage systems, exacerbating urban/surface water flooding. (p17)

### Cliftonville Road, Northampton
* Surface water inundation affected Cliftonville Road and the adjacent verge/footpath area; nearby lower-lying land adjacent to the skate park formed part of the flow pathway toward Bedford Road. (p45) [IMG]
* Flooding at the junction with Bedford Road with lifted manhole covers reported; photograph views from Cliftonville Road show the area affected by surface-water/ponding and sewer surcharge at the junction. (p46) [PAG]

### River Nene
* The River Nene sits within Flood Zone 3 adjacent to Bedford Road; the flood hazard associated with the river area encompassed Bedford Road on 5th August 2013, during which the A428 Bedford Road was temporarily closed because of the depth of flood water and manhole covers being lifted. (p9)
* Elevated water levels in the River Nene in the vicinity of the incident during the 5th August 2013 event may have prevented discharge from drainage systems and contributed to local flooding impacts. (p20)
* A surface water outfall discharging into the River Nene was heavily clogged with debris (metal grate and organic/plastic material), partially blocking flow. The blockage would have impeded drainage into the Nene and increased the risk of upstream surface-water flooding during the flood investigation (documented following the 5 August 2013 event). (p43) [IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Highway Authority
* Recorded and responded to multiple flood-related emergency incidents on Bedford Road. Incidents included manhole covers being dislodged and standing/ deep water on the carriageway, resulting in the road being closed to through traffic. Wardens attended with police, arranged for a Conways 3in1 unit to attend and later re-opened the road. The carriageway flooding and lifted manhole covers caused delays, congestion and required traffic diversions. (p11) [TAB, MUL]

### Northamptonshire County Council
* Highways drainage and other local authority assets were implicated in surface water runoff and insufficient capacity from the highway drainage contributed to flood risk to properties; actions instructed include cleansing, CCTV/dye surveys, capacity assessment and potential carriageway/ditch work to reduce overflow to properties. (p19) [PAG]

### Anglian Water
* Following the 5th August 2013 event Anglian Water Services was asked to assess sources of water entering the public sewerage system and the capacity of their assets because capacity limitations and inflows into the sewer network contributed to flood risk to properties; they were asked to consider works to reduce the risk of sewer water overtopping onto Bedford Road. (p20)

### Environment Agency
* The Environment Agency was asked to review monitoring of water levels in the River Nene in the vicinity of the incident because elevated river levels during the 5th August 2013 event could have prohibited discharges from drainage systems, contributing to surface water flooding locally. The EA was also asked to review river/flood-channel configuration and hydraulic controls to reduce likelihood of recurrence. (p20)

### The Bridge
* Flood channel flow from the Main River Nene passed beneath the concrete bridge south of Bedford Road as the river overflowed into the flood channel during the event. (p44) [IMG]
