

# Dorset flooding 06 Jul 2012 - 13 Jul 2012

Reference document:<br>Dorset County Council July 2012 Flood Investigation Report<br>037\_Dorset/037\_2020\_Dorset/July 2012 Flood Investigation Report.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Dorset
* Many parts of Dorset experienced the worst flooding for over ten years during the event (6–13 July 2012). Intense torrential rain caused flash flooding in rapid-response catchments, larger catchments were affected as floodwaters passed through rivers, and rapid rises in groundwater levels caused groundwater flooding to properties and roads. The rainfall triggered landslips (Eype, Lyme Regis, Beaminster Tunnel) and led to road damage and communities being cut off. (p2)
* {July 2012}: Internal flooding of residential properties and holiday caravans across the county, affecting many communities; some communities (Bridport, Martinstown, Winterbourne Abbas and Winterbourne Steepleton) had the largest counts of internally flooded properties. (p3)
* Severe disruption was occurring across Dorset on 7 July 2012; the River Bride was placed on a Severe Flood Warning and the Flood Guidance Statement indicated a HIGH flood risk for Dorset as impacts were occurring. There was continuing rainfall with ongoing risk of surface water and river flooding in parts of the county. (p8) [PAG]
* By 15:00 on 7 July severe disruption was occurring across Dorset as the flood risk was assessed HIGH; the River Bride was on a Severe Flood Warning. Continued episodes of river and surface-water flooding caused repeated periods of MEDIUM risk (notably 9 July) with significant disruption expected; groundwater flooding began to be noted from 10 July and contributed to ongoing low-level flooding into mid-July. (p9) [PAG]
* Intense rainfall on already-saturated ground (6–7 July) produced rapid rises in river levels, raised groundwater and widespread ponding of surface water across many parts of the county. (p12) [IMG, PAG]
* Intense, slow-moving occluded frontal rainfall on 6–8 July 2012 produced runoff and caused flooding in parts of Dorset (particularly West Dorset). Rain gauges located within the flooded areas were used to assess the rarity/return period of the rainfall totals. (p13) [IMG, PAG]
* Across Dorset approximately 1,700 properties received fluvial and groundwater flood alerts and warnings during the July 2012 event. (p16) [TAB, PAG]
* Over 70 communities across the county were affected by flooding; multiple hub locations held flood clinics and agencies engaged with affected residents to record impacts and provide support. (p17)
* County-level map shows internal flooding incidents across Dorset with the most severe clusters in southern and southwestern parts (notably around Weymouth, Dorchester and Bridport). Darker-blue zones on the map indicate high incidence of internal (internal-property) flooding affecting communities and properties within the county. (p25) [IMG]
* Quant: more than 40 properties flooded along the South Winterbourne; three fatalities; 12m increase in groundwater levels (p2)
* {July 2012}: Quant: over 270 properties; 18 holiday caravans; 71 communities (p3)
* Quant: approximately 1700 properties (p16) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: over 70 communities; over 300 residents (p17)
