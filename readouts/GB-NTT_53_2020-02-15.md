

# Radcliffe On Trent flooding 15 Feb 2020 - 16 Feb 2020

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Radcliffe On Trent February 2020<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/radcliffeontrentsection19reportfebruary2020.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Nottinghamshire
* 38 flood warnings and 16 flood alerts were in place across Nottinghamshire; Via East Midlands Ltd (on behalf of Nottinghamshire County Council) facilitated 51 road closures, placed over 70 flood signs on the network and delivered over 4,000 sandbags in response to the flooding. (p1)
* With saturated ground and heavy rain on 16 February 2020, Nottinghamshire experienced extensive flooding across the area, including impacts to settlements beside the River Trent. (p2)
* Quant: 38 flood warnings; 16 flood alerts; 51 road closures; over 70 flood signs; over 4,000 sandbags (p1)

### Rushcliffe
* Communities in Rushcliffe were affected by flooding; local authorities provided sandbags, emergency response and ongoing recovery support and grants. (p13)

### Radcliffe On Trent
* Parts of Radcliffe on Trent suffered a significant flood event on 16 February 2020: internal property flooding occurred and gardens, curtilages and public highways were affected. (p2) [IMG]
* Radcliffe on Trent catchment was affected by flooding during Storm Dennis (16 February 2020); community-level support and investigations (including resident forums, potential Flood Action Group support and community drop-ins) are being put in place. (p15)
* Quant: 37 reported incidents of internal property flooding (p2) [IMG]

### Clumber Drive, Radcliffe On Trent
* {01 Feb 1977 - 28 Feb 1977, 15 Feb 2020 - 16 Feb 2020}: Overland flood water originated at/behind 87 Clumber Drive and flowed across/along parts of Clumber Drive, passing through properties and toward the open section of Sykes Drain; the Environment Agency map also shows Clumber Drive within a corridor of surface water flood risk. (p4) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Residential street and driveways were submerged by floodwater; photographic evidence shows the road and driveways covered, parked cars partially submerged, and water reaching house boundaries (including water flowing over the boundary of 87 Clumber Drive). (p5) [IMG, PAG]
* Overflow from the surcharged Sykes Drain flowed over the boundary at 87 Clumber Drive and into the garden (water visible flowing through/under hedge and across the garden boundary). (p6) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 1 property (87 Clumber Drive) (p6) [IMG, PAG]

### St Lawrence Boulevard, Radcliffe On Trent
* Area was subjected to flooding on 16th February; internal flooding occurred to 4 properties and the curtilages of several other properties were flooded. (p9)
* An ordinary watercourse running along the rear boundaries of properties surcharged under excessive rainfall on 16 February, causing floodwaters to spread from the rear of properties, resulting in internal flooding to properties and flooding of property curtilages. (p10) [IMG]
* Quant: 4 properties (p9)
* Quant: 4 properties (p10) [IMG]

### The Green, Radcliffe On Trent
* Surface water accumulated at the upper section of The Green and flowed downhill along the street toward the intersection with Main Road. Internal flooding occurred in properties within The Green (caused by overland flow from Main Road into The Green). (p11) [IMG]
* Residents of The Green were affected by flooding during Storm Dennis (16 February 2020); Nottinghamshire County Council has established a local residents forum for The Green and intends further investigation and support. (p15)
* Quant: 3 properties (p11) [IMG]

### Thomas Avenue, Radcliffe On Trent
* Internal flooding of a property caused by water gathering against an exterior wall and seeping through the brickwork into the interior; ongoing investigation and mitigation work with Nottinghamshire County Council to reduce likelihood and impact of reoccurrence. (p12)
* Land in Thomas Avenue generated overland surface-water flows which contributed to downstream flooding during Storm Dennis (16th February 2020). Nottinghamshire County Council Property Team is considering ways to reduce these overland flows to reduce downstream impacts. (p14)
* Quant: 1 property (p12)

### Main Road, Radcliffe On Trent
* Excessive overland flows ran off Main Road into The Green. Flood flow along Main Road split in both directions at the intersection, indicating Main Road carried and conveyed surface water flows during the event. (p11) [IMG]
* Area experienced flooding during Storm Dennis (weekend 15–16 February 2020) that prompted scheduled investigations into the condition and connectivity of the surface water system for the Main Road / The Green area. (p15)

### Addington Court, Radcliffe On Trent
* Flood water flowed down towards the open section of Sykes Drain located in front of Addington Court; the open drain in front of the property was part of the flow path during the event. (p4)

### Nottingham Road, Radcliffe On Trent
* Flooding from the surcharged rear watercourse reached and inundated Nottingham Road at the junction with St Lawrence Boulevard (location shown by the star), contributing to the spread of flooding into adjacent properties. (p10)

### Princes Place, Radcliffe On Trent
* Princes Place estate contains the Skyes Drain and trash screens referenced; downstream culvert blockage on/near the estate was modelled to have been forced into surcharge during Storm Dennis, meaning the open watercourse could not convey flow away effectively during the event. The County Council will liaise with William Davis Ltd to ensure future maintenance of trash screens and Skyes Drain within the estate. (p14) [MUL]

### River Trent
* River Trent is the adjacent main river to Radcliffe on Trent where extensive flooding occurred on 16 Feb 2020; the proximity of the river is linked in the report context to the flooding of the village (though the report text does not explicitly state the river overtopped). (p2) [IMG]
* Quant: 37 reported incidents of internal property flooding (in Radcliffe on Trent) (p2) [IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Sykes Drain
* {01 Feb 1977 - 28 Feb 1977, 15 Feb 2020 - 16 Feb 2020}: The drain was observed surcharged upstream of the culverted section during the event; floodwater flowed overland toward and into the open section of Sykes Drain. Historical investigations (Feb 1977) found blockages at the culvert/trash screen caused flooding, prompting trash-screen modifications. (p4) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* The drain became surcharged upstream of a culverted section, causing the channel to overtop/overflow onto adjacent land and resulting in water flowing over the boundary of 87 Clumber Drive; this contributed to local property and street flooding. (p5) [PAG]
* {15 Feb 2020 - 16 Feb 2020, 17 Feb 2020}: The Sykes Drain was surcharged and holding/transporting large amounts of debris; upstream trash screen was completely blocked so flow routed around the screen, and the trash screen at the culvert mouth was almost entirely blocked — debris and blockage contributed to the drain overtopping and discharging onto adjacent land (including gardens). (p6) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* A newly constructed drainage ditch from the development discharged surface water direct to Sykes Drain, bypassing the onsite attenuation facility. Trash screens and the culvert mouth were heavily loaded with bramble debris, restricting flow; water was observed to bypass the blocked screen and follow an alternative path around the screen and via the new ditch into the drain. (p7) [IMG]
* Trash screens at the mouth of a culvert held an unusually large volume of debris consisting primarily of heavy-set brambles; similar bramble debris was evident along the upstream banks. A strip of adjacent land (shown highlighted in Figure 7) had been cleared prior to the flooding event, and the debris accumulation on the drain and trash screens warranted further investigation. (p8) [IMG, PAG]
* {16 Feb 2020, weekend of 15–16 February 2020}: Is the watercourse associated with the estate's trash screens; although trash screens were reported cleared on 12 February, modelling of a downstream culvert blockage shows the culvert would have been forced into surcharge during Storm Dennis so the open watercourse could not have conveyed flow effectively, influencing flooding dynamics. (p14) [MUL]

### William Davis Homes
* A new drainage ditch constructed by the developer (William Davis Ltd) discharged surface water directly into the Sykes Drain, bypassing the onsite attenuation facility and thereby routing additional surface water directly to the watercourse — a factor contributing to the observed surcharging/overflow. (p6)
* A strip of land (approximately 6–10 m wide) on William Davis Ltd.'s land adjacent to the Sykes Drain had been cleared of brambles prior to the flooding event. Photographic evidence taken after the event (photo dated Monday 17 February) shows the cleared strip and notes that debris had been cleared following the flood; heavy-set bramble debris was evident on upstream banks of the drain. (p8) [IMG]
* Was identified in relation to the event: Nottinghamshire County Council liaised with the company over immediate post‑event watercourse maintenance and served notice requiring removal of a temporary surface water connection from the William Davis Ltd site (implying the site connection was involved in surface water issues during the flood). (p13)
* CCTV survey and modelling relating to the culverted watercourse on the site found a significant blockage of foundation concrete in a brick-built culvert section. The blockage reduces the culvert capacity and, according to modelling, would have forced the culvert into surcharge under the rainfall conditions over the weekend of 15–16 February 2020 (Storm Dennis), meaning water would not have been able to flow away from the open watercourse effectively. William Davis also state their cyclical maintenance cleared the trash screens (cleared on 12 February) and that cleared material was processed into 18mm chippings and stored outside the watercourse flood profile. (p14) [MUL]

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* As the lead local authority for the area, Nottinghamshire County Council was involved in the flood response and investigation for the February 2020 event; actions were delivered on its behalf by Via East Midlands Ltd (including road closures, placement of flood signs and distribution of sandbags). (p1)
* Initiated and coordinated emergency planning; delivered sandbags to affected residents; led the Section 19 Flood Investigation; administered a Flood Hardship Grant; liaised with William Davis Ltd regarding immediate post-event watercourse maintenance; served notice to William Davis Ltd to remove a temporary surface water connection from its site. (p13)
* Quant: 51 road closures; over 70 flood signs; over 4000 sandbags (p1)

### Via East Midlands
* Facilitated immediate flood-response actions on the highway network during Storm Dennis by implementing road closures, deploying warning signs and supplying sandbags to manage flooding impacts on the transport network and help affected communities. (p1)
* Quant: 51 road closures; over 70 flood signs; over 4000 sandbags (p1)

### Rushcliffe Borough Council
* Provided emergency response support and delivered sandbags to affected residents; managed and continue to manage recovery in affected Rushcliffe communities; administered the flood hardship fund, community recovery grant, council tax and business rate relief grant scheme, and property flood resilience grant scheme; arranged additional emotional support via British Red Cross and actively engaged in the Section 19 Flood Investigation. (p13)
