

# Lowdham flooding 02 Jan 2024

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Lowdham – Storm Henk, January 2024<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/lowdham-s19-storm-henk-jan-2024.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Lowdham
* {1999, 2007, 2012, 2013, 2019, 2020, 2023}: Lowdham has a repeated history of flooding. Flooding has occurred along watercourses through the village (notably Cocker Beck) and from surface water, with significant consequences for the community including property inundation; predicted Flood Zone 3 and surface-water flood extents overlap parts of the village. (p2) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* On 2 January 2024 (Storm Henk) heavy rain on already saturated ground caused internal flooding in Lowdham, affecting homes and businesses. The report states 60 properties and businesses experienced internal flooding; some incidents were repeats and many residents were still recovering from Storm Babet. Lowdham also has a long history of flooding (notably 1999 when 300 properties flooded) and has been the subject of previous Section 19 reports. Flood storage works were constructed on the Cricket Pitch in 2000 and a £25m catchment flood alleviation project (preliminary works started September 2023) is intended to reduce future risk. (p3) [IMG]
* Internal flooding affected homes and businesses in central and eastern Lowdham; many residents and businesses experienced distress and some repeated internal-flooding incidents while still recovering from a prior event (Storm Babet). (p4) [IMG, PAG]
* Widespread flooding across the village and catchment: high surface-water flows and river overtopping overwhelmed drainage and defences, flooding properties, roads (including Epperstone By-Pass and Main Street) and open spaces; multiple streets and residential areas experienced internal and isolated internal flooding. (p5) [IMG, PAG]
* Reduced discharge capacity of the Cocker Beck (due to pumps placed in the Beck) caused the Beck to back up and overtop defences earlier than in previous events, leading to flooding of Station Road and properties in Victoria Avenue, Longmoor Avenue, Station Road, The Orchards and other streets; additionally, floodwaters from an overtopped left bank defence flooded The Corner, properties at 7–11 Main Street then flowed down Southwell Road into The Cottages, Willow Holt and Merevale as part of the wider Lowdham flood. (p6)
* {01 Jan 2024}: The Cocker Beck at Lowdham generated a flood warning and localised/isolated flooding: EA issued a Flood Warning for the Cocker Beck; blockages were cleared; temporary pumps were installed to mitigate potential collapse of a riparian channel side and to protect property; residents experienced isolated flooding outside the main residential area; a multi‑agency community drop‑in and information gathering took place. (p8)
* Properties in the village were affected by bow-wave / surface water flooding; volunteers closed roads within the village to limit bow-wave impacts on properties. (p9)
* {1999, 2007, 2012, 2013, 2019, 2020, 2023}: Quant: 300 properties (p2) [IMG, PAG, MUL]

### Nottinghamshire
* Storm Henk on 2 January 2024 brought heavy rainfall on saturated ground resulting in widespread flooding across Nottinghamshire. (p3)

### Lime Tree Gardens, Lowdham
* The report records that several properties in Lime Tree Gardens were flooded during the Storm Henk event. The Environment Agency had placed pumps in the Cocker Beck at Lime Tree Gardens (to bypass a collapsed structure), and these pumps may have reduced the channel capacity and contributed to earlier and significant overtopping upstream. (p5) [PAG]
* Floodwater inundated properties in Lime Tree Gardens as part of a wider event. Pumps were also placed in the Cocker Beck at Lime Tree Gardens to route water around a collapsed structure. (p6) [PAG]
* Quant: six pumps (only two operational) (p5) [PAG]
* Quant: 30 properties (group that included several properties in Lime Tree Gardens) (p6) [PAG]

### Main Street, Lowdham
* A left-bank defence was overtopped, sending floodwater down Main Street and flooding properties at 7 - 11 Main Street and then progressing onto Southwell Road and adjacent residential areas. (p5)
* Quant: properties 7 - 11 Main Street (5 properties) (p5)

### The Corner, Lowdham
* The repaired left bank defence of the Cocker Beck was overtopped, flooding The Corner as floodwater spilled from the Beck; water from the overtopping then progressed toward Main Street and downstream roads. (p5)
* Quant: 5 properties (7-11 Main Street) flooded in the same overtopping sequence (p5)

### Southwell Road, Lowdham
* Floodwater from the overtopped Beck flowed down Southwell Road around and into The Cottages and into residential areas (Willow Holt and Merevale), contributing to significant property flooding downstream. (p5)
* Quant: 30 properties flooded (downstream area including Willow Holt / Merevale) (p5)

### Willow Holt, Lowdham
* Floodwater from overtopping of the left bank and high surface water levels flowed into Willow Holt (and adjoining Merevale), where it contributed to internal flooding across the area described. (p5)
* Quant: 30 properties (p5)

### Caythorpe Road, Lowdham
* {general}: Shown on the predicted surface water flood extents map within/adjacent to surface water flood risk shading; therefore liable to surface water inundation during heavy rainfall. (p3) [IMG]

### Station Road, Lowdham
* The road and its wall overtopped, allowing floodwater onto Station Road itself and contributing to flooding of nearby properties (Station Road, Victoria Avenue, Longmoor Avenue and The Orchards). Overtopping occurred earlier than in previous events due to reduced capacity of the Cocker Beck upstream. (p6)

### Victoria Avenue, Lowdham
* Floodwater, caused by the Cocker Beck backing up and the early overtopping of Station Road, entered and flooded properties on Victoria Avenue. (p6)

### Longmoor Avenue, Lowdham
* Floodwater, caused by the Cocker Beck backing up and the early overtopping of Station Road, entered and flooded properties on Longmoor Avenue. (p6)

### Cocker Beck
* {1999, 2007, 2012, 2013, 2019, 2020, 2023}: The Cocker Beck is the main watercourse through Lowdham and, together with other local watercourses and the village topography, has contributed to repeated flooding of the village. Notable events include the 1999 flood and multiple subsequent floods (2007, 2012, 2013, 2019, 2020, 2023) that affected the community and properties adjacent to the Beck. (p2) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* {1999, 2007, 2012, 2013, 2019, 2020, 2023}: Quant: 300 properties (p2) [IMG, PAG, MUL]

### Epperstone by-Pass
* Epperstone By-Pass acted as a channel for significant overland flows during the event and was overwhelmed by surface water, contributing to conveyance of floodwater through the village. (p5) [IMG]

### Dover Beck
* Area adjacent to the watercourse was highlighted as experiencing isolated internal flooding; proximity of the watercourse to roads and properties indicates interaction of river overflow and surface water leading to internal flooding in that locality. (p5) [IMG]

### River Trent
* Predicted extreme river levels created a continued risk of river flooding, prompting the council to carry out in-person visits to high-risk communities to reinforce preparedness. (p9)


## Topics without coordinates

### Environment Agency
* {2005, 01 Sep 2023 - 30 Sep 2023}: Acting as the lead Risk Management Authority for Main Rivers, the Environment Agency took responsibility for the Cocker Beck (as part of the Critical Ordinary Watercourse project) and secured funding to deliver a £25m flood alleviation project for the catchment; preliminary construction commenced in September 2023 to reduce future flood likelihood and consequences. (p3) [PAG, MUL]
* {01 Oct 2023 - 31 Oct 2023, 02 Jan 2024}: The Environment Agency placed pumps inside the Cocker Beck at Lime Tree Gardens to pump water around a collapsed structure; with only two of six pumps operational this may have prevented full use of the Beck's capacity to discharge water, contributing to earlier and significant overtopping of the wall on Station Road and subsequent property flooding. (p6) [MUL]
* {general}: Cocker Beck at Lowdham threatened properties and channel stability; the Environment Agency issued a Flood Warning for the Cocker Beck, operated flood patrols, cleared blockages before and after peak flows, and deployed temporary pumps to mitigate potential collapse of a riparian channel side and to protect property (pumps were removed once levels receded). (p8)
* Led a community drop-in session in Lowdham following Storm Henk to support and inform affected residents. (p9)
* {2005, 01 Sep 2023 - 30 Sep 2023}: Quant: £25m (p3) [PAG, MUL]
* {01 Oct 2023 - 31 Oct 2023, 02 Jan 2024}: Quant: 6 pumps (2 operational) (p6) [MUL]

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Flooding across the county prompted a coordinated emergency response: 24-hour Emergency Planning support was provided; sandbags were delivered to affected areas and communities; and 69 roads were closed to promote road safety and avoid additional flood damage. Staff were mobilised across departments and worked beyond normal hours to support the response and share resources with district/borough partners. (p8)
* During the Storm Henk event (surface water flooding that moved to a continued risk of river flooding) NCC staff visited impacted communities and vulnerable individuals, delivered and restocked community stores of aqua sacs, helped evacuate people from their homes, coordinated and provided daily public warnings/briefings to councillors and community leaders, contacted affected individuals to identify humanitarian needs and offer support or referral, coordinated clean-up of roads and paths and removal of bulky waste from flooded homes, administered grant funding to eligible impacted households, and led internal and multi‑agency debriefs. (p9)
* As the Lead Local Flood Authority they report having witnessed how flooding devastates communities; they will prioritise the most vulnerable and work with partners and communities to address the likelihood and consequences of future flooding events. (p10)
* Quant: 69 roads (p8)

### Lowdham Flood Alleviation Scheme
* {01 Oct 2023 - 31 Oct 2023, 02 Jan 2024}: Storm Babet and Storm Henk demonstrated the need for a large-scale flood alleviation scheme. The project will provide flood storage upstream of Lowdham to reduce flood risk to the community, better protecting 191 properties and delivering a 1PCSYM Annual Exceedance Probability standard of protection; enabling works started 30/01/2023, main construction started 30/10/2023, expected completion Spring 2026. (p10) [MUL]
* {01 Oct 2023 - 31 Oct 2023, 02 Jan 2024}: Quant: 191 properties (p10) [MUL]

### Cricket Pitch
* {2000}: Following the significant 1999 flooding event, flood storage works were constructed on the Cricket Pitch in 2000 to store floodwater and reduce flood risk in the catchment. (p3)

### Newark and Sherwood
* {01 Jan 2024}: During the flood event the council conducted community visits, assisted vulnerable individuals, delivered flood protection supplies (aqua sacs) and supported evacuations; communications officers warned and informed the public and provided daily briefings to councillors and community leaders. (p8)

### Lowdham Community Flood Signage Scheme
* The signage scheme was activated during Storm Henk; volunteers used it to close roads in the village to limit bow-wave (surface water) flooding impacts on properties. (p9)

### Severn Trent
* Received reports of flooding from sewers in the area, undertook extensive investigations into causes, and contacted affected residents and the Flood Action Group about sewer-related issues. (p9)
