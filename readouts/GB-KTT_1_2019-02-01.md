## Place names with coordinates

### Coombe Hill Infant School
* {2007, 01 Feb 2019 - 28 Feb 2019}: Multiple flooding incidents reported at the school; the site has experienced an ongoing flooding problem over several months. The site is potentially at risk of groundwater-influenced flooding via throughflow and flooding from artificial sources; sub-surface flows associated with the Coombe Conduit beneath the school may affect flood behaviour. (p3) [MUL]
* {2007, early 2019}: Surface water flooding was reported at the school in 2007. A further report in early 2019 stated the school had been experiencing a flooding problem within the school grounds over a period of several weeks. (p7) [MUL]
* Internal flooding of the school building reported on multiple occasions; these repeated internal flood reports triggered a Section 19 flood investigation by the local authority. (p8) [IMG]
* {general}: The site is situated in a topographical low compared to surrounding land; the investigation analysed the hydrological catchment and primary overland flow route(s) showing how surface water/overland flow may reach the school site. (p9) [PAG]
* {01 Feb 2019 - 28 Feb 2019}: The site is located in a topographic low and experienced springs/groundwater emergence on the school site. Large volumes of water appeared on the Adventure Playground mulch, with flows increasing and earth being washed away beneath the playground surface, creating hollows on the school grounds. (p10) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Report analysis focuses on potential surface water and overland-flow flood risk to the school site (including the Adventure Playground) arising from primary flow paths in the local catchment that drain toward Coombe Brook. (p11) [PAG]
* {2007}: A recorded surface water flood event occurred at the school site; runoff from the main road to the north is reported anecdotally to flow toward the school. Mapping shows the school sits within a defined hydrological catchment with primary flow paths and multiple watercourses traversing the catchment, indicating flow connectivity that could deliver surface water to the site. (p12) [IMG]
* {general}: Runoff from the main road to the north of the site flows towards the school, creating a source of surface water runoff onto the school site. The EA Risk of Flooding from Surface Water mapping indicates the majority of the school site is at 'very low' risk of surface water flooding, with areas close to the school building in the northern part of the site at 'low' risk. Anecdotal evidence describes infrequent springs appearing at the site (localised groundwater emergence). (p13)
* {general}: The site has groundwater-influenced flooding issues: throughflow from upstream productive aquifers and permeable superficial deposits towards lower-permeability downstream geology is likely contributing to groundwater-related flooding problems at the school site. (p14) [PAG]
* {general}: The disused Coombe Conduit runs beneath the school; groundwater may be able to enter the remaining pipework and, combined with changes to subsurface flows from 2015 building works, could increase the school's susceptibility to flooding from the conduit. (p15)
* {01 Feb 2019 - 28 Feb 2019}: Site is in a topographical low; runoff from the main road flowed towards the school buildings (north of the site). Large volumes of water appeared on the Adventure Playground play-area mulch, causing the mulch to increase in size and the flow of water to increase quickly. (p18)
* {01 Feb 2019 - 28 Feb 2019, 01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019}: A flooding issue at the school playground was reported. The Local Lead Flood Authority (Kingston) conducted a site visit in February 2019 and concluded a land and structural survey was needed. A further LLFA site visit was carried out in November 2019 where previous flooding issues and the potential for SuDS on the site were discussed. Thames Water assessed sewer sources and concluded the likelihood of the flooding coming from sewer sources was low. (p21) [PAG, MUL]
* Internal property flooding of the school building(s) reported in a written submission; flooding is attributed to groundwater-related impacts (Chapter 3.1.5) and not to public or private sewer failure. (p22)
* The flood risk investigation was triggered by multiple reported flood incidents at Coombe Hill Infant School. Anecdotal evidence from the school's site manager indicated the flooding did not require a response from a Category One Responder. (p23) [PAG]
* {2007, 01 Feb 2019 - 28 Feb 2019}: Repeated groundwater-influenced flooding at the school; the site experienced a flooding problem over a period of several months. Investigation concluded the site is at risk of groundwater throughflow flooding, with risk exacerbated by steep topographic differences, a change from permeable upstream geology (Black Park Gravel Member) to London Clay at the site, and building work that may have altered subsurface flows associated with the Coombe Conduit. (p24) [MUL]
* {01 Aug 2018 - 31 Aug 2018}: Rainfall-return-period analysis in the appendix relates the August 2018 event to Coombe Hill School; the report classifies the August 2018 event at this location as approximately 'under 1 in 5 year'. (p27) [TAB]
* {2007}: Quant: one flood record (2007) (p12) [IMG]
* {2007, 01 Feb 2019 - 28 Feb 2019}: Quant: 2 reported flooding incidents (p24) [MUL]

### Coombe Conduit
* {general}: A historical pipe system running beneath the school is identified as influencing sub-surface flows; it may contribute to groundwater-influenced flooding at the school. Building work may have altered these sub-surface flows, and a survey of the Conduit is recommended to determine its impact on flooding. (p3)
* {general}: The historic (now non‑operational) pipe system could allow groundwater from local springs to enter the remaining pipework; this ingress could transmit groundwater beneath/into nearby buildings (notably Coombe Hill Infant School), potentially causing flooding. (p15)
* The Coombe Conduit runs diagonally across the site and passes adjacent to the proposed building extension. It is identified in the report as potentially placing the site at greater risk of flooding and is implicated in the flooding experienced at the site. (p16) [IMG, PAG]
* {2007, 01 Feb 2019 - 28 Feb 2019}: Sub-surface flows associated with the Coombe Conduit may have been altered by building work and are identified as a factor that exacerbated groundwater-influenced flooding at Coombe Hill Infant School. (p24) [MUL]
* {general}: The Coombe Conduit is identified as potentially affecting sub-surface flows at the nearby school site and therefore may contribute to groundwater-influenced flooding at the school; the LLFA is advised to arrange a survey to determine its impact on sub-surface flows. (p25)
* {2007, 01 Feb 2019 - 28 Feb 2019}: Quant: two reported incidents (2007; February 2019) (p24) [MUL]

### Coombe Hill
* {2007, 01 Feb 2019 - 28 Feb 2019}: Coombe Hill Infant School (located in Coombe Hill) experienced repeated groundwater-influenced flooding incidents; the school reported flooding over a period of several months and this triggered a formal flood risk investigation. (p24) [MUL]
* {2007, 01 Feb 2019 - 28 Feb 2019}: Quant: two reported incidents (2007; February 2019) (p24) [MUL]

### Coombe Brook
* {general}: Primary flow paths from the catchment combine and discharge into Coombe Brook (a tributary of the Beverley Brook), making it a receptor for runoff and part of the identified flood routing that may affect downstream areas including the school site. (p11)


## Topics without coordinates

### Beverley Brook
* Flow paths from the defined hydrological catchment combine and flow into Coombe Brook, which is a tributary of the Beverley Brook; Beverley Brook is therefore identified as a downstream watercourse that could convey elevated flows and contribute to river flooding affecting the catchment and downstream sites (including Coombe Hill Infant School). (p11)

### Kingston Upon Thames Council
* {01 Feb 2019 - 28 Feb 2019, 01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019}: Responded to a reported flooding issue at the school playground: Kingston (as LLFA/authority) conducted a site visit in February 2019, concluded a land and structural survey was needed, and carried out a further site visit in November 2019 where previous flooding and potential incorporation of SuDS on the site were discussed. It is unknown if the recommended survey was carried out. (p21) [PAG, MUL]
