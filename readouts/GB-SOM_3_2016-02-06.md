

# Norton Sub Hamdon flooding 06 Feb 2016

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Rectory Lane, Great Street and Little Street, Norton Sub Hamdon, Somerset. 6th February 2016<br>113\_Somerset County/Flood Investigation Report Norton Sub Hamdon.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Norton Sub Hamdon
* Obstructions in the watercourse downstream of Norton Sub Hamdon were identified as contributors to the flood event; SCC Flood Management Team and FWAG met with landowners and resolved these obstructions during the post-event response. (p4)
* Properties in the village reported internal flooding. Downstream of Norton Sub Hamdon there was evidence of substantial natural debris and vegetation (fallen branches, tree trunks, reeds) and other obstructions forming dams that backed-up the stream and increased flood impacts; pinch-points (including Pill Bridge and a boundary wall 60 m upstream) and an undersized diversion channel were cited as contributing factors. (p14) [IMG]
* Inspection found numerous large obstructions (including tree trunks) in the watercourse downstream of Little Street along the River Parrett Trail; these acted as debris traps leading to blockages that likely contributed to flooding in Norton Sub Hamdon (including the drainage failures on Rectory Lane). (p16)
* Village experienced a damaging flood event that triggered a Section 19 investigation. Flooding issues included channel blockages and pinch points on the local watercourse, prompting community meetings, investigation by the LLFA/FRM team and consideration of feasibility and alleviation measures. (p18)
* The village experienced flooding consistent with Environment Agency flood risk mapping. The event prompted multi-agency investigation (FRM site visits, landowner engagement, sewer/pumping station investigations by Wessex Water) and recommendations for resident Flood Action Plans and property-level protection. Highway drainage review was proposed for Rectory Lane, Great Street, New Road and Little Street. (p19)

### Rectory Lane, Norton Sub Hamdon
* {general}: Rectory Lane is described as a flow route and receptor for runoff from surrounding agricultural land, the bridle way and highway drains; during sustained/heavy rainfall spill-over and converted flow routes have led to overflow into Rectory Lane and into properties (specifically the rear garden of 5 Glebelands). (p10) [PAG]
* Highway drains adjacent to properties surcharged and began to flood Rectory Lane; the highway flooded to depths reported between 100 mm and 1200 mm. The surcharge of drains adjacent to properties (Homefield, Clarens and Apple Lodge) contributed to the lane flooding. (p12)
* Drains surcharged onto the highway causing increased surface water flow along Rectory Lane into Great Street and Little Street; flood depth at the northern end of Rectory Lane was measured at 1.2 m at the peak of the event. (p15)
* Drains outside the property known as 'the Stables' experienced a capacity failure during the flood: the drain outfall became ineffective once the adjacent watercourse rose to the outfall height, causing local drainage failure and flooding on Rectory Lane. (p16)
* Surface-water flooding of the highway / overland flow across the carriageway, prompting Somerset County Council (Highway Authority) to review the frequency of highway drain clearance for Rectory Lane. (p19)

### Little Street, Norton Sub Hamdon
* {September 2012}: In September 2012 sewer debris in Little Street reached capacity following torrential rain, resulting in sewer-related flooding in Little Street. (p11)
* The highway (Little Street) was inundated by floodwater during the event; road flooding depths reported between 100 mm and 1200 mm, causing significant highway inundation and likely disruption to access. (p12)
* Surface water routed from Rectory Lane and Great Street flowed on to Little Street, causing street flooding during the event. (p15)
* The watercourse downstream of Little Street was found to have numerous large obstructions (including tree trunks) that trapped vegetation and debris, forming blockages that impeded flow and were identified as likely contributors to flooding downstream toward the River Parrett. (p16)
* Surface-water flooding of the highway / overland flow across the carriageway, prompting Somerset County Council (Highway Authority) to review the frequency of highway drain clearance for Little Street. (p19)

### New Road, Norton Sub Hamdon
* Surface run-off from surrounding agricultural land flowed onto and down New Road, entering highway drains and culverts and contributing flows that ran east toward Rectory Lane; during persistent/heavy rainfall this route caused spill-over toward the bridleway and downstream watercourse. (p10)
* On 2016-02-06 a significant volume of surface water run-off travelled via New Road (and its highway drain), conveying water through the catchment toward Pill Bridge Stream; this contributed to the stream breaching its east bank downstream and to flooding of local properties (more than 8 properties flooded). (p11) [PAG]
* Silt-laden surface water from ploughed/harvested fields to the west flowed down New Road at a 'phenomenal rate' into drainage and sewage systems, carrying silt and likely contributing to drainage/sewer blockages and local flooding. (p14)
* Surface-water flooding of the highway / overland flow across the carriageway, prompting Somerset County Council (Highway Authority) to review the frequency of highway drain clearance for New Road. (p19)

### Great Street, Norton Sub Hamdon
* The highway (Great Street) was inundated by floodwater during the event; road flooding depths reported between 100 mm and 1200 mm, causing significant highway inundation and likely disruption to access. (p12)
* Surface water from surcharged drains and multiple outfalls flowed along Rectory Lane into Great Street, causing street flooding during the event. (p15)
* Surface-water flooding of the highway / overland flow across the carriageway, prompting Somerset County Council (Highway Authority) to review the frequency of highway drain clearance for Great Street. (p19)

### Skinners Lane, Norton Sub Hamdon
* The highway (Skinners Lane) was flooded during the event. (p12)
* Outfalls and submerged drains rapidly backed up and became fully charged, causing drains to surcharge onto the highway at Skinners Lane and increasing surface water flow onto the carriageway during the event. (p15)

### Matt's Lane, Stoke-Sub-Hamdon
* A significant volume of surface run‑off travelled via Matts Lane into downstream ditches and the Pill Bridge Stream on 2016-02-06, contributing to the stream overtopping and the flooding of local properties. (p11)

### River Parrett
* The watercourse and banks along the River Parrett Trail were found to have numerous large obstructions (tree trunks, brush and debris) that acted as traps and caused blockages; these blockages were identified as the most likely cause contributing to flooding. (p16)


## Topics without coordinates

### Pill Bridge Stream
* {general}: The Pill Bridge Stream (referred to as the Little Norton/Pill Bridge stream) is described as having a large catchment receiving runoff from agricultural land, the scarp of Ham Hill and overland flows from the bridle way; during sustained/heavy rainfall these flows convert routes (bridle way, culverts, highway drains) into watercourses that outfall to the stream and have resulted in overflow into Rectory Lane and into the rear garden of 5 Glebelands. (p10)
* Upstream surface run‑off arriving via New Road, the recreation ground ditch, Matts Lane and the bridleway increased flows in the Pill Bridge Stream so much that it breached its east bank adjacent to the property known as The Stables and a nearby public footpath on 2016-02-06; this breach/overflow contributed to flooding of local properties. (p11) [PAG]
* The stream's increased volume breached its east bank adjacent to the property known as The Stables and a nearby public footpath. (p12)
* The Pill Bridge watercourse was identified by residents as a flow restriction ('pinch point') contributing to local flooding in Norton Sub Hamdon. FRM inspections found blockages and a large dead willow close to falling into the channel; the willow was agreed to be felled to reduce the risk of further blockage. The two‑span arch bridge at Pill Bridge was noted as a potential restriction to flow. (p19)

### Pill Bridge
* {general}: Pill Bridge is identified in the district council's past flood alleviation scheme (a relief culvert at Pill Bridge was installed), indicating it was affected by previous flooding and required infrastructure to manage flood flows. (p10) [PAG]
* Pill Bridge functioned as a pinch-point during sustained heavy rainfall: the upstream inlet (measured at 1.0 m high x 1.3 m wide) and the adjacent 30-year-old flood-alleviation box channel were described as too small, producing a convergent restriction that backed up flow and contributed to upstream flooding of properties. (p14) [PAG, IMG]
* The stream is constricted at the Pill Bridge inlet creating a pinch point that caused flood water levels to rise and fall rapidly and contributed to the adjacent banks breaching during the event. (p15)
* Identified by residents and investigators as a 'pinch point' on the watercourse that restricted flow during the flood, contributing to local flooding. Considered for hydraulic modelling and potential bypass improvements but constrained by Grade II listing, heritage approval requirements and downstream risk concerns; no remedial works progressed. (p18) [PAG]
* The reach of Pill Bridge Stream from The Stables to Pill Bridge was inspected by the FRM team as part of post‑flood investigations; the watercourse in this reach was found to have been cleared and works agreed with landowners to remove a large dead willow to reduce flood risk. (p19)

### Glebelands, Norton Sub Hamdon
* The bridleway and culverted flows outfall into the Little Norton watercourse at the rear garden of a property in Glebelands, causing flood water to discharge into the property's rear garden. (p10)

### Somerset County Council
* SCC's Flood and Water Management Team carried out site visits (23/02/2016 and 24/02/2016) following the 2016-02-06 flood, identifying numerous large obstructions (tree trunks, accumulated brush, vegetation and debris) in the watercourse downstream of Little Street along the River Parrett Trail to the River Parrett that had caused blockages. SCC assessed that the existing 30-year-old flood alleviation scheme (culverts and trash screens) only provided protection to approximately a 1-in-10 year event and could not be readily improved short-term given site constraints. SCC wrote to local landowners requesting maintenance works (deadline extended to 2016-05-31), engaged with FWAG for land-management advice, coordinated with SSDC, and communicated findings to the Parish Council and community, including discussion of riparian responsibilities and Property Level Protection options. (p17) [PAG]
* SCC (including its Structures team) were engaged in the post-event response: they advised on constraints at Pill Bridge (a two-span arch, Grade II listed) and the implications of any structural works, contributed to decisions not to undertake bridge works due to heritage approval, downstream impacts and budget constraints, and supported the FRM Team's investigation and feasibility planning. (p18) [MUL]
* As the Highway Authority, Somerset County Council was responsible for highway drainage maintenance and for reviewing drainage clearance frequency in flood‑susceptible streets in Norton Sub Hamdon following the flooding, to reduce surface water impacts on the highway. (p19)

### River Parrett Trail
* The watercourse and banks along the River Parrett Trail were found to be poorly maintained with numerous large obstructions (including tree trunks across the channel) that trapped brush, vegetation and debris, forming blockages that impeded flow and were identified as likely contributors to flooding into the River Parrett. (p16)

### South Somerset Council
* South Somerset District Council exercised responsibilities relating to flooding response: they have powers to require Wessex Water to clean up and disinfect areas after sewage entered drains and surface water following flooding, and have previously undertaken mitigation works in the area. (p19)

### Highway Authority
* Highway drainage and maintenance were implicated in the flood response: the Highways Authority reviewed and committed to review drain clearance frequency for streets identified as susceptible (Rectory Lane, Great Street, New Road, Little Street) because of surface water flooding issues affecting the highway network in Norton Sub Hamdon. (p19)
