### River Rother

Shortest lag time between peak rainfall and peak flow in the sub-catchment was recorded near Staveley (8 hours) with the longest at Woodhouse Mill. (p3)
* The River Whitting (a tributary of the River Rother) overtopped/was implicated in flooding on the floodplain at Station Road, contributing to internal flooding of nearby properties. (p10) [PAG, IMG]
* The Don and Rother management catchment (including the Rother) is shown as the context for the recorded Storm Babet flood outline; watercourses and culverts within this management catchment intersect the recorded flood extent indicating river/urban flooding affected the area. (p25) [IMG]
* Flooding of the River Rother produced the recorded flood outline during Storm Babet that closely followed the river and inundated adjacent residential streets in South Chesterfield, contributing to internal flooding of properties. (p33) [IMG]
* The River Rother overtopped/was the primary source of flooding in the Chesterfield South community; flooding from the Rother produced most of the flooded homes on the adjacent floodplain (most flooded homes were west of the river). Flood defenses (walls and embankments) are present along the river. (p34) [IMG, PAG]
* Flooding from the River Rother affected the Chesterfield South community. West bank defences (two flood walls and Hawthorne Street embankment) provided protection, while there are no engineered defences on the east bank. The Avenue Flood Balancing Reservoir upstream operated as intended during the event and stored water. (p35) [PAG, IMG]
* The River Rother was the primary source of flooding: it overtopped flood defence structures at several locations causing inundation of low-lying areas. ### River Hipper
* Quicker and earlier river responses to the heavy rainfall from Storm Babet were recorded on the River Hipper (near Chesterfield and Holymoorside); gauging stations produced the highest or second-highest ranked flows or levels on record in response to Storm Babet. (p3)
* Flooding from the River Hipper (ordinary watercourse through Holymoorside) inundated residential properties in the village, causing internal flooding. (p14)
* Flows through Holymoorside on its floodplain and contributed to flooding during the event; flood outlines were recorded along the watercourse and at nearby road crossings, associated with internal flooding of local properties. (p15) [PAG, IMG]
* High river levels at the Holymoorside gauge on 20 October 2023 (rise beginning ~00:00, peaking ~12:00) contributed to the flood extent in Holymoorside and inundation of nearby residential properties and a business. River Hipper levels reached a record gauge reading and are identified as a causal contributor to the internal flooding of properties on The Alders, New Road and Loads Road. (p17) [IMG, PAG]
* The River Hipper rose rapidly (peak gauge at Holymoorside on 20 Oct) and overtopped its banks at multiple locations through the village and near Old Mill Pond; overtopping entered properties from the rear (including The Alders, New Road and Loads Road) and contributed directly to the internal flooding of homes and the overtopping of Old Mill Pond. (p18)
* The river overtopped its banks at multiple locations near Old Mill Pond and through Holymoorside; Old Mill Pond overtopped local to a weir. Overtopping upstream of Loads Road bridge and restricted bridge arch capacity caused floodwater to flow east along Loads Road and pool at the Loads Road / New Road low spot, contributing to internal flooding of properties (including The Alders and two homes at the New Road/Loads Road junction). (p19) [PAG]
* The River Hipper flooded during the storm and is identified as the main river flowing through the community; floodwaters from the River Hipper resulted in internal flooding of nearby properties (all flooded properties are within 75 m of the river). * River Hipper water levels rose during the event, reaching a recorded peak (gauge c.20 m upstream of Walton Road bridge reached 3.59 m, the highest on record) on 20 Oct, with levels rising from early morning and peaking at 13:00; these high river levels caused flooding of the nearby residential properties. (p22) [PAG, IMG]
* The River Hipper exceeded its channel and breached its banks at multiple locations in the community. At Chapel Lane West the river breached the north bank and flowed north up the street. River water entered five homes on Walton Road through their rear gardens and flooded them internally. River flows are also understood to have been a dominant source of flooding at Chapel Lane West and are likely connected to flooding affecting Grove Cottages (possible flows through drainage holes in the riverside wall). Residents reported waist‑high water at the south end of Chapel Street West adjacent to the River Hipper. (p23) [IMG, PAG]
* The River Hipper flowed through Brampton and was a principal flood source during Storm Babet; it contributed to internal flooding across the community. The Hipper is heavily modified and culverted at multiple locations within the community, which is described in the report as part of the flooding mechanism. (p24)
* River Hipper overtopped/caused fluvial flooding across the local floodplain during the event, inundating built-up areas south of Chatsworth Road (including Wheatbridge Road, Dock Walk and Anna Street West). Topographic data shows flooded properties sit at similar elevation to the top bank, with low spots in the floodplain contributing to inundation. (p25) [IMG]

### Chesterfield
* {18 Oct 2023 - 21 Oct 2023}: Part of the Don and Rother sub-catchment that experienced hydrological conditions (rainfall and river responses) during Storm Babet; the River Rother flows through the centre of Chesterfield and was within the area analysed for the event. (p1)
* A retail district (with large-footprint businesses and some residential properties) located on the River Whitting floodplain was flooded. Properties sit close to the top of the river bank and lower than surrounding ground. Flooding during 19 Oct 2023 - 21 Oct 2023 was more extensive than the June 2007 river flood. The flooded properties are within Flood Zone 3 and are also mapped as high risk of surface water flooding. (p11) [IMG]
* Floodwater pooled at a low spot on Station Road with reported depths that impeded the road; at least one property suffered internal flooding as indicated by a ~50 cm watermark. A resident on Station Road made a sandbag request on 20 October at 15:00. (p14)
* River level exceedance of the River Hipper caused flooding across Chesterfield. Properties on multiple streets requested sandbags on 20/10/2023; some properties were inaccessible due to floodwater. Fire and Rescue attended the community. Debris and land drainage blockages were recorded constraining channel capacity; a collapsed wall and other channel/retaining wall defects were reported. Environment Agency and local authorities carried out post-event channel clearances and inspections, and assisted removal of debris and PFR scheme applications. (p32) [TAB]
* Internal flooding of residential properties in South Chesterfield concentrated either side of the River Rother; recorded flood outline during Storm Babet inundated the community, causing internal flooding of homes. (p33) [IMG, PAG]

Local flood defences on the River Rother (west bank flood walls and embankments) were a feature of the event and the Avenue Flood Balancing Reservoir upstream operated during the event. (p35) [PAG]
