## Place names with coordinates

### Tunbridge Wells
* Significant flooding in parts of Tunbridge Wells on 24 August 2015: several properties were inundated and there was disruption to traffic and transportation; the flooding was widespread across parts of the town. (p5)
* Tunbridge Wells experienced surface-water/urban flooding on 24 August 2015 that caused internal flooding to commercial and residential properties, flooded highways and pedestrian areas making them impassable, damage to drainage/culvert infrastructure and flooding of the railway track leading to line closure. (p7) [TAB, PAG]
* Central Tunbridge Wells locations (including The Pantiles, Nevill Street, Market Street, London Road, Castle Street and Mount Pleasant Road) experienced internal flooding of commercial and residential properties, impassable pedestrian and highway areas and localised transport disruption. (p8) [IMG]
* Widespread flooding across the town on 24 August 2015: commercial and residential properties internally flooded; highways and pedestrian areas flooded and impassable; railway track flooded causing closure of the Tonbridge–Hastings line; local watercourse/culvert damage (culvert headwall damaged). Emergency services pumped water from properties and basements. (p9) [IMG]
* {2009, 2012, 24 Aug 2015}: Central Royal Tunbridge Wells (the Pantiles area) experienced surface-water/flash flooding concentrated at low points (junction of Nevill Street, The Pantiles and Warwick Park; Market Street; Sussex Mews). Floodwaters converged along flow routes with limited overland drainage; many buildings in the area (including those with basements below street level) were affected by flooding. The town has also experienced localised foul and surface-water sewer flooding in the Warwick Park area in earlier events. (p16) [IMG, MUL]
* {13 Aug 2015, 24 Aug 2015, 16 Sep 2015, 21 Sep 2015}: Heavy rainfall and exceedance of public sewer capacity caused surface water and sewer-related flooding in the Tunbridge Wells area, including internal flooding of properties in the Cumberland Walk / Nevill Park / Warwick Park block. (p18) [MUL]
* Overflow of the River Grom at the entrance to a culvert in Warwick Park caused internal flooding to one private property; floodwater collected in several gardens to a depth in excess of 1 m. High flows and debris damaged the culvert headwall's trash screen and impeded flow, and the trash screen was unsafe to maintain during the high flows. (p21) [IMG]
* Tunbridge Wells Railway Station (within the town) had its track bed flooded when an overflowing manhole released water into the railway cutting, causing a temporary closure of the Tonbridge to Hastings line. (p27) [IMG]
* Intense rainfall on 24/08/2015 exceeded drainage capacity causing surface water to collect in topographic low points across Tunbridge Wells; flood depths reached levels that entered property. Combined sewer surcharge at Tunbridge Wells railway station caused flooding of the track bed. (p28) [PAG]
* Intense rainfall on 24 August 2015 caused surface water/urban flooding across Tunbridge Wells. Floodwater collected in topographical low points, producing highway and localised urban flooding throughout the town. (p29) [PAG]
* Quant: 2 properties pumped out at Nevill Street; 2 properties pumped out at Market Street; 1 property internally flooded at Warwick Park; at least 2 properties externally affected on London Road; internal flooding to 1 commercial property at Castle Street; internal flooding to 1 commercial property at Mount Pleasant Road. (p7) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 2 properties pumped out by 2 fire engines (Nevill Street); 2 properties pumped out by 2 fire engines (Market Street); 1 residential property internally flooded (Warwick Park); 1 lower-ground-floor property internally flooded (Sussex Mews); at least 2 properties affected externally (London Road); 1 commercial property internally flooded (Castle Street); 1 commercial property internally flooded (Mount Pleasant Road) (p9) [IMG]
* {13 Aug 2015, 24 Aug 2015, 16 Sep 2015, 21 Sep 2015}: Quant: 1 residential property; 2 commercial properties (p18) [MUL]
* Quant: one property; gardens flooded to a depth in excess of 1m (p21) [IMG]
* Quant: 0.15 m depth to track bed (p27) [IMG]

### Nevill Street, Tunbridge Wells
* Highway flooded and impassable by cars; fire service pumped out water from properties. (p7) [TAB, PAG]
* Two properties required pumping out by fire crews; the highway was flooded and impassable to cars. (p8) [PAG, IMG]
* Surface water ponding in excess of 0.5 m depth along Nevill Street between the junctions with London Road and Market Street; floodwater reached shop doorsteps and affected vehicles; floodwaters had deposited silt and gravel on the carriageway and sidewalks; the road had drained within two hours of rainfall ceasing. (p15) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface water / urban flooding at the low-lying junction with The Pantiles and Warwick Park (approx. 80.3mAOD). Flow routes converged in this low point, leading to inundation at street level and contributing to basement flooding of nearby buildings. Local media published photographs of flooding at this junction on the event date. (p16)
* {01 Nov 2013 - 30 Nov 2013, 01 Dec 2013 - 31 Dec 2013, 01 Feb 2014 - 28 Feb 2014, 01 Oct 2014 - 31 Oct 2014, 24 Aug 2015}: Combined sewer network surcharged during intense rainfall causing drains to back up into properties; surface water could not enter the sewer network quickly enough and accumulated in low points, reaching depths sufficient to enter properties in some locations. (p17) [MUL]
* {12 Aug 2015 - 17 Aug 2015, 13 Aug 2015, 24 Aug 2015, 16 Sep 2015, 21 Sep 2015}: Highway drainage blockages caused by road resurfacing works (12–17 August 2015) and damaged drainage pipework near Nevill Street contributed to blocked drains and increased surface water flows toward low points, raising flood risk and contributing to internal flooding at nearby properties. (p18) [PAG, MUL]
* Surface water flooding during the 24 August 2015 event with highway gullies on the western side of Nevill Street (adjacent to the Pantiles) found during clean-up to contain some tarmac in their sumps; these deposits were not significant and did not block outlet pipes. A nearby manhole showed a build-up of silt at an access to a non-return valve but this was not causing a blockage; CCTV showed the non-return valve to be in full working order. Damaged pipework near Warwick Park (close to Nevill Street) from utility works was noted and may have contributed to higher surface water flows towards low points on Nevill Street. Small amounts of solid debris were recovered during maintenance/clean-up. (p19) [PAG]
* Internal flooding of properties requiring pumping out; drains were cleared by responders and CCTV survey of public drainage adjacent to Nevill Street was carried out to assess the system. (p20)
* Highway surface water flooding occurred in the Nevill Street/Warwick Park area during the intense rainfall on 24 August 2015, contributing to highway flooding and use of combined sewer capacity; investigation recommended directing highway drainage to the culverted River Grom to improve resilience and free up combined sewer capacity. (p29)
* Quant: 2 properties; 2 fire engines (p7) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 2 properties; 2 fire engines (p8) [PAG, IMG]

### River Grom
* Overflowing watercourse caused gardens to flood and led to internal flooding of a lower-ground property; flow damaged a culvert headwall. (p8) [PAG, IMG]
* {general}: The open (un-culverted) upstream section of the River Grom has experienced flooding in the past. The river drains areas north of the railway and around the Nevill Cricket Ground and is registered as a public sewer; a Combined Sewer Overflow (CSO) and surface water sewers discharge into the culverted section. (p13) [PAG]
* High flows in the River Grom on 24 August 2015 overwhelmed culvert and channel capacity, leading to overflow at the culvert entrance, internal flooding to one property and debris-related damage to the culvert trash screen. (p21)
* The culverted section/entrance to the River Grom was implicated as a receptor/route for surface water during the 24 August 2015 event; recommendations include assessing connection of highway drainage to the culvert and improving the trash screen at the river entrance to manage flows. (p29)
