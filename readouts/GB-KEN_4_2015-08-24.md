(p17) [MUL]

These issues may have contributed to higher surface water flows toward the low point at Nevill Street during the 24 August 2015 heavy rainfall and related flooding. (p18) [PAG, MUL]

### Tunbridge Wells
* The Tunbridge Wells area was affected by flooding (report heading states a flood event impacted the area). (p1) [PAG]
* Significant flooding on 24 August 2015 that inundated properties and caused disruption to traffic and transportation. (p5)
* Widespread flooding reported across the Tunbridge Wells area on 24 August 2015: highways and pedestrian areas flooded and impassable; multiple residential and commercial properties internally flooded; railway track flooded and closed until flooding subsided. (p7) [TAB, PAG]
* Multiple localised incidents of flooding occurred across central Tunbridge Wells on the date of the event, affecting streets, public spaces and transport access in the town centre. (p8) [IMG]
* Localised, intense summer storm produced heavy rainfall over Tunbridge Wells, coinciding with the first reports of flooding received at 17:34 on the day; this caused surface water / flash flooding in the Tunbridge Wells area. (p9) [IMG]
* Central Royal Tunbridge Wells (notably the Pantiles/Nevill Street/Warwick Park area) experienced surface-water flooding on 24 August 2015, reported in local media with photographs. The area includes low points (e.g. Nevill Street, The Pantiles, Warwick Park, Market Street, Sussex Mews) where floodwater accumulated, and many buildings affected have basements below street level. The uFMfSW map shows these locations as high risk for surface-water flooding. Existing drainage discharges to the public combined sewer and the area has a history of sewer hydraulic overload (reported at Warwick Park in 2009 and 2012), contributing to flood impacts. (p16) [IMG]
* {13 Aug 2015, 24 Aug 2015, 16 Sep 2015, 21 Sep 2015}: Public combined sewer capacity was exceeded during the 24 August 2015 event, causing flooding across the town; a concrete blockage in a combined sewer lateral (recovered from the pipe) likely increased the extent of flooding. Internal flooding of properties in the area between Cumberland Walk, Nevill Park and Warwick Park was reported on 13 Aug, 16 Sep and 21 Sep (subsequent events). (p18) [MUL]
* The central area of Royal Tunbridge Wells (area of interest) experienced surface water flooding risk and localized surface water flooding during the event, with impacts concentrated around London Road and Castle Street. (p24) [IMG]
* Flooding of the railway track bed caused by an overflowing manhole; required temporary closure of the Tonbridge to Hastings rail line. (p27) [IMG]
* On 24/08/2015 intense rainfall exceeded drainage capacity across Tunbridge Wells, causing surface water to collect in topographical low points and reach depths sufficient to enter property; combined sewer surcharging occurred at the railway station causing flooding to the track bed and disruption to train services. (p28) [PAG]
* Quant: 2 properties (Nevill Street); 2 properties (Market Street); 1 property (Warwick Park); 1 property (Sussex Mews); at least 2 properties (London Road); 1 commercial property (Castle Street); 1 commercial property (Mount Pleasant Road) (p7) [TAB, PAG]
* {13 Aug 2015, 24 Aug 2015, 16 Sep 2015, 21 Sep 2015}: Quant: one residential property; two commercial properties (p18) [MUL]
* Quant: 0.15 m (depth of floodwater on the station track bed) (p27) [IMG]

### Warwick Park, Tunbridge Wells
* Residential basements and gardens were flooded; one property flooded internally; gardens flooded due to an overflowing watercourse; culvert headwall was damaged by flow. Fire service pumped out water using a light portable pump. (p7) [TAB, PAG]
* Central shopping and pedestrian areas (Nevill Street, The Pantiles, Cumberland Walk, Warwick Park, Market Street) experienced localised flooding and inundation during the event, affecting public spaces and streets. (p8)
* Localised flooding occurred in Warwick Park adjacent to Nevill Street as part of the wider surface water inundation in the Pantiles area. (p15) [PAG]
* {2009, 2012, 24 Aug 2015}: Warwick Park lies at a low point within the affected area; it is within the area shown as high risk of surface water flooding and the culverted River Grom runs beneath it. The Tunbridge Wells Surface Water Management Plan records prior instances of foul and surface-water flooding here due to hydraulic overload of sewers. The park and nearby properties were impacted by floodwater during the event. (p16) [PAG, MUL]
* {2009, 2012, 01 Nov 2013 - 30 Nov 2013, 01 Dec 2013 - 31 Dec 2013, 01 Feb 2014 - 28 Feb 2014, 01 Oct 2014 - 31 Oct 2014, 24 Aug 2015}: Drainage hydraulic overload and a blockage in the combined sewer system (lead to surcharging of the network feeding a submersible pump at Warwick Park) during heavy rainfall, causing surface water and sewer-related flooding that affected the block of properties served by the pump (between Cumberland Walk, Nevill Park and Warwick Park); surface water also accumulated in low points reaching depths sufficient to enter properties in some locations. (p17) [PAG, MUL]
* {13 Aug 2015, 24 Aug 2015, 16 Sep 2015, 21 Sep 2015}: A piece of concrete was recovered from a combined sewer lateral pipe leading to a submersible pump at Warwick Park; this blockage is linked to increasing the extent of flooding during the 24 August 2015 event and is a contributory factor in repeat internal flooding reported on 13 Aug, 16 Sep and 21 Sep. Reports of blocked highway drains along Warwick Park were received and noted during a site walkover; a drain near Nevill Street had pipework damaged and local drain access for cleaning has been problematic. (p18) [PAG, MUL]
* The River Grom overflowed the culvert entrance during heavy rainfall causing internal flooding to one property; water collected in several gardens to a depth in excess of 1 m; high flows and debris damaged the culvert headwall's trash screen. KCC later programmed drainage cleansing for Warwick Park and reported one damaged drain for repair; four drains remained requiring further work after cleansing. (p20) [PAG]
* River Grom overflowed the culvert entrance in Warwick Park during heavy rainfall, causing internal flooding to one property; water collected in several gardens to depths in excess of 1 m; high flows and debris damaged the culvert headwall's trash screen. (p21)
* Identified as an area requiring gulley cleaning following the 24 August 2015 event to address surface water/highway flooding and reduce future local flood depths. (p29)
* Quant: 1 fire engine; 1 property (p7) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 1 property; 4 drains (p20) [PAG]
* Quant: one property; gardens flooded to >1 m depth (p21)

## Topics without coordinates

### Kent County Council
* Deployed a road sweeper to clean silt and gravel deposited by floodwater on the road following the flooding. (p15)
* KCC received reports of blocked drains along Warwick Park and recorded blockages during a site walkover; the highway drainage engineer identified damaged drain pipework near Nevill Street. These drainage issues were noted as contributing to increased surface water flows toward low points during very heavy rainfall, including the 24 August 2015 event. (p18)
* Kent County Council (highway drainage engineer and teams) received reports of blocked drains, inspected and undertook cleansing and CCTV surveys during/after the 24th August 2015 flood event. KCC identified a drain near Warwick Park with pipework damaged by utility works, recovered a small amount of solid debris from gullies, and confirmed the non-return valve accessed via the Nevill Street manhole was in working order. (p19) [PAG]
* {24 Aug 2015, 18 Nov 2015}: Deployed a duty officer to site on 24 August 2015 and arranged clearance of debris from the highway after flood water subsided; assisted KFRS to clear drains to aid pumping out of properties on Nevill Street; programmed cleansing of drainage in Warwick Park (18 November 2015) and reported one damaged drain for repair; committed to increase maintenance frequency of highway drains in Nevill Street and Warwick Park to twice a year; undertook CCTV survey of publicly maintainable drainage along and adjacent to Nevill Street (no significant defects found). (p20) [MUL]
* Inspected the trash screen on the culvert entrance after the flood; found that trash carried by floodwater had impeded the trash screen and that the screen would be very difficult and dangerous to keep clear during a high-flow event. (p22)
* Identified and tasked with response actions after the 24 August 2015 event: programme and undertake gulley cleaning in Warwick Park, investigate overland flow through Calverley Park and Calverley Grounds, liaise about the River Grom entrance trash screen, and provide local flood‑risk advice to residents to reduce highway and surface water flooding risk. (p29)

### Southern Water
* Undertook CCTV survey in the Pantiles area and removed a blockage caused by a lump of concrete; removed significant grit and stone build-up from the Warwick Park submersible pumping station wet well (which may have contributed to reduced pumping capacity during the event); inspected the Neville Street CSO screen and completed minor maintenance; updated public sewer maps with recent survey data and are hydraulically modelling the Warwick Park pump. (p20)
* Combined public trunk sewers in the vicinity of Tunbridge Wells Station surcharged during the event on 24/08/2015; Southern Water carried out CCTV surveys on trunk sewers in and around London Road and the station as part of the response and found no defects. (p28)

### Network Rail
