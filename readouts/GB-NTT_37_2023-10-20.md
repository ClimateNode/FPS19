

# Kirklington flooding 20 Oct 2023

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Kirklington – Storm Babet, October 2023<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/kirklington-s19-storm-babet-oct-2023.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Kirklington
* Sustained heavy rainfall during Storm Babet caused surface water flooding in Kirklington; saturated ground led to rapid flooding of roads and properties. (p1)
* Internal property flooding occurred across areas of Kirklington, affecting properties on multiple streets (listed below). (p2) [PAG]
* The village centre and routes through Kirklington were affected by surface water flooding during the storm; Main Street (A617) experienced flows overtopping the highway and internal property flooding, and the Environment Agency mapping shows the highest surface-water flood risk following the river corridor through the centre of Kirklington. (p3) [IMG]
* The village experienced surface-water flows along the A617/Main Street and overtopping of local watercourses (Northern Drain, Southern Drain, Edingley Beck, River Greet) which led to internal property flooding at multiple locations within Kirklington. (p4) [PAG]
* Quant: 4 properties on Main Street; 1 property on Church Lane; 3 properties on Southwell Road; 2 properties in The Crops (p2) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 property (Northern Drain); 2 properties (Southern Drain); 2 properties (Edingley Beck); 1 property (River Greet); 6 properties total reported from watercourse overtopping (p4) [PAG]

### Main Street, Kirklington
* Internal property flooding affected properties on Main Street (A617). (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* During Storm Babet substantial runoff from agricultural land north and south of the A617 flowed down the road, overtopped the highway at low/dropped kerbs and caused internal flooding to properties on Main Street; flows were also supplemented by runoff entering the A617 from the east. (p3) [PAG]
* Internal property flooding occurred across Main Street after surface-water flows (originating on surrounding agricultural land and via the A617) overtopped the highway and entered properties through low/dropped kerbs and other low points. (p4) [PAG]
* Quant: 4 properties (p2) [IMG, PAG]

### Nottinghamshire
* Provided short-term recovery aid for internal (property) flooding via a Community Flood Recovery Grant; visited flood-affected residents and businesses to establish sources/mechanisms and severity of flooding; delivered sandbags to affected areas; closed 77 roads across the county to promote road safety and avoid additional flood damage; provided 24-hour emergency planning support; led the Section 19 flood investigation and collated/published verified flood impact data on Resilience Direct; led procurement and delivery of DEFRA Property Flood Resilience Repair Grant Scheme. (p6)
* Quant: 77 roads (p6)

### Newark and Sherwood
* Visited impacted communities and vulnerable individuals; delivered aqua sacs and assisted with evacuations; provided emergency accommodation where necessary; arranged cleanup of roads and paths and removal of bulky waste from flooded homes as risk shifted from surface water flooding to ongoing river flooding; contacted affected individuals to identify humanitarian needs and deliver or arrange support; commenced recovery role including delivering grant funding to eligible residents. (p6)

### Bilsthorpe
* Community impacted by flooding associated with Storm Babet (20 Oct 2023); actions described include development of a community flood signage scheme, work with NCC Flood Risk Management to develop a local flood action group, and restocking of aqua sacs by parish/town councils. (p7)

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Officers played a key role in responding to the Major Incident affecting the county; initiated a Community Flood Recovery Grant to support individuals who experienced internal flooding; gathered on-site information and fed it back to other RMAs and partners; collated and published verified flood impact data. (p5)
* Acted as Lead Local Flood Authority during and after the incident (20 Oct 2023): coordinated response and recovery, delivered grant funding to eligible households, ran internal and multi‑agency debriefs, supported parish and town councils to restock aqua sacs, developed Flood Warden / Road Closure training and a community flood signage scheme (Bilsthorpe), supported creation of local flood action groups, and is reviewing Property Flood Resilience and riparian enforcement actions. (p7)
