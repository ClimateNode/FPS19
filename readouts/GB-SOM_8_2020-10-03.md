

# Shepton Mallet flooding 03 Oct 2020 - 04 Oct 2020

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Croscombe, Bowlish & Shepton Mallet, 3rd & 4th October 2020<br>113\_Somerset County/Croscombe, Bowlish, Shepton Mallet - October 2020.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### River Sheppey
* Identified as a source of fluvial flooding for the event; contributed to internal flooding of properties (26 properties across Croscombe, Bowlish and Shepton Mallet in total) and associated raised groundwater/seepage impacts. (p5) [TAB]
* The River Sheppey (main river) is heavily modified and culverted through Shepton Mallet; its convergence with the culverted Collett Park Stream and other tributaries contributed to the mapped flooding at the intersection near 4 Leg Square / The Old Surgery and in the north‑eastern town area (including impacts at Lower Lane and adjacent streets). (p16) [PAG, IMG]
* {general}: The River Sheppey channel/culvert through Shepton Mallet is associated with the highest mapped flood risk (Flood Zone 3) concentrated along the river corridor; flood risk extends to adjacent streets (e.g. Longbridge, Lower Lane, Quarr, Garston Street). The channel is heavily modified and culverted through the town (including a culvert discharge near Lower Lane), contributing to localized flood risk in the town centre. (p17) [PAG, IMG]
* {February 1990, March 1996, August 1996, December 2008, November 2011, July 2012}: Historically high river levels have been recorded during multiple notable flood events, contributing to property and local flooding (historic events recorded across the area). (p18) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* River Sheppey overtopped/caused flooding of Long Street / Jack's Lane in Croscombe resulting in highway flooding and approximately 10 properties flooded. (p19) [TAB, MUL]
* The River Sheppey contributed to flooding across Shepton Mallet and nearby Croscombe, causing inundation of streets and multiple properties (examples in the report include Long Street, Church Street, West Shepton and other local streets). (p20) [PAG]
* Associated with Storm Alex heavy rainfall across the catchment (2–4 Oct 2020) leading to high river levels and increased surface runoff; contributed to widespread flooding in the catchment with internal flooding of properties, road closures and travel disruption in nearby settlements (eg Croscombe, Bowlish, Shepton Mallet). (p21) [PAG, IMG]
* The River Sheppey exceeded capacity, producing a rapid surge of water that caused flooding in Croscombe (vicinity of the A371, Long Street, Jack's Lane and Old Street Lane). Approximately 10 properties were reported flooded, with a further 1–2 potential additional properties identified during a 4 December site visit. Four properties on Long Street were flooded internally despite having Property Flood Resilience measures (flood boards, toilet bungs, non-return valves and pumps) installed in 2017. Local residents reported water levels rose very suddenly and to levels not seen in about twenty years. (p23) [IMG]
* River levels rose during the 3–4 Oct 2020 event and exceeded the height of some private rear-garden bridges; owners reported river water tracking underneath the road and entering properties through floor flagstones and subfloor voids, causing internal flooding (including one report of ~300 mm at a front door). (p25)
* Raised bed levels and a narrowing of the channel (noted in front of Manor House and reported at Jack's Bridge) reduced conveyance and contributed to fluvial flooding of properties (notably Manor House) during the 3–4 October 2020 event; community members requested dredging to increase channel capacity. (p27) [PAG]
* River Sheppey exceeded channel capacity during the 3–4 October 2020 event, producing a sudden surge that caused flooding through Croscombe. Flood water flowed through Jack's Bridge spillways on 3 and 4 October 2020, culverts and crossings were identified as potential pinch points and blockages, and surface water/gully surcharging occurred upstream of a culvert inlet on Long Street. Approximately ten properties flooded in Croscombe; four properties on Long Street flooded internally (despite fitted PFR measures). Manor House experienced internal flooding from the mill leat with depths of c.100 mm in one ground-floor room and up to 380 mm elsewhere on the ground floor. The event and channel behaviour also raised concerns about faster conveyance of flows downstream to areas such as Wells. (p28) [PAG]
* Flows along the River Sheppey significantly exceeded channel capacity during the 3–4 October 2020 event, contributing to flooding of adjacent areas (notably Bowlish and Darshill). Residents reported sewerage discharges into the river, evidenced by deposition of toilet paper and wet wipes. (p31) [PAG, IMG]
* River flows significantly exceeded channel capacity, overtopping at locations including the weir at Weirside (flows spilled from the weir and into the culvert under the A371) and contributing to downstream internal property flooding. Residents reported deposition of sewerage detritus (toilet paper and wet wipes) in the river, indicating sewerage discharges into the River Sheppey during the event. (p32) [PAG]
* The Sheppey (the watercourse) experienced high flows within long culverted reaches; culvert capacities were exceeded during the 3–4 Oct 2020 event and contributed to local flooding and surcharge issues in adjacent areas. (p34)
* Historic channel structures (culverts, mill leats, bifurcations, weirs) and elevated flows caused parts of the River Sheppey to exceed conveyance capacity; this led to flooding in Croscombe, Bowlish and Shepton Mallet as culverts and other structures were overwhelmed. (p38) [PAG]
* The River Sheppey and associated weirs/culverts were implicated in the October 3–4, 2020 flooding; locations through Bowlish (weirs/culverts near the Horseshoe Inn and Ham Mill/Weirside) and sections through Shepton Mallet (including Collett Park Stream, the vicinity of Leg Square and under the prison) were targeted for CCTV survey and some locations were later identified as requiring repair. (p40)
* Flooding in the River Sheppey catchment contributed to surface water and sewer surcharging in nearby streets and estates (references to impacts at Pike Hill and the Shaftsgate estate running down Bowlish Lane). The event prompted a flood alert for the catchment and recommendations for stricter surface water management around the River Sheppey. (p41)

### Croscombe
* 10 properties internally flooded (part of the event). Maximum reported internal depth 380 mm in one property. Predominant duration of internal flooding up to 24 hours. Strategic highway flooding estimated up to 200 mm. (p5) [TAB]
* Localised flooding affected the village during the event of 2020-10-03 to 2020-10-04, prompting a site walkover (4 December 2020) with SCC, Wessex Water, Mendip District Council and local residents and formation/activity of the Croscombe Flood Committee to gather information and support recovery and investigation. (p8)
* The village experienced flooding during the October 2020 event with properties affected in parts of Croscombe. During a site walk-over the Croscombe Flood Committee reported that no properties flooded east of the junction of Rock Street and the A371 (Long Street), implying flooding occurred elsewhere in the village; the investigation therefore focused on areas around the A371/Long Street and nearby lanes. (p11)
* {general}: Flood risk mapping shows high, medium and low surface-water flood risk concentrated along the River Sheppey through the centre of Croscombe. High-risk areas are tightly clustered along the river and adjacent low-lying land; potential flow paths follow roads and natural depressions and could affect properties and infrastructure near the A371, St Mary’s Church, the playing field and other nearby assets. (p13) [PAG, IMG]
* Properties in Croscombe have flooded from surface water and from blocked culverts/drainage; drainage improvements (additional gullies) were installed by Area Highways after the 2008 event to reduce surface water impacts. (p18) [PAG, MUL]
* 03–04 October 2020: River Sheppey flooding in Croscombe (Long Street / Jack's Lane) flooded the highway and about 10 properties in the village. (p19) [PAG, TAB, MUL]
* Flooding from the River Sheppey and intense surface-water runoff affected the village: multiple properties flooded (records show clusters of property impacts including up to 10 properties on Long Street / Jack's Lane, 4 properties on Long Street & Church Street, 3 properties elsewhere, and isolated single properties), highways (Long Street, western end of village, Thrupe Lane and other local roads) were inundated, and local drainage/culverts were blocked contributing to the flooding. (p20) [PAG]
* Multiple locations in Croscombe were affected by flooding during Storm Alex. Surface water and River Sheppey-related flooding are reported to have caused internal flooding of properties (noted counts include clusters of 3 properties in one entry) and highway impacts. (p21) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* Approximately 10 properties were flooded in the village during the event; a further 1–2 properties at the western end were identified as potentially flooded during a site visit. Local residents described the source as the River Sheppey exceeding capacity with a rapid surge in water levels. (p23) [IMG]
* Around 10 properties were reported flooded during the 3–4 October 2020 event (with a further 1–2 properties identified as possibly flooded during a site visit). Flooding occurred in the vicinity of A371/Long Street, Jack's Lane and Old Street Lane. Long Street acted as a conduit for exceedance flow from west of The George Inn to the Elderwell footpath. Four properties on Long Street flooded internally (despite previous PFR measures). Manor House flooded internally via the mill leat with recorded depths of c.100 mm in one ground-floor room and c.380 mm elsewhere on the ground floor. Jack's Bridge spillways were discharging flood water, a culvert (~150 m length) adjacent to A371/Long Street and outfalling by Laurel Villa was identified as potentially blocked, and gullies upstream of the culvert were reported to have surcharged. An Environment Agency flood alert was issued at 10:21 on 2020-10-03. (p28)
* Flooding on Long Street near the George Inn made travel impassable westwards towards Wells; flooding outside the village to the west; the A371 Long Street west of Croscombe was flooded and one car was written off. Temporary traffic diversions were put in place to reroute vehicles around the flooded section. (p30) [MUL]
* Flooding on the A371 (Long Street) to the west of the village and flood outside the village to the west caused travel disruption, closure of the A371 through the west end of the village, and implementation of temporary diversions; at least one vehicle was written off. (p31) [PAG]
* The village experienced flooding in the event described (contrasted with Shepton Mallet in the report). (p34)
* Flooding occurred in the village as flows in the River Sheppey and rapid surface water runoff exceeded conveyance capacity (including culverts and other structures), leading to inundation. The exceeded river capacity also had a follow-on impact on the foul sewer network with potential ingress of flood water into sewers and potential sewer discharge. (p38)
* Multiple properties in Croscombe experienced internal property flooding despite having previously installed Property Flood Resilience (PFR) measures; PFR measures failed to prevent internal inundation and require review and possible remedial actions. (p40) [MUL]
* {21 Nov 2016}: The report to Croscombe Parish Council concerns the flood of 2016-11-21; that event prompted CCTV surveys and a programme to map weirs, culverts and identify maintenance/upgrade responsibilities. (p41) [PAG]
* Village experienced flooding (residents requested additional hydrological information and monitoring). The event prompted community requests for a rain gauge and for event-by-event rainfall analysis to identify vulnerable locations, and engagement with the Croscombe Flooding Committee and local authorities to develop mitigation and planning. (p42) [PAG]

* Flooding occurred in Shepton Mallet on 2020-10-03 to 2020-10-04; this prompted an online meeting with Shepton Town Council (14 December 2020) to present the event to SCC, MDC, EA, Wessex Water and community members as part of the investigation and response. (p8)
* Surface water / urban flooding where multiple culverted watercourses (River Sheppey, Collett Park Stream and tributaries) converge in the north‑eastern part of the town; flooding is indicated at the intersection near 4 Leg Square / The Old Surgery affecting the Leg Square / Gaol Lane / Lower Lane / Cornhill area. (p16) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* {February 1990}: Shepton Mallet experienced flooding during the event. (p17) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* {February 1990, March 1996, August 1996, December 2008, November 2011, July 2012}: Numerous historic flood events have affected properties in Shepton Mallet (records of property flooding from surface water, blocked culverts/drainage and high river levels). (p18) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* {11 Jul 2012}: 11 July 2012 and other dates (recorded historically): River Sheppey and surface-water/ drainage incidents have flooded highways and properties in parts of Shepton Mallet (examples in 2012 include impacts on Cannards Grave Road, Lower Lane and other streets). (p19) [TAB, MUL]
* Widespread flooding across Shepton Mallet during the event: surface water on multiple highways, blocked culverts/drainage, and river flooding from the River Sheppey. Numerous streets and several properties were recorded flooded (report lists multiple affected properties on Long Street, Church Street, Jack's Lane, Leg Lane/Leg Square, West Shepton and others). (p20) [PAG]
* During Storm Alex (early October 2020) Shepton Mallet experienced widespread flooding causing internal flooding of properties, road closures and travel disruption across the town (reported impacts include affected highways and multiple flooded properties, e.g. Leg Lane and Leg Square). (p21) [PAG, IMG]
* During 3–4 October 2020 the area experienced significant flooding: multiple properties had internal flooding (including water rising through floor flagstones and ingress from beneath floors), river levels exceeded some private garden bridges, culverts (including one adjacent to A371 Long Street) were observed vegetated/potentially blocked and were reported blocked during the event, Jack's Bridge was reported blocked/exceeded capacity, and surface water run-off flowed through spillways cut into Jack's Bridge. One property reported ~300 mm of water at the front door. (p24)
* On 3–4 Oct 2020 Shepton Mallet experienced flooding from a combination of surface water and manhole surcharge caused by high water levels in the culverted River Sheppey and Collett Park Stream (both culverts described as beyond capacity). Reports describe a 'gush of water almost resembling a waterfall', water flowing down Gaol Lane into Leg Square, and surcharge from manholes (including referenced manholes). (p34)
* Flooding occurred in Shepton Mallet when River Sheppey flows and rapid surface water runoff exceeded the capacity of river structures (including culverts and weirs), leading to inundation. The exceeded river capacity impacted the foul sewer network, with potential ingress of flood waters into sewers and possible discharge from the sewer network. (p38)
* The River Sheppey (through Bowlish, near the Horseshoe Inn and Ham Mill/Weirside) had weirs and culverts identified for targeted CCTV survey following the event; surveys found locations where repair works are required to address flood-related damage and reduce future flood risk. (p40) [MUL]

### Jack's Bridge
