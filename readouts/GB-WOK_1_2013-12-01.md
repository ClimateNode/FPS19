### River Loddon
* Fluvial (river) flooding along the River Loddon during the winter 2013/14 event. Aerial photography and parish reports identified some of the worst-affected areas along the Loddon, and multiple parishes in the borough (Arborfield, Swallowfield, Shinfield, Charvil and Hurst) were associated with Loddon-related flooding in the investigation. (p13) [TAB, PAG]
* Areas along the River Loddon were among the worst affected in the borough; aerial photography and reports indicate the river contributed to flooding of adjacent areas and properties. (p14)
* River Loddon contributed river flooding to Swallowfield during the winter 2013-14 event, as one of the sources causing flooding in the village. (p22)
* The River Loddon came out of bank and flooded the fields to the east and west of the watercourse; the floodwater did not reach properties or highways. (p23)
* The River Loddon overtopped its banks north of Swallowfield and flooded fields to the east and west of the channel. The flood water did not inundate properties or highways. (p24) [PAG]
* The River Loddon burst its banks north of the A327 Arborfield Road, sending significant volumes of water toward the road and contributing to flooding of nearby properties and raised water in rear gardens. (p28) [PAG]
* The River Loddon (Main River) came out of bank during winter 2013-14, raising lake levels and contributing to flooding of adjacent areas in Charvil, including inundation of lakes, roads and external areas of properties. (p32) [PAG]
* High water levels on the River Loddon resulted in fluvial flooding that impacted Whistley Mill Lane and nearby properties; debris and fallen trees in the river exacerbated the flooding severity and duration. (p37)
* Flooding of the River Loddon inundated the Loddon Drive area during the winter 2013-14 event. Sewage flooding also occurred when sewage was released from the Thames Water Sewage Treatment Works into the flooded River Loddon and when private sewage treatment facilities in individual residences were inundated. (p41)
* River Loddon came out of bank in the Loddon Drive/Wargrave area during the winter 2013–14 event, contributing to prolonged flooding of the island and properties. The river had been silted/clogged prior to the event (slowing flows). The downstream sewage treatment works discharged untreated effluent into the River Loddon during the event, so floodwater in the river was contaminated by sewage; private foul drainage in the area was likely inundated. (p43) [PAG]
* Flooding from the River Loddon flowed across the floodplain into properties (including The River House); flows were reported to be fast with a strong current, making access unsafe. (p44) [PAG]

### Swallowfield
* {general}: Parish is shown on the borough map as affected during the winter 2013/14 floods; experienced parish-level property flooding associated with the high river and groundwater levels and regional rivers overflowing their banks. (p9) [IMG]

* Was affected by surface water (pluvial) flooding, experienced sewer flooding, and was the only parish affected by groundwater flooding during the winter 2013/14 flood events. (p12)
* Surface water flooding affected Swallowfield during the winter 2013/14 event; Table 1-1 lists Swallowfield as affected and notes River Blackwater and River Loddon as valley influences with 'Surface Water' as the primary source. (p13) [TAB]
* Properties in Swallowfield experienced external property flooding during the winter 2013/14 event. (p14) [TAB]
* The village suffered flooding from three sources in winter 2013-14: surface water runoff from surrounding farmland, sewage from a surcharging Thames Water foul network, and river flooding from both the Loddon and Blackwater. High groundwater levels worsened flooding. Highway flooding was evident across the village. (p22) [PAG]

Separately, ingress of surface water and groundwater into the foul sewer network during the winter 2013-14 event caused sewage flooding in the village: households suffered blocked lavatories and non-flowing wastewater; foul water mixed with flood water on local streets and sewage debris was left on roads requiring clearing. (p25) [PAG]
* Ingress of floodwater into the foul sewer system led to reduced sewer capacity, resulting in sewage back-up and sewage flooding affecting the village after periods of heavy rainfall. (p26)

### Wokingham Unitary Authority
* {13 Dec 2013 - 25 Dec 2013, 30 Dec 2013 - 08 Jan 2014, late January - first week February 2014}: Between 13 December and Christmas Day 2013 heavy rainfall (up to 100mm in places) caused the River Loddon and the River Blackwater to burst their banks. Repeated storms through late December, early January and into January–February 2014 kept river and groundwater levels high, contributing to inundation of the Thames floodplain and localised property flooding in areas close to the River Loddon, River Thames and River Blackwater. (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* Recurrent heavy rainfall and high river and groundwater levels caused rivers (including the River Loddon and River Blackwater) to burst banks and added large volumes of water to the Thames floodplain, resulting in widespread internal and external property flooding across multiple parishes within the borough; Lower Thames reached highest levels for more than 65 years. (p8) [PAG, IMG]
* {winter 2013/14}: Widespread flooding across the borough in winter 2013/14: River Loddon and River Blackwater burst their banks (noted between 13 Dec and Christmas Day 2013), sustained heavy rainfall and high groundwater produced repeated high river levels and inundation of floodplain areas, causing property flooding across Wokingham. (p9) [PAG, IMG]
* During the winter 2013/14 (01 Dec 2013 - 28 Feb 2014) parts of Wokingham Borough experienced multiple sources of flooding: river flooding affected settlements within the borough (notably Wargrave, Shinfield and Charvil); surface water (pluvial) flooding affected areas including Swallowfield and Charvil; sewer flooding occurred in Swallowfield and Hurst; and high groundwater levels in parts of Wokingham contributed to other types of flooding. (p12) [PAG]
* Winter 2013/14 flooding across Wokingham Borough affected six parishes and multiple villages; properties were flooded both internally and externally and some residents were displaced. Site visits (Sept–Nov 2014) found a small number of houses still uninhabitable due to flood damage. (p14) [PAG]
* Experienced fluvial flood warnings and on-the-ground impacts during winter 2013/14: roads were flooded and impassable or worsening flooding to properties (prompting road closures), sandbags were supplied to residents, blocked drainage/structures were cleared and pumps used, and some residents suffered sewage backup requiring provision of Portaloos. (p15) [PAG]
* Quant: 88 properties flooded in the borough total; 40 properties flooded internally; 48 properties flooded externally; approximately 5 houses still uninhabited (site visits Sept–Nov 2014). (p14) [PAG]

### Whistley Mill Lane, Wokingham Unitary Authority
* Experienced significant fluvial flooding during the winter 2013-14 event; a number of residential and commercial properties along Whistley Mill Lane were affected by this flooding. (p35)
* Fluvial flooding from the River Loddon caused the river to come out of bank at the ford and flood along the lane; properties and yards (including Lands End Stables) became inaccessible by car (only 4x4 access for some properties) and residents had to wade to access properties; yard, outbuildings, animal stables and commercial units at Lands End Stables were flooded; borehole and septic tank were inundated causing loss of clean drinking water.
