### Calderdale
* Severe flooding across the borough on 9 February 2020 when river levels rose rapidly following intense, localised rainfall. Residential and commercial properties were inundated, transport and communications infrastructure were disrupted and recovery/planning actions were required. (p3) [PAG]
* The February 2020 storm caused widespread flooding across Calderdale towns (Hebden Bridge, Todmorden, Mytholmroyd, Elland, Sowerby Bridge, Luddenden) producing mass property and infrastructure damage: major roads and bridges were seriously damaged; a significant number of residential and business properties were flooded; eight schools were temporarily closed (two schools experienced significant damage); council buildings including Hebden Bridge library suffered damage. (p9) [IMG]
* {22 Jun 2012, 06 Jul 2012 - 09 Jul 2012, 25 Aug 2012, 29 Jul 2013, 15 Nov 2015, 12 Dec 2015, 25 Dec 2015 - 26 Dec 2015, 19 Nov 2016 - 22 Nov 2016, 21 Oct 2017}: Repeated river, surface-water and sewer flooding across the borough in multiple historic events. Impacts included large numbers of properties flooded, extensive damage to highways and drainage infrastructure, bridges and culverts, damage to substations, schools and other critical infrastructure, and in one event a building was washed away in Mytholmroyd. (p10) [PAG, MUL]
* {29 Jul 2013, 15 Nov 2015, 12 Dec 2015, 25 Dec 2015 - 26 Dec 2015, 19 Nov 2016 - 22 Nov 2016}: Multiple events produced surface-water and river flooding along the corridor. 29 July 2013 heavy rain caused overtopping and surface-water driven flooding affecting Walsden, Todmorden and Hebden Bridge with damage to rail (Calderdale Line at Walsden Station), a culvert on Walsden Water, and highway/drainage on the A6033 and A646. Surface-water flooding affected Todmorden on 15 November 2015 and 12 December 2015. The flood wave in Nov 2016 travelled from Todmorden to Brighouse producing a series of reports and property inundation. Rapid river rises on 25-26 December 2015 caused significant river flooding affecting Brighouse and other downstream communities. (p11) [MUL]
* {29 Jul 2013, 15 Nov 2015, 12 Dec 2015, 25 Dec 2015 - 26 Dec 2015, 19 Nov 2016 - 22 Nov 2016, 21 Oct 2017, 02 Apr 2018, 16 Mar 2019, 28 Jul 2019, 07 Nov 2019}: Multiple flood events across the borough caused widespread property, business and transport impacts. Impacts included: properties flooded in Walsden, Todmorden, Hebden Bridge, Mytholmroyd, Sowerby Bridge, Brighouse, Cornholme, Callis and other localities; damage to railway infrastructure (Calderdale Line at Walsden Station; trainline flooded near Bridge 96 causing suspension of service); highway and drainage damage on the Rochdale (A6033) and Burnley Road (A646); a building washed away in Mytholmroyd; damage to substations, schools, roads and bridges including Elland Bridge; numerous road closures and localised cellar/internal flooding. Flooding mechanisms included river bank overtopping on the River Calder and tributaries, surface water/runoff from hilltops, overwhelmed sewer capacity and blocked/impeded drainage features and culverts. (p12) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* {15 Nov 2015}: Widespread flooding across the borough during the 15 November 2015 event that prompted post‑event data collection and multi‑agency response; CMBC Flood Risk Management Team and Highways Department collected data following the flood in collaboration with the Environment Agency, fire service, Canal & River Trust, Yorkshire Water Services, Network Rail, Northern Powergrid and local flood groups. (p14)
* Extensive fluvial and pluvial flooding on 9 February 2020 (Storm Ciara) that caused flooded highways and transport infrastructure, and inundation of residential and business properties and other essential infrastructure across the borough. (p16) [IMG]
* {26 Dec 2015, 09 Feb 2020 - 10 Feb 2020}: The Calderdale area experienced an intense, concentrated rainfall event in early February 2020: gauges across Calderdale recorded very high return period estimates (notably at Gorpley and Halifax/Ogden locations), indicating an extreme precipitation episode that affected the borough and formed the focus of the February 2020 flood investigation. The event is compared against depth–duration–frequency curves to estimate rarity and duration (notably 9‑ and 12‑hour durations). (p19) [MUL]

Calder gauges also show a secondary peak on the falling limb about 12 hours after the event peak. (p25) [IMG]
