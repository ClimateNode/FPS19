

# Girton flooding 02 Jan 2024

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Girton – Storm Henk, January 2024<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/girton-s19-storm-henk-jan-2024.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Girton
* {01 Jan 2024 - 31 Jan 2024}: Girton was the subject of a Section 19 investigation following flooding associated with Storm Henk; the report title and introduction link the January 2024 flood event to Girton. (p1) [PAG]
* River Trent and the Fleet flooded out of bank during Storm Henk (02/01/2024). The Trent surcharging prevented the Fleet discharging, which in turn caused the Mill Dam Dyke and other surface-water assets to surcharge; this led to internal flooding in the village and associated evacuations. (p3) [PAG]
* The mapped area including Girton falls within Flood Zone 3 on the Flood map for Planning and was identified as being affected by the flooding event; indicated high risk of river flooding across the western and central parts of the village during the event. (p4) [IMG]
* River Trent flooding at Girton prompted a Flood Warning; high water levels caused blockages which were cleared once water levels receded. A multi-agency community drop-in was held and information officers visited to gather impact data. (p6)
* Quant: 25 properties (p3) [PAG]

### Nottinghamshire
* Storm Henk on 02/01/2024 produced heavy rainfall that led to widespread flooding across Nottinghamshire. River Trent surcharging and resulting knock-on failures of local watercourses and surface-water assets caused flooding across catchments along the Trent corridor. (p3)
* Flooding across the county led to delivery of sandbags to affected communities and the closure of roads to protect at-risk areas and promote road safety. (p6)
* Quant: 69 roads (p6)

### Newark and Sherwood
* Communities across the district experienced surface water flooding which then continued as river flooding. The event led to evacuations (residents helped to evacuate their homes), the need for emergency accommodation, distribution of large numbers of aqua sacs, and subsequent cleanup operations (cleaning roads and paths and removal of bulky waste from flooded homes). The council also contacted affected individuals to identify humanitarian needs and commenced recovery actions including grant funding where eligible. (p6)

### River Trent
* The River Trent surcharged and flooded out of bank, directly preventing The Fleet from discharging to it and contributing to internal flooding of properties in Girton. (p3) [PAG]
* High river levels on the River Trent caused flooding at Girton, triggering a Flood Warning and resulting in blockages that were cleared after water levels receded. (p6)
* Quant: 25 properties (p3) [PAG]


## Topics without coordinates

### The Fleet
* The Fleet flooded out of bank (was surcharged) and was unable to discharge into the River Trent, contributing to knock-on impacts to Mill Dam Dyke and internal flooding of village properties. (p3)
* Quant: 25 properties (p3)

### Mill Dam Dyke
* Experienced a knock-on surcharge (unable to discharge effectively) because upstream watercourses (River Trent and The Fleet) were surcharged, contributing to wider failure of surface water assets and internal flooding in the village. (p3)

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Major incident with significant impacts across the county including internal flooding of residents and businesses. Council officers attended sites to verify internal flooding, offered support/advice, collated and published verified flood impact data, provided 24-hour emergency planning support, chaired Strategic/Tactical/Recovery coordination, initiated a Community Flood Recovery Grant, visited flood-affected residents and businesses to establish sources and severity, and led the Section 19 Flood Investigation. (p5)

### Girton Community Flood Signage Scheme
* The scheme was activated during the storm; volunteers closed several roads within the village to limit the impact of bow-wave flooding on properties. (p7)
