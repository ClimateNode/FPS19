

# Nayland flooding 14 Aug 2020 - 15 Aug 2020

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Birch Street, Stoke Road & Mill Street, Nayland With Wissington<br>124\_Suffolk County/124\_2021\_Suffolk County/section-19-report-birch-street-nayland-with-wissington-aug-21.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Suffolk
* Nayland with Wissington experienced surface water runoff and localised surface water flooding due to intense rainfall and overwhelmed highway drainage. (p4) [PAG]
* {general}: Suffolk County Council (Highway Authority / LLFA) carried out post‑flood gully cleansing and are leading investigations to progress and implement solutions to the existing highway surface water drainage problems; also providing residents with information on property‑level resilience measures. (p5) [PAG]

### Nayland
* The investigation area (Nayland with Wissington) experienced surface water flooding following intense rainfall; surface water flowed from Gravel Hill through Birch Street and Stoke Road to Mill Street. Internal flooding of properties occurred during the single flood incident. (p2) [MUL]
* The parish area (Nayland with Wissington) experienced a short, intense rainfall event leading to surface water runoff that drained to low points at the junctions of local streets (Birch Street/Bear Street and a low point on Mill Street), producing localised surface water flooding of the highway and splash impacts to adjacent properties. (p4)
* Quant: 5 properties (p2) [MUL]

### Birch Street, Nayland
* Surface water flowed from Gravel Hill on to Birch Street (then onward to Stoke Road and Mill Street). (p2) [PAG, MUL]
* Surface water flowed onto Birch Street and collected at the low point (junction with Bear Street), causing internal flooding to properties on the street. (p3)
* Surface water runoff from the surrounding catchment flowed along Gravel Hill through Birch Street. On Birch Street the surface water was mainly contained within the highway, but vehicles driving through standing water caused water to splash up into adjacent properties. The highway drainage system on Birch Street was unable to cope due to heavy siltation in old pipework, reducing drainage capacity and slowing drainage after storms. (p4) [PAG]
* Quant: 7 properties internally flooded (p3)

### Stoke Road, Nayland
* Surface water flowed from Gravel Hill on to Birch Street and Stoke Road (then onward to Mill Street). (p2) [PAG, MUL]
* Surface water flowed onto Stoke Road from uphill, contributing to internal flooding of properties on the road. (p3)
* Quant: 2 properties internally flooded (p3)

### Mill Street, Nayland
* Surface water flowed on to Mill Street (after flowing from Gravel Hill via Birch Street and Stoke Road). (p2) [PAG, MUL]
* Surface water flowed to a second low point on Mill Street, causing internal flooding of properties on the street. (p3)
* Quant: 2 properties internally flooded (p3)

### Gravel Hill, Nayland
* Surface water runoff from Gravel Hill flowed downhill onto Birch Street and Stoke Road and contributed to flooding at downstream low points. (p3)
* Identified as the upstream catchment/high ground where surface water exceeded capacity and flowed overland from Gravel Hill into Birch Street and onward to Bear Street, initiating the local surface water flooding sequence. (p4)
* Quant: Contributed to internal flooding of 11 properties (p3)

### Bear Street, Nayland
* A low point at the junction of Birch Street and Bear Street collected surface water runoff, contributing to local flooding and impacts to nearby properties. (p3)
* Surface water runoff drained to the low point at the junction of Birch Street and Bear Street causing standing water at the junction and splash into properties adjacent to the road when vehicles passed through at speed. (p4) [PAG]

### Bear Street, Nayland
* Surface water followed the overland exceedance route from Gravel Hill through Birch Street and onto Bear Street, producing standing water on the highway and causing splash into adjacent properties. (p4) [PAG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Suffolk County Council
* As Lead Local Flood Authority, SCC determined a Section 19 investigation was necessary following surface water flooding in Nayland with Wissington that caused internal flooding in the investigation area and published/ notified RMAs; investigation commenced 18/08/2020. (p2)
* Highway drainage on Birch Street was unable to cope with the volume of surface water runoff, leading to overland flow from Gravel Hill through Birch Street onto Bear Street; water was mainly contained within the highway but vehicle splash affected adjacent properties. Suffolk Highways (part of Suffolk County Council) undertook post‑flood gully cleansing and are progressing investigations and potential solutions to bypass or replace the silted surface water drainage system. (p4)
* Quant: 5 properties (p2)

### SCC Highway
* Highway surface-water drainage was overwhelmed/insufficient during the flood event, prompting post-flood cleansing of gullies and requiring the Highway Authority to investigate and implement solutions (bypass/replace existing surface water drainage) to address highway drainage problems that contributed to surface-water flooding. (p5) [TAB]
