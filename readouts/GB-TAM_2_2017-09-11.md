

# Mossley flooding 11 Sep 2017

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Investigation Report: Micklehurst Road, Mossley Flood Event<br>129\_Tameside/129\_2017\_Tameside/FloodResponseMicklehurstAut17.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Micklehurst Road, Mossley
* The road was inundated with flowing and pooled floodwater, causing road obstruction and surrounding parked vehicles to be partially submerged; normal access and activity on the street was disrupted. (p1) [PAG, MUL]
* The highway was flooded when the flow of the Micklehurst Brook Culvert was disrupted; the road flooding formed part of the event that affected nearby properties. (p7)
* Culvert collapse at the side/rear of 29 Micklehurst Road following extensive rainfall led to flooding in and around a number of properties and the highway; downstream culvert damage created a void beneath a hardstanding area; a temporary road closure was put in place between numbers 9 and 43 to minimise risk. (p11)
* {11 Sep 2017, 05 Oct 2017}: Properties on Micklehurst Road were flooded internally and externally. A culvert in the rear garden of 33 Micklehurst Road became blocked and surcharged (5 October 2017); the stone culvert was found blocked with debris and the structure unstable. Remedial works followed. (p12) [TAB, MUL]
* {12 Sep 2017}: Shallow surface water covered the full width of the road, flooding parked cars and affecting access to residential properties; a resident is seen observing from a doorway. (p13) [IMG]
* Flooding related to a collapsed culvert at the rear of properties on Micklehurst Road led to road closure, grating clearing at the confluence of Staley and Micklehurst Brooks and surcharging water. A collapse adjacent to 29 Micklehurst Road was exposed and, while repairing that, a further collapse and surcharging occurred to the rear of 33 Micklehurst Road with damage requiring replacement construction. (p15)
* Surcharge flows between rear gardens of properties on Micklehurst Road and Earnshaw Clough were managed by re‑profiling an overground channel and restoring the culvert; works on the brook/culvert in the vicinity of garages opposite 34 Micklehurst Road were identified and restored, and the road was closed for these works with reopening expected by autumn 2018. (p16) [PAG]
* Road was closed while culvert condition and failures in the vicinity of garages opposite 34 Micklehurst Road were investigated and repaired; works were required to restore culverted sections and allow the road to be reopened. (p17) [IMG, PAG]
* A culvert serving Micklehurst Road (from the rear of 33 Micklehurst Road to the grating at the rear of 63/65) was inspected after flood-related issues; a CCTV survey was limited because large rocks and stones were located close to access points, flow in the culvert was noted as good but not at capacity, and movement of these rocks could restrict flow. It was recommended to review the culvert further and replace the old grating at the rear of 63/65 with a modern inlet structure. The Environment Agency installed CCTV at the confluence of Staley and Micklehurst Brooks to remotely monitor debris on the grating for proactive maintenance. (p18) [PAG]
* Residents on Micklehurst Road were affected by the autumn 2017 flood; they received newsletters, attended community meetings and were kept informed about restoration works and timescales. (p19)
* A culvert and inlet located between 35–65 Micklehurst Road were identified as problematic during the September 2017 Tameside flood; the road and adjacent properties were affected and the culvert/inlet were recommended for upgrading and re-profiling to reduce future flooding. (p20) [TAB, PAG]
* A blocked culvert behind 29 Micklehurst Road caused discharge of water and led to property flooding in the residential area. Emergency response measures included placing sandbags around a manhole to prevent overflow, deploying flood barriers and sandbags along the road, and sandbagging the entrance of a stone residential building. Repair work to address the blocked culvert was ongoing. (p22) [IMG]
* Quant: 1 culvert collapse at 29 Micklehurst Road; road closure spanning properties numbered 9–43 (p11)
* {11 Sep 2017, 05 Oct 2017}: Quant: 10 properties internally flooded; 27 properties externally flooded; 5 properties internally and externally flooded (p12) [TAB, MUL]
* Quant: 2 properties (29 and 33); damage extended over 3 metres in length (p15)

### Tameside Metropolitan District
* {general}: Metal trash screen at the mouth of a culvert had water visibly flowing through it with leaves and debris accumulated at the base. The screen was preventing large debris from entering the culvert but shows that high flows / transported debris were present and could risk blockage if not cleared. (p5) [TAB, PAG]
* On 11 September 2017 the flow in the Micklehurst Brook Culvert was disrupted, causing flooding that affected Micklehurst Road (the highway) and a number of properties in Mossley. (p6) [PAG]
* A flood event on 11 September 2017 in Mossley (within the Metropolitan Borough of Tameside) affected the highway and a number of properties. (p7)
* The flood event placed pressure on local infrastructure across the borough and required a significant multi‑agency response (local emergency services, EA, TMBC). (p11) [IMG, PAG]
* Intensive upland rainfall on 11 September 2017 caused flooding of a number of properties and the highway downstream. Flooding was driven by runoff from Saddleworth Moor and Buckton Moor and was exacerbated by debris in the Micklehurst Brook culvert and the culvert's age, construction and abrupt changes in direction. (p21)

Garden restoration works for affected properties were delayed by winter conditions but were part of the post‑flood recovery. (p16) [PAG]

### Micklehurst Brook
* Debris at the confluence with Staley contributed to grating blockage and localised flooding; downstream culvert collapse adjacent to properties (29 and 33 Micklehurst Road) caused surcharging and collapse that required excavation and replacement. (p15)
* The Environment Agency identified the Micklehurst Brook (Main River) downstream of the confluence with Staley Brook as requiring culvert restoration following the flood investigation. Works focused on culvert condition in the vicinity of garages opposite 34 Micklehurst Road and additional culvert sections were scheduled for restoration to reduce surcharging and flooding towards the River Tame. (p16) [PAG]
* {11 Sep 2017, autumn 2018}: Culverted sections of Micklehurst Brook were found to be failed or blocked; sections were assessed and restored (including renewal of failed culvert sections and installation of access chambers) to bring the culvert back to expected standard and reduce flood risk towards the River Tame. (p17) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Debris was washed into or existing debris was disturbed in the Micklehurst Brook culvert; the culvert’s age, construction and abrupt changes of direction contributed to blockage, which led to flooding downstream that affected properties and the highway. (p21)

### Saddleworth Moor
* Intensive rainfall on 11 September 2017 occurred on the uplands of Saddleworth Moor (and Buckton Moor), generating large runoff volumes that had a major impact downstream and contributed to property and highway flooding. (p21)

### Tameside Metropolitan Borough Council
* Following extensive rainfall a culvert collapsed at the side/rear of 29 Micklehurst Road causing flooding in and around a number of properties and the highway; the collapse created a void beneath a downstream hardstanding area and a temporary road closure was put in place between numbers 9 and 43 Micklehurst Road. (p10) [IMG]
* Tameside MBC was directly involved in the response to flooding and culvert collapse; local authority actions included managing the temporary road closure and addressing infrastructure damage. (p11)
* {05 Oct 2017}: Tameside MBC Engineers undertook remedial works in response to the culvert surcharge and blockage at Micklehurst Road: constructed two new access chambers and installed a new 10 m length of culvert over the following approximately four months. (p12)
* Tameside MBC staff attended the grating to the rear of 63/65 Micklehurst Road and excavated a suspected blockage adjacent to 29 Micklehurst Road, exposing a fully collapsed culvert with debris (tyres, bricks, garden items, pallet). Surcharging of water caused a further collapse at the rear of number 33 extending over three metres and requiring replacement works. (p15)
* Funded restoration works (via a Grant in Aid bid) alongside the Environment Agency to repair culverts and undertake channel works following the flooding, to restore the brook/culvert to expected standard. (p16)
* Tameside MBC assessed the culvert section serving Micklehurst Road following flooding issues, carried out a CCTV survey (limited by large rocks), undertook recent works on the section from the rear of 33 Micklehurst Road to the grating at the rear of 63/65, and committed to keep residents informed. (p18) [PAG]
* Led communications and coordination with the Environment Agency to keep residents of Micklehurst Road and the wider Mossley community informed about actions, timescales and restoration works following the autumn 2017 flood; produced and distributed newsletters and took part in community meetings. (p19)
* {01 Sep 2017 - 30 Sep 2017, 11 Sep 2017}: Tameside MBC (Highways and LLFA) were involved in the response to the September 2017 flood; highways drains and a culvert/inlet on Micklehurst Road were identified for maintenance or upgrade. TMBC was recommended to maintain highway drains, assess inlet designation, seek funding to upgrade the culvert between 35–65 Micklehurst Road, and ensure planning applications are flood-risk assessed. (p20) [TAB, PAG]
* Tameside MBC carried out a Section 19 investigation into the September 2017 flood (11 September 2017), met with residents and with other RMAs (United Utilities, Environment Agency, AGMA), and recommended actions to increase resilience following the event. (p21)
* Quant: 29 Micklehurst Road; temporary road closure between numbers 9 and 43 Micklehurst Road (p10) [IMG]
* Quant: excavation approximately 2 metres deep; collapse extended over 3 metres in length (p15)

### New HDPE Culvert
* Culvert restoration/installation undertaken as part of post‑flood repairs; re‑profiling of an overground channel was completed to return surcharge flows into the culvert (flow returns to the culvert at the rear of 33 Micklehurst Road). Garden and land restoration associated with the works was delayed by adverse winter weather and completed later in spring 2018. (p16)

### Culvert
* A section of the Micklehurst Brook culvert in the vicinity of garages opposite 34 Micklehurst Road was found to be blocked/failed after the autumn 2017 event and required restoration. The failed culvert section has been renewed and new access chambers/maintenance access provided to reduce future blockage-related flooding; works necessitated temporary closure of Micklehurst Road while repairs were carried out. (p17) [IMG]
