### Torbay
* Torbay experienced a continuous five-hour period of rain with a short period of extremely intense rainfall on 27th August; the intense short-duration rainfall produced conditions associated with surface water / flash flooding across the Torbay area (rain gauges in Torquay, Paignton and Brixham recorded intense totals during the event). (p5)
* The area experienced flooding impacts during the intense storm: flooded roads required diversion arrangements; road gullies and trash screens were blocked and cleared; blown manhole covers were replaced (indicative of surcharged sewers); multiple calls were received reporting flooding throughout the day. (p6) [PAG]
* Torbay experienced a flooding event on 27 August 2020 that prompted investigations and remedial actions (drainage/gully clearing, sewer system condition checks, watercourse maintenance and advice to property owners on groundwater/flood protection). (p20)

### Torbay Council
* Torbay Council received multiple calls (first at 15:00) and TOR2 staff were deployed to manage flood impacts: set diversion arrangements for flooded roads, install warning signs, sandbag locations to prevent highway flooding, replace blown manhole covers (assumed surcharged public combined sewer), clear blocked road gullies, clear trash screens and carry out post-event clean-up. (p6) [PAG]
* Following the flooding event in Torbay on 27 August 2020 the council, as Lead Local Flood Authority, is responsible for coordinating investigations and delivery of recommended actions (inspection/clearing of highway drainage and gully cleaning, investigation of surface water and sewer issues, and overseeing maintenance of watercourses) to reduce future flood risk. (p20)
* A flooding event occurred within Torbay on 27 August 2020; vulnerable areas within the council area were identified and affected, prompting the council (as Lead Local Flood Authority) to prioritise and monitor delivery of recommended actions to reduce flood risk. (p21) [PAG]

### Occombe Valley Watercourse
* Hydraulic overload of the Occombe Valley watercourse causing internal flooding to properties (Manor Crescent) and external flooding to Occombe Valley Road. (p16) [TAB]
* {general}: Hydraulic overload of the Occombe Valley watercourse caused external flooding to the highway (Occombe Valley Road). (p17)

### Cockington Catchment
* The catchment experienced an intense 15-minute rainfall event which the report uses to derive a hydraulic frequency: the event equates to a return period of 1 in 15 years for the Cockington catchment. (p6)
* Quant: return period of 1 in 15 years (p6)

### Somerset Fire and Rescue Service
* Devon and Somerset Fire & Rescue attended a number of flooded properties during the storm event to assist with pumping out. (p6)

### South West Water
* South West Water's call centre received numerous telephone calls regarding flooding during the storm. Where manhole covers were replaced by Torbay Council's contractor it is assumed the public combined sewer was surcharged. (p6)
* Identified as a Risk Management Authority to investigate the condition and capacity of the combined sewer system in the affected areas following the Torbay flood event and to identify necessary repairs or improvement works to reduce future sewer-related flooding. (p20) [PAG]

### Fleet
* Flooding started high in the catchment of the River Fleet and contributed to surcharging of the combined sewer network upstream (leading to downstream urban/highway flooding). (p7)

### Fleet Catchment
* Due to lack of capacity in the combined sewer network much flood water bypassed retention tanks (including the tank in Ellacombe Park), contributing to flooding in the catchment. (p8) [TAB]

### Environment Agency
* Responsible for investigations and maintenance where flooding is from an ordinary watercourse or main river; may enforce maintenance to reduce river/ordinary watercourse related flooding as part of the response to the Torbay flood event. (p20) [PAG]
