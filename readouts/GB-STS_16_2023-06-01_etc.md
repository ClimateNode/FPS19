

# Tutbury flooding 01 Jun 2023 - 31 Jul 2023 and Anslow flooding 01 Jun 2023 - 31 Jul 2023

### Anslow
* Main Street in Anslow was flooded with water covering the road, people walking through floodwater and at least one vehicle partially submerged; roads, vehicles and local properties were disrupted by the surface water exceedance. (p5) [IMG]
* Anslow experienced surface water/urban flooding caused by extreme rainfall that exceeded the design standard of the drainage infrastructure; flooding occurred on the dates given in the report and prompted identification of improvements to reduce future impacts. (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* Floodwater accumulated in the middle of the village (Main Road and Bushton Lane), causing flooding to residential properties and businesses. (p7) [IMG, MUL]
* Intense short-duration rainfall events in June and July 2023 produced rapid runoff through the local catchment, causing surface-water pooling in the central built-up area and along Main Road. Depression storage in the central area reached depths up to 65 cm and stored about 680 m3, resulting in local inundation of the road and adjacent open spaces. (p13) [IMG]

Flooding occurred during the June–July 2023 storm events recorded in the area. (p14) [IMG, PAG]
* Residents and businesses reported internal property flooding within the village. (p15) [PAG, IMG]
* Significant surface-water flooding from very intense, short-duration thunderstorms in June–July 2023; residents reported foul sewage mixed with floodwater. Drainage networks in the village were jetted and CCTV-inspected as part of the response. (p22)
* High-intensity storms overwhelmed local highway drainage leading to an identified defect in the drainage/gully system. The defect was repaired and additional drainage and an overflow connection between networks were installed to reduce future flood impacts. (p23)
* Highway and local drainage were affected leading to remedial works: gullies were jetted and cleansed; a defect near Bushton Lane was repaired; a new Birco channel drain was installed and two systems were connected to provide an overflow. * Quant: 65 cm; 680 m3 (p13) [IMG]

### Mill Fleam

* Observed heavy silt accumulation within the watercourse; siltation was reported as likely to restrict outfall and surface water sewer discharge during heavy storms, adversely affecting flow within the Mill Fleam and increasing local flood risk. (p19) [PAG, IMG]
* Identified as the local watercourse conveying surface water runoff during the intense, localised July flooding; the Environment Agency will undertake a maintenance assessment to determine if remedial works are required following the event. (p20)
* The Mill Fleam watercourse around the sewer outfalls was found to be heavily silted; this was identified as a factor that could impede discharge during heavy storms and thus contribute to local flooding. Environment Agency maintenance of the channel is under review. (p23)
* Identified as the receiving watercourse for local drainage networks in the area affected by the intense thunderstorms on 8 July 2023; associated with the flooding incident in Tutbury that produced internal flooding of residential and commercial properties and significant highway flooding. (p26)

## Topics without coordinates

### Severn Trent
* Surface water sewers for which Severn Trent Water is responsible would not be expected to convey the extreme rainfall volumes, contributing to surface water exceedance and flooding of roads, properties and vehicles during the intense storms. (p5)
* Carried out investigations of assets that outfall to the Mill Fleam and confirmed their outfalls were clear; reported the Mill Fleam to be heavily silted, likely to restrict flow from Severn Trent's surface water pipe network and outfalls during heavy storm conditions and thereby affect flow in the Mill Fleam. (p19) [PAG]
* Residents reported foul sewage mixed with floodwater. Although Severn Trent had not directly received reports from residents, they will CCTV the sewer network in the area to confirm whether sewers were affected. (p22)
* Inspected outfalls and carried out checks on the sewer network in Tutbury following the significant flooding (8 July 2023); reported no network concerns at the time. Committed to continue responding to reports of obstructions and to carry out CCTV of the sewer at the junction of Bushton Lane and Main Road in Anslow and share results with the LLFA. (p24) [TAB]

### Highway Authority
* Highway drainage (gullies and highway drains) were unable to convey the extreme volume of surface water during the intense short-duration thunderstorms, leading to surface water exceedance along highways and localised flooding of low-points, resulting in flooded roads and stranded/impeded vehicles. (p5)
* Undertook immediate response works: jetted and CCTV-inspected drainage networks, identified and repaired a defect in the red network adjacent to Bushton Road junction, installed a new Birco drain to increase inlet capacity to the green network, and created a new connection from the red to the green network to increase resilience. (p22)
* Intense short-duration thunderstorms overwhelmed highway drainage and gullies. The Highway Authority inspected and cleansed gullies in affected areas, jetting and cleansing gullies (Tutbury). Some gullies had higher silt levels and were cleansed and running on departure. In Anslow a gully/drainage defect was identified and repaired and additional drainage and an overflow connection between networks was installed. (p23) [PAG]

### Staffordshire County Council
* As the Highway Authority, assets under its responsibility (highway gullies and drains) were implicated in the events where drainage could not convey the rainfall volume, contributing to surface water exceedance along highways. (p5)
* County Council highway gullies and interconnecting pipework were involved in the response: teams jetted and cleansed gullies across the affected area; concluded gullies were operating within tolerances on the day but were unable to cope with the volume of water caused by the rapid rise of the Mill Fleam. (p17)

### Tutbury Catchment
* The Tutbury catchment (the 77 ha area draining to Bridge Street) produced runoff volumes and flow rates that contributed to surface water/urban flooding in Tutbury and at Bridge Street. (p8) [IMG]

### Anslow Catchment
* The catchment (approx. 9 ha) draining towards Main Road produced rapid runoff during the intense rainfall events. A 20 mm rainfall (assumed no infiltration) would generate an estimated total runoff of ~1800 m3 and, over one hour, flows in excess of 500 l/s, contributing to downstream surface-water flooding and ponding on Main Road. (p13)
