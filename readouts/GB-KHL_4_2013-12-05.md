

# Kingston Upon Hull flooding 05 Dec 2013

Reference document:<br>Hull City Council Flood Investigation Report: December 2013 City Centre Tidal Surge Flood Event<br>064\_Kingston upon Hull, City of/TidalSurgeDecember2013.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Kingston Upon Hull
* Record tidal levels and a positive surge caused seawater ingress into parts of the city (notably English Street), flows spread into the city centre and as far west as Hessle Road; defences limited overtopping but low points (Riverside Quay/Albert Dock) allowed seawater into the dock which then flowed into the city; overall large-scale tidal flooding impacts across the city. (p4)
* Tidal inundation (from the Humber tidal source) caused flooding of properties and roads across central and southern parts of Kingston upon Hull including the city centre and docklands; flood flow routes are indicated across the urban area on the map. (p8) [IMG]
* Flooding entered the city around the time severe flood warnings were issued (18:56 on 5th December); CCTV recorded deep, fast-flowing flood waters in the English Street area; riverside defences (e.g. Victoria Dock) showed evidence of overtopping as surge and wind/wave direction changed and forecasts increased. (p12)
* Flood flows moved through the city centre, causing severe traffic disruption, inundation of buildings and vehicles, and closure of the A63. (p16)
* City defences generally withstood the tidal level; the council opened the Civic control room, set up rest centres and evacuated residents from care homes on Victoria Dock Village and Ferensway. (p18)
* The city experienced tidal flood threat and impacts on 5 December; defences prevented inundation across vast parts of the city but flood flows did pass into the city where defences were overtopped (including sewer and riverside inundation in parts of the urban area). (p19) [PAG]
* Experienced a tidal surge that produced record tide levels in the city; existing flood defences protected properties but warnings were issued close to the onset of flooding limiting time for emergency deployment. Recommendations include full topographic surveys of all flooded locations, review of incident room activities, and incorporation of model findings into planning and policy. (p22) [TAB]
* Quant: 115 businesses; 149 residential properties; 19,000 properties protected (p4)
* Quant: 114 businesses and 149 residential properties (p12)
* Quant: more than 50 residents (p18)
* Quant: more than 19,000 properties (p22) [TAB]

### River Humber
* The storm surge moved down the East coast into the Humber Estuary and caused tidal flooding in East Yorkshire; resulted in flooding on the north bank (East Riding) and significant flooding on the south bank of the Humber. (p4) [PAG]
* A tidal surge in the Humber arrived about 30 minutes before the astronomical tide, producing a peak tidal level recorded at 5.8mAOD at Hull which caused significant ingress of seawater into the city (English Street area and city centre) and contributed to widespread flooding. (p5)
* A tidal surge increased levels in the River Humber, causing overtopping and contributing to flooding across the city's southern boundary; this surge led to overtopping and road flooding at locations including St Andrews Quay retail park and Victoria Dock Village residential area. (p6)
* Tidal surge from the River Humber was the sole flood source for the event on 5 December 2013, driving tidal inundation into Kingston upon Hull that produced the mapped flooded properties, flooded roads and flood flow routes shown in Figure 1. (p8) [IMG, PAG]
* A tidal surge on 5 December 2026 (initial forecasts 5.5m AOD, later increased by about 400mm) combined with changing wind/wave direction increased surge levels and the risk of overtopping defences, prompted severe flood warnings and consideration of evacuations and led to flooding into Hull. (p12)
* High sea levels and surge tides on the Humber contributed to overtopping of riverside defences and consequent flooding across the city, leading to inundation of properties and businesses. (p13) [PAG]
* Tidal surge/flows in the Humber caused overtopping of defences and contributed to tidal inundation and overwash of riverside areas affecting the city and port facilities. (p19)
* Quant: more than 400 properties (p4) [PAG]

### Albert Dock
* At the height of the tidal surge (peak ~5.8mAOD) sea water entered into Albert Dock over defences and through the lock gates; once the dock rapidly filled this water flowed onwards into the city and contributed to flooding of the city centre. (p5)
* Identified as the origin/source area for tidal ingress during the event; low defences allowed tidal water to enter the dock area and into local sewer/drainage, contributing to sewer surcharging and downstream flooding impacts. (p14) [PAG]
* Defence levels at Albert Dock are known to be lower than other parts of the city and were a focus during the flood event; temporary and long‑term raising of defences was being considered following the event. The dock was the subject of defence operations during the 5th December tidal/extreme levels. (p17) [IMG]
* Riverside areas of Albert Dock were overwashed by tidal waters; the volume of water grossly exceeded the dock basin's storage capacity and so could not prevent flood flows entering the city. (p19)
* Tidal surge caused overtopping into the dock, raising dock levels; once the dock filled, flood waters passed from the dock into the city. Investigation found that water entering the dock over low points in the riverside defences far exceeded the amount entering through the lock. The dock provided some informal storage which limited impacts elsewhere but did not prevent significant flooding on the date of the event. (p21)
* Operational procedures for the lock gates were not fully finalised during the tidal surge; recommendations call for full and final implementation of procedures and consideration of optimising agreements so the dock can provide an informal flood storage role. Defence improvements in the Albert Dock area were identified as a highest priority following the event. (p22) [TAB]
* The riverside quay at Albert Dock had the lowest defence levels in the city; significant tidal flood waters overwhelmed the dock and flowed through it into adjacent areas (notably English Street). (p23) [PAG]

### Environment Agency
* The Environment Agency owns the Reverse Head Restraint System (RHRS) for the dock lock; details of operation with ABP were incomplete at the time and the RHRS/vertical bolt locking system could not be fully deployed during the event. The EA is a lead agency for investigating and progressing improvements to Humber frontage defences following the flood. (p5)
* Environment Agency forecasts predicted a high tide surge that tested defences; flood warnings were issued close to the expected surge peak and were issued almost at the same time as the onset of flooding in the city (CCTV shows deep, fast-flowing flood waters in the English Street area at the time severe warnings were issued). (p12)
* The EA managed and closed council-operated flood gates at 16:00 and placed large sandbags as part of the response; minor seepage was noted around an EA-managed Dry Dock flood gate, which led to standing water ponding on nearby streets. (p14)
* The Environment Agency supplied the Reverse Head Restraint System (RHRS) intended to keep the Albert Dock lock gates closed during extreme tidal levels and were appraising works to renew/raise Hull Humber frontage defences as a response to flood risk. The EA was directly involved in defence provision and operational matters during the 5th December event. (p17)
* The Environment Agency operated and managed the city’s defences during the event and surveyed all defences around the estuary following the flood. (p18)
* Environment Agency managed and operated the city's tidal flood defences and warning/forecasting arrangements; their work defended vast parts of the city from tidal inundation and their operations were key to defence performance during the event. (p19)

### Salt End Pumping Site
* Pumping operations were initiated at the Salt End pumping site to evacuate tidal flood water from the inundated sewerage system as part of Yorkshire Water's response. (p19)
* Quant: more than 350,000 tonnes of tidal flood water (p19)

### Saltend Water Treatment Works
* Received discharge of flood waters from the sewerage network during the recession of the Humber tidal surge, acting as the discharge point for pumped/flowed flood water. (p5)

### Dry Dock Flood Gate
* Minor seepage at an expansion joint and around the flood gate allowed standing water to pond on Nelson Street and Humber Street; the gate was closed as part of the flood response. (p14)

### Mytongate Roundabout
* Flood flows spread to either side of the roundabout causing severe traffic disruption in the area. (p16)

### Staples Car Park
* Flow route was observed across the Staples car park, with water spreading into adjacent streets. (p16)

### A63
* Flood flows caused severe traffic disruption and led to closure of the A63 on the evening of 5th December; the road remained closed into 6th December and reopened at midday. (p16) [IMG]
