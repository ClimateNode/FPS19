

# Hellidon flooding 02 Jan 2014

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Church Lane, Hellidon, 2nd January 2014<br>090\_West Northamptonshire/Hellidon - January 2014.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Church Lane, Hellidon
* {general}: Surface water from fields immediately south of Church Lane flowed onto the public highway because the historic field ditches were completely filled with fine‑grained sandy soil and there is no means at the field access low point to prevent surface water entering the road. (p10)
* {02 Jan 2014, 14 Feb 2014}: Surface water flowing off agricultural land ponded locally at the access point to the field and around the highway-facing retaining wall of a residential garage building on Church Lane. At least one property's garage was internally flooded. (p11) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* {23 Dec 2013 - 04 Jan 2014}: Surface water flowed off agricultural land onto Church Lane and ponded around the highway-facing retaining wall of a residential garage. Surface water entered the residential garage causing damage to stored items, deposited soil and standing water requiring significant cleaning. Water was held against the retaining wall, seeping through and applying water pressure with potential structural damage to the wall. (p12)
* {02 Jan 2014, 14 Feb 2014}: Muddy, orange-brown runoff and surface water covered parts of the lane and carriageway, reducing accessibility and creating a road safety hazard; runoff appeared to originate from adjacent land/hedgerow and to have flowed across the road. (p39) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Muddy, sediment-laden water flowed along the left edge of the paved road, crossed onto the grass verge and pooled near a stone wall; runoff covered sections of the lane and deposited sediment on adjacent grass and near property boundaries. (p40) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Standing muddy/brown water entered a basement/utility room at properties on Church Lane, covering the floor and reaching the bottom rung of a metal step ladder and partially submerging stored items and an appliance; water is contaminated with sediment. Surface water runoff flowed from the paved road onto the adjacent grassy verge, causing waterlogging and some erosion of the roadside and covering tyre tracks. Localised standing muddy water collected at the base of a stone garden wall, partially submerging small plants. (p41) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Muddy floodwater pooled against a stone wall and inundated an adjacent garden/landscaped area; clusters of plants were partially submerged and soil erosion/runoff is evident. (p42) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Surface-water runoff from adjacent fields deposited muddy water and sediment across the road surface, causing soil deposits and visible damage to the carriageway and verges; created a hazardous, muddy surface for vehicles and pedestrians. (p45) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface water and runoff deposited fine-grained soil and mud onto the roadside and paved surface, causing damage to the verge (muddy tire tracks, disturbed verge) and soil deposits on the road; stakes mark the extent of the damaged verge. (p46) [IMG, PAG]
* {02 Jan 2014, 14 Feb 2014}: Quant: 2 incidents of garage internal flooding (02/01/2014; 14/02/2014) (p11) [TAB, PAG, MUL]

### Hellidon
* Subject of a Northamptonshire County Council flood investigation (Church Lane, Hellidon). (p2)
* Intense short-duration rainfall caused existing land drainage and watercourses to be unable to convey runoff, so excess surface water flowed over ground following contours into low points in the village, resulting in internal flooding of property(ies). (p6) [PAG]
* Intense short-duration rainfall caused existing land drainage and watercourses to be unable to collect runoff, so excess surface water flowed over ground following natural contours to low points in the village, resulting in internal flooding of property(ies). (p7) [PAG, MUL]
* Internal flooding of a residential property (water crossed the threshold). The property experienced internal flooding on multiple occasions; one documented incident occurred on 2 January 2014. (p9) [MUL]
* Incident Plans (Appendix C) indicate reported routes of surface water through Hellidon during the event. (p13) [PAG]
* Residents and properties in Hellidon were identified as being at risk during the Flood Incident; the report advises affected residents to take protective actions (sign up to flood warnings, implement property-level protection, prepare community flood plans, nominate flood wardens and move valuables to higher ground). (p15)
* Intense rainfall on a saturated catchment caused surface water and river flooding in the village. Existing drainage systems, land drainage ditches and watercourses were unable to cope, causing water to flow into other areas and into drainage systems not designed for the additional volume. Run-off from agricultural land was identified as a significant contributing factor. Field drainage systems and outlets were reported to be in poor condition or non-existent, and the routing of surface water from the south of the village through Hellidon to watercourses in the north is unclear — all of which increased flood risk to vulnerable properties in the village. (p17)
* {general}: Surface water routing from agricultural land in the south through the village to watercourses at the north is unclear; field drainage systems and outlets are poor or non‑existent or silted, increasing the risk of surface water flooding to vulnerable properties in the village. (p18) [PAG]
* Quant: one property; more than one occasion in the last 5 years (p6) [PAG]
* Quant: one property (p7) [PAG, MUL]
* Quant: 1 property (p9) [MUL]

### Stockwell Lane, Hellidon
* {24 Dec 2013, 01 Jan 2014 - 28 Feb 2014, 02 Jan 2014, 01 Feb 2014 - 28 Feb 2014}: Residential properties on Stockwell Lane were affected by surface water flowing from the south. At one residential property preventative measures were taken to stop internal flooding and one residential property experienced internal flooding to the kitchen. Highway flooding occurred on Stockwell Lane; road gullies were blocked and one residential property was reported at risk. (p11) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* {23 Dec 2013 - 04 Jan 2014}: Surface water flowed with significant velocity and volume through gardens on Stockwell Lane causing damage to paving and garden items. Surface water entered the kitchen of a residential property on Stockwell Lane (impacts not specified). A separate incident of concrete mixture blocking the gully/drainage on Stockwell Lane contributed to ponding on nearby Stockwell Road. (p12)
* Surface water drainage from the catchment including the corridor from Back Lane to Stockwell Lane was identified as causing flood risk to the area and adjacent properties; insufficient drainage capacity and blocked/failed piped/culvert sections contributed to flooding risk. (p20)
* {general}: Surface water drainage along Stockwell Lane (part of the system from Back Lane to Stockwell Lane) is associated with runoff that creates flood risk to properties; ownership/maintenance of this surface water drainage is unclear and identified for investigation. (p21) [PAG]
* A natural low point and field slope identified as the probable route for a culvert conveying water between Cox's Lane and Stockwell Lane; topography channels overland flow along this route, potentially concentrating runoff and transporting soil deposits toward the culvert alignment. (p47)
* {24 Dec 2013, 01 Jan 2014 - 28 Feb 2014, 02 Jan 2014, 01 Feb 2014 - 28 Feb 2014}: Quant: one residential property internal flooding to kitchen; one residential property at risk (p11) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* {23 Dec 2013 - 04 Jan 2014}: Quant: one residential property (p12)
