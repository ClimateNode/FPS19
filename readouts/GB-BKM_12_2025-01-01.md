### Medmenham
* The village experienced flooding when very high River Thames levels rose and came up the main route (Ferry Lane), inundating roads and flooding houses. High river levels also prevented highways and field drains from discharging. (p4) [MUL]
* A flood incident occurred in Medmenham during the January–February 2014 event, prompting an investigation. The village lies immediately adjacent to the River Thames with the main route (Ferry Lane) running down to the river; the settlement includes low-lying open areas, fields and drainage ditches alongside the Thames that are described in the investigation context as relevant to the flood incident. (p6) [PAG]
* The village is on a natural floodplain south of the A4155 and was affected by flooding dominated by the River Thames. Flood Zone 3 / the 1-in-100-year model extent covers almost the entire village; low-lying land (~30–31 m AOD) and properties along Ferry Lane and near New Lock Lane lie within the floodplain and were at risk of inundation. There are no flood defences on this stretch of river, increasing exposure. (p10)
* {general}: Most of the village lies south of the A4155 on natural floodplain and is shown as Flood Zone 3 — the River Thames dominates flooding for the village. Low-lying parts (around 30–31m AOD), particularly south of the A4155 and near drainage channels, are at high risk of inundation in 1-in-100 year and more extreme river flooding; there are no defences along this stretch of river. (p11) [PAG, IMG]
* The village lies on the natural Thames floodplain south of the A4155 and is shown as being within Flood Zone 3. Flooding in the area is dominated by the River Thames; low-lying parts of the village (notably south of the A4155 and along Ferry Lane, with additional properties near New Lock Lane) are at high risk of inundation. There are no flood defences along this stretch of river and river levels are controlled by nearby locks and weirs, contributing to flood risk. (p12) [PAG]
* {2000/01, 2003, 2012}: Medmenham lies largely on the natural floodplain (Flood Zone 3). Historical floods affected the village: a groundwater flooding event in 2000/01 affected properties; high river levels in 2012 caused flooding around Medmenham; the 2003 Thames floods (the highest since 1947) caused extensive damage to houses in Medmenham and surrounding areas. There are no flood defences along this stretch of the Thames and levels are constrained by upstream and downstream locks and weirs. (p13) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* {2000/01, 2003, 2012}: Multiple flood incidents affecting the village: a groundwater flooding event in 2000/01 affected properties; the 2003 floods caused extensive damage to houses; high river levels in 2012 caused flooding around Medmenham. Flooding patterns concentrate on Ferry Lane South, Ferry Lane North and New Lock Lane, with the majority of properties affected in the southern half of Ferry Lane. (p14) [PAG, MUL]
* The River Thames rose and broke its banks west of Medmenham, sending floodwater north-east across the floodplain into the village. Flooding progressed up Ferry Lane from the slipway, affected the eastern side of Medmenham Abbey and, once banks had breached further west, created a dominant NE flow that inundated wetlands and led to property flooding in the southern half of Ferry Lane (notably south of the white bridge and Abbey Cottages). Some raised properties were surrounded by water. (p15) [PAG]
* When the River Thames was high the land drains backed up and broke their banks, sending floodwater westwards into the main part of Medmenham and inundating gardens and properties; the situation was worsened when the Thames breached its banks west of Ferry Lane. (p16)
* {December 2013, January - February 2014}: Medmenham was impacted by river flooding in January and February 2014 (onset December 2013) with flood marks and resident reports indicating inundation. Local groundwater emergence also caused prolonged flooding of the back gardens of one bungalow. (p17) [PAG, MUL]
* Flooding from the River Thames overtopping affected the Medmenham area in January–February 2014. Properties were inundated; most residents remained in their homes, some used pumps to limit ingress, and two houses were evacuated. Access to some houses was prevented by deep floodwater on local roads (notably Ferry Lane). A tractor taxi was used to move people because roads were unsafe. (p18) [IMG, PAG]
* Flooding from the River Thames and backed-up flows inundated gardens, drives and local roads. Gardens and drive were fully immersed to depths reported between 30 cm and 1 m (about 0.5 m average). A house flooded to a maximum depth of 20 cm in February 2014; other houses had no water inside in January 2014. The boat yard area was flooded (estimated inches depth). Water reached up Ferry Lane to Southlands and covered a bridge; water was reported just inside Abbey Gates and at the level of Weybrooks garage. Bucks Fire & Rescue Service recorded 15 properties flooded in Medmenham during the event. (p21) [TAB, MUL]
* Low-lying areas in Medmenham were flooded when Thames water flowed up Ferry Lane from the south, flooding the road and houses. Ferry Lane and the road were made impassable (only passable by boat or tractor). During the February 2014 episode water was still coming up Ferry Lane (noted 21 Feb) then by 22 Feb was mostly draining via roadside ditches and puddles remained. (p23) [MUL]
* The River Thames at very high levels caused flooding in Medmenham. 23 properties were affected, 10 experienced internal flooding and two homes were evacuated. Around half of properties were impacted either by internal flooding or restricted access; the disruption was extensive and occurred twice over the January–February 2014 events. (p27)
* Experienced flooding associated with the River Thames that led to calls for improved emergency response, temporary defences, community flood planning and investigation of capital works to reduce future flood risk. (p28) [TAB]

* Quant: Ferry Lane water depth up to 80cm; access flooded via Ferry Lane to 60cm (p19) [TAB]

### The Mill
* Gardens were fully immersed and an outbuilding was flooded; owners verified the January 2014 flooding was the same level as January 2003. (p20)
* Quant: 8 acres (p20)

### Mary's Cottage
* The house and its gardens were flooded during the event. (p20)

### Abbey Gates
* Floodwater entered at Abbey Gates and into the adjacent house; houses experienced internal flooding (house water reached a maximum of 20 cm in February 2014). Gardens and drives around properties were extensively inundated (garden/drive depths reported between 30 cm and 1 m, about 0.5 m average). By 12 January water had receded and by 23:00 all water was out of the house. (p21) [TAB, MUL]
* Quant: 20 cm (maximum internal flooding in February 2014); garden/drive depths between 30 cm and 1 m (0.5 m average) (p21) [TAB, MUL]

### Southlands
* Floodwater extended along Ferry Lane up to the Southlands area; water reached but did not enter houses above Abbey Cottages during the peak of the event. (p21) [TAB]
* Floodwater reached up to Southlands on Ferry Lane with fast-flowing water reported at that location; flows from higher ground met at or near Southlands and residents opposite were putting sandbags in front of garages. (p22) [TAB, MUL]

### Ferry Nab
* Floodwater reached Ferry Nab on 12 January 2014. Measured depth at the site was 35 cm on 12/01/2014; after applying a 13 cm correction the recorded peak value is 48 cm. (p34) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 35 cm; 48 cm (p34) [TAB, PAG]

### Buckinghamshire County Council
* Medmenham (low-lying land) was inundated when River Thames floodwaters flowed up Ferry Lane and across fields; Ferry Lane was made impassable except by boat or tractor and houses were flooded to significant depth. (p24)

### Hurley Weir
* Land drains that drain upstream and downstream of Hurley Weir backed up when the River Thames was high; these drains broke their banks and contributed to downstream flooding into the wetlands and westwards towards Medmenham. (p16)

### Medmenham Mill
* The Mill race at Medmenham Mill fed the main drain. During high River Thames levels the drainage fed from the mill race contributed to drain backup and bank failure downstream, driving flooding into the wetlands and westwards toward Medmenham properties. (p16)

### Memenham Gates
* The garden at Memenham Gates was flooded when land drains backed up as the River Thames was high and when the Thames breached its banks to the west of Ferry Lane. (p16)

### Common Moor
* Flooding of the north of Ferry Lane including Common Moor when land drains backed up as the River Thames was high, contributing to property flooding in the area. (p16)

### No 6 Abbey Cottages
* No 6 had no water inside the house but the garden was flooded; access to the property was limited by flooding on Ferry Lane. (p19) [TAB]

### Weybrooks Garage
* Floodwater reached the level of the Weybrooks garage during the event; surrounding water covered the nearby bridge and affected access in the area. (p21) [TAB]

### Willow Cottage
* Front paving of Willow Cottage was reported wet during the flood episode; flooding in the locality prompted sandbagging by neighbours and subsequent recession of water. (p22) [TAB]

### Hambleden Lock
* The River Thames broke its banks south of Hambleden Lock, allowing water to flow across fields toward the site of the former Water Research Centre and contributing to flooding on Ferry Lane. (p24)
