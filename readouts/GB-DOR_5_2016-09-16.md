

# Wimborne Minster flooding 16 Sep 2016

## Place names with coordinates

### The Square, Wimborne Minster
* The Square was within the area described as most affected by surface water flooding during the heavy storm on 16 September 2016; it forms the northern boundary of the affected area in the town centre. (p6) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: The surface water sewer from West Borough falls towards The Square and turns under the main pedestrian area; the East Borough sewer connects under the eastern footpath. The Square contains a slot highway drainage system (Wimborne Square Enhancement Scheme) with gullies and silt traps; if slot drains surcharge or block, pavement levels are designed to convey excess flow along the slot drain line, indicating a risk of surface-water surcharge/ponding and sewer-related surface flooding in the pedestrian area. (p7) [PAG]
* Surface water flood risk extends across The Square in the 1 in 100 year event, and a commercial property in The Square was reported as experiencing internal and external flooding following the heavy storm. (p8) [PAG]
* Internal flooding reported to 1 commercial property; the reporter indicated a further 2 commercial properties were also affected. (p9) [TAB]
* Surface water run-off from the public highway collected at a slot highway drainage location immediately in front of a commercial property during the storm and could not drain quickly enough, resulting in surface water entering and causing internal flooding of the commercial property. (p10) [IMG, PAG]
* {01 Sep 2006 - 30 Sep 2006, 01 Nov 2007 - 30 Nov 2007, 01 Aug 2011 - 31 Aug 2011, 01 Jul 2014 - 31 Jul 2014, 16 Sep 2016}: Surface water run-off from the road and pavement collected at the location of slot highway drainage on the eastern side of The Square during the storm in the early hours of 16 September 2016 and entered a commercial property, causing internal flooding. The slot drainage was later found to be blocked and required jetting. Records and proprietor account also identify previous similar occurrences (including an event thought to be in August 2011) where standing water and bow waves from vehicular movement contributed to water entering the same commercial property. (p11) [PAG, MUL]
* Slot drain jetting was carried out throughout The Square following the storm; the Wimborne Square Enhancement Scheme had installed slot drains and paved footpaths intended to pass excess flows if slot drains surcharged or blocked. (p12) [IMG]
* Quant: three commercial properties (total across The Square, High Street and East Street) (p8) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 commercial property internally flooded; 2 additional commercial properties affected (p9) [TAB]
* Quant: 1 commercial property (p10) [IMG, PAG]

### Wimborne Minster
* The town centre experienced a storm with heavy rainfall in the early hours of 16 September 2016; the town centre area suffered surface water flooding (urban/surface water inundation) affecting the area bounded by The Square (north), East Street (south), High Street (west) and the River Allen (east). (p6)
* Reports of internal and external flooding affecting commercial properties located in The Square, High Street and East Street. (p9) [TAB]
* A heavy storm in the early hours of 16 September 2016 caused surface water/urban flooding across Wimborne Town Centre. In the following days there were reports of commercial properties suffering internal flooding, specifically in The Square, High Street and East Street. (p10) [IMG, MUL]
* {01 Sep 2006 - 30 Sep 2006, 01 Nov 2007 - 30 Nov 2007, 01 Aug 2011 - 31 Aug 2011, 01 Jul 2014 - 31 Jul 2014}: Commercial premises on East Street in Wimborne suffered internal flooding from surface water runoff collected at a low point on the highway (outside 3–5 East Street). Highway flooding during intense rainfall is reported to occur regularly. (p13) [PAG, MUL]
* Highway flooding occurs regularly during intense rainfall, causing surface water flooding in the town centre and affecting highway drainage infrastructure; this flooding is reported likely to have occurred during the events/incidents referenced in the report. (p14) [PAG]
* The town centre experienced surface water flooding risk and/or reported surface water flooding incidents; this prompted actions to review and clear highway drainage, collect as-built records for the Wimborne Square Enhancement Scheme, review surface water sewers and historical flooding information, and consider property-level resilience measures. (p15) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 3 commercial properties reported flooded (p9) [TAB]
* {01 Sep 2006 - 30 Sep 2006, 01 Nov 2007 - 30 Nov 2007, 01 Aug 2011 - 31 Aug 2011, 01 Jul 2014 - 31 Jul 2014}: Quant: properties at 3 to 5 East Street (p13) [PAG, MUL]
* Quant: three locations (p15) [TAB, PAG]

### High Street, Wimborne Minster
* High Street formed the western boundary of the area most affected by surface water flooding in the town centre during the heavy rainfall event on 16 September 2016; the surface water sewer runs southerly along High Street as described in the drainage section. (p6) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: The surface water system falls south along High Street; there is an overflow connection into Mill Lane. During heavy rainfall the High Street is a flow path for surface water toward Mill Lane and the River Allen, creating a risk of surface water flow/ponding and overflow into the connected drainage on Mill Lane. (p7)
* Surface water flowed along the southern end of High Street during heavy rainfall, with internal and external flooding reported at a commercial property in High Street during the storm. Surface water flood maps show risk at the southern end of High Street in a 1 in 30 year event and across High Street in a 1 in 100 year event. The slot drain along High Street (ending between Nos. 1 and 7) was designed with gullies and pavement profiles to convey excess flow if the slot drain surcharged or blocked. (p8) [PAG]
* Internal flooding reported to 1 commercial property; the reporter indicated a further 3 commercial properties were also affected. (p9) [TAB]
* Reports were received of commercial properties on High Street suffering internal flooding during/after the heavy storm in the early hours of 16th September 2016. (p10)
* A commercial property on the eastern side of High Street reported internal flooding during the storm in the early hours of 16 September 2016. Rainfall run-off collected immediately in front of the property and entered when it reached sufficient depth; additional surface water run-off from the car park to the rear collected along the northern wall and entered through the side of the premises. (p11) [PAG]
* Surface water run-off collected immediately in front of properties and contributed to internal flooding; the slot highway drainage system to the north of the affected property was found to be in need of jetting and was subsequently jetted throughout High Street. (p12)
* Quant: three commercial properties (total across The Square, High Street and East Street) (p8) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 commercial property internally flooded; 3 additional commercial properties affected (p9) [TAB]

### Mill Lane, Wimborne Minster
* Mill Lane is part of the surface water drainage network with an overflow connection from the High Street surface water sewer and an outfall into the River Allen south of the bridge. During the 16/09/2016 storm the town-centre surface water system (which includes this overflow route) was implicated in the surface water flooding of the town centre. (p6) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Mill Lane receives an overflow connection from the High Street surface water system and has an outfall into the River Allen to the south of the bridge, so it is a location where overflowing surface water from the High Street may pond or discharge toward the river during heavy rainfall. (p7)

### River Allen
* {general}: The River Allen is the receptor for multiple surface water outfalls (from Mill Lane and from the paved area in front of The Library). The map and text indicate flood-prone zones and flood defenses concentrated near the river, so the river corridor is linked to both river flooding and surface-water flow/ponding at its banks and nearby streets. (p7) [PAG]

### The Library
* {general}: The surface water system turns eastward under the paved area in front of The Library and has an outfall into the River Allen immediately north of the footbridge, indicating that the area in front of The Library and its immediate surroundings are part of a surface water flow route and are at risk of ponding or discharge during heavy rainfall. (p7)


## Topics without coordinates

### East Borough
* The surface water sewer in East Borough connects into the town-centre surface water system under the eastern side of The Square; this connection places East Borough within the drainage network associated with the area most affected by surface water flooding during the storm. (p6) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Surface water sewer in East Borough connects into the main surface water system under the eastern side of The Square, presenting a pathway for surface water to enter The Square and the downstream drainage system during heavy rainfall, with potential for flow or ponding if the system is overwhelmed. (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* {01 Aug 2011 - 31 Aug 2011}: On a previous occasion thought to be August 2011, surface water run-off collected at the highway drainage locations in the East Borough leg of The Square, contributing (with vehicle-induced bow waves) to water entering a commercial property. (p11)

### Wessex Water
* {general}: The town's surface water sewer network connects into the existing Wessex Water surface water drainage system (flows from West Borough and East Borough, via The Square and High Street, with overflows/outfalls to Mill Lane and the River Allen); the system is therefore implicated in potential surcharge/overflow during heavy rainfall. (p7)
* Wessex Water's surface water sewers and drainage infrastructure in Wimborne Town Centre were linked to reported surface water flooding incidents; the company was asked to review infrastructure and reported incidents and to undertake hydraulic assessments. (p15) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: three locations (p15) [TAB, PAG]

### West Borough
* The surface water sewer in West Borough falls towards The Square and feeds into the town-centre surface water system that was within the area most affected by surface water flooding during the storm event. West Borough therefore formed part of the drainage route into the flooded town centre. (p6) [IMG]
* {01 Aug 2011 - 31 Aug 2011}: On a previous occasion thought to be August 2011, surface water run-off collected at the highway drainage locations in the West Borough leg of The Square; vehicular movement through the standing water caused bow waves that contributed to water entering a commercial property. (p11)

### East Dorset District Council
* {01 Sep 2006 - 30 Sep 2006, 01 Nov 2007 - 30 Nov 2007, 01 Aug 2011 - 31 Aug 2011, 01 Jul 2014 - 31 Jul 2014, 16 Sep 2016}: The council's slot highway drainage in The Square suffered blockages (likely from debris associated with events held in the Square) which allowed surface water run-off to collect and enter a commercial property; council inspected and arranged jetting throughout The Square and High Street and inspected surface water manholes. (p11) [MUL]
* {01 Sep 2006 - 30 Sep 2006, 01 Nov 2007 - 30 Nov 2007, 01 Aug 2011 - 31 Aug 2011, 01 Jul 2014 - 31 Jul 2014, 16 Sep 2016}: Quant: four recorded flooding events (September 2006; November 2007; August 2011; July 2014) (p11) [MUL]

### Highways
* Highway drainage infrastructure (drains, gullies and culverts) in Wimborne Town Centre was implicated in surface water / urban flooding risk; actions taken included reviews of connections to the surface water network and routine clearance to reduce surface water flood risk. (p15) [TAB, PAG]
