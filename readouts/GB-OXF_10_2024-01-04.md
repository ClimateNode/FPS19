

# Didcot flooding 04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024

## Place names with coordinates

### Moor Ditch
* Moor Ditch, a main river, experienced hydraulic overload and was overwhelmed, causing flooding of Harriers Park and its access road (impassable to vehicles) and contributing to internal flooding of business premises. (p2)
* {03 Jan 2024 - 04 Jan 2024}: Hydraulic overload and surcharge of the Moor Ditch along its southern and northern banks over an approximate 220 m length caused overbank flooding of adjacent land; the surcharge likely continued downstream though no further properties were recorded as affected (northern side protection by an artificial earth bund limited downstream impacts). (p3) [PAG]
* {01 Jan 2024 - 31 Jan 2024}: Channel overtopping and conveyance contributed to flooding of adjacent commercial areas (Harriers Park / south of Moor Ditch) leading to internal flooding of businesses; recommended monitoring and maintenance (removal of tree branches, keep channel profile clear), consideration of bank raising, remodelling and natural flood management to reduce flows through the catchment. (p4) [TAB, PAG]
* Channel obstruction and channel-form issues identified; monitoring and maintenance (removal of tree branches) recommended to restore the channel profile, and the area has been placed on a pipeline for re-modelling. Bank-raising either side of the Moor Ditch was suggested as a potential flood defence to protect nearby commercial properties. (p5) [PAG]
* The Moor Ditch surcharged its channel in the early hours of 05 January 2024 and overtopped/overflowed, causing floodwater to enter Harriers Park commercial units and the estate road. (p15)
* The ditch surcharged its channel along the northern bank between the downstream end of the access bridge and the Harriers Park land boundary, sending water onto Harriers Park and contributing to inundation of units and the access road. (p16) [PAG]
* The Moor Ditch surcharged its channel along the northern bank between the downstream end of the access bridge and the end of the Harriers Park land boundary, overtopping and contributing to flooding of adjacent commercial premises and Harriers Park. (p17) [PAG]
* Reduced channel capacity (tree branches, heavy siltation) and subsequent overtopping/overflow of Moor Ditch led to flooding of the adjacent commercial forecourt; parked vehicles were partially submerged (photograph dated 5 Jan 2024). The site also abstracts up to approximately 15,000 l/day from Moor Ditch for a cooling tower, indicating very large water volumes in the channel at the time. Hydraulic modelling and topographical survey were used to assess flood levels. (p18) [PAG]
* Moor Ditch overtopped/local water levels rose above bank levels during the event, resulting in inundation of adjacent areas (including Harriers Park and commercial areas on the south side). Observed flood extent reached approximately 52.35 mAOD, exceeding some modelled node levels and indicating greater inflows (from Ginge Brook) than predicted. (p19) [TAB, PAG]
* During the event on 4–5 Jan 2024 Moor Ditch experienced higher flows/levels than predicted by EA modelling, resulting in inundation that entered adjacent commercial sites. Observed inundation reached approximately 52.35 m, exceeding the lowest bank level along the commercial property boundary (52.08 m), allowing water to enter the commercial properties at Harriers Park, the commercial property on the south side of Moor Ditch, and the access road to Harriers Park. No blockage was recorded; investigation suggests additional inflows from Ginge Brook contributed to the higher volumes. (p20) [PAG]
* The Moor Ditch surcharged because runoff from prolonged heavy rainfall exceeded its capacity, causing extensive flooding onto adjacent commercial areas. (p21) [PAG]
* Moor Ditch was identified as contributing flood risk to adjacent commercial properties: recommendations call for monitoring and removal of tree branches/debris, ensuring channel profile is consistent, maintaining ordinary watercourses at rear/side of properties, consideration of bank raising on either side of Moor Ditch as flood defence, and placing the area on modelling/review pipelines to assess flood risk. (p27) [TAB, PAG]
* Overtopping/overflow from the Moor Ditch caused flooding affecting commercial premises in the Harriers Park area; prompted consideration of bank-raising on either side of the ditch as a flood defence. (p28) [PAG]
* The lowest bank level of Moor Ditch (52.08 m) is identified as the critical overflow point where the ditch will surcharge and allow floodwater to enter the adjacent commercial property. (p35) [IMG]
* {general}: Modelled in-channel flood levels and flows along the Moor Ditch reach (Didcot to the Thames confluence). Model nodes (MD31, MD34, MD35, MD36, MD37, MD38, MD41, MD45, MD47) show flood levels up to 52.78 mAOD (0.1PCSYM AEP) and typical 1PCSYM AEP levels in the range ~51.19–52.61 mAOD at different nodes. Modelled flows peak up to 5.62 m3/s (0.1PCSYM AEP) with lower-node 1PCSYM AEP flows around 2.64–4.02 m3/s depending on location and climate change allowance scenario. These model results define the mapped 5PCSYM, 1PCSYM and 0.1PCSYM AEP flood outlines for the reach. (p41) [TAB]
* Quant: 14 business premises (p2)

### Harriers Park
* Flooding of the Harriers Park estate caused by hydraulic overload of the Moor Ditch catchment and connected main river (Moor Ditch). (p2)
* {03 Jan 2024 - 04 Jan 2024}: Flooding within Harriers Park resulting from Moor Ditch surcharge — area adjacent to the channel inundated to a peak depth referenced against unit 13's FFL, with the northern side largely protected by an artificial earth bund which limited further downstream property impact. (p3)
* {01 Jan 2024 - 31 Jan 2024}: Commercial premises within Harriers Park experienced internal flooding; recommendations include Property Flood Resilience (PFR) measures, monitoring and maintenance of the park's surface water drainage system, sign-up to EA flood warnings and setting up a local flood group. (p4) [TAB]
* Commercial premises in Harriers Park are at risk from flooding related to the Moor Ditch; recommended actions include establishing a local flood group and signing up to the EA flood warning system; consideration of bank raising to defend commercial properties. (p5)
* Harriers Park was inundated during the January 2024 flood event following prolonged wet weather and especially heavy rainfall on 03–04 Jan 2024. High flows on the Ginge Brook (gauge near Steventon) and surface water runoff contributed to fluvial and surface-water flooding of the park/adjacent areas. (p9) [PAG]
* Commercial units and the estate access road were inundated after the Moor Ditch surcharged in the early hours of 05 January 2024. Business owners had no warning and were unable to deploy defensive measures, resulting in internal flooding of units and disruption to businesses. (p15) [PAG]
* Floodwater entered commercial units and the access road; internal floor levels and stored goods were inundated. Photographs taken on 5 January show water inside a business unit and across the estate access. (p16) [IMG, PAG]
* Flooding occurred when the Moor Ditch surcharged in the early hours of 05 Jan 2024; the large forecourt and south-side commercial area were inundated to an average peak depth of approximately 0.25 m. The site’s surface water drainage outfall was impeded by significant silt, and tree branches and silted banks were noted as reducing channel capacity, which would have affected drainage and may have exacerbated flooding. (p17) [PAG]
* Site inundated during the event with water reaching approximately 52.35 mAOD; bank at Moor Ditch alongside the park is at 52.14 m allowing water to enter the site. No blockage was recorded; observed inundation exceeded the Environment Agency modelled 1PCSYM and 0.1PCSYM levels at nearby nodes, suggesting higher flows into Moor Ditch than modelled. (p19)
* Commercial properties and the access road at Harriers Park were flooded when the Moor Ditch surcharged during exceptional, prolonged rainfall; this was the worst flooding at Harriers Park for 10 years. (p21)
* Commercial premises within Harriers Park experienced internal flooding risk/impacts. Recommendations note the need for property flood resilience measures, monitoring and maintenance of the surface water drainage system, setting up a flood group for Harriers Park, and signing up premises to EA flood warning systems to reduce future internal flooding of businesses. (p27) [TAB]
* Commercial premises at Harriers Park were flooded by overflow from the Moor Ditch, leading to proposals for bank raising, establishment of a local flood group and advice to sign up to EA flood warnings. (p28) [PAG]
* Units on the side of the estate directly behind the river/ditch bank would become quickly inundated when the Moor Ditch surcharges the lowest ground level; the estate is the first point of surcharge alongside the ditch so floodwater would enter premises rapidly. (p33)
* Harriers Park is shown on the survey with a surface water drainage outfall and an artificial spillway; the lowest ground adjacent to Harriers Park is identified as the first point of surcharge when the Moor Ditch overtops, leading to rapid inundation of the area behind the bank and affecting units on that side of the estate. (p34) [IMG, PAG]
* {03 Jan 2024 - 04 Jan 2024}: Quant: approximately 3.5 ha flooded; peak depth approximately 0.25 m (approximate elevation 52.35 m relative to unit 13 FFL); surcharge along approximately 220 m of channel; bund offset approximately 5 m and construction height approximately 2 m. (p3)
* Quant: peak flood depth of 0.25 m within business units and on the estate road; average peak flood level 0.25 m (p15) [PAG]
* Quant: 0.25 m average peak flood level within units and the access road (p16) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 0.25 m peak flood depth; 1 commercial property (p17) [PAG]
* Quant: approximate peak flood depth of 0.25 m; approximate area affected 3.5 ha; worst in 10 years (p21)

### Ginge Brook
* Ginge Brook surcharged its channel and likely produced exceedance flow that drained into the Moor Ditch, further overwhelming the system and contributing to flooding of Harriers Park and nearby areas. (p2)
* {20 Jul 2007, 01 Dec 2013 - 31 Jan 2014, 01 Dec 2023 - 31 Jan 2024, 04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024}: Elevated river levels recorded on Ginge Brook (gauge near Steventon) have been associated with historic flooding at Harriers Park; river-level data for July 2007, Dec 2013–Jan 2014 and Dec 2023–Jan 2024 were reviewed and linked to flooding at Harriers Park. (p9) [MUL]
* Elevated river levels on Ginge Brook during the Dec 2023–Jan 2024 period produced river flooding that resulted in flooding at Harriers Park. Historical gauge records reviewed (Jul 2007, Dec 2013–Jan 2014 and Dec 2023–Jan 2024) correspond to three flood events affecting the same area; historic peaks include 1.463 m on 20 Jul 2007 and a maximum of 1.32 m in the Dec 2013–Jan 2014 period. (p10) [IMG, PAG]
* River levels on Ginge Brook rose sharply, reaching a peak level of 1.439 m on 05 Jan 2024. This level was the highest recorded at the Steventon gauge since installation in 2006 except for 1.463 m on 20 Jul 2007, indicating a significant high-flow / river flooding event on and around 05 Jan 2024. (p11) [IMG]
* High levels in the Ginge Brook are identified as a possible contributing factor that increased flow paths into the Moor Ditch, exacerbating the surcharging and downstream flooding. (p21)
* {20 Jul 2007, 01 Dec 2013 - 31 Jan 2014, 01 Dec 2023 - 31 Jan 2024, 04 Jan 2024 - 05 Jan 2024}: Quant: three flood events (p9) [MUL]
* Quant: 3 flood events (p10) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 1.439 m (05 Jan 2024); 1.463 m (20 Jul 2007) (p11) [IMG]

### Didcot
* Internal and external flooding affected business premises in the town; the January 4th event caused internal flooding to 14 business units and made the access road to Harriers Park impassable due to depth of floodwater. (p2)
* Areas of Didcot were affected by flooding associated with prolonged wet weather and especially heavy rainfall on 03–04 Jan 2024; this produced fluvial and surface-water flooding across parts of the Vale of White Horse and is therefore likely to have affected Didcot. (p8)
* Parts of Didcot were inundated on 4–5 January 2024 where Moor Ditch overtopping allowed water to enter adjacent sites: Harriers Park and commercial properties on the south side of Moor Ditch were reported inundated to around 52.35 mAOD. The event exceeded local model predictions at nearby nodes. (p19) [TAB]
* Quant: 14 business units (p2)

### Abingdon
* Persistent heavy rainfall across the River Ock catchment, including over 30 mm on 03 and 04 January 2024, produced exceptionally high river flows at the Abingdon gauge and caused flooding in the River Ock catchment on 04 January 2024. The River Ock at Abingdon recorded average flows above 16 m3/s in early January, indicating river-flood conditions affecting the Abingdon gauge location and surrounding low-lying areas along the Ock. (p14) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: over 30 mm of rain on 03 January 2024; over 30 mm of rain on 04 January 2024; average flows of over 16 m3/s on 05 January 2024; average flows of over 16 m3/s on 06 January 2024; gauge has recorded average daily flows above 16 m3/s on 3 occasions (p14) [IMG, PAG]

### Oxfordshire County Council
* Moor Ditch is described as a watercourse contributing to flood risk in the Harriers Park area; maintenance (removal of tree branches, ensuring channel profile) and placing the area on the Environment Agency pipeline for re‑modelling were recommended, and bank‑raising either side of the ditch was proposed to protect adjacent properties. (p5)
* Acted as the Lead Local Flood Authority responsible for investigating the Didcot flood incident under Section 19 of the Flood and Water Management Act; maintaining a register of assets affecting surface runoff, groundwater or ordinary watercourses; notifying and advising third-party asset owners about their assets and maintenance responsibilities; and recording flooding hotspots across the county. (p22)
* Listed as a consulting stakeholder in reviews of modelling and consideration of bank-raising measures following the Harriers Park flooding. (p28) [PAG]

### Moor Ditch Catchment
* Hydraulic overload within the Moor Ditch catchment led to flooding that resulted in inundation of Harriers Park and surrounding areas and contributed to internal and external flooding of business premises; the access road to Harriers Park was impassable to vehicles due to depth of flooding. (p2)
* Quant: 14 business premises (p2)

### Ock Catchment
* Persistent heavy rainfall across the River Ock catchment and very high river flows in early January 2024 produced flooding across the catchment. The unusually high flows recorded at the Abingdon gauge contributed to flooding impacts observed on 04–05 January 2024 (including hydraulic overload of connected channels such as the Moor Ditch). (p15)
* Quant: average flows of over 16 m3/s on 05 and 06 January 2024; long‑term daily average flow 0.92 m3/s; recorded above 16 m3/s on 3 occasions since the 1980s (July 2007; February 2014; January 2024) (p15)

### Environment Agency
* Commercial premises within Harriers Park experienced internal flooding associated with flows in the Moor Ditch and surface water drainage failing; recommendations include monitoring/maintenance of the surface water drainage system, maintenance of the Moor Ditch and artificial spillway, and consideration of bank raising to protect commercial properties. (p27)
* Acting in response to flooding at Harriers Park: tasked to place the area on a modelling pipeline and review national modelling/NaFRA2 representations, to review bank-raising options alongside local stakeholders, and to promote sign-up to the EA flood warning system. (p28) [PAG]
