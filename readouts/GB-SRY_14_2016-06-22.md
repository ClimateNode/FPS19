

# Hinchley Wood flooding 22 Jun 2016 - 23 Jun 2016

## Place names with coordinates

### Hengest Avenue, Hinchley Wood
* Watercourses in the Hengest Avenue area reached capacity during the storm, resulting in flooding of roads and properties and internal flooding of houses. (p4)
* Internal flooding of residential properties on Hengest Avenue. (p5)
* Rear gardens of properties on Hengest Road (sic) were flooded when a blocked trash grille adjacent to a footpath caused water to back up, run onto the footpath and under the fence into gardens. (p10)
* High rainfall on 22–23 June 2016 caused the local drainage network to reach capacity, resulting in external and internal flooding of properties and road flooding on Hengest Avenue. Blocked ditch/trash screen and a blocked twin pipe system were identified contributors. Post-event works included clearing the trash screen, digging out a ditch section, and maintenance of ordinary watercourse and culverts to improve capacity. (p12)
* {01 May 2016 - 31 May 2016}: Modelled surface water flood extents indicate notable flood risk along Hengest Avenue, with the highest risk shown in the 1 in 30 and 1 in 100 categories on the May 2016 map; flood risk follows roadside and low-lying drainage patterns. (p17) [PAG, IMG]
* The silted southern boundary ditch overflowed, causing water to flow down Hengest Avenue and resulting in flooding to the front of properties on Hengest Avenue. Blocked watercourse features nearby also caused backing up of water that affected nearby gardens (rear of Hengest Road properties). (p18) [PAG, IMG]
* High localised rainfall combined with restricted ditch capacity (silted southern boundary ditch) caused water to spill out of the banks and flow down Hengest Avenue, resulting in flooding to the front of properties. Ditch clearance works were later carried out west of Hengest Avenue to restore capacity. (p19) [PAG, IMG]
* Quant: 10 internally flooded houses (p4)
* Quant: 10 houses (p5)
* {01 May 2016 - 31 May 2016}: Quant: 1 in 30 return period; 1 in 100 return period; 1 in 1000 return period (p17) [PAG, IMG]
* Quant: ditch width reduced to approximately one third of the upstream width (p19) [PAG, IMG]

### Hinchley Wood
* The storm centred on a catchment around Hinchley Wood; localised flooding in the Hengest Avenue and Clay Lane area resulted from watercourses reaching capacity, causing flooding of roads and property. (p4)
* Surface water flooding in Hinchley Wood led to internal flooding of residential properties on Hengest Avenue. (p5) [IMG]
* Hinchley Wood was the focus of the intense rainfall and experienced localised surface water flooding; surface water flow routes are described east of The Rythe with runoff directed north towards the A309. The area sustained the majority of the storm accumulation during the event. (p7) [PAG, MUL]
* {autumn 1968, 22 Jun 2016 - 23 Jun 2016}: Localised surface water and ordinary watercourse flooding occurred on 22 June 2016 - 23 June 2016; watercourse maintenance was carried out following the incident. A previous historic event in Autumn 1968 is recorded for the area where the ordinary watercourse channel capacity was exceeded. (p9) [MUL]
* High localised rainfall combined with two areas of restricted ditch capacity caused flooding across the Hingchley Wood estate. A silted southern boundary ditch reduced conveyance to about one third of upstream width, causing water to spill out of banks and flow down Hengest Avenue, flooding the front of properties. A blocked trash grille caused backing up and flooding to the rear gardens of Hengest Road properties. (p10) [PAG]
* {autumn 1968, 01 May 2016 - 31 May 2016}: Modelled surface water flood extents for the Hinchley Wood area show risk across the area at 1 in 30, 1 in 100 and 1 in 1000 return periods. Historic recorded flooding for the area includes a previous event in Autumn 1968 when an ordinary watercourse channel capacity was exceeded. The area north of Claygate Lane (including the A309) is identified as having potential for groundwater flooding to reach the surface based on BGS susceptibility mapping. (p17) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Combination of very high localised rainfall and two areas of restricted ditch capacity caused flooding of the Hinchley Wood estate. The southern boundary ditch was heavily silted (reduced to ~one third of upstream width) causing water to spill out of banks and flow down Hengest Avenue, flooding the fronts of properties. A blocked trash grille caused water to back up and run onto a footpath and under a fence into rear gardens. A blockage in one pipe of a twin-pipe culvert at the northern section reduced culvert capacity and caused garden flooding along Claygate Lane. Runoff from Surbiton Golf Course ditches also could not be accepted by the watercourse during the high rainfall event and contributed to flooding. (p18) [PAG]
* Quant: 10 internally flooded houses (p4)
* Quant: 10 houses (p5) [IMG]
* {autumn 1968, 01 May 2016 - 31 May 2016}: Quant: 1 previous recorded flood event (Autumn 1968); 1 in 30 return period; 1 in 100 return period; 1 in 1000 return period (p17) [IMG, PAG, MUL]

### Elmbridge
* A storm event on 22–23 June caused localised flooding throughout the borough. (p4)
* Localised surface water flooding occurred across the borough during intense rainfall on 22–23 June 2016. The event produced only a small response in the river telemetry network and no fluvial flood alerts were issued. (p7) [PAG]
* {autumn 1968, 22 Jun 2016 - 23 Jun 2016}: Elmbridge Borough Council was contacted by residents during recovery. Modelled and mapped evidence and the report note concentrations of external property flooding roads and historic flood outlines within the Elmbridge area; the borough also has a recorded historic ordinary watercourse capacity exceedance in Autumn 1968. The June 2016 event resulted in surface water/ordinary watercourse flooding affecting parts of the borough (including Hinchley Wood). (p18) [IMG, MUL]

### Surrey
* Localized surface water and ordinary watercourse flooding on 22-23 June 2016. Internal property flooding and carriageway flooding were reported. Flooding occurred down Hengest Avenue after the southern boundary ditch was silted and reduced to about one third of upstream width, causing water to spill from banks and flood property fronts. A blocked trash grille at a watercourse/culvert junction backed water up and caused flooding to rear gardens of Hengest Road properties. A partial blockage in one pipe of a twin-pipe culvert at the northern section reduced culvert capacity and caused gardens along Claygate Lane to flood. Run-off from Surbiton Golf Course ditches could not be accepted by the watercourse during the high rainfall event. (p19) [IMG]

### Claygate Lane, Hinchley Wood
* Part of the Hengest Avenue / Clay Lane (sic) area where localised flooding occurred due to watercourses reaching capacity, causing flooding of roads and property and internal property flooding. (p4)
* Quant: 10 internally flooded houses (p4)

### Claygate Lane, Thames Ditton
* A twin‑pipe culvert at the northern section had a blockage in one pipe, severely reducing culvert capacity. This caused water to back up and flood gardens along Claygate Lane. (p10)

### Surbtion Golf Course
* Ditches on the golf course, which connect to the local watercourse at Hengest Avenue, delivered run‑off that the watercourse could not accept during the high‑rainfall event, contributing to flooding of the adjacent estate. (p10)
