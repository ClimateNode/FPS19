### Coverack
* Extreme, very intense rainfall produced flash flooding: around fifty properties flooded; the road through the village was destroyed; a landslide caused loss of the coast path. (p1)
* Significant, unprecedented and potentially life‑threatening flooding affected the community on 18th July 2017; residents and emergency services acted swiftly and there were no fatalities. (p4) [PAG]
* On 18th July 2017 Coverack experienced an unprecedented, potentially life‑threatening flash flood caused by extreme, concentrated rainfall. Many homes, businesses and elements of local infrastructure were severely damaged. River channels and banks were extensively eroded and became blocked by large quantities of rocks, sediment and other debris; there were landslips and cliff erosion in places. Emergency response and community action were required and, despite the severity, no lives were lost. (p5)
* Extensive damage to residents' and business properties and possessions; risk to life during the event; landslips and cliff erosion occurred or were caused risk; river channels and banks of local watercourses suffered extensive erosion and became blocked, with large quantities of rock, sediment and debris transported downstream and disrupting flow conveyance. (p6) [PAG]
* Coverack was flooded on 18th July 2017 when intense local rainfall produced rapid runoff from the three small watercourses draining the catchment through the village, causing inundation, erosion and damage to drainage/highway assets and triggering post‑event inspections and repair support processes by the Environment Agency and Cornwall Council. (p7) [IMG, TAB, PAG]
* The village was subjected to an extraordinary, highly localised storm concentrated on the South Coverack Stream catchment that produced rapid surface-water and flash flooding across the community; residents reported the 18th July event as unprecedented in living memory. (p9) [PAG]
* Rapid, intense flows formed a 'wall of water' down the main road with little warning. Internal flooding affected many homes; fast-flowing, and in places deep, water carried large rocks and debris. One property was surrounded by fast-flowing water and occupants were rescued by a coastguard helicopter. The event posed a significant risk to life. (p10) [PAG]
* Internal flooding of many properties; extensive damage to homes and businesses and possessions; erosion of land, river channels and the road network; pollution from damaged infrastructure (including sewers and domestic oil supplies). ### Cornwall
* {2017}: Multiple flood events across the county in 2017: repeated flash and surface-water flooding in Falmouth, Camborne/Redruth and Polperro; overtopping and storm-surge coastal flooding at Penryn (Exchequers Quay), St Mawes (Square and Kings Road; one property flooded), Kingsand (seafront road and properties), Looe (Buller Street, Hannafore Road; surcharged drains between Quay Street and Fore Street), Portmellon (main through road flooded by seawall overtopping), Mevagissey quayside overtopped, Seaton car park and recreational areas flooded and road flooded to ~400mm depth, Penzance Promenade overtopped causing flooding on Western Promenade Road. The Old Millhouse pub in Polperro suffered flash flooding and firefighters used pumps to clear water in the Mill Hill area. Police declared a major incident for Camborne and Redruth due to surface water flooding; Fire and Rescue attended multiple incidents. The extremely intense Coverack event caused around fifty properties to flood, destruction of the village road and a landslide that removed the coast path; neighbouring St Keverne had eleven properties flooded, Porthallow one property flooded and flood damage occurred at Porthoustock. Crantock experienced flooding around a public toilet block and the Village Stores. (p1) [PAG]
