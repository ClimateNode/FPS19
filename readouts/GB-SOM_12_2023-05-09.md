### Milverton
* On 9 May 2023 unprecedented rainfall produced an extraordinary level of surface water flooding that overwhelmed parish drainage. Runoff and channelled flows were directed toward properties causing extensive damage: mud from nearby fields inundated twenty properties, collapsing structures allowed water entry, and the parish-wide drainage infrastructure proved inadequate. (p5) [MUL]
* On 2023-05-09 Milverton experienced surface-water (pluvial) flooding. Multiple, spatially separated areas of the village were impacted (concentrated near the town centre with one outlying area to the southwest); properties were internally flooded — in total 18 residential and 2 commercial properties flooded internally across Woodbarton, Sand Street, Creedwell Close and Lower Lovelynch. (p7) [IMG]
* The iShare screenshot for Milverton shows marked flooding points and flooding lines within the village (notably on/near High Street), and multiple drainage features and gullies mapped nearby, indicating localised flooding in the village during the incident under investigation. (p21) [PAG, IMG]
* iShare maps and accompanying figures show flooding lines and flooding points within Milverton and surface-water velocity and surface-water-risk mapping for the village. Gullies and other drainage features are concentrated along main roads and streets and are co-located with mapped flooding lines/points, indicating surface-water flooding affected streets and local drainage infrastructure. (p22) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* Observed surface-water flow routed through Milverton town centre (flow segment 2 on the 9 May 2023 map). The map shows overland water movement within the town converging into lower elevations around Milverton, indicating streets/low-lying central areas were affected by overland flow on 2023-05-09. (p25) [PAG, IMG]
* On 2023-05-09 intense overland flow and ponding occurred in central Milverton: water travelling from a northern shallow valley washed across a track into a pond and pooled on an access road forming a large temporary pond; water exited the pond, flowed through an arable field north of the orchard and cascaded eastwards toward Woodbarton, inundating parts of Sand Street/Woodbarton area. (p26) [PAG, IMG]
* Flooding on 2023-05-09 disrupted school collection (parents/guardians unable to collect pupils), many vehicles drove through the village creating bow waves that exacerbated flooding in some properties; properties were left inundated with mud for up to a year after the event and at least one resident had not returned home because of flood damage. (p35)
* On 2023-05-09 exceptional/intense rainfall overwhelmed drainage across the parish, causing surface water/urban flooding. Multiple properties experienced internal flooding with extensive damage; mud from a nearby field was deposited into homes. Structural collapse in places allowed floodwater to enter several properties. Drainage infrastructure was insufficient to convey flows, directing water toward properties and increasing damage. (p39) [PAG, MUL]
* On 2023-05-09, debris carried from fields and a sudden collapse of material at upstream hedge lines caused a rush of water, mud and debris that blocked the inlet screen to a culvert. The blocked screen led to the culvert being overtopped and properties (notably in Woodbarton and Sand Street within Milverton) were flooded. Flooding receded quickly after a wall collapsed allowing runoff and firefighters cleared debris from the culvert entrance to restore flow. (p45)

### Lower Lovelynch Farm
* Properties on Lower Lovelynch experienced internal flooding during the heavy rainfall on 9 May 2023; included in the count of 18 residential and 2 commercial properties flooded internally in the village. (p7)
* Segment 3 continues to Lower Lovelynch where flows converge toward lower elevations; the map shows concentrated surface water movement reaching Lower Lovelynch on 9 May 2023. (p25) [IMG]

### Somerset County Council
* On 2023-05-09 Somerset Council was the principal local authority managing response to widespread surface-water/urban flooding across the county. During the event the community reported they were unable to contact the Council; emergency resources were thinly spread which delayed response to Milverton, and Highways presence only commenced on 2023-05-10. The Council's EPRR later identified that intelligence gathering during the May 2023 floods was ad-hoc and public reporting of flooding to the Council was low, slowing the establishment of the full incident scope. (p35)
