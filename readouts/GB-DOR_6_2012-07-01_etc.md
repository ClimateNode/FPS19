### River South Winterbourne
* {2003-01}: Assessed as the source of fluvial flooding threatening properties adjacent to the watercourse throughout the study area; river flooding risk is concentrated in a narrow margin either side of the watercourse. The river/its valley setting is also linked with elevated groundwater flood risk at nearby villages. (p20) [PAG]
* {general}: Fluvial flooding from the South Winterbourne affected properties adjacent to the watercourse; the river’s flows are closely linked to high groundwater which contributed to floodwater entering properties and limited the extent of flooding to a narrow margin either side of the channel. Historic records indicate the combined groundwater and river flooding has caused repeat flooding in the local villages. (p21)
* {July 2012}: Heavy rainfall and a rapid 9.7 m rise in groundwater (7–9 July 2012) increased flow in the South Winterbourne, causing river levels to exceed channel capacity along much of its length, flooding at channel restrictions and producing overland flow and spring emergence that affected properties and infrastructure. (p23)
* {July 2012}: The river experienced coincident increases in flow and stage during the early July 2012 groundwater rise, with a pronounced peak in early July. (p27) [PAG]
* {01 Jul 2012 - 31 Jul 2012}: Received augmentation discharges from Wessex Water and reportedly very large volumes of water (including from a released reservoir), increasing flows in the watercourse and contributing to downstream flooding of properties. (p29) [PAG]
* {01 Jul 2012 - 31 Jul 2012, 01 Nov 2012 - 30 Nov 2012, 01 Dec 2012 - 31 Dec 2012}: The South Winterbourne experienced episodic very high flows in 2012 that coincided with flood events: a sharp spike in mean daily flow in July 2012 associated with the July 2012 flood, and further elevated peaks in November 2012 and December 2012. (p30) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* {01 Jul 2012 - 31 Jul 2012, 01 Nov 2012 - 30 Nov 2012}: During the July 2012 event groundwater volumes and pressure were very high, with groundwater emerging through concrete floors in the area. In November 2012 river levels rose (the report notes the river level rise during that event); the Wessex Water augmentation was suspended before the November river rise and did not contribute to that flooding. (p31) [MUL]
* {01 Jul 2012 - 31 Jul 2012, 01 Nov 2012 - 30 Nov 2012, 01 Dec 2012 - 31 Dec 2012}: The South Winterbourne channel has limited capacity through the study area, which was identified as a key contributor to fluvial flooding risk affecting the local villages; blockages (including low bridges and other permanent obstructions) increase upstream flood risk and constrained channel alignment (by properties and the road) limits options for widening or diversion. * {general}: The channel through the study area has limited capacity which is identified as a key contributing factor to fluvial flooding risk affecting the three villages; channel blockages (eg. from low bridges, debris) increase upstream flood risk. * {general}: The South Winterbourne watercourse contributes to flood risk in the villages; inadequate maintenance and fragmented riparian ownership increase the likelihood and severity of flooding during high‑rainfall and groundwater events and improvement of its maintenance is recommended. (p36)
* {general}: Limited channel capacity in the South Winterbourne and adjacent below‑ground drains/ditches contributes to fluvial and groundwater flood risk to nearby properties; increasing capacity was identified as a potential measure to reduce that risk (though may not help for high return period events). (p37) [TAB]
* {general}: Poor conveyance and risk of blockage of the South Winterbourne contribute to fluvial (river) flood risk; minor improvements and improved maintenance were identified as measures to increase flow conveyance and reduce flood risk to local villages. (p41)
