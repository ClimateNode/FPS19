

# Wigan Metropolitan District flooding 23 Sep 2012 - 26 Sep 2012

## Place names with coordinates

### Eleanor Street, Wigan Metropolitan District
* Road inundated with water reaching up to the doorsteps of terraced houses; pavement partially submerged to the top of the curb; sandbags placed at some doorways indicating attempts to prevent water ingress; visual evidence of several inches of standing water across the street causing residential disruption. (p1)
* Severe prolonged rainfall caused predominant surface water flooding which overloaded the combined sewer and prevented the highway drainage from discharging. Highway drainage was also prevented from discharging where it outfalls to the River Douglas due to elevated river levels. Eight residential properties and one commercial property were flooded internally. (p4)
* A localized flood affected central and southern portions of Eleanor Street, inundating both sides of the street and adjacent lots. Properties along the street flooded internally; sandbags and pumps were deployed to limit damage. (p7) [PAG, IMG]
* Severe rainfall caused the United Utilities combined sewer to surcharge, which prevented the highway drainage system on Eleanor Street from discharging into it, resulting in surface water inundation of the highway drainage on Eleanor Street. (p9)
* Listed as one of the areas protected by the attenuation tank installed after the Sep 2012 flood to reduce future surface-water flooding. (p14)
* The Eleanor Street area was affected by the September 2012 flooding. Mitigation works (installation of two new gullies and connection of four lowest existing gullies to the new pumped road drainage) were implemented to reduce surface water entering the United Utilities combined sewer; residential properties in the Eleanor Street area were identified for removal from Flood Zone 3 into an Area Benefitting from Defence. (p16)
* Surface water inundated the street, covering the carriageway and reaching the front steps and doorways of terraced houses. Floodwater entered properties causing internal flooding; access to homes and the road was obstructed. Sandbags were deployed at property entrances. (p18) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface water floodwater covered the full width of the road, reaching up to the wheels of parked cars, floating debris and tipping a green wheelie bin. Floodwater entered adjacent properties causing internal flooding of ground-floor interiors — murky water covering floors to the base of walls and submerging mats, leading to contamination and property damage. (p19) [IMG, PAG]
* Internal flooding of a property on Eleanor Street: water covered interior tiled floors and a bathroom floor, partially submerging the base of the toilet and the bathtub panel. Personal items and fittings were displaced or partially submerged; water appeared dark and likely contaminated, rendering rooms unusable and requiring cleanup and repairs. (p20) [PAG]
* Outdoor areas and a construction/storage yard were inundated with standing water, partially submerging stacked construction materials and reducing site accessibility; risk to material integrity documented. (p21) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 8 residential properties; 1 commercial property (p4)
* Quant: 8 residential properties; 1 commercial property; flood depths 300 mm–750 mm (p7) [PAG, IMG]

### Wallgate, Wigan Metropolitan District
* Severe prolonged rainfall caused predominant surface water flooding which overloaded the combined sewer and prevented the highway drainage from discharging. Highway drainage was also prevented from discharging where it outfalls to the River Douglas due to elevated river levels. Eight residential properties and one commercial property were flooded internally. (p4)
* Portion of Wallgate was within the mapped inundation area during the September 2012 event; flooding resulted from United Utilities sewer and highway drainage systems being at capacity, causing local inundation along the sewer corridor. (p8) [IMG]
* Gullies on the highway drainage at Wallgate were unable to discharge due to a surcharged combined sewer, producing predominantly surface water flooding of the highway and surrounding commercial and residential properties; raised River Douglas levels may have caused flow back up the drainage, compounding impacts. (p9) [PAG]
* Flooding occurred at Wallgate during the September 2012 event; this flooding impacted the United Utilities combined sewer and prevented gullies on the highway drainage system at Wallgate from discharging, worsening surface-water drainage. (p16)
* Roadway underpass and adjacent road surface were flooded with standing water covering the carriageway and extending onto the sidewalk, obstructing traffic and indicating drainage capacity was exceeded. (p22) [IMG]
* Commercial property area at Wallgate was inundated with floodwater covering the road and forecourts, impeding access and movement; pedestrians and vehicles were affected and barriers were partially submerged. (p24) [IMG]
* Quant: 8 residential properties; 1 commercial property (p4)

### Saddle Junction, Wigan Metropolitan District
* Historically affected by fluvial flooding due to the River Douglas overtopping; more recent flood events in the area have been attributed to pluvial (surface water) flooding. (p4)
* Area inundated during the September 2012 event when United Utilities sewer and the highway drainage system were at capacity, resulting in local flooding tied to sewer infrastructure and low-lying ground. (p8) [IMG]
* Severe rainfall overloaded the combined sewer so gullies at the Saddle Junction could not discharge, causing predominantly surface water flooding of the highway and contributing to flooding of surrounding commercial and residential properties; rising River Douglas levels also likely flowed back up the highway drainage, compounding the flooding. (p9) [PAG]
* Area identified as being protected by the newly installed attenuation tank to reduce surface-water flooding risk following the Sep 2012 event. (p14)
* The road at Saddle Junction was flooded such that water covered the carriageway, rendering the road impassable; vehicles were stopped before the flooded section and normal traffic flow was disrupted. (p23) [IMG]

### Wigan Metropolitan District
* The report attributes severe rainfall (23 Sep 2012 - 26 Sep 2012) that resulted in surcharge of United Utilities' sewer system, contributing to hydraulic/sewer flooding in the Wigan area and prompting recommendations for record-keeping and management of flood-risk assets. (p15) [TAB]
* Surface water flooding occurred on Eleanor Street (in Wigan). (p17)

### Melverley Street, Wigan Metropolitan District
* The surcharged United Utilities combined sewer prevented the highway drainage system on Melverley Street from discharging, contributing to surface water inundation of the highway drainage on Melverley Street. (p9)
* Surface water flooding affected the Melverley Street / Eleanor Street area during the September 2012 event. Highway gullies were prevented from discharging due to sewer and local flooding, causing surface water to accumulate and impact residential properties and nearby commercial businesses. Subsequent mitigation (Saddle relief road works and pumped drainage) was proposed to reduce future risk and to reclassify the area out of Flood Zone 3. (p16)

### River Douglas
* River levels rose significantly and historically have overtopped. During the event river levels rose above the highway drain outfall pipe, preventing the highway drainage system from discharging into the river and contributing to surface water flooding in the Wallgate/Eleanor Street area. (p4)
* Prolonged/severe rainfall caused River Douglas levels to rise significantly above the highway drain level, producing backflow into the highway drainage system, compounding surface water flooding of the highway and nearby commercial and residential properties and contributing to inundation and surcharge of the combined sewer system. (p9)

### Kwik Fit
* Surface water floodwaters entered the commercial premises and reached the building entrance; the shopfront and immediate pavement were submerged, rendering the street and entrance impassable and threatening the business. (p22) [IMG]
* Floodwater submerged the shop forecourt and reached up to the bottom of the doors, preventing access and causing operational disruption. Street furniture (metal barriers) and a traffic signal were partially submerged; pedestrians were seen wading through the water. (p24) [IMG]

### Adam Bridge
* River levels at Adam Bridge rose significantly above the highway drain during prolonged rainfall, causing potential backflow into the highway drainage system. This backflow compounded surface water flooding of the highway and surrounding commercial and residential properties and would have inundated the combined sewerage system, contributing to sewer surcharge. (p9)


## Topics without coordinates

### United Utilities
* The combined sewer was surcharged during prolonged rainfall; it did not surcharge to a level causing foul internal sewer flooding but was surcharged sufficiently to prevent the highway drainage system from discharging into it, contributing to internal flooding of properties on Wallgate/Eleanor Street. (p4)
* United Utilities' sewer was at capacity during the September 2012 event, contributing to inundation of the area and internal flooding of properties. (p7)
* The United Utilities combined sewer was overloaded and became surcharged during severe rainfall, preventing highway gullies from discharging and contributing to surface water flooding of the highway and nearby properties. (p9) [TAB]
* {general}: Maintain the DG5 register of properties and areas that have suffered or are likely to suffer flooding from public foul, combined or surface water sewers due to hydraulic overloading; required to undertake capacity improvements to alleviate sewer flooding problems during AMP (2010-15), though funding limits mean not all instances can be addressed. (p12)
* Surcharge of public foul/combined/surface water sewers during the severe rainfall (23–26 Sep 2012) caused hydraulic/sewer flooding; required United Utilities to maintain the DG5 register of properties/areas that suffered or were at risk and to investigate feasibility of capacity improvements to alleviate sewer flooding. (p15) [TAB]
* The United Utilities combined sewer in Wigan was surcharged by severe rainfall during the September 2012 event. Although overloaded, the sewer did not surcharge to foul flooding; the surcharge, together with flooding at Wallgate, prevented highway gullies from discharging into the combined sewer and contributed to surface-water flooding issues. (p16) [PAG]
* Quant: 8 residential properties; 1 commercial property (p7)

### Wigan Council
* Acted as Lead Local Flood Authority for the flood investigation into the 23–26 Sep 2012 event; facilitated information sharing between risk management authorities and the community and recommended/oversaw mitigation works (including securing funding and implementing attenuation measures) to reduce future surface water flooding. (p14) [PAG]
* Investigated flood incidents arising from severe rainfall (23–26 Sep 2012); responsible for recording flooded properties and significant flood‑risk assets, coordinating information sharing between risk management authorities and the community, and facilitating remedial actions such as serving notices under the Land Drainage Act where flows were impeded. (p15) [TAB]
* Quant: 912m³ (attenuation tank storage) (p14) [PAG]

### Wallgate Bridge
* Gullies at Wallgate Bridge could not discharge because the combined sewer was surcharged, contributing to surface water flooding of the highway; a low road gully under Wallgate Bridge relative to Adam Bridge and elevated River Douglas levels likely allowed river water to flow back up the highway drainage, compounding flooding to the highway and nearby properties. (p9)

### Highways
* Severe rainfall overloaded the highway drainage and road gullies (including at Eleanor Street, Melverley Street, Saddle Junction and Wallgate Bridge) so they could not discharge into the United Utilities combined sewer, causing surface water flooding of the highway and contributing to flooding of surrounding commercial and residential properties. (p9) [TAB]
