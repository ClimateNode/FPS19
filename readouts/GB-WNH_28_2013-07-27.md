### West End, West Haddon

(p10) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* Surface water flowed into and along West End, overtopping kerbs and gullies. Three residential properties were very close to internal flooding; three properties had external areas flooded to their rear and were close to internal flooding; three properties had external/frontage flooding that residents manually prevented entering buildings; at least one property was internally flooded. A commercial property was also reported internally flooded during the event. (p11) [PAG]
* Flow of water was experienced to the rear of three properties on West End near High Street. At low points on West End the flow in the public highway overtopped kerb lines into private property at relatively shallow depths. Road gullies were unable to accept the quantity of water causing ponding and surcharging of manholes and gullies. A large pond formed at a private access road off West End due to run-off. (p12) [PAG]
* A large pond formed on a private access road off West End from runoff from the public highway, producing ponding of about 100mm outside properties and causing internal flooding of two properties to about 25mm on the ground floor. Separately, at the final length of the public surface water sewer at the rear of a property on West End, a reduction in pipe size led to a large area of ponding covering gardens and other open space. (p13)

### West Haddon
* Intense short-duration rainfall overwhelmed drainage, causing excess surface water to flow over ground to low points in the village and resulting in internal flooding of at least one property (recurring). (p5) [PAG]
* Intense short-duration rainfall overwhelmed existing drainage so excess surface water flowed overland following natural contours into low points in the village, causing internal flooding of at least one property on more than one occasion. (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* An intense rainfall event on 27 July 2013 exceeded the capacity of drainage systems causing flooding in the village. Internal flooding occurred in at least one property; at least three other properties were at high risk and residents manually prevented ingress. Drainage system capacity was exceeded and a Preliminary Drainage Investigation was carried out. (p8) [MUL]
* An intense rainfall event (evening of 27 July 2013) produced multiple surface water overland flows through the village, causing ponding, road flooding and a series of property flooding incidents across the village (see specific streets). Road gullies were overwhelmed and ejected water. (p11)
* The village experienced surface water flooding during the event: the surface water sewer accepting run-off from private property and the public highway had insufficient capacity leading to ponding and surcharging of manholes and road gullies; surface water flowed across three natural catchments but was prevented from discharging to natural watercourses due to levels of the public highway and developed private property; highway overtopping led to shallow ingress into private properties; a large pond formed off West End. (p12)
* Ponding formed at the final length of a public surface water sewer at the rear of a property on West End, covering gardens and other open space; the sewer pipe size reduces at this location which contributed to the ponding. (p14)
* Identified as being at risk during the Flood Incident; residents are advised to take actions such as property-level protection, community resilience measures, and signing up for flood warnings. (p16)
* Intense short-duration rainfall overwhelmed existing drainage across the village. Drainage systems were surcharged and ejected water to the surface; surface water flowed toward and concentrated at the village low points, causing overtopping of the public highway and exposing properties at low points to flooding. Surface water across the village was directed to a single watercourse discharge location. (p17)
* Surface water across the village does not follow natural contours and is instead directed to a single watercourse discharge location, concentrating runoff and creating surface water flooding risk; report recommends reinstating historic drainage routes, intrusive investigations (dye/CCTV), re-excavation of ditches, repair or replacement of piped/culvert sections, dredging/trimming watercourses and a village-wide maintenance/asset plan to reduce the flood risk. (p18)
* The parish (specifically The Green and West End areas) experienced repeated surface water flooding prior to drainage works in 2007; residents linked the change in local flooding behaviour to a nearby housing development. (p37) [PAG]
* Areas within the village (including The Green and West End) experienced surface water/urban flooding, with streets inundated, parked vehicles partially submerged and traffic affected. (p40)

### High Street, West Haddon
* Surface water flowed the full width of the carriageway; water fountains were observed ejecting from road gullies on High Street. The velocity and depth of flow presented a danger and flows contributed to flooding at adjacent properties and to the rear of three properties near High Street. (p11) [PAG]
* {01 Jun 2013 - 30 Jun 2013, 27 Jul 2013}: Road gullies were unable to accept the quantity of water, with water ejecting from gullies; areas of ponding and surcharging of manholes and road gullies were reported. A sink hole beneath the carriageway on High Street was reported by residents and repaired (reported cause: sandy soils washed away by ground water). (p12) [PAG, MUL]
* {01 Jul 2007 - 31 Aug 2007}: High Street was the location of surface water brick culvert lining works in July–August 2007 carried out in response to the area's history of flooding; the culvert was relined with plastic pipe of a considerably smaller cross-sectional area (as reported). (p37) [PAG]
* {15 Aug 2007}: Drainage works in July–August 2007 inserted a smaller plastic lining into the existing brick surface-water culvert; damage and several blockages were discovered during the work. After heavy rainfall on 15 August 2007 the culvert and gully at the location were at full capacity and flooding still occurred despite the new lining. (p38)

### West Haddon Bypass
* Drainage associated with the bypass (including balancing ponds and their outlet pipes) can become blocked or have insufficient capacity, impeding flow to the receiving watercourse and increasing surface water runoff risk to adjacent properties; recommended actions include dredging/de-vegetation of outlet pipes, CCTV surveys and regular drainage cleansing. (p19)


## Topics without coordinates

### Highway Authority
* {01 Jun 2013 - 30 Jun 2013}: Repaired a sink hole beneath the High Street carriageway (reported June 2013) that was attributed to sandy soils being washed away by ground water. (p12)

### Northamptonshire County Council
* Highway drainage assets (including gullies, kerbs and cambers) in the village and on the A428 West Haddon Bypass are linked to surface water runoff that can cause flood risk to private properties; NCC Highways are to cleanse drains regularly, assess asset capacity, review gully and kerb positions and consider works (including dredging/de-vegetation and CCTV of outlet pipes) to reduce runoff to properties. (p19)

### Anglian Water
* {general}: Public sewerage system may receive additional water sources or be of insufficient capacity, which can lead to flood risk to properties; Anglian Water is to assess sources entering the sewer system, assess asset capacity and consider routing surface water to natural catchment outfalls to reduce property flood risk. (p19)

### Daventry District Council
* Daventry District Council is to consult with the Environment Agency on planning to reduce flood risk and may use enforcement powers under the Land Drainage Act to require riparian owners to maintain watercourses to reduce surface water runoff and groundwater emergence that affect properties. (p19)

### A428 East Balancing Pond
* {general}: Main inlet headwall showing active water flow; main outlet headwall partially obstructed by debris and overgrown vegetation, reducing drainage capacity. The condition could impair the pond’s ability to discharge runoff and contribute to upstream surface water accumulation. (p51) [IMG, PAG]
