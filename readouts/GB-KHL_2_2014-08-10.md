

# Kingston Upon Hull flooding 10 Aug 2014

Reference document:<br>Hull City Council Flood Investigation Report: Section 19 – Heavy Rain August 2014<br>064\_Kingston upon Hull, City of/HeavyRainAugust2014.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Kingston Upon Hull
* Urban streets were inundated with standing water after heavy rain; vehicles were forced to drive through flooded roads, producing large splashes and indicating transport disruption and hazards for road users and pedestrians. (p1) [PAG]
* Widespread highway flooding across the city; two properties flooded during the intense storms; many areas flooded before the receiving drainage infrastructure could convey flows. (p4)
* {2007}: Parts of the city were flooded in 2007: flood flows from overwhelmed streams and ditches affected western neighbourhoods (Anlaby Park, Orchard Park, Derringham) and extensive surface water flooding occurred across large areas of the east where drainage systems could not cope. (p8) [IMG]
* {2007, 25 Aug 2012}: Widespread surface water flooding in 2007 when drains and drainage systems were overwhelmed, causing extensive flooding across the city. A separate intense storm on 25th August 2012 caused further localized flooding concentrated in a central belt. (p9) [PAG, MUL]
* As Lead Local Flood Authority, Hull City Council received advance warnings for the 10 August 2014 surface-water event and carried out pre-event actions: targeting identified problem gullies and hotspots for gully-clearing and checking/cleaning council-owned trash screens on ordinary watercourses to reduce the risk of gully blockage. (p24) [PAG]
* Surface water (pluvial) flooding across the city on 10th August 2014 caused by intense, localised rainfall and high winds. Multiple areas experienced flooding; residents undertook self-help measures while others looked to the council for response. The event highlighted lack of warning for this flood source. (p27) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 2 properties (p4)
* {2007, 25 Aug 2012}: Quant: more than 1300 businesses; 8600 residential properties; 19 residential properties; 2 commercial properties (p9) [PAG, MUL]

## Topics without coordinates

### Yorkshire Water
* Monitored sewer levels and operated pumps at key stations to manage flows during the pluvial event. Pumping operations were time-delayed relative to the onset of intense rainfall, which limited immediate effectiveness in some localised areas. (p22)
* Yorkshire Water's trunk and branch sewers (Drypool, Sutton and Newington are mentioned) overloaded during the event, resulting in smaller YW and highway drainage systems being overwhelmed across the area. (p24) [PAG]
* Yorkshire Water operated its infrastructure and pumped drainage network during the event; the system worked as designed but was of limited effectiveness because intense, isolated rainfall caused surcharging. They carried out jetting and cleansing operations and continue to be involved in post-event work in the Hawkshead Green and Merlin Close / Kestral Way areas; observations indicate wider problems in the branch sewer network. (p25)
* Investigated and responded to impacts associated with the 10 August 2014 pluvial event; undertook remediation planning and interventions, tasked with resolving sewer-flooding problems (including work at Hawkshead Green), reviewing mapping and working with Hull City Council on priority joint solutions and customer reporting guidance. (p27)

### Hull City Council
* Conducted investigations into drainage assets and the causes of the pluvial flood on 10 August 2014; identified locations in the east and west of the city where road infrastructure and public realm can be modified to manage future surface-water flooding. (p22)
* As Lead Local Flood Authority and highways authority, the council managed highway drainage response during the storm, maintained contact with residents, supported Yorkshire Water's further work, and implemented/adapted highway drainage measures (for example at Noddle Hill Way) to store road runoff and reduce property inundation in future events. The storm overwhelmed many roadside drains and gullies beyond design standards. (p25)
* As Lead Local Flood Authority, responded to the pluvial event on 10 August 2014, carried out Section 19 investigations and developed remediation and planned interventions (citywide small flood alleviation schemes, gully replacement, GIS of hotspots and community communication). (p27) [TAB, PAG]
* Responded to intense localized rainfall on 10th August 2014 that caused extensive road flooding and isolated property flooding across the city; council operational teams were engaged in immediate response. The event exposed that existing sewers and road gullies were unable to cope with the speed and intensity of runoff, prompting council recommendations to replace road gullies in key risk areas, develop GIS layers of historical flooding hot spots to support operations, and improve communication with residents about surface water flood risk and resilience. (p28) [PAG]
