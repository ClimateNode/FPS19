

# Denford flooding 21 Nov 2012

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Duck End, Denford, 21st Novermber 2012<br>090\_North Northamptonshire/Denford-FIR.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Denford
* Intense, short-duration rainfall on a saturated catchment produced excess surface water that flowed following natural contours to areas in and around Duck End, Chapel Lane and Freeman's Lane within Denford; internal flooding of at least one property was reported and had occurred four times in one year. (p5) [PAG]
* Internal flooding was reported at Duck End (in Denford). At least one property suffered internal flooding during the incident; that property had experienced internal flooding four times within one year. (p6) [PAG]
* Significant rainfall on the Denford catchment on 21 November 2012 produced high volumes of surface water that flowed into parts of the village and resulted in internal flooding at Duck End (within Denford). (p8)
* Flooding across the village: rear gardens of properties and the highway (High Street) were affected; ponding occurred in Duck End and Chapel Lane; rear gardens of properties on Freeman's Lane were affected; agricultural fields south of Spinney Rise were submerged; foul water from the public sewer was present in flood water at Duck End. (p11)
* Flooding occurred in Denford; NCC can suggest possible causes and make recommendations to mitigate flood risk but lacks the mandate or funding under the FandWMA to tackle all identified causes. (p12) [PAG]
* Intense, short-duration rainfall on a saturated catchment overwhelmed existing drainage systems and watercourses in Denford, causing water to pond, flow into areas and drainage systems unable to manage the additional water, and resulting in localised flooding across the village. (p14)
* Intense rainfall onto a saturated catchment and insufficient drainage/ordinary watercourse capacity led to overtopping of the ordinary watercourse east of the village. Overtopping produced a high‑velocity flow into Denford that spanned the full highway width; existing drainage systems and watercourses were unable to cope, causing ponding and flood flows into other areas. (p15)
* Overspill of land drainage led to flooding of neighbouring properties, the public highway and the public sewerage system; flows from high ground contributed to properties being affected. (p16)
* Properties adjacent to the River Nene in Denford are affected by flood risk from the main river; the report recommends the Environment Agency consider flood defence works at the River Nene to reduce the likelihood of flooding and recommends documents to residents affected by surface water runoff from neighbouring land and emergence of groundwater. (p18)
* Quant: one property; four times in one year (p5) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 property; 4 incidents in one year (p6) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 property; 2 properties (p8)

### Duck End, Denford
* Intense rainfall onto a saturated catchment caused excess surface water to flow over ground into Duck End; drainage was ineffective and at least one property experienced internal flooding (recurring events). (p5) [PAG]
* High volumes of surface water flowed to Duck End from the south and east. Internal flooding affected at least one property to ground-floor living areas. Two properties required continuous efforts by residents and the Fire Service to prevent internal flooding. (p8)
* Large area of ponding throughout Duck End. Flood water at Duck End was reported to include foul water from the public sewerage system; a nearby highway gully in poor condition impaired drainage at a low point. (p11)
* {general}: Road gully at the junction with Freeman's Lane requires cleaning and consideration for replacement because highway drainage capacity/issues at this junction contribute to surface water runoff that creates flood risk to nearby properties; highway drainage/culvert maintenance is recommended to reduce surface water flow to areas at risk. (p17)
* Quant: one property; four times in one year (p5) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 property; 2 properties (p8)

### Chapel Lane, Denford
* Excess surface water from intense rainfall flowed over ground following natural contours into areas in and around Chapel Lane. (p6)
* A culvert in the rear garden of a property on Chapel Lane collapsed during the flood incident and at the time of investigation the route was an open ditch; flows from upstream ditches reached the rear garden, flowed to the front of the property and entered the surface water public sewerage system. Replacement pipework is planned (may require consent to ensure capacity). (p9)
* Ponding developed throughout Chapel Lane as the ordinary watercourse overspilled; drainage through a rear garden and to the front of a property on Chapel Lane collapsed under the high flow, causing water to find an overland route toward property thresholds (residents and Fire Service had to force water away to prevent internal flooding). (p10)
* {21 Dec 2012}: Large area of ponding throughout Chapel Lane due to heavy flow in the ordinary watercourse being unable to discharge quickly enough. (p11)
* {29 Jan 2013}: A land drainage ditch at No. 9 Chapel Lane collapsed and was subsequently re-excavated. The collapse disturbed garden areas (front and rear), exposed soil and scattered debris were visible, and the ditch runs close to the house and property boundaries. (p40) [IMG]
* {29 Jan 2013}: Quant: 1 property (No. 9 Chapel Lane); ditch approximately 0.5–1 metre wide; affected front and rear gardens (p40) [IMG]

### Freeman's Lane, Denford
* Excess surface water from intense rainfall flowed over ground following natural contours into areas in and around Freeman's Lane. (p6)
* Rear gardens of properties on Freeman's Lane were affected by ponding from the overspilling ordinary watercourse; a property on Freeman's Lane would have been flooded without continuous efforts by residents and the Fire Service to force water away. (p10)
* Rear gardens of properties on Freeman's Lane were affected by ponding because the ordinary watercourse could not flow away quickly enough. (p11)
* {general}: The road gully at the junction with Duck End is noted for cleaning/replacement; insufficient highway drainage capacity at this junction is linked to surface water runoff that poses flood risk to nearby properties, and maintenance/replacement is recommended. (p17)
* Quant: one property (p10)

### High Street, Denford
* Where the ordinary watercourse meets the River Nene, flooding occurred affecting the rear gardens of residential properties and the highway of High Street; the ordinary watercourse culvert beneath High Street is identified as a factor in this flooding. (p10)
* {general}: Residents on High Street, Denford are described as affected by flood risk from the main river; the Environment Agency is asked to provide advice to those affected on managing flood risk from the River Nene. (p18)

### Spinney Rise, Denford
* Large areas of agricultural fields south of Spinney Rise were submerged in flood water during the flooding incident. (p11)
* Drainage accepting runoff from fields south of Spinney Rise lacked capacity for the flows experienced; the drainage route runs through residential property before entering the ordinary watercourse, increasing the risk of flooding to residential properties. (p15)

### River Nene
* River Nene rose in level so that properties adjacent came close to being flooded; rear gardens were affected by the raised river levels. (p11)
* Raised water levels in the River Nene may have contributed to the flooding by preventing the ordinary watercourse from discharging to the river, exacerbating flooding in the area. (p15)
* The River Nene is identified as the main river causing or contributing to flooding adjacent to Denford; the report recommends consideration of flood defence works at the River Nene and assessment of contributory factors at outfalls to the main river. (p18)

### Brawns Spinney
* Areas of flattened grass adjacent to the watercourse in Brawns Spinney observed during the investigation are considered evidence of overtopping of banks under heavy flows. (p10)


## Topics without coordinates

### A45
* Runoff from the A45 entered a purpose-built pond and then discharged via an outflow/overflow to a ditch system that connects to the ordinary watercourse; this road runoff contributed to the surface-water flooding of the ordinary watercourse and downstream properties during the main event. (p9) [PAG]
* The pond serving drainage to the A45 had a steady outflow via a 300mm diameter outlet; a second pond about 300 metres west of the A45 was full to capacity and was overtopping to the ordinary watercourse with a strong discharge through its outlet pipes, indicating the drainage system was at capacity and discharging to the watercourse. (p10)
* Balancing pond adjacent to the A45 Road was overflowing; water extended beyond the pond onto surrounding grassy/reedy land. The outlet pipe and the base of the overflow spillway for the balancing pond (at the A45 Road location) showed overflow/active discharge. (p43) [IMG, PAG]
* Outlet pipe at the base of the balancing pond's overflow spillway was partially submerged and discharging water, indicating overflow conditions impacting the spillway/outlet infrastructure. (p44) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 300mm diameter outlet (p10)
* Quant: 1 pond; 1 outlet pipe and base of overflow spillway (p43) [IMG, PAG]

### Denford Catchment
* Significant rainfall fell on the Denford catchment on 21 November 2012, generating high volumes of surface water runoff that flowed to Duck End and contributed to internal flooding of properties. (p8)
* The Denford catchment received significant rainfall on 21 November 2012, producing saturated ground and runoff that led to surface-water flooding affecting people and properties in Denford (the catchment-scale rainfall/runoff was the cause of the flood event). (p9)
* Quant: 1 property; 2 properties (p8)
