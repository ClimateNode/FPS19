

# Denford flooding 21 Nov 2012

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Duck End, Denford, 21st Novermber 2012<br>090\_North Northamptonshire/Denford-FIR.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Denford
* Intense, short-duration rainfall on a saturated catchment produced excess surface water that flowed following natural contours to areas in and around Duck End, Chapel Lane and Freeman's Lane within Denford; internal flooding of at least one property was reported and had occurred four times in one year. (p5) [PAG]
* Internal flooding was reported at Duck End (in Denford). At least one property suffered internal flooding during the incident; that property had experienced internal flooding four times within one year. (p6) [PAG]
* The village experienced significant rainfall on the Denford catchment leading to high volumes of surface water flowing into lower areas (including Duck End), resulting in internal flooding of at least one property and two properties requiring continuous efforts to prevent internal flooding. (p8)
* Gardens of residential properties and the highway on High Street were affected by floodwater; an ordinary watercourse culvert beneath High Street was a contributing factor. The River Nene rose to levels that brought properties adjacent to it close to flooding and caused rear gardens to be affected. Large areas of ponding occurred throughout Duck End and Chapel Lane and in the rear gardens of properties on Freeman's Lane because the ordinary watercourse could not discharge quickly enough. At the junction of Duck End and Freeman's Lane a poorly functioning highway gully prevented satisfactory drain down. Large agricultural fields south of Spinney Rise were submerged. Floodwater at Duck End included foul water from the public sewerage system. (p11)
* Intense, short-duration rainfall on a saturated catchment caused existing drainage systems and watercourses to be unable to cope. Water flowed into areas and drainage systems not designed to manage the additional volume, producing ponding and flooding across Denford. (p14)
* Intense rainfall on a saturated catchment overwhelmed drainage and watercourses. An ordinary watercourse east of the village was overtopped, producing high-velocity flow into Denford that spanned the full highway width. Existing drainage and culverts (Duck End and High Street) lacked capacity or were in poor repair, preventing conveyance to the River Nene and causing ponding and flooding within the village. (p15)
* Overflow and inability of an ordinary watercourse to discharge into the River Nene led to land drainage overspilling onto neighbouring properties, the public highway and the public sewerage system within the village. (p16)
* Properties adjacent to the River Nene were exposed to river flooding; residents were also affected by surface water runoff from neighbouring land and by the emergence of groundwater. Report recommends consideration of flood defence works on the River Nene and assessment of outfalls, highway drainage and pond function to reduce downstream flood risk. (p18)
* A pond between Denford and the A45 Road overflowed, with water extending beyond the pond onto adjacent grassy and reedy land surrounding Denford. (p43) [IMG]
* Quant: one property; four times in one year (p5) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 property; 4 incidents in one year (p6) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 property internally flooded; 2 properties required continuous efforts to prevent internal flooding; 4 incidents in one year (p8)

### Chapel Lane, Denford
* Excess surface water from intense rainfall flowed over ground following natural contours into areas in and around Chapel Lane. (p6)
* An existing culvert in a property on Chapel Lane collapsed during the flood incident and was an open ditch at the time of the Flood Incident Investigation; the collapsed culvert is to be replaced to restore the drainage route. (p9)
* The ordinary watercourse overspilled and produced large areas of ponding through Chapel Lane; the drainage through the rear garden of a property on Chapel Lane collapsed, producing an overland flow to the thresholds of a neighbouring property which would have flooded without continuous efforts by residents and the Fire Service. (p10)
* Large area of ponding throughout Chapel Lane caused by heavy flow in the ordinary watercourse being unable to flow away quickly enough. (p11)
* Collapse of an unlined land drainage ditch running through the front and rear gardens of No. 9 Chapel Lane, followed by re-excavation; exposed soil, debris and damaged garden areas adjacent to the house. (p40) [IMG]
* Land drainage ditch entering a culvert immediately south of Chapel Lane was recorded; the culvert entrance was partially obscured by vegetation and fencing, noted as a drainage entry point adjacent to residential properties during the investigation. (p42) [IMG]
* Quant: one property (p10)
* Quant: 1 property (No. 9 Chapel Lane) (p40) [IMG]

### Duck End, Denford
* Intense rainfall onto a saturated catchment caused excess surface water to flow over ground into Duck End; drainage was ineffective and at least one property experienced internal flooding (recurring events). (p5) [PAG]
* High volumes of surface water flowed to Duck End from the south and east during heavy rainfall on the Denford catchment, causing internal flooding to ground-floor living areas of at least one property. Two properties required continuous efforts by residents and the Fire Service to prevent internal flooding. (p8)
* Large area of ponding throughout Duck End. Flood water at Duck End is reported to have included foul water from the public sewerage system (contaminated floodwater). (p11)
* Surface water from the highway overwhelmed the road drainage at the junction with Freeman's Lane, contributing to flood risk to nearby properties; recommendation to clean or replace the road gully and carry out highway drainage cleansing and capacity improvements. (p17)
* Ordinary watercourse enters a culvert at the junction (location marked by a white fence). Roadworks, traffic cones and disturbed ground are present at the junction documenting recent repair/maintenance of the culvert/drainage infrastructure, consistent with local surface water flooding and drainage failure. (p41)
* Quant: one property; four times in one year (p5) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 property internally flooded; 2 properties required continuous efforts to prevent internal flooding; 4 incidents in one year (p8)

### Freeman's Lane, Denford
* Excess surface water from intense rainfall flowed over ground following natural contours into areas in and around Freeman's Lane. (p6)
* Ponding from the ordinary watercourse inundated rear gardens of properties on Freeman's Lane; continuous efforts by residents and the Fire Service prevented internal flooding of a property which otherwise would have flooded. (p10)
* Rear gardens of properties on Freeman's Lane were affected by ponding; a highway gully at the junction with Duck End was in poor condition and did not allow satisfactory drainage to the ordinary watercourse. (p11)
* Surface water flooding at the junction with Duck End (road gully issue) contributed to flood risk to adjacent properties; actions recommended include gully cleaning/replacement and assessment of highway drainage capacity. (p17)
* Identified low point on Freeman's Lane where surface water collected. A roadside drainage channel and culvert adjacent to the lane are shown with traffic cones and signs and disturbed ground, indicating recent maintenance/repair associated with surface water drainage problems. (p41) [PAG]
* Low point on Freeman's Lane where water and mud accumulated on the carriageway and verge; the junction with Duck End is identified as the location where an ordinary watercourse enters a culvert (marked by a white fence), noted during the investigation. (p42) [PAG]
* Quant: one property (p10)

### Northamptonshire
* The Northamptonshire Lead Local Flood Authority carried out a formal Section 19 investigation into the flood incident at Duck End, Denford because internal flooding of at least one property had been experienced four times in one year. (p7)
* Quant: 1 property; 4 incidents in one year (p7)

### High Street, Denford
* Where the ordinary watercourse meets the River Nene, flooding occurred that affected rear gardens of residential properties and flooded the highway on High Street; the culvert beneath High Street is cited as a contributing factor. (p10)
* Residents on High Street were identified as affected by flooding from main rivers; advice to affected residents was recommended. (p18)

### Spinney Rise, Denford
* Large areas of agricultural fields south of Spinney Rise were submerged in flood water during the flooding incident. (p11)
* Runoff from fields to the south produced flows that exceeded the capacity of the drainage system accepting that runoff. The drainage route runs through residential property before entering the ordinary watercourse, increasing the likelihood of flows affecting residential properties and raising flood risk to those properties. (p15)

### River Nene
* The River Nene rose in level so that properties adjacent to it came close to being flooded; rear gardens were affected. High river levels may have reduced discharge from the ordinary watercourse, contributing to local ponding. (p11)
* Raised water levels in the River Nene are reported as a likely contributory factor, because elevated river levels reduced the ability of the ordinary watercourse to discharge into the Nene, exacerbating flooding upstream. (p15)
* The River Nene contributed to flooding of properties adjacent to its channel in Denford; the report recommends considering flood defence works and assessing contributory factors at outfalls to the main river. (p18)

### Brawns Spinney
* {general}: Areas of flattened grass adjacent to the ordinary watercourse were observed, considered to be evidence of bank overtopping under heavy flows. (p10)


## Topics without coordinates

### A45
* Run-off from the A45 (which has a low point adjacent to the origin of the ordinary watercourse) fed surface-water flow into the Denford ordinary watercourse system. These high volumes of surface water contributed to flooding at Duck End that resulted in internal flooding of residential properties. (p8) [PAG]
* The balancing pond adjacent to the A45 Road overflowed; the outlet pipe and base of the overflow spillway for the balancing pond were involved in the overflow, discharging water beyond the pond's normal limits onto the surrounding area near the A45. (p43) [IMG, PAG]
* Balancing pond adjacent to the A45 overflowed. The outlet pipe and base of the overflow spillway were partially submerged and discharging water; surrounding ground was waterlogged with standing water and muddy surface. An outlet drainage ditch received the overflow and contained visible flow, with vegetation and debris present. (p44) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 1 property; 2 properties (p8) [PAG]

### Northamptonshire Fire and Rescue Service
* The Fire Service undertook continuous efforts, together with residents, to prevent internal flooding of two properties during the surface-water flooding event. (p8)
* Responded during the event and carried out continuous efforts to force water away, preventing flooding of properties on Freeman's Lane and a property at Chapel Lane. (p10)
* Quant: 2 properties (p8)

### Denford Catchment
* Significant rainfall fell on the Denford catchment on 21st November 2012, producing high volumes of surface water runoff that flowed to Duck End and contributed to internal flooding of properties. (p8)
* Significant rainfall on 21 November 2012 produced runoff across the Denford catchment, causing high water levels and flooding that affected people and property in the area. (p9)

### Northamptonshire County Council
* NCC (through LLFA, Emergency Planning and Highways) was involved in the flood response and recommended measures: advising residents on property-level protection, instating a Flood Warden and community flood plan, carrying out highway drainage cleansing, and assessing/replacing gullies, culverts and other assets to reduce flood risk to properties. (p17)

### Highways Agency
* The Highways Agency was tasked to inspect and maintain highway drainage (per the HA Network Manual), update its asset inventory, and to survey the form and function of a pond and assess whether increased surface water collection is increasing flows into the ordinary watercourse — all actions to address highway-related surface water contributions to downstream property flooding. (p17)
