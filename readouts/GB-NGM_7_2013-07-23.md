

# Nottingham Unitary Authority flooding 23 Jul 2013

## Place names with coordinates

### Daron Gardens, Top Valley
* Daron Gardens is highlighted in the report map and is the subject of a Section 19 flood investigation, indicating it was affected by flooding. The map highlights the street segment within a residential block in Top Valley. (p1) [IMG]
* Properties in Daron Gardens suffered internal flooding as part of the Top Valley event caused by the intense storm. (p3)
* Internal (over the doorstep) flooding of residential properties. (p5)
* Properties experienced internal flooding; local topography (head of a valley) and an approximately 500 m2 impervious parking area at the rear of properties contributed to overland runoff and ingress to homes. (p7) [PAG]
* Properties on Daron Gardens were affected by surface water flooding during the event on 23rd July 2013 (the report treats this area as having experienced the flood event on that date). (p9) [PAG]
* Properties on Daron Gardens were flooded during and after the intense rainfall on 23rd July 2013 from rapid overland sheet flow and ponding; sheet runoff from playing fields transported soil and debris into gully pots, bitmac footpaths channelled flows towards the street, highway drainage was overwhelmed, and a resident reported faeces and black sludge in floodwater indicating foul sewer network overload. (p11) [PAG]
* Overland surface-water flow from the playing fields and along footpaths entered Daron Gardens. Saturated ground prevented infiltration and sheet flow from the steep bank carried silt and debris into gullies. The gully network was overwhelmed and kerbs were breached; water pooled and entered properties via wall vents, airbricks and door frames, moving through houses and exiting via back doors/gardens. A resident reported faeces and black sludge in the flood water, indicating the foul sewer network was also overloaded and contributed to contamination of floodwater. (p12) [IMG, PAG]
* The local gully network was overwhelmed and the kerb was breached so water pooled at the lower-threshold properties and entered dwellings via wall vents, air bricks and door frames. Floodwater then exited properties via back doors and gardens and continued flowing downslope. A dividing brick wall at the north end likely caused additional pooling before properties were flooded. (p13) [IMG, PAG]
* Overland surface water pooled behind a dividing brick wall at the north end of Daron Gardens. Once a footpath gully became overwhelmed, water breached the kerb and pooled within the hollow of the houses until levels overtopped the grass verge at the south-east end, producing overland flow that entered properties. This caused internal flooding of houses; there is also evidence the foul sewer network was overwhelmed and contributed to the flooding, though this contribution is considered unlikely to be significant given the location near the head of the drainage catchment. (p14) [PAG]
* Overland (surface water) flow caused internal flooding of residential properties; foul sewer network was reported to have been overwhelmed and to have contributed to flooding though this contribution was considered unlikely to be significant given the location near the head of the drainage catchment. (p15) [IMG, PAG]
* Internal flooding occurred with damage to flooring, walls, kitchens and furniture; residents had to vacate properties while repair work was carried out. (p17) [TAB]
* Intense rainfall generated large volumes of surface water runoff that overwhelmed drainage systems, causing water to pond in low-lying areas and resulting in ingress of water to properties. (p18)
* Intense, flashy rainfall exceeded local drainage and sewer capacity causing surface water to pond and enter properties on Daron Gardens. Residents attempted to unblock footpath gullies but this was ineffective and properties were flooded; residents were unaware of the flood risk and had no preventative measures to deploy. (p19) [PAG]
* Surface water flooding affected the Daron Gardens area during the 23rd July 2013 event. The flooding prompted a Flood Defence Grant Aid funding bid (completed March 2014) and led to planned measures including an optioneering study, a topographic survey and a future surface water flood risk management scheme. Gullies on Daron Gardens have been placed on a targeted gully‑cleansing regime for more frequent maintenance to reduce surface water runoff to the public sewer. (p20) [TAB, PAG]
* Surface-water flooding on 23rd July 2013 affected properties on the street and prompted placing gullies on a targeted gully-cleansing regime for more frequent maintenance; residents were given recommendations to improve property-level resilience and prepare flood plans. (p21) [TAB]
* Residential properties were flooded internally during the extreme rainfall event; properties lie in a low-lying part of the natural valley and experienced extensive external area flooding. (p23)

### Edern Gardens, Top Valley
* Edern Gardens is highlighted in the report map and is the subject of a Section 19 flood investigation, indicating it was affected by flooding. The map highlights the street segment within a residential block in Top Valley. (p1) [IMG]
* Properties in Edern Gardens suffered internal flooding as part of the Top Valley event caused by the intense storm. (p3)
* Internal (over the doorstep) flooding of residential properties. (p5)
* Properties experienced internal flooding; Edern Gardens is located downslope at a low spot within the area, making it a receptor for runoff from upslope areas. (p7) [PAG]
* Properties were flooded during and after the intense rainfall on 23rd July 2013 due to rapid overland flow and ponding in low-lying areas; runoff from nearby streets (Penllech Close and Colmon Close) likely contributed to overland flow and pooling. (p11)
* Surface water converged and concentrated at Edern Garden during the 23rd July 2013 event as part of the primary flow path, with flows then exiting toward the playing fields. (p12) [IMG]
* Rapid surface water flow down the slope used footways and roads as conduits directing water towards properties on Edern Gardens; low-lying ground and a natural hollow plus potentially overwhelmed road gullies allowed pooling and kerb breach that contributed to flooding of properties on Edern Gardens. (p15) [PAG]
* Properties on Edern Gardens were flooded when pooled surface water breached the kerb and entered properties. Pooling occurred because gullies at a low spot on nearby Colmon Close were likely overwhelmed and the public surface water sewer may have been at capacity, and because surface water from saturated footpaths and green areas on Parkview Road flowed overland into this lower-lying location. (p16) [IMG, PAG]
* Overland flow led to internal flooding of properties with damage to flooring, kitchens, walls, doors, plaster and furniture. A low spot in the footpath allowed water to pool and exacerbate flooding. (p17) [TAB, PAG]
* Intense rainfall generated large volumes of surface water runoff that overwhelmed drainage systems, causing water to pond in low-lying areas and resulting in ingress of water to properties. (p18)
* Surface water from intense rainfall was unable to drain into the highway drainage/sewer system and flooded properties on Edern Gardens; adopted footpath gullies were difficult to access and residents had limited ability to prevent ingress. (p19)
* Surface water flooding affected the Edern Gardens area during the 23rd July 2013 event. The flooding prompted a Flood Defence Grant Aid funding bid (completed March 2014) and led to planned measures including an optioneering study, a topographic survey and a future surface water flood risk management scheme to be constructed once partnership funding is secured. (p20) [TAB]
* Properties on Edern Gardens were affected by the 23rd July 2013 flooding and are the subject of resident/landlord recommendations to improve resilience (prepare flood plans, consider property-level protection). (p21)
* Residential properties were flooded internally during the extreme rainfall event; properties lie in a low-lying part of the natural valley and experienced extensive external area flooding. (p23)

### Nottingham Unitary Authority
* Nottingham is the broader administrative area containing Top Valley and the streets investigated; the report concerns a flood event in this area on 23 July 2013. (p1)
* An intense storm on the evening of 23 July 2013 caused widespread flooding across the city, resulting in residential properties being flooded. (p3)
* A number of properties in the area flooded; this prompted the City Council to carry out a formal Section 19 flood investigation and produce this report summarising causes and impacts. (p4)
* The report area within Nottingham experienced surface water flooding associated with the 23rd July 2013 event; the report treats this locality as affected by that event. (p9)
* An intense storm on 23rd July 2013 passed across the north of Nottingham City; the resulting surface-water flood event began during the storm, with rainfall exceeding sewer design standards and causing urban/surface-water flooding. Severn Trent Water classified the event as 'extreme'. (p10)
* Properties on Daron Gardens were affected by the 23rd July 2013 flooding. As a response gullies on Daron Gardens have been placed on a Targeted Gully Cleansing Regime for more frequent maintenance to improve removal of surface water. (p21) [TAB]
* A series of heavy rainfall events passed over the city on 22–23 July 2013 with a fourth event on 23rd July causing flooding that resulted in internal flooding of 11 residential properties in the Top Valley area and extensive external flooding. (p23) [PAG]
* Quant: over 100 residential properties (p3)
* Quant: 11 residential properties (total across Daron Gardens and Edern Gardens) (p23) [PAG]

### Top Valley
* Top Valley (the locality containing Daron Gardens and Edern Gardens) is the area investigated under Section 19 for flooding affecting those streets on 23 July 2013. (p1)
* The Daron Gardens and Edern Gardens area of Top Valley experienced internal flooding of residential properties during the intense storm. (p3)
* An intense storm on 23rd July 2013 moved across the north of Nottingham City including the Top Valley area and initiated a flood event. Surface-water runoff overwhelmed drainage and exceeded sewer design capacity, producing urban / surface-water flooding in the area; Severn Trent Water classed the event as 'extreme'. (p10)
* Areas of green open space within the wider Top Valley area were saturated by rainfall before and during the event on 23rd July 2013, contributing to rapid overland flow and ponding that fed flooding downslope. (p11)
* Area experienced a series of heavy rainfall events culminating on 23rd July 2013 that led to internal flooding of 11 properties in Daron Gardens and Edern Gardens and extensive external flooding in the valley. (p23)
* Quant: 11 properties (p3)
* Quant: 11 residential properties (total across Daron Gardens and Edern Gardens) (p23)

### Colmon Close, Top Valley
* Surface water flowed along the street as part of the primary flow path during the 23rd July 2013 flood event, forming part of the routed runoff through the residential area. (p12) [IMG]
* Road gullies at a low spot on the bend of Colmon Close collected surface water from uphill areas; during the storm these gullies may have been overwhelmed, causing local pooling at the low spot and contributing to kerb breach and downstream flooding of Edern Gardens. (p15)
* A low spot on Colmon Close with two road gullies collected surface water from Penllech Close and uphill on Colmon Close; during the storm the gullies were possibly overwhelmed (and the public surface water sewer potentially at capacity), allowing water to pool and breach the kerb — a contributor to flooding of properties on Edern Gardens. (p16) [IMG, PAG]

### Parkview Road, Top Valley

## Topics without coordinates

### Severn Trent
* The public sewer network was exceeded by the intense storm on 23 July 2013, resulting in sewer flooding/overwhelmed sewers in the area; Severn Trent Water classified the event as 'extreme'. (p10)
* Classified the 23 July 2013 rainfall as 'extreme' because it exceeded the modern public sewer design standard (1 in 30 year) and therefore the sewerage system capacity was exceeded; noted the need to work in partnership with the council on future surface water solutions but determined the event did not meet criteria for a sewer capacity improvement scheme at this time. (p19)
* Recorded the 23rd July 2013 flood event on company records; carried out inspections and remedial work to maintain public sewer serviceability (including blockage removal) and will monitor future internal/external flooding incidents to identify triggers for capital investment. (p21) [TAB]
* Severn Trent Water (responsible for public sewers) was identified as a Risk Management Authority for the incident; it investigated the flooding in partnership with Nottingham City Council. The company determined the extreme rainfall did not meet its criteria for capital capacity improvements for the event, and agreed actions focusing on continued maintenance of existing drainage and sewer systems so they operate at maximum efficiency during future heavy rainfall. (p23) [PAG]
* Quant: 11 residential properties (p23) [PAG]

### Nottingham City Council
* Responded to the 23 July 2013 flooding by recording and maintaining road gullies around Daron Gardens and Edern Gardens (annual maintenance instituted post-event), accepting responsibility for maintaining adopted footpath gullies (access limited), targeting the area for a long‑term surface water diversion scheme toward Southglade Park, submitting funding bids (March 2014) and managing distribution of Repair and Renew Grant applications to affected residents. (p19)
* Following flooding on 23rd July 2013, road gullies on Daron Gardens were placed on a Targeted Gully Cleansing Regime and ongoing gully maintenance was carried out to maximise transfer of surface water to the public sewer network; residents were advised to report blocked gullies and future flooding incidents. (p21) [TAB]
