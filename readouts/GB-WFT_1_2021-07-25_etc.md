### London Borough of Waltham Forest
* {25 Jul 2021, 07 Aug 2021 - 08 Aug 2021}: Borough‑wide flooding investigation following intense rainfall on 25 July 2021 and 7–8 August 2021. The report examines surface water / urban flooding impacts across the borough. (p1) [MUL]
* {25 Jul 2021, 07 Aug 2021, 08 Aug 2021}: On 25 July 2021 intense torrential rainfall caused widespread flooding across the borough (most affected areas included Walthamstow, Wood Street, Leyton and Leytonstone, focused on the Fillebrook catchment). Water entered homes, schools and businesses, and affected Whipps Cross Hospital; eyewitness reports in worst-hit areas indicated water levels up to 700mm, vehicles were submerged and there was widespread loss of power. Subsequent rainfall on 7 and 8 August 2021 triggered further flooding in these areas. (p6) [MUL]
* {25 Jul 2021}: Intense rainfall on 25 July 2021 far exceeded the design capacity of the borough drainage system and acted as the underlying catalyst for widespread flooding across the borough; the report identifies specific areas of the borough as particularly sensitive and prone to flooding, amplified by a Victorian-era drainage system. (p7) [PAG]
* {25 Jul 2021, 07 Aug 2021, 08 Aug 2021}: Flooding occurred across Waltham Forest during the events of 25 July 2021 and 7–8 August 2021; the report summarises the extent, impacts and mechanisms and identifies a need for increased surface water network capacity and other mitigation measures (for example SUDS and sewer upgrades). (p8) [MUL]
* {general}: The River Lee system and associated channels and brooks form the main river network along the borough’s western and southern edges, concentrating river flood risk across Waltham Forest (context for flood risk described on the map). (p13) [IMG]
* {general}: The borough is subject to both fluvial (river) and pluvial (surface water) flood risk. The River Lea, Ching Brook and Dagenham Brook are identified as the three main rivers/watercourses that are the primary sources of fluvial flooding. The largely culverted Fillebrooke can contribute to localised fluvial flooding at its open channel sections and influences surface water flooding where it forms part of the Thames Water sewer network. Thirteen Critical Drainage Areas (CDAs) have been identified across the borough, with five main CDAs prioritised for drainage management (Group4_064, Group4_067, Group4_054, Group4_048, Group4_047). These CDAs represent corridors and low-lying locations (river valleys, underpasses, railway cuttings/embankments, topographical low points) that are susceptible to deep surface water ponding and sewer-influenced flooding. (p16) [IMG]
* {2007, 2009, 2012, 2016}: Map and borough records show localized areas within the borough at risk of surface water flooding (1-in-30, 1-in-100 and 1-in-1000 year probabilities).
