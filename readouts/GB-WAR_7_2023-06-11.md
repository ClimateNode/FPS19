## Place names with coordinates

### Hurley
* At least nine properties experienced internal flooding due to surface water; businesses were also affected. (p3)
* Flooding on 11 June 2023 led to internal flooding of residential properties in the locality; partners and WCC undertook remedial works to local drainage infrastructure and engaged with residents to provide advice and identify impacts. (p4) [PAG]
* A concentrated band of very high-intensity rainfall passed over Hurley on 11 June 2023 (peak between c.19:00–20:00). Hydromaster pinpoint data show intense, short‑duration rainfall at the village during the event, consistent with conditions that produce surface-water (flash) flooding in the locality. (p6) [IMG, PAG]
* A band of intense, short-duration rainfall passed directly over Hurley on the evening of 11 June 2023, producing extreme surface water/flash-flooding conditions in the village (rapid accumulation of runoff and high local flood risk). (p7) [PAG, IMG]
* On 11th June 2023 intense, highly localised rainfall caused highway flooding, external flooding of gardens and garages, and internal flooding of properties in the village; anecdotal and resident reports indicated multiple flood reports with hotspots at Knowle Hill and Kings Close. (p8) [PAG]
* The village experienced flooding during the event on 11 June 2023. The LLFA investigation focused on the central residential area (the green-circle area on the map) where flood reports were concentrated. (p9) [IMG]
* Short-duration, high-intensity rainfall on 11th June 2023 produced widespread surface water flows and ponding on highways, inundation of artificial drainage networks (including sewers), and exceedance flow routing through the built environment that channelled water towards properties. (p10) [PAG]
* Environment Agency surface water extent mapping shows concentrated surface water flooding along the main road through Hurley, with flood routes along Knowle Hill and Queensway and extensive surface water spread in the southwest quadrant of the village affecting built-up and open areas. (p11) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface water flooding affected the village during the storm on 11th June 2023. EA surface water mapping shows flood routes through the village (notably along Queensway and Knowle Hill); combined system surcharging and manhole discharge contributed to flood levels and to flooding of properties. (p12) [PAG]
* Across the village, the combined drainage network reached capacity causing surface water to collect on highways (e.g. Knowle Hill) and route toward properties resulting in property flooding. Sewers surcharging (surcharged manholes) worsened flood volumes. Investigations and remedial actions (jetting, spot repairs, PFR installations in previous events, planned culvert works and additional gulleys) have been undertaken or proposed to reduce future impacts. (p13)
* {2020, 11 Jun 2023}: On 11 June 2023 heavy rainfall caused surface water runoff and surcharge of the combined sewer network so flows could not be captured. Surface water pooled, overtopped kerbs and flowed into properties and highways. Both internal and external property flooding occurred across the village; private/blocked drainage and highway runoff worsened impacts. (p16) [PAG, MUL]
* {2012, 2020, 2021}: Historic and repeated flooding recorded in the village: standalone reports of highway drainage and localised flooding in 2012 and 2021; a 2020 event influenced by surface-water run-off caused both internal and external property flooding. (p17) [IMG, TAB, PAG, MUL]
* Quant: 9 properties (p3)
* Quant: 9 properties (p4) [PAG]
* {2020, 11 Jun 2023}: Quant: 9 residential properties internally flooded; 1 commercial property internally flooded; 5 properties externally flooded (p16) [PAG, MUL]

### Kings Close, Hurley
* During the 11th June 2023 event this area generated a high volume of flood reports indicating localised flooding within Kings Close. (p8) [PAG]
* King's Close lies within the central investigation focus in Hurley and was included in the LLFA's investigation of flooding from the event on 11 June 2023. (p9)
* The road's angled decline on 11th June 2023 caused exceedance surface water flows to route between properties and into rear gardens; restricted drainage capacity prevented rapid entry to the drainage network, leading to internal and garden flooding and flows reaching property fronts. (p10)
* Drainage within the street is unadopted (developer/management company responsibility); the development outfalls to the Severn Trent surface water sewer. During the heavy rainfall surface water was not drained away quickly enough, creating exceedance routes and contributing to local flooding where the artificial drainage was restricted or reached capacity. (p11) [PAG]
* The combined drainage system in Hurley reached capacity, surcharging and discharging via manholes which contributed to surface-water flooding affecting the Kings Close development and properties there. Drainage condition and maintenance were identified as contributing factors. (p12)
* Properties at a lower level were inundated when flows were funnelled down the decline of the road; floodwater entered through front doors and back gardens. The drainage within Kings Close is private; a CCTV survey has been organised by WCC FRM to investigate. (p16)

### Knowle Hill, Hurley
* During the 11th June 2023 event this area generated a high volume of flood reports indicating localised flooding within Knowle Hill. (p8)
* Knowle Hill lies within the central Hurley investigation focus and received reported flooding during the event; impacts are recorded as part of the Hurley flood investigation. (p9) [IMG]
* Surface water flowed down the road creating exceedance routes that carried water over footways into properties; water entered properties via front doors and airbricks. Rear flooding and internal flooding were reported due to groundwater/hydrostatic pressure. Surcharging of the Severn Trent combined sewer network and overwhelmed WCC highway drains contributed to garden and internal flooding. (p11) [IMG]
* The highway drainage network reached capacity causing surface water to collect on the road and route towards adjacent properties, resulting in property flooding; combined sewer system surcharge (surcharged manholes) worsened flood volumes. WCC Highways cleared debris (jetting) and identified a parallel ditch and culvert constraints; further culvert enlargement and maintenance works have been planned. (p13)
* {2020, 11 Jun 2023}: Highway and network reached capacity so water pooled on the carriageway, overtopped kerbs and flowed downhill into several properties which flooded internally and externally. Previously fitted Property Flood Resilience measures (after 2020) did not prevent ingress via brickwork, air vents and floors. (p16) [MUL]
* {2020}: Properties on Knowle Hill were affected by flooding (internal and external flooding referenced in 2020) leading to installation of PFR measures; highway drainage and ditching issues adjacent to the lower half of Knowle Hill require remedial works and cyclic gully cleansing. (p17) [TAB]

### Queensway, Warwick
* Highway drainage reached capacity during intense rainfall on 11th June 2023, causing large amounts of surface water ponding on the highway and creating flow routes that channelled water towards adjacent properties and into residential areas. (p10)
* EA surface water extent mapping and narrative indicate flood routes were present on Queensway during the storm, with surface water flowing along the main road and contributing to local flooding in the area. (p11) [IMG]
* Surface-water flood routes ran along Queensway and were exacerbated during the storm, increasing runoff and causing flooding along the street (surface water flow paths shown on EA mapping). (p12) [PAG]
* Surcharge and surface water runoff from Queensway contributed additional flow into the local drainage and into neighbouring low-lying properties (including Kings Close), exacerbating flooding. (p16)

## Topics without coordinates

### Warwickshire County Council
* WCC's Flood Risk Management team led the Section 19 investigation and recovery phase after the 11 June 2023 flooding: they worked with partners to identify affected residents, provided advice and guidance, and undertook remedial works to drainage infrastructure where required. (p4)
* WCC officers (Flood Risk Management and County Highways) attended Hurley after the event; the heavy rainfall affected the capability of WCC highway drainage, which was overwhelmed and contributed to surface water exceedance routes along the highway. (p11) [PAG]
* WCC Flood Risk Management team investigated the event, concluded ordinary watercourses did not have an adverse impact on this flooding, contacted riparian owners to ensure watercourses are maintained, and coordinated/communicated planned maintenance actions to reduce future surface water impacts on the village. (p13) [PAG]
* Following the 11th June 2023 flood event WCC received reports of property flooding across Hurley and contacted responsible housing companies to request information and engage on protecting properties and tenants; no responses were received from those companies. (p14)
* Warwickshire County Council, as highway authority, is implicated because highway drainage reached capacity during the heavy rainfall on 11th June 2023; pooled water overtopped kerbs and flowed towards lower-lying properties (notably on Knowle Hill and Kings Close), causing internal and external flooding. WCC hold records of previous events (2012, 2020, 2021) and have been investigating highway drainage following the event. (p16)
* {2012, 2021}: Highway drainage and localised flooding issues have been recorded (notably referenced in historical reports); actions include scheduling cyclic gully cleansing on Knowle Hill and across adoptable highway within Hurley and consideration of altering highway/kerb levels to improve exceedance flow routes. (p17) [TAB, MUL]
* Quant: 9 properties (p4)
* Quant: 9 residential properties internally flooded; 1 commercial property internally flooded; 5 properties externally flooded (p16)

### Severn Trent
* The Severn Trent Water combined sewer network in Hurley surcharged on 11th June 2023, contributing to floodwater that travelled towards properties (notably on Kings Close) and interacting with surface water exceedance routes. (p10)
* The Severn Trent combined sewer network reached capacity and surcharged during the storm, contributing to reported garden flooding and reducing the ability to accept surface water runoff from developments and highways. (p11) [PAG]
* The combined sewer system in Hurley reached capacity during the storm, surcharging and flooding out of manholes in areas of the village and contributing to surface water flooding across Hurley. (p12)
* Severn Trent reported the combined sewer network reached capacity and that surcharged manholes contributed to surface water routing onto the highway and into properties. They undertook investigative and remedial actions including jetting sewer networks (including Elm Grove), spot repairs for defects (root ingress, cracks), and have carried out or overseen PFR installations previously; they also completed hydraulic modelling to review PFR effectiveness. (p13)
* Severn Trent Water’s combined sewer network in Hurley contributed to the flooding on 11th June 2023 by surcharging (sewer surcharge from chambers within the highway and private gardens), which prevented additional surface water entering the network and contributed to internal and external property flooding. The company had previously fitted Property Flood Resilience measures for some properties after 2020 flooding and had addressed root ingress found in 2020. (p16)
* {2020}: Following flooding reported in 2020, Severn Trent identified and rectified significant root ingress in its network, surveyed and cleansed the sewer network as required, and fitted property flood resilience (PFR) measures in multiple properties. (p17) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 9 residential properties internally flooded; 1 commercial property internally flooded; 5 properties externally flooded (p16)
