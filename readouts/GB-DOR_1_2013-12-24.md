

# Dorset flooding 24 Dec 2013

## Place names with coordinates

### Palmersford Roundabout
* {general}: The Uddens Water passes beneath the A31 Palmersford Roundabout via a large concrete box culvert (6 m wide, 2.5 m high). A surface water sewer enters the Uddens Water via a piped connection inside this large culvert; another sewer discharges immediately downstream of the culvert. (p6)
* Part of the Palmersford Roundabout on the A31 was flooded; parts of the adjacent road network were inundated during the event. (p7) [PAG]
* Part of the Palmersford Roundabout on the A31 was flooded during the event; it was a source/direction of overland flow entering the Gladelands site. (p8) [PAG]
* Flooding observed on the A31 at the Palmersford Roundabout; photographic evidence used to estimate peak water level on the roundabout during the event. (p9) [PAG]
* The roundabout was flooded first as upstream water levels rose due to downstream controls; floodwaters from the roundabout then entered the adjacent Gladelands site. (p11)
* The Palmersford roundabout was flooded as upstream water levels rose following surcharge of the downstream A31 culvert and backing up of the Uddens; floodwaters from the roundabout then entered the adjacent Gladelands site. (p12) [PAG]
* The large culvert beneath the A31 Palmersford roundabout was identified as a critical drainage asset to manage flood risk; it was recommended that the culvert be cleared and desilted regularly to reduce blockages that contributed to the December 2013 flooding. (p15)
* Roundabout and approaching carriageway were heavily flooded; water covered the full width of the road making the junction/roundabout impassable to vehicles and creating a transport hazard. (p21) [PAG]
* Carriageway and roundabout area submerged by standing surface water making the road impassable; traffic management in place (road closed sign, cones) and police attendance. (p22) [IMG, PAG]

### Uddens Water
* {general}: The Uddens Water is the major watercourse in the area (Main River). Within the study area it is open channel through woodland and it passes beneath the A31 Palmersford Roundabout via a 6 m wide, 2.5 m high concrete box culvert; it also receives flows from a tributary and from surface water sewers that connect into the river (one connection inside the box culvert beneath the Palmersford Roundabout and another immediately downstream). (p6)
* Water levels in the Uddens rose above bank/above ground, producing overbank/overland flows that entered the Gladelands site from the north‑west and contributed to the flooding; downstream level at the culvert was estimated from photographs. (p8) [PAG]
* Elevated river levels downstream of the A31 Palmersford Roundabout culvert; photographic evidence and pumping-station telemetry show peak levels during the event and debris accumulation in the channel. (p9) [PAG]
* Contemporary reports during the 24th December 2013 flood of tree, wood and other debris building up in the channel; debris accumulation was present at the time of the flood event. (p10) [PAG]
* Downstream water levels on the Uddens rose (reaching at least 12.80mAOD during the event), which surcharged culverts and backed up the open channel, drowning smaller culverts and causing flow restrictions that forced upstream water levels to rise and produce flooding upstream. (p11)
* Uddens Water was identified as having elevated water levels (subject of modelling) that contributed to surcharging downstream culverts and backing up flow, producing upstream flooding that affected Palmersford roundabout and Gladelands. (p12)
* Elevated downstream water levels in the Uddens Water caused overbanking; floodwater entered the Gladelands site, joined other flows in low areas and flooded residential park homes, prompting an emergency evacuation of residents. (p14)
* High river levels with raised flow; river water partially submerged a riverside fence and extended into adjacent vegetation, indicating river-bank inundation. (p23) [IMG]

### Gladelands Park
* A number of residential park homes on the Gladelands site were flooded. (p5)
* {general}: The Gladelands site lies at a local low point immediately south of the A31; overland surface water flows collect on the site and are trapped by the A31 dual carriageway embankment which effectively forms a dam between the site and the Uddens Water. (p6)
* A significant part of the Gladelands site was inundated when overland flows from two directions joined in lower areas of the site, flooding properties and prompting an emergency evacuation of residents. (p8)
* Photographs show the Gladelands site was flooded during the event; photographic analysis was used to assess extent and levels at the site. (p9)
* Site experienced flooding on 24 December 2013. Hydraulic modelling concluded the only viable explanation that matched the observed flood at Gladelands was elevated water levels in the Uddens Water downstream of the A31/Palmersford Roundabout; debris in the Uddens Water was also reported at the time of the event. (p10)
* Floodwaters entered the Gladelands site after upstream water levels rose due to downstream elevated levels on the Uddens; the 600mm culvert crossing the site and an upstream box culvert were drowned out, allowing floodwater from the Palmersford roundabout and the A31 to inundate land to the west of the site and the site itself. (p11) [PAG]
* Floodwaters entered the Gladelands site after downstream water levels (>= 12.80mAOD) surcharged the A31 Palmersford Road culvert and backed up the open channel of the Uddens; the site's 600mm culvert and an upstream box culvert were drowned, allowing floodwater from the Palmersford roundabout and adjacent land west of the site to inundate the site. (p12) [PAG]
* Floodwater from overland flows (entering from the A348 to the east and from the north west) collected in the low areas of the Gladelands site, flooding residential park homes and prompting an emergency evacuation of residents. (p14)
* Park and adjacent access areas were inundated: water covered roads, pathways and grassed areas; signage and fencing partially submerged; buildings/park homes visible behind the waterline and access rendered impassable. (p21) [IMG]
* {general}: Quant: approximately 100 residential park homes (p6)

### Dorset

### Ferndown
* The Gladelands site in Ferndown was affected by the 24 December 2013 flood, with residential park homes flooded. (p5)
* The Gladelands site in Ferndown experienced flooding when overland flows and elevated downstream water levels caused floodwater to enter and collect in low-lying areas, resulting in property flooding and evacuations. (p14)

### Priory Road, Ferndown

## Topics without coordinates

### A31
* {general}: The A31 dual carriageway embankment effectively forms a dam between the Gladelands site and the Uddens Water, causing overland surface water flows to collect on and be trapped by the Gladelands site. (p6)
* Parts of the adjacent road network were flooded, in particular part of the Palmersford Roundabout on the A31. (p8) [PAG]
* Photographs show the A31 at Palmersford Roundabout was inundated during the event; photographic analysis estimated water level on the roundabout at the time to be approximately 13.1–13.2mAOD. (p9)
* Downstream river levels rose to at least 12.80mAOD and surcharged the A31 Palmersford Road culvert, impeding flow. The surcharge contributed to backing up the Uddens, flooding the Palmersford roundabout and causing upstream flooding that affected the adjacent Gladelands site. (p12) [PAG]
* Carriageway of the A31 was flooded, with water covering significant portions of the road and rendering it impassable to traffic; vehicles approaching the flooded section were at risk. (p21)

### Sewage Pumping Station
* {24 Dec 2013, 25 Dec 2013}: Telemetry within the station wet well recorded elevated water levels; the station did not flood (external flood water levels remained below the cover slab). Recorded levels peaked just after midnight on 24 December and remained elevated for approximately 12 hours before returning to normal by midday on 25 December. (p8) [PAG, MUL]
* Wet well telemetry recorded elevated water levels due to external flood water. The pumping station did not flood; flood levels remained below the cover slab (cover slab level 12.99mAOD). Levels peaked just after midnight on 24 December and remained elevated for about 12 hours, returning to normal by midday 25 December. (p9) [PAG]
* Floodwater encroached onto the grassy area immediately adjacent to the pumping station; water reached the fence line and extended close to the building. (p23) [IMG]

### Wessex Water
* {24 Dec 2013, 25 Dec 2013}: Wessex Water's telemetry at their sewage pumping station recorded elevated wet well water levels (peaking just after midnight on 24 December) associated with the flood; Wessex Water provided photographs and telemetry used to assess downstream water levels. (p8) [PAG, MUL]
* Telemetry from Wessex Water’s sewage pumping station wet well recorded elevated water levels caused by external flood water; the station did not flood and external flood water remained below the pumping station cover slab. Wessex Water supplied photographs used to estimate downstream water levels. (p9) [PAG]
* Two Wessex Water‑operated surface water sewers in the A31 dual carriageway east of the site were identified as requiring inspection and cleaning because they may have contributed to the flood impacts; inspection/cleaning was recommended as an interim action following the December 2013 event. (p15)

### A348
* Overland flow from the east (associated with the A348) entered the Gladelands site and contributed to floodwater collecting in the low areas of the site. (p14)
