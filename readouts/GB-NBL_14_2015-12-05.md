

# Corbridge flooding 05 Dec 2015

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Corbridge<br>091\_Northumberland/Corbridge\_FIR\_2015.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### River Tyne
* The River Tyne swelled (with its tributaries) causing overtopping of the defences at Corbridge and contributing to widespread flooding of properties and businesses in the area. (p1) [IMG]
* {2015}: River Tyne overtopped embankments during the 2015 flood event and drove the main floodwater flows into the built-up Corbridge/Tyne Valley area, causing embankment overtopping and downstream inundation of low-lying areas. (p3) [IMG]
* Quant: 43 residential properties; 12 businesses; 2 other buildings (p1) [IMG]

### Northumberland
* Areas of Northumberland were affected by flooding during Storm Desmond (4–5 Dec 2015), including Corbridge where defences overtopped and flooding occurred mainly on the southern side of the River Tyne; 43 residential properties were internally flooded, 12 businesses flooded and the caravan park sustained heavy damage. (p1) [IMG]
* Quant: 43 residential properties; 12 businesses; 2 other buildings (p1) [IMG]

### North England
* Experienced heavy and persistent rainfall during Storm Desmond which led to swollen rivers and flooding in areas including Corbridge; this regional rainfall drove the river and surface-water flooding reported. (p1)

### Corbridge
* Defences at Corbridge overtopped when the River Tyne and its tributaries swelled. Flooding affected the southern side of the river with internal flooding of homes, damage to businesses and to a nearby caravan park; families were rescued from upper floors. Surface water problems also occurred around Station Road in the weeks afterwards. Temporary repairs to flood defences were made and recovery/mitigation actions (pumps, clean-up, PLR grants, drainage investigations) were undertaken. (p1) [IMG, TAB, PAG]
* Flooding in Corbridge damaged/affected local flood defences (temporary repairs carried out), caused damage to highway infrastructure (repairs made), affected the sewer network (CCTV and cleansing carried out; sewer outfalls checked) and affected properties (property-level resilience grants made available). Community recovery and an initial economic assessment after the floods were completed. (p2) [TAB]
* Floodwaters from the River Tyne overtopped embankments and flowed across low-lying parts of Corbridge (Dilston Haugh, Station Road, The Stanners, Tinkler’s Bank), causing ponding, embankment erosion, cemetery damage and impacts to nearby sports club facilities; photographs and callouts on the map document property and infrastructure water damage. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 43 residential properties; 12 businesses; 2 other buildings (p1) [IMG, TAB, PAG]

### Station Road, Corbridge
* {01 Jan 2016 - 31 Jan 2016}: Persistent rain throughout January contributed to localised surface-water issues in the Station Road area, prompting investigation of surface-water drainage options. (p1) [PAG]
* Surface water flooding affected the area around Station Road, prompting investigations into surface water drainage options and appraisal of preferred options for further investigation. (p2) [TAB]
* Floodwater routed past overtopped embankments flowed across Station Road, causing the road to be inundated and contributing to downstream property/infrastructure flooding as shown in callouts and flow arrows. (p3) [IMG]

### Tinkler's Bank, Corbridge
* Tinkler's Bank experienced water ponding and accumulation of routed floodwater after embankment overtopping, as noted by callouts and photographic evidence on the map. (p3)

### The Stanners
* Surface water flooding affected the Stanners area, leading to investigations into drainage options to identify preferred solutions. (p2) [TAB]
* Floodwaters passed into The Stanners area after embankment overtopping, with callouts indicating ponding and property flooding impacts in this low-lying zone. (p3) [IMG]

### Kielder Water
* {general}: NW and the EA reviewed reservoir operating levels as part of flood risk management: discussions were held about reducing the maximum water level at Kielder Reservoir to provide greater flood water attenuation. The reservoir's modelling was transferred to new software (Aquator) to determine acceptable maximum levels; testing was proposed and any viable changes to maximum water level were to be implemented from 1 November 2016. (p2) [TAB]

### Corbridge Rugby Club
* Floodwaters reached the Rugby Club grounds (sports club), with the map highlighting impacts on sports-club facilities resulting from routed floodwater and overtopping mechanisms. (p3) [IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Caravan Park
* The caravan park, located just beyond the river bank, sustained heavy damage as a result of the floodwaters. (p1)
* Quant: 1 caravan park (p1)

### Northumberland County Council
* Northumberland County Council carried out temporary repairs to flood defences (to be made permanent later), provided property-level resilience grants, supported high-volume pump and clean-up activity, attended community action group meetings on recovery, and investigated surface water drainage and in-channel gravel impacts around Station Road and the Stanners following the flood. (p1) [IMG]

### Environment Agency
* Flooding affected local flood defences in Corbridge; temporary repairs were carried out (to be made permanent). The EA modelled the impact of in‑channel gravel and found it would contribute little or no change to water levels. Vegetation on the gravel island was identified as having a significant effect on flows and removal of vegetation is proposed to improve flow during high flows. The EA completed an initial economic assessment following the floods and engaged with the local community and Parish Council on recovery. (p2) [TAB]

### Dilston Haugh
* Floodwaters from the River Tyne overtopped embankments and flowed across the low-lying Dilston Haugh, allowing river water to bypass defenses and move inland. (p3) [IMG]

### Cricket Club
* Floodwater affected the Cricket Club grounds; the map highlights vulnerability and impacts to sports-club areas from overflow and ponding during the event. (p3) [IMG]
