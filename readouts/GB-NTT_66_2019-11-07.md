

# Shireoaks flooding 07 Nov 2019

## Place names with coordinates

### Shireoaks
* The village experienced extensive flooding during the heavy rainfall event; saturated ground following an exceptionally wet autumn contributed to flooding across the area. (p1) [PAG]
* A significant flood event affected parts of the village: internal property flooding, flooding of public highways, and inundation of several gardens and curtilages. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Internal property flooding across the village (25 reported incidents); public highways in the village were flooded; several gardens and curtilages were affected by floodwater. (p3) [IMG]
* The town was affected by the November flood event: properties were internally flooded; community flood-resilience store sandbags were distributed by volunteers; volunteers also checked and cleared culverts and watercourses which reduced the number of internally flooded properties. (p6) [PAG]
* A flooding incident prompted continuing investigations into causes and a funding bid for a full catchment study into flood risk; community support measures (flood warden scheme, local sandbag stores, community flood signage) are being referenced as responses to the incident. (p7)
* Quant: 25 reported incidents of internal property flooding (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 25 incidents of internal property flooding (p3) [IMG]

### Nottinghamshire
* Nottinghamshire experienced extensive flooding following intense rainfall and saturated ground; a major incident was declared for the county. (p1) [PAG]
* {01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019}: Severe weather across the county triggered a major incident declaration by the Nottinghamshire Local Resilience Forum. The Environment Agency monitored river levels and issued widespread flood communications across the county. (p2)
* Quant: 66 road closures; over 750 flood signs placed; over 5000 sandbags delivered (p1) [PAG]
* {01 Nov 2019 - 30 Nov 2019}: Quant: 38 flood warnings; 16 flood alerts (p2)

### East Midlands
* Parts of the region experienced extensive flooding caused by exceptionally intense rainfall on 7 November 2019 and saturated ground, resulting in widespread flood conditions across the East Midlands. (p1)

### Shireoaks Road, Shireoaks
* Recorded internal flooding in residences and impacts to the public highway; one of the largest affected zones in the village during the event. (p3) [IMG]
* Surface water runoff from saturated land and from Shireoaks Common collected on Shireoaks Road; highway drainage was overwhelmed causing deep ponding that spread into front gardens. Some drivers created large bow waves which washed water into the thresholds of properties on Shireoaks Road. Later, River Ryton overtopped banks in places and flooded into the back gardens of some Shireoaks Road properties, leaving some properties threatened by surface water at the front and river flooding at the rear. (p4) [PAG]
* Road became unpassable and was closed due to flooding. (p6)

### Thorpe Lane, Shireoaks
* Recorded internal flooding in the street/area; part of the public highway network affected during the event. (p3) [IMG]
* The location where the River Ryton flows under Thorpe Lane had a bridge/large culvert whose capacity was insufficient; this created an obstruction that contributed to the river overtopping banks and flooding rear gardens of some properties on Shireoaks Road. (p4) [PAG]

### Shireoaks Common, Shireoaks
* A smaller area of internal flooding was recorded at Shireoaks Common during the event. (p3) [IMG]
* Surface water ran down Shireoaks Common (from beyond the train tracks) using the road as a channel; combined with runoff from hills, this water flowed through rear gardens and properties on Shireoaks Common and contributed to collection of water on Shireoaks Road. (p4)

### Bethel Terrace, Shireoaks
* One of the largest zones with recorded internal property flooding during the event. (p3) [IMG]
* Surface water from fields behind the cricket pitch and from the direction of the lakes flowed into the Bethel Terrace area and through properties there, moving through rear gardens and onward to properties on Shireoaks Common and onto the highway. (p4)


## Topics without coordinates

### River Ryton
* Fluvial (river) flooding occurred in the area during the event; the River Ryton runs through Shireoaks and may have contributed to the river (fluvial) flooding that affected the village. (p2)
* The river exceeded its channel capacity (fluvial) and contributed to flooding in Shireoaks. (p3)
* River levels rose to a peak (2.16 m) on 07/11/2019; where the river flowed under Thorpe Lane the bridge/culvert capacity was insufficient and created an obstruction, forcing the river to overtop its banks in various places and flood into the back gardens of some Shireoaks Road properties. (p4) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 2.16 m peak river level (p4) [IMG, PAG]
