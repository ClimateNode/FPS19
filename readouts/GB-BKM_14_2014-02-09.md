## Place names with coordinates

### Willowbank Island
* Internal flooding of residential properties and business premises in the Willowbank area caused by overflow/interaction of the River Colne channels (branches, weirs and sluices) around the residential area. (p6) [PAG]
* {general}: Willowbank is part of the area reported to have experienced previous flooding (early 2000s). A flood alleviation scheme (banks) was installed in 1988 as part of the Lower Colne Improvements Scheme to protect Willowbank. (p15)
* Fluvial (river) flooding in February 2014 caused by over-topping of river banks and of a low flood-defence bund; resulted in defined flow routes and inundation across the Willowbank and Oakside residential area. (p19) [PAG]
* River overtopping/outflow occurred near the gauging station weir at the northern end of Willowbank Island; water then flowed overland from that location into downstream Oakside areas. (p20)
* Rising river levels and a reported possible blockage at the southern end prompted response. Bucks Flood & Rescue Service opened the Allied Mills sluice at Kings Mill Cottage which resulted in upstream levels dropping. Debris on the access bridge indicated water reached over the soffit level. Sandbags were requested to protect low spots in the Willowbank recreation area. (p22)
* Surface-water flooding occurred in the Willowbank recreation area; site operative requested sandbags to protect two low spots and locations were passed to the response gang. Initial flooding in Willowbank was caused by surface water that could not drain away due to poorly maintained drainage channels and then exacerbated by high river levels. (p23) [TAB, PAG]
* River Colne passes through New Denham and around Willowbank (classified as Main River); fluvial flood risk is identified and local open surface water ditches and piped systems (feeding from Alder Road, Willow Avenue and Hawthorn Drive) can back up and deliver water into properties. (p24)
* The area between Poplar Road and High Bridge is at high flood risk. Parts are protected by flood defences (designed to 1 in 100 year) which prevented internal flooding within the defended area, but defences did overtop at a location and caused external flooding of gardens. (p27)
* Initial flooding in the Willowbank area was from surface water that could not flow away because of high river levels, leading to inundation of the area and contributing to onward fluvial flooding. (p28)
* Floodwater entered private gardens on Willow Crescent West and Willow Avenue; embankment vulnerability adjacent to the Willowbank residential area was identified and recommendations were made to raise/strengthen embankments. (p29) [TAB]
* A breach of the river right bank upstream of the gauging weir occurred and surface water drainage/piped systems in the Willowbank area were affected, causing flooding risk to properties and businesses (including on Oxford Road). The event prompted surveys of the breached bank, investigations of the River Colne modelling and flood warning accuracy, and actions relating to the surface water system (ownership, CCTV inspection) to address flood impacts. (p30)
* Photographs taken in the Willowbank area document elevated river levels during the event, with water reaching the gauge board and coming close to the riverside footpath and nearby houses, indicating river flooding that threatened adjacent properties and vegetation. (p36)
* Reported overland flow route from the River Colne near the gauging station at the north of Willowbank Island contributed to flow paths across the island area and toward Oakside; area associated with sandbagging activity affecting nearby Oakside properties. (p43)
* Quant: five or more properties; two or more business premises (p6) [PAG]
* Quant: numbers 25-29; numbers 42-44; at least 4 properties (Willowbank and Oakside) (p20) [PAG]
* Quant: water level 32.61mAOD (p36)

(p1)

* {general}: Oxford Road is shown on the Environment Agency fluvial maps as lying within Flood Zone 3 adjacent to the parade of shops (1 in 100 year river flood risk) and further up near number 50 in Flood Zone 2 (1 in 1000 year). Surface-water mapping and the council’s map overlay also indicate surface-water flood risk overlapping main roads/urban areas, identifying Oxford Road as vulnerable to surface-water inundation. (p14) [IMG]
* {2002}: Recorded flood event at Oxford Road, Denham in 2002 (PFRA Ref No. SBD-109). (p15)
* Oxford Road was within the estimated fluvial flood extent in February 2014; floodwater reached the road as a result of over-topping of banks/low flood defence bunds shown on the map, causing flooding of the carriageway and exposure of adjacent properties to floodwater. (p19) [IMG]
* Floodwater travelled down through car parks to properties on Oxford Road, flooding the parade of shops and causing internal flooding to businesses (including the hair salon and carpet shop), and affecting the pub and filling station. (p20) [PAG]
* Floodwaters followed flow routes into the parade of shops on Oxford Road, causing internal flooding to businesses (including the hair salon and Willow Carpets) and to nearby properties. (p21) [IMG, PAG]
* Rear of Oxford Road properties experienced floodwater ingress; some downpipes and soil vent pipes were in poor repair and flood water may have come up through these pipes into properties. A request was made for 6 sandbags to fill a gap at the rear of 20A Oxford Road to prevent water coming from the River Colne. (p22) [TAB]
* The parade of shops along Oxford Road were flooded due to high river levels, channel restrictions and poor pipe overflow systems that caused water to back up into properties. A request was made for 6 sandbags to protect a gap at the rear of 20A Oxford Road. (p23) [PAG]
* Internal flooding of properties and businesses along Oxford Road. The parade of shops had two properties flooded internally. River/mill-channel restrictions and likely surcharged sewer/surface-water outlets and soil vent pipes caused backing up into properties. The area is in Flood Zone 3 and is unprotected by defences. (p27)
* Business properties in the parade of shops along Oxford Road were flooded. Flooding was caused by backing up of flow due to surcharged and underwater soil vent pipes and surface-water outlets into the restricted mill channel/river, combined with high river (fluvial) levels; the area is in Flood Zone 3 and is undefended. (p28) [PAG]
* Business properties in the parade of shops along Oxford Road were flooded due to backing up of flows and poor pipe/overflow systems causing water to back up into properties. (p29) [TAB, PAG]
* Floodwater flowed through a gap beside the hair salon at 20a Oxford Rd and into the parade of shops/pavement; the gap is documented as a route by which flood water entered the property area. (p47)

### New Denham

The River Colne was very high and overtopped its banks upstream of Willowbank; floodwater flowed across fields and into the area around Oakside and houses on Oxford Road. The river channel also overtopped alongside the shops on Oxford Road, with floodwater flowing into shops. (p4)
* The Willowbank residential area between two branches of the River Colne experienced internal flooding of properties; the Environment Agency surveyed the low bank upstream of Willowbank and contacted residents and the residents association to gather details and discuss options following the flood. (p5) [PAG]
* Area-wide flooding event affecting the New Denham locality (including Willowbank) with internal flooding of properties and business premises. (p6) [PAG]
* {2001, 2002, 2003, 2004, 2005, 2006}: The area experienced previous flooding in the early 2000s. Recorded local flood incidents (from PFRA references) include: 2006 - 5 Campion Close, Denham; 2005 - 15 Lower Road, Denham; 2004 - 4a Middle Road, Denham; 2003 - Willets Lane, Denham; 2003 - 32 Denham Lodge; 2003 - Denham Green Lane; 2002 - Oxford Road, Denham; 2001 - Station Parade, Denham. (p15) [MUL]
* Flow in the river/channel was backed up due to lack of flood defences and channel restrictions (overbuilds and access tracks), and poor pipe overflow systems caused water to back up into properties. (p24)
* Heavy rainfall and saturated ground led to surface water flooding in roads and gardens; high river levels and poorly maintained channels prevented discharge. A breach of the right river bank upstream of the weir sent water through fields into Oakside and on to Oxford Road, contributing to internal flooding of properties. (p27)
* Garden areas on Willow Crescent West and Willow Avenue were inundated. The parade of shops on Oxford Road experienced internal flooding as business properties were flooded; the report attributes this to backing-up and poor pipe overflow systems allowing water into properties. (p29) [TAB]
* Properties and residents were impacted by flooding, prompting distribution of guidance, offers to assist with Repair and Renewal Grant applications, requests to revisit River Colne modelling and to check flood warning accuracy, and investigations including a survey of a breached right bank upstream of the gauging weir. ### River Colne
* Branches and channels of the River Colne run through and around Willowbank (weirs, sluices and splits to the Grand Union canal); these channels were implicated in the flooding that caused internal flooding of properties and businesses in Willowbank. (p6)
* The River (Colne) reached elevated fluvial levels during the event; modelled flood level was approximately 1.278 m (between the 1 in 50-year and 1 in 100-year return periods). High river levels reduced the ability of local surface-water drains to discharge to the river, affecting drainage on Willowbank and adjacent areas; Environment Agency banks and the Willowbank weirs controlled and split flows around Willowbank island during the high flows. (p16)
* High flows in the River Colne flowed around King's Island and fed water into the Grand Union Canal, contributing to elevated canal levels and downstream flooding; a sluice next to King's Island main gate was opened (to manage flows). (p18)
* River Colne overflowed / conveyed floodwater into adjacent open land and along mapped flow routes, the water then flowed through car parks and along streets into properties on Oxford Road, the nearby pub and filling station. (p21) [IMG]
* Channel restrictions and lack of flood defences caused the river to back up through New Denham (around Willowbank), contributing to fluvial flooding and resulting in water backing up into properties via poor pipe overflow systems. (p24)
* River Colne was shown adjacent to a flood defence where flooding/interaction with the defence occurred; an overtopping location exposed the flood kerb and contributed to inundation of adjacent land. (p40) [IMG]
* Quant: five or more properties; two or more business premises (p6)
* Quant: 1.278 m; between 1 in 50-year and 1 in 100-year return period (p16)
