* Intense, short-duration rainfall on 19 July 2017 caused flash/surface-water flooding across Tunbridge Wells, producing urban flooding in shopping and residential areas (figures show locations of flooding in Royal Victoria Place and High Brooms). (p10) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface water from surrounding hills and roads converged in the central area (The Pantiles / Nevill Street / Warwick Park low point ~80.3mAOD). Converging flow routes and limited ability for water to flow away, combined with a small local pump serving basements, increased loads on drainage and the pump and led to surface water and sewer-related flooding in the central area, including flooding of basements and water on highways during the 19 July 2017 event. (p18) [IMG]
* Excessive rainfall exceeded drainage capacity across the area, overwhelming systems and causing surface water to accumulate in low points and combined sewers to surcharge into properties (including areas such as Mount Pleasant Road, Castle Street and Warwick Park). (p19)
* On 19th July 2017 excessive/intensive rainfall exceeded the capacity of the town's drainage and combined sewer systems. Surface water accumulated in topographic low points and entered properties; parts of the town (including London Road, Castle Street and the Royal Victoria Place area) experienced overwhelmed gullies/combined sewers and blocked drains. Kent County Council attended (water had cleared from some highways by 03:54) and drains were cleansed and audited following the event. (p20)
* Following the 19 July 2017 flood event, KCC undertook drainage cleansing on roads in Tunbridge Wells that were affected by flooding; a resident flood action group was formed (facilitated by the National Flood Forum) to address local flood issues; natural flood management measures were delivered in local green spaces (Hilbert Woods and Grosvenor Park) to reduce peak flows. (p26)
* Intense rainfall exceeded drainage design standards so surface water could not enter drainage networks quickly enough; drainage capacity was exceeded and floodwaters flowed to local low points causing flooding across the town and to properties. (p28) [PAG]
* Surface water flooding formed on Nevill Street due to rapid overland flow from the Common; overland flow discharged onto surrounding roads and posed risk to downstream properties. (p29) [PAG]

### River Grom
* {general}: The open (above-ground) section upstream of the culvert has experienced flooding historically. The culverted section beneath central Tunbridge Wells has no recorded flooding, but a consented Combined Sewer Overflow (CSO) from the public combined sewer in the Pantiles area discharges into the culvert when the sewer capacity is exceeded, creating a mechanism for high flows to enter the culvert. (p13) [IMG, PAG]
* The River Grom overflowed at the culvert entrance near Rodmell Road during the heavy rainfall, inundating gardens on the north‑eastern side of Warwick Park and causing internal flooding to a property; culvert entrance arrangements may have impeded flow and contributed to the flooding. (p17)
* {general}: The River Grom is culverted beneath Warwick Park and to the south of The Pantiles; its culverted alignment is part of the local drainage configuration through which surface-water flow routes run, influencing concentrations of surface-water flood risk in the area. (p18)
* The culverted River Grom was likely overwhelmed by excessive rainfall, contributing to local flooding; the culvert entrance arrangement (largely closed with a small opening and lower entrance chamber) is likely to impede inflow and may lead to flooding of gardens at Warwick Park. A CCTV inspection found only minor deposits and a root mass but no major defects. (p19) [PAG]
* {2015}: The culverted sections and trash screen at the entrance to the River Grom culvert limited conveyance and, if blocked, contributed to water backing up and flooding. A large obstruction of woody debris was estimated to cause a 40PCSYM loss of culvert cross-sectional area, reducing capacity and impeding flow; the limited floodplain where the culvert runs meant excess flow flooded open sections or entered properties near the watercourse. KCC replaced the trash screen to improve flow and allow safer, more effective maintenance. (p25)
* KCC replaced the previous vertical trash screens at the River Grom entrance with a single raked trash screen to allow safer and more effective clearing of debris during higher flows; the River Grom culvert was subject to CCTV inspection to assess for damage, defects and blockages after the 2017 flood. (p26) [PAG]
* The River Grom reached peak flows and overflowed at the entrance to the culvert system, contributing to flooding adjacent to properties as surface water drainage discharged into the watercourse. (p28) [PAG]
* Quant: one property (p17)
