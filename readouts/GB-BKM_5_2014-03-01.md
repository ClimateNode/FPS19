

# Chalfont St Giles flooding March–April 2014

## Place names with coordinates

### Chalfont St Giles
* Exceptionally high groundwater levels in the chalk aquifer caused groundwater flooding in the village, which affected local critical infrastructure including the Affinity Water pumping station, the British Telecom telephone exchange, and the main access road to the village shops. (p4)
* {March-April 2014}: Internal flooding of commercial properties and flooding of critical infrastructure (telephone exchange). (p5)
* The locality affected by the flood incident was the High Street — the village centre experienced flooding during the event. (p7) [PAG]
* Surface-water and fluvial flooding affected the valley bottom and centre of the village. Flood extents from the central watercourse overlap residential and commercial property footprints and threaten the main road through the village (Pheasant Hill / High Street); the valley topography funnels groundwater, surface water and fluvial flow into the village centre increasing flood depths and extent. (p12) [PAG, IMG]
* {23 Nov 2006, 27 Nov 2006, 07 Dec 2006, 10 Jan 2007, 18 Jan 2007, 24 Jan 2007, 15 Feb 2007, 12 Aug 2010}: Multiple surface-water flooding incidents were recorded at residential/road locations across the village. Specific impacts included flood incidents at Bottom House Farm Lane, Bottrells Lane, Gorelands Lane, Narcot Lane, Nightingales Lane, The Lagger and Townfield Lane. Drainage system faults and defects were recorded: possible collapse in pipe at two locations adjacent to gullies, a damaged kerb weir, a borehole not working, blocked/silted gullies (four gullies reported), and reported blockages/root damage requiring camera investigation and excavation. (p13) [PAG, IMG, TAB, MUL]
* The local river (Misbourne) was at bank full and in spate during the event, causing minor flooding of bankside gardens and farmland in the Chalfont St Giles locality. No reports indicated failings or blockages in highway or surface water drainage that would have worsened the flooding. (p17) [PAG]
* Exceptionally high groundwater levels in the chalk aquifer flooded parts of the village: a public water supply pumping station and the BT telephone exchange were flooded; over-pumping from the BT exchange caused flooding of the high street and a carpark, the main street was reduced to one direction of traffic, and there was an economic impact on small businesses. (p18) [PAG]
* Exceptionally high groundwater levels in the chalk aquifer caused groundwater flooding across the village that affected local critical infrastructure: the Affinity Water pumping station and the BT telephone exchange were impacted, and the main access road to the shops in the village centre was flooded, restricting access. (p23)
* Groundwater flooding in spring 2014 impacted the village: local critical infrastructure was affected (Affinity Water pumping station and the BT telephone exchange) and the main access road to the village shops was flooded. (p24) [TAB, PAG]
* {March-April 2014}: Quant: 4 commercial premises; 1 BT telephone exchange (p5)
* {23 Nov 2006, 27 Nov 2006, 07 Dec 2006, 10 Jan 2007, 18 Jan 2007, 24 Jan 2007, 15 Feb 2007, 12 Aug 2010}: Quant: 1 incident (Bottom House Farm Lane); 2 incidents (Bottrells Lane); 1 incident (Gorelands Lane); 1 incident (Narcot Lane); 2 incidents (Nightingales Lane); 1 incident (The Lagger); 1 incident (Townfield Lane); 4 gullies silted/blocked; 2 pipe collapse locations (p13) [PAG, IMG, TAB, MUL]

### Little Missenden
* {07 Feb 2014, 08 Feb 2014, March–April 2014}: The EA telemetered river-level gauge at Little Missenden recorded exceptionally high river levels during the three-month event; the period is recorded as number 1 of the top 10 levels for the site, with a peak on 08 March 2014 contributing to flooding in the catchment. (p15) [PAG, MUL]
* {07 Feb 2014, 08 Feb 2014, March–April 2014}: The Little Missenden gauge recorded exceptionally high river stages during Dec 2013–Apr 2014 associated with fluvial flooding of the River Misbourne influenced by exceptionally high groundwater. The period was treated as one event influenced by groundwater, with multiple high peaks providing an early indication of high flows upstream. (p16) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* {December 2013 - MArch 2014, 07 Feb 2014, 08 Feb 2014, March–April 2014}: The Little Missenden gauge recorded one of the highest events on record for the site during the three-month event culminating in early March 2014, providing an early warning of high upstream flows relative to Chalfont St Giles. The hydrograph shows pronounced high river heads in February–March 2014 with the main peak on 08 March 2014 and additional peaks in early February 2014. (p17) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* {December 2013 - MArch 2014, 07 Feb 2014, 08 Feb 2014, March–April 2014}: Quant: peak level 459 mm; maximum 0.459 mSG; range of recorded levels 443 mm; event ranked number 1 of top 10 levels at this site (p17) [PAG, IMG, MUL]

### Affinity Water
* {11 Feb 2014}: An Affinity Water public drinking-water pumping station upstream of Chalfont St Giles ceased pumping due to groundwater flooding (pumping station flooded). (p18)
* The Chalfont St Giles pumping station owned/operated by Affinity Water was impacted by exceptionally high groundwater levels (groundwater flooding). The event prompted recommendations to improve the pumping station's resilience and to write procedures for pumping excess groundwater during flood events. (p24) [TAB, PAG]

### Wellmans
* {general}: A drainage system opposite Wellmans was cleaned and flowing but a collapse in the last gully on a separate line was recorded; the collapsed gully required excavation and represented a local drainage failure risk. (p13) [PAG, IMG, TAB]

### British Telecommunications
* British Telecommunications had its telephone exchange flooded by high groundwater and undertook pumping of groundwater from the basement as part of the incident response; their pumping activity contributed to flooding impacts on the high street and carpark. (p18) [PAG]

### Chiltern District Council
* {11 Feb 2014, March–April 2014}: Coordinated the incident response in partnership with stakeholders, arranged meetings to facilitate partnership working and manage the pumping operations to remove groundwater from the BT telephone exchange. Responded to flooding of Affinity Water public supply pumping station (Affinity Water ceased pumping as a result of groundwater flooding). (p18) [MUL]

### Transport for Buckinghamshire
* Provided road layout alterations, a ramp and pipes across the road to allow pumping/discharge of groundwater from the BT telephone exchange and to manage water on the road. (p19)

### Chalfont St Giles Pumping Station
* The Affinity Water pumping station was impacted by exceptionally high groundwater during the March–April 2014 event; the facility was flooded/affected as part of the local critical infrastructure impacted by groundwater flooding. (p24) [TAB]
