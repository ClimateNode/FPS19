

# Chalfont St Giles flooding March–April 2014

## Place names with coordinates

### Chalfont St Giles
* Exceptionally high groundwater levels in the chalk aquifer caused groundwater flooding in the village, which affected local critical infrastructure including the Affinity Water pumping station, the British Telecom telephone exchange, and the main access road to the village shops. (p4)
* {March-April 2014}: Internal flooding of commercial properties and flooding of critical infrastructure (telephone exchange). (p5)
* Surface water and fluvial flooding affected the village centre: commercial and residential properties along the central watercourse and the road corridor (Pheasant Hill / High Street) were shown to be at risk. The valley funnels groundwater, surface water and river flow into the valley bottom, concentrating floodwater into the centre of the village. (p12) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: The valley at Chalfont St Giles funnels water from the chalk aquifer, surface water and fluvial flows into the valley bottom and village centre, placing commercial and residential properties and the road (Pheasant Hill/High Street) at risk. Historical records show repeated surface-water flooding incidents at multiple roads and local drainage defects (blocked gullies, borehole failure, possible pipe collapses) that contributed to flooding and impaired drainage. (p13) [IMG, TAB, PAG]
* Area experienced high river levels in the period; river (Misbourne) was at bank full and in spate with some minor flooding of bankside gardens and farmland in the locality. No reported failures or damage to highway or surface water drainage features within the Chalfont St Giles locality were reported as contributing to the flooding. (p17) [PAG, MUL]
* High groundwater levels flooded local infrastructure (Affinity Water pumping station and the BT telephone exchange). Over‑pumping from the BT exchange caused flooding of the high street, forced closure of the car park and required the main street to be reduced to one direction of traffic, producing an economic impact on small businesses in the village. (p18) [PAG]
* Exceptionally high groundwater levels in the chalk aquifer caused groundwater flooding across the village that affected local critical infrastructure: the Affinity Water pumping station and the BT telephone exchange were impacted, and the main access road to the shops in the village centre was flooded, restricting access. (p23)
* Spring 2014 groundwater flooding affected the village: local critical infrastructure was impacted (Affinity Water pumping station and the BT telephone exchange) and the main access road to the shops in the centre of the village was affected. (p24) [TAB, PAG]

* {general}: Quant: 1 incident at Bottom House Farm Lane (15/02/2007); 2 incidents at Bottrells Lane (24/01/2007); 1 incident at Gorelands Lane (18/01/2007); 1 incident at Narcot Lane (27/11/2006); 2 incidents at Nightingales Lane (10/01/2007); 1 incident at The Lagger (07/12/2006); 1 incident at Townfield Lane (23/11/2006); 4 gullies silted and blocked (Roughwood Lane). ### Little Missenden
* {Dec 2013–Apr 2014, 07 Feb 2014 - 08 Feb 2014, March–April 2014}: The telemetered river level gauge at Little Missenden recorded exceptionally high river levels during the Dec 2013–Apr 2014 period; the event is listed as number 1 in the gauge's top 10 levels and the peak on 08 March 2014 indicates severe fluvial flooding in the River Misbourne at this location. (p15) [PAG, MUL]
* {07 Feb 2014, 08 Feb 2014, March–April 2014}: The Little Missenden gauge recorded exceptionally high river stages during Dec 2013–Apr 2014 associated with fluvial flooding of the River Misbourne influenced by exceptionally high groundwater. The period was treated as one event influenced by groundwater, with multiple high peaks providing an early indication of high flows upstream. (p16) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* {March - April 2014}: The Little Missenden gauge recorded exceptionally high river levels during the three-month event leading to March 2014, indicating the River Misbourne was at bank full and in spate. The event peaked on 08 March 2014 (459 mm) with other high peaks on 07–08 February 2014. Minor flooding affected bankside gardens and farmland nearby. The gauge provided early warning of high flows upstream of Chalfont St Giles. (p17) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* {March - April 2014}: Quant: 459 mm (peak level on 08 March 2014); 443 mm (range between lowest and highest recorded levels in the period); ranked number 1 of the top 10 levels recorded at the gauge (p17) [IMG, PAG, MUL]

### Chalfont St Peter
* {March - April 2014}: Experienced fluvial flooding from the River Misbourne and groundwater flooding driven by prolonged above‑average winter rainfall and chalk aquifer recharge; river flows were elevated and contributed to flooding in the area, with upstream gauge records showing the event as one of the highest on record. (p15)
