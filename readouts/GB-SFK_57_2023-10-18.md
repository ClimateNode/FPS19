## Place names with coordinates

### Debenham
* Debenham was one of the most severely impacted locations. Intense rainfall overwhelmed five natural flow routes and the capacity of existing drainage; these flows converged with the River Deben (which was also in flood), causing internal flooding of properties, and disruption to infrastructure and services. The village was divided into nine impacted zones for reporting. (p4)
* Internal flooding to properties (residential and commercial) within the village; report focuses on Debenham and describes probable sources, observed flow paths through the community and receptors affected. (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* Debenham experienced rapid rise in flood waters during Storm Babet as fluvial (main river) and pluvial (surface water/overland) flows combined. Properties were internally flooded and some were flooded before the Aspall gauge reached its trigger level or a warning was issued. (p8) [PAG]
* The village experienced significant internal flooding of residential and commercial properties. Flood water approached from fluvial sources (tributaries and the River Deben), pluvial surface-water runoff, and combinations of both. Properties were flooded before the Aspall Watercourse gauge reached its trigger level and before a Flood Warning was issued. (p9) [IMG, PAG]
* {1936, 1937, 1944, 1947, 1956, 1968, 1993}: Historic significant flooding in the village driven by high levels on the River Deben and Cherry Tree Brook; notable flood depths were recorded in central parts of Debenham (see listed streets/places). (p11) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* {1936, 1937, 1944, 1947, 1956, 1968, 1993}: Historic significant flood incidents recorded (1936, 1937, 1944, 1947, 1956, 1968 and 1993). Historic flooding was driven by high levels on the River Deben and Cherry Tree Brook, with significant flood depths recorded in Market Square, Water Lane, Priory Lane, south of the High Street near the fire station and at Cross Green. (p12) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* {1936, 1937, 1944, 1947, 1956, 1968, 1993}: Multiple historically significant flood incidents recorded in the village; high flood depths reported in Market Square, Water Lane, Priory Lane, south of the High Street near the fire station and at Cross Green. (p13) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Pluvial (surface water) flood risk concentrated in the low-lying village where five major flow paths converge on the River Deben. Seven streets (Chancery Lane, Aspall Road, Water Lane, Priory Lane, High Street, Kenton Road and Low Road) are shown as being at risk of flooding. The village can act as temporary attenuation during intense rainfall, retaining flood water until discharge to the south; during significant storms infrastructure and watercourses have been observed to be overwhelmed. Local geology (Lowestoft Formation diamicton) produced low infiltration during Storm Babet, increasing runoff into the village. (p14) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface-water / overland flow flooding driven by low-permeability soils and multiple flow paths. The report links these local soil conditions and antecedent rainfall to runoff during Storm Babet, which increased flood risk in Debenham. (p15) [IMG, PAG]
* {01 May 2023 - 31 May 2023, 18 Oct 2023 - 21 Oct 2023}: Extreme rainfall (Storm Babet) combined with low-lying ground, multiple flow paths and low-permeability surrounding soils caused watercourses and a culvert to be overwhelmed. Floodwater from interconnecting watercourses and an overwhelmed/partially blocked culvert flowed onto the highway (Bellwell Lane) and into the village, inundating properties and roads; silt and a headwall forcing flow onto the road were identified as factors that impeded conveyance downstream. (p16) [PAG, MUL]
* Flood waters from the local watercourse and surface runoff converged in the village; a small number of properties on Bellwell Lane were reported flooded. Drainage assets were overwhelmed and a culvert under Bellwell Lane may have been blocked or impeded, contributing to flooding. (p17) [PAG]

* {18 Oct 2023 - 21 Oct 2023, 01 Nov 2023 - 02 Nov 2023}: Overtopping of the Derry Brook and overwhelmed watercourses caused flood water to spill onto the highway which then acted as a secondary flow path into the central village catchment. Concerns were raised about surface-water management at recent housing developments. (p21) [MUL]
