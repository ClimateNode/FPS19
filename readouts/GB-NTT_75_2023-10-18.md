

# Sutton-On-Trent flooding 18 Oct 2023 - 20 Oct 2023

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Sutton On Trent – Storm Babet, October 2023<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/sutton-on-trent-s19-storm-babet-oct-2023.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Sutton-On-Trent
* Roads and properties in the village were flooded as a result of intense rainfall following a period of saturated ground; flooding of roads and properties occurred more quickly than would after a dry period. (p1)
* Internal flooding affected residential properties (water entered homes) and additional flooding affected gardens and outbuildings; roads and properties flooded faster because the ground was already saturated. (p2) [PAG]
* Internal flooding in two distinct parts of Sutton on Trent during Storm Babet: northern area (Grassthorpe) flooded by surface runoff/overland flow from a saturated catchment; Mill Close area flooded when the adjacent watercourse (Cocker Beck) overtopped and flowed out of bank. Residents also reported groundwater coming up through property floors. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface water runoff from intense antecedent rainfall flowed into local watercourses which became overwhelmed; Cocker Beck (and local drains) over-topped their banks and spilled onto land, producing overland flows that caused flooding across low-lying parts of the village. The pattern of inundation correlates with Flood Zone 2/3 shown on the Environment Agency maps and is described as occurring during Storm Babet. (p4) [IMG]
* The village (Sutton on Trent) experienced flooding that correlates with the map's predicted surface-water flood extents. Highest surface-water flood risk is indicated to the west and north of the village; medium-to-high risk zones extend into parts of the village and some roads/built-up areas, indicating vulnerability during the event. (p5) [IMG]
* Quant: 17 residential properties (p2) [PAG]

### Nottinghamshire
* County-wide flood response and disruption: 77 roads were closed to promote road safety and prevent additional flood damage; sandbags were delivered to affected areas and communities; community flood signage schemes were supported; communications channels were used to update the public, partners and elected members during the event. (p7) [PAG]
* Quant: 77 roads (p7) [PAG]

### Newark and Sherwood
* Extensive local impacts and response: staff visited impacted communities and vulnerable individuals; evacuations supported and people helped to evacuate homes; emergency accommodation provided; many aqua sacs were delivered; council coordinated clean-up of roads and removal of bulky waste from flooded homes; council conducted in-person visits to high-risk communities due to predicted extreme River Trent levels and later delivered grant funding to eligible impacted residents. (p7)

### Palmer Road, Sutton-On-Trent
* Map (Figure 5) shows parts of Palmer Road lie within medium-to-high surface water flood risk zones, indicating exposure to surface water flooding during the main event (Storm Babet). (p5) [IMG]

### Great North Road, Sutton-On-Trent
* Figure 5 indicates portions of Great North Road fall within medium-to-high surface water flood risk zones, so the road is potentially affected by surface water flooding associated with the event (Storm Babet). (p5) [IMG]

### River Trent
* Roads and properties in the village were flooded due to heavy rainfall on already saturated ground during the Storm Babet event. (p1)
* Flooding and high flood risk concentrated adjacent to the River Trent; Figures show Flood Zone 3 (highest risk) along the eastern side of Sutton on Trent and Carlton on Trent next to the river, indicating the river contributed to inundation of those areas during the event mapped. (p4) [IMG]
* Predicted extreme river levels from the River Trent increased river flooding risk, prompting council in-person visits to high-risk communities and influencing flood response actions. (p7)

### Holme Farm
* Surface water runoff from a saturated catchment flowed into local watercourses that converged near Holme Farm; the watercourse was overwhelmed and over-topped its banks, causing overland flows and localised flooding in the area around Holme Farm. (p4) [IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Responded to a Major Incident with significant county impacts: verified internal flooding at sites, offered support/guidance to affected residents and businesses, visited flooded properties to establish sources/mechanisms and severity, published verified flood impact data on Resilience Direct, initiated a Community Flood Recovery Grant for individuals affected by internal flooding, provided 24-hour Emergency Planning support, chaired Strategic/Tactical/Recovery coordination groups, and initiated and led the Section 19 Flood Investigation. (p6)

### Marshalls Coaches
* Overland surface water flows and an over-topping watercourse converged in the area adjacent to Marshalls Coaches, causing localised flooding where the watercourses meet. (p4) [IMG]

### Darlton Dyke
* Surface water from a saturated catchment flowed into the local watercourses that drain to Darlton Dyke. The volume of runoff overwhelmed the watercourses, spilling out of bank and producing overland flows that contributed to flooding in the Sutton on Trent area; Darlton Dyke forms part of the drainage route to the River Trent and therefore contributed to the flood routing in the catchment. (p4) [IMG]
