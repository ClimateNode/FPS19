### Worcester Park
* Surface water flooding on 25 July 2021 caused internal and external flooding across the Worcester Park area. Reported impacts include internal property flooding and external (street/adjacent) flooding; main streets/areas reported affected include Avon Close, Edenfield Gardens, The Avenue, Stoneleigh Park Road, Woodlands Avenue, Dewsbury Gardens, Ardrossan Gardens and Kinross Avenue. (p3) [PAG]
* Surface water flooding on 25 July 2021 caused internal and external flooding across the residential area. The main streets reported affected include Avon Close, Edenfield Gardens, The Avenue, Stoneleigh Park Road, Woodlands Avenue, Dewsbury Gardens, Ardrossan Gardens and Kinross Avenue. (p4) [IMG, TAB]
* Lower-elevation parts of Worcester Park experienced property flooding; historical record of fluvial flooding impacted Avon Close; area reported to have flooded in July 2007. (Impacts in this settlement were the subject of the investigation into the 25th July 2021 event.) (p5) [PAG, MUL]
* Area experienced repeated sewer-flooding incidents (recorded in London Borough of Sutton SWMP) and was affected by rapid river-level rise in the Beverley Brook on 25 July 2021 that submerged drainage outlets, caused hydraulic locking of surface water outlets and contributed to local flooding. The area also has a historical recorded flood in July 2007. (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* {25 Jul 2021, 28 Jul 2021}: Surface water drainage from the Worcester Park area conveyed rapidly into Beverley Brook, contributing to rapid river level rises; properties in Avon Close (within Worcester Park) experienced surface water flooding front, rear and internal flooding with water entering via surcharged gullies, manholes and wastepipes. (p7) [MUL]
* Surface water drainage from Worcester Park was carried into the local Thames Water surface water system (including the 750mm main pipe) that discharges to the Beverley Brook; the local culverted tributary/drainage network was implicated in the event. (p10)
* On 25 July 2021 intense rainfall produced one of the highest recorded Beverley Brook water levels at Worcester Park; the brook rose above the Thames Water drainage outlet, preventing discharge, causing the surface water pipe network to back up and surcharge at gullies and manholes, which prevented overland runoff from draining and produced local surface water flooding. (p11)
* Surface water from multiple sub-catchments discharged via surface water pipes into the Beverley Brook, causing rapid rises and increasing risk of outlets becoming blocked; contributed to flooding in the Worcester Park area. (p12) [PAG]
* Water level at the Worcester Park gauge rose above the defined 'Flooding Possible' threshold, indicating flood conditions at the gauge location; levels spiked on 25/07 and then fell back into the typical range. (p19) [IMG]

### Beverley Brook
* {general}: Multiple historical records of flooding are associated with the Beverley Brook; one historical record is noted to have impacted Avon Close in the Worcester Park area. The brook is the receiving watercourse for local ditches/watercourses from the Worcester Park sub‑catchment and has been linked to local flooding. (p5) [PAG]
* Beverley Brook is the watercourse responsible for recorded flooding in the area. Telemetry shows a very sharp rise during the 25 July 2021 event that brought river levels to 2.76 mASD (within 100 mm of the July 2007 peak of 2.85 mASD), rapidly submerging drainage outlets and contributing to hydraulic locking and flooding of adjacent areas including Worcester Park. (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* {25 Jul 2021, 28 Jul 2021}: Rapid, sharp rise in river level on 25th July 2021 submerged drainage outlets and caused hydraulic lock; contributed to surcharging of gullies and manholes and linked foul sewer flooding (including reported high levels again on 28th July 2021). (p7) [MUL]
* The Thames Water surface water system (including a 750mm main pipe running from Avon Close) discharges into the Beverley Brook; culverted former tributaries now feed the brook via the drainage network and are therefore implicated in the local flood dynamics. (p10) [PAG]
* On 25 July 2021 water levels in the Beverley Brook rose to one of the highest recorded levels; the elevated brook level exceeded the height of the Thames Water drainage outlet, preventing discharge from the surface water network and causing the piped system to back up and surcharge at gullies and manholes. (p11)
* Received surface water discharges from multiple sub-catchments and surface water pipes, causing rapid responses and a risk of outlet blockage that increased downstream flood risk. (p12) [PAG]
* {general}: The map and accompanying analysis identify Beverley Brook as the main river corridor where the highest fluvial flood risk (EA Flood Zone 3) is concentrated, indicating the brook is the primary watercourse that can cause fluvial flooding affecting adjacent properties and infrastructure within the catchment. (p17) [IMG]
* {general}: Quant: one recorded instance impacting Avon Close (p5) [PAG]
* Quant: River level 2.76 mASD on 25 July 2021; 2.85 mASD recorded in July 2007 (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* {25 Jul 2021, 28 Jul 2021}: Quant: River level 2.76mASD on 25th July 2021; peak recorded 2.85mASD (July 2007) (p7) [MUL]

### Avon Close, Cuddington

Water subsided by the following morning. Additional foul sewer flooding was reported on 28 July 2021, likely linked to high Beverley Brook levels. (p7) [PAG, MUL]

(p6) [PAG]

### London Borough of Kingston Upon Thames
* High water levels in the Beverley Brook rose above the Thames Water surface water outlet, causing an exceedance that prevented the outlet from discharging. This contributed to surface water backing up in the drainage network and surcharging at gullies and manholes, exacerbating flooding in the Worcester Park area. (p11)
* Sub-catchments within the borough discharge surface water via pipes into the Beverley Brook; during the event these contributed runoff to the brook, increasing the brook's rapid response and downstream flood risk to Worcester Park. (p12) [PAG]

### Thames Water
* Thames Water's surface water drainage network that discharges to Beverley Brook had outlets submerged during the 25 July 2021 event, causing hydraulic lock and contributing to inability to discharge surface water and local flooding in Worcester Park and surrounding areas. (p6)
* Thames Water’s surface water drainage network was surveyed and camera-inspected: the 750mm pipe was serviceable (some increased silt deposition noted at the outlet), a silt obstruction in the 225mm pipe was jetted and confirmed serviceable, and the 350mm pipe was confirmed serviceable. Thames Water confirmed connectivity between Avon Close and other nearby streets' drainage into the Beverley Brook. (p10) [PAG]
* Thames Water’s surface water drainage and culverted former tributaries in the area were operational but were overwhelmed on 25 July 2021; elevated Beverley Brook levels prevented discharge at the drainage outlet, causing the Thames Water pipe network to back up, surcharge at manholes/gullies and contribute to surface water flooding. (p11)
* Thames Water's surface water drainage system discharged simultaneously into the Beverley Brook during heavy rainfall, producing rapid surges; overloading and misconnections in the foul and surface systems were identified as contributing to surcharge and flood risk. (p12) [PAG]
* Quant: 4 large surface water pipes discharging to Beverley Brook (p6)
* Quant: 225mm pipe obstruction removed; 750mm, 350mm and 225mm pipes identified in the surveyed network (p10) [PAG]
* Quant: four outlets (p12) [PAG]
