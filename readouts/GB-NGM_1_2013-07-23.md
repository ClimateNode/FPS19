### Bulwell
* Bulwell (the locality containing Bestwood Road) was the area impacted by the 23 July 2013 flood event; the report map and descriptions focus on water features and residential areas in this locality. (p1)
* Part of the north of the city affected by the intense storm; Bestwood Road in Bulwell experienced internal flooding to residential properties. (p3)
* Areas of green open space in the wider Bulwell area were saturated in the 24 hours before and during the intense rainfall, contributing to rapid overland flow and surface water ponding that fed runoff toward Bestwood Road. (p10)
* Road gullies and highway drains in the Bulwell area were likely overwhelmed by high volumes and flow of surface water (flowing down Bestwood Road), contributing to ponding and inability of surface water to drain into the public sewer network. (p14)
* Areas adjacent to the river corridor in Bulwell lie within mapped flood zones; the river corridor floodplain runs through residential areas and alongside River Park, placing those parts of Bulwell at risk and recorded as flood-affected in the report for the 23 July 2013 event. (p23) [IMG]
* Quant: 6 properties (p3)

### Nottingham Unitary Authority
* Nottingham (administrative area containing Bulwell and Bestwood Road) included the location of the 23 July 2013 flooding; the report maps the affected local streets within the city boundary. (p1)
* An intense thunderstorm on the evening of 23 July 2013 caused flooding across the city, resulting in residential properties being flooded. (p3)
* {2011, 23 Jul 2013}: Residential properties within Nottingham (specifically on Bestwood Road) experienced external flooding during the rainfall event on 23rd July 2013; a resident reported external flooding in 2011 at the same location. (p7) [PAG, MUL]
* A series of heavy rainfall events saturated the ground and overwhelmed available storage in underground drainage networks across the city, resulting in internal flooding of properties (notably 6 properties on Bestwood Road). (p18) [PAG]
* Quant: over 100 residential properties (p3)
* Quant: 6 properties (p18) [PAG]

### Bestwood
* {2011, 23 Jul 2013}: External flooding of residential properties on Bestwood Road; external areas of properties (gardens/grounds) were inundated during the flood event. (p7) [PAG, MUL]
* Intense rainfall generated large volumes of surface-water runoff that overwhelmed drainage, causing water to pond in low-lying areas. External flooding affected properties and the road; property damage reported (external damage to stairs, floorboards, doors, walls and furniture). (p14)

### Rise Park
* Local areas of Rise Park were saturated prior to and during the storm, producing rapid overland flow and ponding; surface water from surrounding higher ground flowed down towards Bestwood Road, with roadways conveying runoff. (p10)

### Bulwell Forest
* {general}: Green open space and adjacent golf course to the north were described as saturated from previous storms, producing excess surface water runoff volume that contributed runoff but was unlikely to have reached Bestwood Road due to an artificial bund (old railway track). (p12)

### Hucknall Road, Bulwell
* Surface water flowed down Hucknall Road during the intense rainfall and was likely channelled into Bestwood Road, increasing flood volumes at the low point; highway gullies were unable to drain, exacerbating surface water accumulation. (p10)
* Surface water was conveyed along the main trunk of Hucknall Road during the storm and was channelled into Bestwood Road at the junction, acting as a flow route that contributed to the flooding on Bestwood Road. (p11)

### River Leen
* {general}: The Flood Map for Planning shows a small part of Bestwood Road lies within Flood Zone 2, indicating risk of flooding from the River Leen (annual probability between 1 in 100 and 1 in 1000). The report notes the nearby railway embankment would likely reduce the risk of flooding from the River Leen to Bestwood Road. (p8)
* River water levels were raised during and after the storm, which likely tide-locked surface water outfalls and reduced the ability of surface water to drain to the river, contributing to surface water flooding in the area. (p11)
* The River Leen's interaction with public surface water sewer outfalls was identified as potentially influencing sewer capacity and contributing to flooding; an analysis of the outfalls into the River Leen is planned. (p15)

### Top Valley
* Road network in the Top Valley area falls towards Hucknall Road and acted as conduits for surface water during the storm on 23 July 2013, contributing to flows that were directed into Bestwood Road low points. (p10)


## Topics without coordinates

### Severn Trent
* The intense storm on 23rd July 2013 produced rainfall exceeding sewer design standards and likely overwhelmed the public sewer network, contributing to flooding of properties on Bestwood Road. Severn Trent Water classed the event as 'extreme'. (p9)
* The public sewer network was described as being at capacity during the storm, preventing surface water from draining away into the sewer system and thereby contributing to the flooding observed. (p14) [PAG]
* Severn Trent Water's public sewer network was exceeded by the extreme rainfall; the event was classified as 'extreme' and the sewer network near Bestwood Road was identified for further investigation (CCTV and outfall analysis) rather than immediate capacity upgrades. (p15)
* Recorded the 23rd July 2013 flooding on their records; committed to investigative analysis of the public sewer network at Bestwood Road to identify factors affecting free flow into the River Leen, and to inspect and remediate (e.g. remove blockages) to maintain sewer serviceability following flooding. (p16) [TAB]

### Nottingham City Council
* Highway drainage assets managed by the City Council (road gullies and highway drains) were likely overwhelmed by the storm; because the highway drainage connects into the public sewer network, capacity issues in the sewer contributed to surface-water ponding and flooding in the area. (p14) [PAG]
* Nottingham City Council carried out increased maintenance of road gullies on Bestwood Road, placed those gullies on a Targeted Gully Cleansing Regime for more frequent cleaning, and will make Repair and Renew grant applications available to residents affected by internal flooding. (p15) [PAG]
* Maintained road gullies and placed gullies on a Targeted Gully Cleansing Regime for Bestwood Road to help transfer surface water to the public sewer network; made Repair and Renew grant application forms available to properties that suffered internal flooding between 1 April 2013 and 31 March 2014. (p16) [TAB]
