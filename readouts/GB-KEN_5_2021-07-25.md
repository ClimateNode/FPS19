### Bethersden
* Intense rainfall on 25 July 2021 caused local river levels (Bethersden Stream and an unnamed tributary of the River Beult) to rise quickly, surcharging highway surface water drainage and culverts (under Church Hill and Norton Lane). Sewer network was reported unable to cope in isolated areas. Approximately 33 properties were flooded (22 internally, 11 externally). (p1)
* Flooding affected properties within the village: at least one property was flooded internally (reported ~90 cm of floodwater) and other properties experienced external flooding (two properties on The Street). (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Lower-lying areas of the village associated with the two tributaries experienced surface-water and tributary/river flooding; the high-risk areas on surface-water mapping largely correspond to the properties affected in the 25 July 2021 event. (p4)
* Intense, localized rainfall on 25 July 2021 led to flooding in Bethersden, with flooding reported to start at approximately 15:00 as a result of the extreme hourly rainfall. The local Environment Agency rain gauge is located in Bethersden and recorded the extreme event used to characterise the flood. (p5)
* {general}: The village has experienced multiple historic flood events that caused property flooding, external property flooding, sewer flooding, blocked drains/gullies, carriageway flooding and a surcharged culvert at various streets within the village. (p7)
* Intense rainfall on 25 July 2021 overwhelmed drainage and watercourses, causing main‑river overtopping and surface water runoff that flooded properties in the village. (p8)
* Overland flows from land to the south overwhelmed the rear land drainage ditch south of Bailey Fields, which overtopped and led to flooding of properties along Ashford Road. Debris was reported near drainage pipes south of Bailey Fields. Kent Fire and Rescue Service attended on 25 July 2021 to pump out floodwater from multiple properties. Subsequent KCC Highways surveys (2022) identified defects in the surface water drainage system (deformed/partially collapsed pipes and root masses) that would have reduced system capacity. (p9)
* On 25 July 2021 culverts (including beneath Church Hill and Norton Lane) surcharged and sent floodwater onto adjacent roads; an unnamed tributary and collapsed culvert beneath Union Chapel caused surcharge and flooding of surrounding properties. Emergency responders (KCC, KFRS) attended and pump and clearance operations were undertaken. Subsequent actions included raising a pond bank north of Sunnyside and remedial culvert/pipe cleaning and repairs. (p12) [PAG]
* Intense rainfall on 25 July 2021 overwhelmed local surface water drainage systems and local watercourses, causing internal flooding to properties and carriageway flooding of local roads in the village. (p13) [PAG]
* On 25 July 2021 intense rainfall overwhelmed local surface water drainage and local watercourses, causing flooding to properties and roads. High water levels in receiving watercourses prevented the drainage network from discharging. (p14) [PAG]
