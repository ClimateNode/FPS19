

# Sunningwell flooding 31 Mar 2023 - 01 Apr 2023

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Flood Investigation Report: Sunningwell, Vale of White Horse, Oxfordshire<br>095\_Oxfordshire County/S19-Flood-Investigation-Sunningwell-Final-Report-with-appendices.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Sunningwell
* {31 Mar 2023 - 01 Apr 2023, 13 Oct 2023}: Exceptionally heavy rainfall on 31 March–1 April 2023 caused overland flow from surrounding hills that overwhelmed the watercourse through the village. Water passed through gardens, garages and properties toward the watercourse. Surface-water runoff also overloaded foul sewers leading to reports of foul flooding in properties. The event extent was reported worse than July 2007. Highway-only flooding was also reported on 13 October 2023. (p2) [TAB, MUL]
* {general}: Much of the village is shown as being at high risk from surface water flooding. Surface water flood maps indicate areas of the village have a greater than 3.3PCSYM chance of flooding each year (shown as dark blue on the EA surface water maps). The village is in Flood Zone 1 for fluvial/tidal risk but smaller local catchments have not been modelled by the Environment Agency, so surface water flood risk affects the settlement. (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* Environment Agency surface-water flood maps show much of the village is at high risk (>3.3PCSYM annual chance). A small watercourse runs through the village centre (culverted under some property accesses); two main carrier drains convey flows through the centre to a small pond on land owned by the Parish Council. Springs where Lower Greensand sandstone meets clay feed these watercourses, channelling runoff towards the Bayworth / Sunningwell area. (p8) [IMG, PAG]
* {2001, 2007, 2012}: Flooding affected properties in the village. A small watercourse runs through the centre of the village (culverted in places) with two main carrier drains transferring water through the centre to a small pond on land owned by the Parish Council; these watercourses/drainage features are implicated in local flooding of properties. (p9) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* {2001, 2007, 2012}: Flooding affected property in the village in previous events. (p10) [IMG, MUL]
* The village experienced river and overland flooding when the brook burst its banks and the village pond overflowed on 31 March–1 April 2023. Floodwater flowed along Sunningwell Road, through gardens and garages, and surrounded several properties. Foul drains were overwhelmed in parts of the village, causing foul-water contamination of floodwater and hydraulic overload of sewers in some locations. (p11)
* Overland flow paths and ordinary watercourses converge toward the village and the village pond catchment, directing runoff into low-lying parts of Sunningwell and increasing local surface water flood risk. The existing watercourse through the village was surveyed as part of the investigation. (p14) [IMG]
* The catchment draining to Sunningwell Brook received the event rainfall used in the hydrology modelling. This produced an estimated total rainfall volume of 65,243 m3 across the catchment and a modeled peak flow in the watercourse of around 0.83 m3/s during the 31 Mar 2023–01 Apr 2023 event. (p15) [IMG, PAG]
* Sunningwell experienced multiple flood mechanisms during 31 Mar 2023 - 01 Apr 2023. Open channel reaches and culverts through the village were heavily silted, reducing conveyance capacity and contributing to overbanking and garden/property flooding. Oxfordshire Highways inspected and cleared silt from culverts under Sunningwell Road. A 900mm diameter culvert running to the rear gardens of Sunningwell Road was approximately 50PCSYM silted, reducing its capacity and restricting flow. Significant overland flow from the surrounding hillside passed through and around several properties, increasing peak flows. Overloaded foul sewers in the village (Thames Water's network) caused internal flooding, notably in the Penn Lane / Sunningwell Road and Bullsmead areas. Thames Water are investigating and identified a non-return valve near the Penn Lane / Sunningwell Road junction that was installed in the wrong chamber and does not provide intended protection. (p18) [PAG]
* On 31 Mar–1 Apr 2023 exceptionally heavy rainfall and saturated soils produced significant overland flow from surrounding hillsides, which overwhelmed the watercourse through the centre of the village and caused water to pass through gardens, garages and properties. The event also overloaded foul sewers, producing reports of foul (sewer) flooding. Local gullies and grips were observed filled with silt and some culverted sections of the watercourse were restricting flow, exacerbating flood impacts. (p19) [PAG]
* The village experienced surface water flooding during the event, prompting a Section 19 investigation and recommendations for property-level protection, highway drainage assessment and community flood planning. (p28)
* Quant: 12 properties affected by internal flooding; 6 properties with ground-floor internal flooding; 2 cellars flooded; 5 properties with internal garage flooding; up to 30cm water depth in some properties (p11)

### Sunningwell Road, Sunningwell
* {31 Mar 2023 - 01 Apr 2023, 13 Oct 2023, 31 March / 1 April 2023}: Overland flow from surrounding hills and an overwhelmed watercourse sent water through gardens, garages and properties; properties on Sunningwell Road were affected. Surface water during the event also overloaded foul sewers (reports of foul flooding). Silted channels and restricted culverts increased pressure on the watercourse through properties on Sunningwell Road. (p2) [TAB, MUL]
* Flow along the watercourse through/adjacent to Sunningwell Road placed pressure on properties; measures such as a bypass/overflow channel and increased pond capacity are proposed to reduce that flood pressure. (p3)
* Location at the Penn Lane / Sunningwell Road junction near which a property experienced sewer backflow. (p4)
* Floodwater flowed along Sunningwell Road where it enters the village from the north; in parts flow reached knee depth. Pond overflow and overland flow caused water to run along the road and back through properties' gardens and garages. (p11)
* A 600mm diameter culvert carrying the watercourse under Sunningwell Road was reported as being blocked during the event; this reported blockage prompted inspection. The inspection found no capacity concerns with the culvert, but the reported blockage indicates it contributed to local flow restriction during the flood. (p16)
* Flow from the watercourse passed under Sunningwell Road through culverts, contributed to overland flows into properties and gardens, and the nearby foul sewer network overloaded causing internal flooding in the area. A downstream 900mm culvert running from the village pond into rear gardens was heavily silted, reducing hydraulic capacity and contributing to local flooding and flow restrictions. (p18) [PAG]
* Area around the Penn Lane / Sunningwell Road junction experienced foul (sewer) flooding when surface water entered the foul sewer network during the 31 Mar–1 Apr 2023 event, contributing to capacity failure and property flooding in the vicinity. (p19) [PAG]
* Surface water flow was connecting under the main road via a 375mm culvert from a field ditch to the east, contributing to local flood risk; proposals include increasing storage in the field ditch and modelling the drainage to reduce flows under the road. (p27) [PAG]
* {13 Oct 2023}: Water flowing into and through the brick culvert entrance on Sunningwell Road; muddy, active flow observed at the culvert entrance indicating high channel flows and active drainage into the culvert. The culvert has angled metal bars acting as a debris screen and was carrying muddy water. (p40) [IMG, PAG]
* Muddy, swollen watercourse running through the back garden with visibly high water levels reaching the garden steps; evidence of recent flooding that poses flood risk to the property and garden area. (p41) [IMG]
* Quant: 1 property (p4)

### Oxfordshire
* {31 Mar 2023 - 01 Apr 2023, 13 Oct 2023}: Highway drainage was overwhelmed and grips/gullies were full of silt; highway flooding was reported (including a later highway-only event on 13 October 2023). The highway authority carried out cleansing and desilted drainage assets (including the main twin 600mm diameter sewers linking to the village green) to restore capacity. (p2) [TAB, MUL]
* Oxfordshire Highways arranged inspection and clearance of silt from culverts under Sunningwell Road following the flood; works were targeted at culverts that contributed to restricted flow. (p18)

* {13 Oct 2023}: High water levels in a watercourse running through front gardens of residential properties, with muddy flow across paved paths and garden areas; photographic evidence shows water encroaching close to houses and garden features, indicating garden flooding and erosion/runoff impacts. * {13 Oct 2023}: Surface water runoff from an agricultural field to the east and elevated water in local channels flowed toward and through front and back gardens of properties on Boars Hill; saturated field surfaces and standing/muddy water channels were observed, indicating overland flow reaching residential gardens and margins adjacent to the highway. (p40) [IMG, PAG]

### Highways

Recommendations include reviewing grip positions, installing additional grips, ensuring culverts are clear and increasing cleansing frequency under the Countywide Highway Maintenance Programme. (p2) [TAB, PAG, MUL]

### Culverts
* Culverts (including a culvert outfall at the village green pond) were noted as silted/restricting flow; silted culvert outfalls contributed to reduced conveyance and flood risk and are recommended for desilting and CCTV inspection for root ingress/damage. (p2) [TAB, PAG]
* Silt accumulation at the culvert outfall (at the village green pond) and potential root ingress/damage were identified; recommendation to clear silt and carry out CCTV to check for root ingress/damage to restore conveyance and reduce flood risk to the pond and nearby properties. (p3)

### Sunningwell Brook
* The watercourse drains the catchment north of the village and is implicated in the village's flooding history; it is likely to have been involved in flood processes during the 31 Mar – 1 Apr 2023 event (investigation context). (p10) [IMG]
* During the 31 Mar 2023–01 Apr 2023 event the watercourse experienced high flows that filled the valley channel and overtopped/fully filled structures. The top timber check dam was likely full and water had scoured around the side of the top structure; flow was restricted downstream by a 225mm pipe causing a lake to form behind it; the attenuation area contains variable silt which reduced conveyance/storage and influenced discharge through the restriction. (p16)

### Vale of the White Horse Council
