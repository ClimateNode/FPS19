

# Deenethorpe flooding 14 Feb 2014

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Deenethorpe, nr Corby, 14th February 2014<br>090\_North Northamptonshire/Deenethorpe-Flood-Investigation.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Deenethorpe
* Intense short-duration rainfall led to excess surface water flowing over ground to low points in the village because land drainage systems and watercourses could not convey the runoff, resulting in internal flooding of at least one property. (p6) [PAG]
* Intense short-duration rainfall overwhelmed land drainage and watercourses so that excess surface water flowed to low points in the village, causing internal flooding to at least one property; the property has experienced internal flooding on more than one occasion in the five years prior to the investigation. (p7) [PAG]
* Internal flooding of a residential property in the new housing development at the north of the village; the affected property has experienced internal flooding on more than one occasion within the last five years. The report focuses on the new housing development area where the flooding was reported. (p9)
* Surface water from agricultural runoff flowed from fields into the rear gardens of residential properties in the housing development north of Deenethorpe, flooding patios and garages; water then flowed to the front of properties onto a shared private drive and caused ponding in Deenethorpe Lane. (p11) [TAB, PAG]
* Surface water from neighbouring agricultural fields flowed into rear gardens of a housing development north of Deenethorpe. At least two properties experienced muddy surface water overtopping property boundaries at significant velocity, reaching approximately 100mm depth on patios. One property had water enter and cover the garage floor and an external air‑source heating unit was at risk of damage; large deposits of soil were left across a patio and inside the garage requiring clearance. At a second property the water (including groundwater emerging from a steep banked garden) lifted slab paving at the rear requiring re‑laying. Residents used sandbags to prevent water entering airbricks at a neighbouring property. (p12)
* Reported routes of surface water were observed through the village during the flood event; the incident plans for the event indicate surface water flow paths through Deenethorpe. (p13) [PAG]
* Intense rainfall on a saturated catchment caused existing drainage systems, land drainage ditches and watercourses in Deenethorpe to be unable to cope. Surface water and runoff from agricultural land flowed through the housing development towards other drainage routes, causing flooding of vulnerable properties and local drainage failure. (p17)
* Surface water in the village could not drain effectively to the downstream watercourse because Willow Brook did not flow or function satisfactorily (manmade controls forming an artificial lake downstream of the A43 raised water levels), increasing local flood risk and impairing drainage from Deenethorpe. (p18) [PAG]
* Surface water runoff from neighbouring land and the emergence of groundwater affected residents in the village, creating flood risk to properties. (p21)

### Willow Brook
* Very high water levels upstream to Osier Bed Lane and at the inlet to the Deene Park lake due to high silt levels; these high river levels contributed to standing water on nearby roads and ditches. (p10)
* Willow Brook did not function satisfactorily due to manmade controls forming an artificial lake downstream of the A43; this affected water levels and reduced the brook's ability to receive surface water from Deenethorpe, contributing to local flooding and drainage problems. (p17)
* Restricted flow due to manmade controls forming an artificial lake downstream of the A43 has elevated water levels around Osier Bed Lane and impaired the ability of surface water from Deenethorpe to drain into the brook, increasing local flood risk. (p18) [PAG]
* High water level immediately upstream of the A43 road bridge and artificial lake, with water nearly reaching the base of the bridge arch, indicating elevated river levels and risk of overflow. (p48) [IMG, PAG]
* An open water course was observed channeling muddy, active flow from the culvert outlet into Willow Brook, demonstrating hydrological connectivity and overland flow reaching the brook during the event. (p51) [IMG, PAG]

### Deenethorpe Lane, Deenethorpe
* Ditch system north of the housing development and the culvert crossing Deenethorpe Lane were in poor condition with high water levels and silted pipes, reducing discharge to Willow Brook and impairing drainage. (p10)
* Ponding occurred in Deenethorpe Lane as water from agricultural fields and private drives accumulated; a ditch system and a culvert crossing Deenethorpe Lane to Willow Brook were observed with high water levels and silted pipes, contributing to drainage problems. (p11) [TAB, PAG]
* Surface water from properties and shared driveways flowed onto Deenethorpe Lane, causing ponding across the carriageway until the water drained via road gullies or verge grip drains. (p12)
* Culvert beneath Deenethorpe Lane discharged into a shallow muddy pool; road gully and verge grip conveyed surface water/runoff from the lane toward the culvert and downstream channel. Sediment accumulation and vegetation encroachment observed at the outlet and adjacent banks, with visible active flow indicating overland drainage and localised channelled surface-water flow during the event. (p51) [IMG, PAG]

### Osier Bed Lane, Deenethorpe
* Areas of standing water were observed on the carriageway and stagnant standing water in ditches alongside Osier Bed Lane as a result of very high water levels in Willow Brook and silt-affected flow. (p10)
* Water levels around Osier Bed Lane were affected by the impaired functioning of Willow Brook, reducing the ability for surface water from Deenethorpe to drain into the brook and contributing to local flooding and drainage issues. (p17)
* Standing water present in the carriageway at Osier Bed Lane and stagnant water in adjacent ditches near the bridge, affecting the road and local drainage. (p47) [IMG]
* Standing water on the carriageway and stagnant water in adjacent roadside ditches, indicating poor drainage and water accumulation on the road surface. (p48) [IMG, PAG]


## Topics without coordinates

### The Bridge
* Photographic description and notes indicate water at the culvert/bridge location and standing water on the nearby carriageway and ditches, affecting the bridge area and its immediate surroundings. (p47) [IMG]
* Water level observed nearly reaching the base of the bridge arch, reducing clearance and presenting a vulnerability to overtopping or obstruction from high flows. (p48) [IMG, PAG]

### Deene Park
* Very high levels of silt were observed at the inlet to the estate lake and water levels at the lake inlet were very high as a result of silted conditions in Willow Brook. (p10)

### Anglian Water
* Surface water entered the public sewerage system; the company was asked to assess sources of water entering sewers and the capacity of assets because insufficient capacity could lead to sewer-related flood risk to properties. (p21)

### East Northamptonshire Council
* Surface water/overland flow associated with a housing development and neighbouring land created flood risk to properties; the Council was instructed to review planning and construction records and to use enforcement powers where riparian maintenance failures increase flood risk. (p21)
