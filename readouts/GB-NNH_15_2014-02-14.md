

# Deenethorpe flooding 14 Feb 2014

## Place names with coordinates

### Deenethorpe
* Intense short-duration rainfall led to excess surface water flowing over ground to low points in the village because land drainage systems and watercourses could not convey the runoff, resulting in internal flooding of at least one property. (p6) [PAG]
* Intense short-duration rainfall overwhelmed land drainage and watercourses so that excess surface water flowed to low points in the village, causing internal flooding to at least one property; the property has experienced internal flooding on more than one occasion in the five years prior to the investigation. (p7) [PAG]
* Internal flooding of a residential property in the new housing development at the north of the village. The property experienced internal flooding on 14th February 2014 and had been internally flooded on more than one occasion in the previous five years. (p9)
* {01 Dec 2012 - 31 Dec 2012, 14 Feb 2014}: Surface water from agricultural fields flowed into rear gardens of residential properties in the housing development north of Deenethorpe, flooding a patio and a garage, flowing to the front of properties and a shared private drive, and causing ponding in Deenethorpe Lane. On 14/02/14 a second property was confirmed affected. (p11) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* Surface water from agricultural fields flowed into rear gardens of a housing development north of Deenethorpe. At two properties muddy surface water flowed over property boundaries at significant velocity, reaching approximately 100mm depth on patios. Surface water entered a garage covering the floor and putting an external air-source heating unit at risk. Large deposits of soil were left across a patio area and inside a garage requiring clearance. At a second property surface water and groundwater lifted slab paving at the rear requiring re‑laying. Residents used sandbags to prevent water entering airbricks. (p12)
* Incident Plans (Appendix C) indicate reported routes of surface water across the village during the event. (p13) [PAG]
* Intense rainfall on a saturated catchment caused existing drainage systems, land drainage ditches and watercourses in Deenethorpe to be unable to cope, causing water to flow into other areas and drainage systems that could not manage the additional quantity of water. Run-off from agricultural land was a significant factor increasing flood volume and risk to vulnerable properties. (p17)
* Surface water runoff from Deenethorpe could not drain effectively to Willow Brook because the brook did not flow or function satisfactorily (see Willow Brook), increasing local flood risk. (p18) [PAG]
* Properties in Deenethorpe were affected by surface water runoff from neighbouring land and by the emergence of groundwater during the 14th February 2014 event. The report recommends reviewing drainage for the housing development north of Deenethorpe to confirm overland-flow mitigation measures were designed and implemented. (p21)

### Willow Brook
* Surface water in the village drains to the Main River Willow Brook; the investigation into the 14th February 2014 event recorded internal flooding of one property in the new housing development at the north of Deenethorpe, with runoff discharging to Willow Brook. (p9) [PAG]
* Very high silt levels at the Deene Park lake inlet and very high water levels in Willow Brook upstream to Osier Bed Lane were observed; these high river levels caused backwater and contributed to standing water on nearby roads and ditches. (p10)
* Runoff and ponded surface water from Deenethorpe Lane drained via road gullies and verge grip drains into Willow Brook, increasing flows in the brook during the event. (p12)
* Willow Brook did not appear to flow or function satisfactorily due to manmade controls forming an artificial lake downstream of the A43, which affected water levels and reduced the brook's ability to receive surface water runoff from Deenethorpe, exacerbating local flooding issues. (p17)
* The brook was impeded by man‑made controls forming an artificial lake downstream of the A43, causing elevated water levels around Osier Bed Lane and reducing the ability for surface water from Deenethorpe to drain into the watercourse. (p18) [PAG]
* High water level observed immediately upstream of the A43 road bridge and artificial lake, with water close to the base of the bridge arch, indicating limited clearance and risk of overflow. (p48) [IMG, PAG]
* Standing/stagnant water observed in nearby ditches and drainage features adjacent to Willow Brook; verge grips noted between Deenethorpe Lane and Willow Brook opposite the housing development, indicating local overland flow/runoff collecting towards the brook and nearby drainage. These conditions were recorded in association with the investigation of standing water on nearby carriageways. (p49) [IMG, PAG]
* Received discharge from the culvert outlet and the open water course; the channel from the culvert delivered pooled and flowing water into Willow Brook, increasing local flow and conveying sediment/vegetation from the outlet and channel. (p51) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 1 property (p9) [PAG]

* Intense rainfall over a saturated catchment caused surface water runoff; existing drainage systems, land drainage ditches and watercourses were unable to cope, causing water to flow into other areas and overwhelm drainage systems. (p16)

### Deenethorpe Lane, Deenethorpe
* A culvert with headwalls crossing Deenethorpe Lane (north of the housing development) was observed in poor condition with silted pipes and high water levels; field boundary ditches drain to this culvert allowing runoff to discharge to Willow Brook, contributing to flooding risk on/around the lane. (p10)
* {01 Dec 2012 - 31 Dec 2012, 14 Feb 2014}: Ponding occurred in Deenethorpe Lane as water from agricultural fields flowed toward properties; a culvert crossing Deenethorpe Lane to Willow Brook was observed with high water levels, silted pipes and poor maintenance. (p11) [TAB, PAG, MUL]
* Surface water flowed onto Deenethorpe Lane causing ponding across the carriageway until the water drained via road gullies or verge grip drains. (p12)
* Culvert system beneath Deenethorpe Lane discharged water at its outlet, forming a shallow pooled area and feeding an open, muddy channel that ran from the culvert towards Willow Brook. Verge grips and a road gully on Deenethorpe Lane (opposite the housing development) conveyed surface water towards the culvert outlet, creating flow and local pooling at the lane verge. (p51) [IMG, PAG]

### Osier Bed Lane, Deenethorpe
* Areas of standing water were observed on the carriageway of Osier Bed Lane; stagnant standing water was also seen in roadside ditches alongside the lane, attributable to very high Willow Brook water levels upstream. (p10)
* Water levels around Osier Bed Lane were affected because Willow Brook did not function satisfactorily (due to downstream controls/artificial lake), which could impair the ability for surface water from Deenethorpe to drain into the brook and increase local flood risk. (p17)
* Standing water on the carriageway and stagnant water in adjacent roadside ditches, causing carriageway inundation, reduced access and hazards to vehicles and pedestrians; indicates poor drainage at the location. (p48) [IMG, PAG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Osier Bed Lane Bridge
* The surface water sewer discharges to Willow Brook at Osier Bed Lane Bridge; high water levels in Willow Brook were observed upstream to Osier Bed Lane, indicating elevated river levels at/near the bridge and likely reduced capacity of the discharge point. (p10)
* Standing water covered the carriageway on Osier Bed Lane and adjacent ditches contained stagnant water. A brick culvert/discharge point at the bridge was observed discharging into the adjacent watercourse close to residential properties. (p47) [IMG]

### Deene Park
* Very high silt levels were observed at the inlet to the artificial lake at the Deene Park estate. Water levels at the lake inlet, and in Willow Brook upstream to Osier Bed Lane, were very high as a result of the high silt levels. (p10)

### Anglian Water
* Surface water entered the public sewerage system during the 14th February 2014 event, prompting concern that public sewer capacity may have been insufficient and could have contributed to flood risk to properties. Anglian Water was asked to assess sources entering the sewer system, assess asset capacity where insufficient, and develop a detailed asset plan to share with the LLFA and the community. (p21)

### The Bridge
* High water level beneath the stone road bridge (near the A43) with water nearly reaching the base of the arch, reducing clearance and posing risk of overtopping; associated standing water noted at the Osier Bed Lane bridge carriageway where a discharge point is present. (p48) [IMG, PAG]
