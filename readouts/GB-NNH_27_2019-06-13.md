

# Corby flooding 13 Jun 2019

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Railway Adjacent to Pen Green Lane, Corby, 13 June 2019<br>090\_North Northamptonshire/Flood investigation report - Railway adjacent to Pen Green Lane, Corby.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Corby
* Relatively heavy rainfall and poorly maintained watercourses/control structures caused an unmanaged overflow; excess surface water flowed to localised low points in Corby, including flooding the railway adjacent to Pen Green Lane. Critical infrastructure (the railway) was affected for more than three hours. (p5)
* Flooding affected the railway adjacent to Pen Green Lane in Corby, constituting an impact on critical infrastructure within the town. (p7)
* Intense, concentrated rainfall over 10–13 June 2019 led to surface-water/flash flooding in Corby on 13 June 2019. Local river levels in the wider Nene/Witham area had been notably/exceptionally high in the week prior (5–11 June), preceding the event. The Corby weather station recorded a marked concentration of the week’s rainfall in the days immediately before and including 13 June, coincident with the flood event. (p9)
* A flood incident occurred in the north of Corby, affecting land on and adjacent to the Midland Main Line railway. (p10)
* The flood incident occurred in the immediate locality within Corby where the tributary/main river flows; blocked culverts and poor maintenance caused raised upstream water levels, spillway flows and filling of balancing ponds, contributing to localised flooding. (p14)
* Parts of Corby experienced surface water/urban flooding during heavy rainfall when surface water sewers outfell to the tributary and balancing ponds. The flow control culvert/weir and balancing ponds were overwhelmed/compromised by restricted flows and poor maintenance, producing pond overflow and downstream flood routing toward Pen Green Lane and the railway cutting. (p15)
* Localized flooding on 13 June 2019 driven by overtopping of Pond 2 and subsequent overland flow across Pen Green Lane and down a railway cutting, causing a landslip onto the railway and standing water that affected safe operation of trains. (p17)
* The area around the railway cutting south of Corby Tunnel experienced runoff, standing water on tracks and a landslip onto the railway. Railway operations were disrupted, emergency response was required on site, and Network Rail carried out clearance works. (p18)

### Pen Green Lane, Corby
* Excess surface water from unmanaged overflow flowed over ground to localised low points, flooding the railway adjacent to Pen Green Lane. The railway (critical infrastructure) was affected by flooding for more than three hours from the onset of flooding. (p5)
* A short access culvert beneath Pen Green Lane was reported to be almost completely blocked at the time of the flood incident, restricting flow and contributing to the flood event affecting downstream conveyance. (p14) [PAG]
* Overtopping from Pond 2 flowed across Pen Green Lane on 13 June 2019, conveying floodwater east across disused land to the top of the railway cutting. (p17)
* The culvert under Pen Green Lane was reported almost completely blocked. The blockage diverted excess flow to off-line balancing ponds, causing the ponds to fill and contributing to downstream overtopping and flow onto the railway cutting. (p18)
* Water spilled over the low point on Pen Green Lane from Pond 2, causing overtopping of the carriageway. (p33) [IMG, PAG]
* Water from Pond 2 spilled over at a low point on Pen Green Lane, inundating the road. (p34) [IMG, PAG]

### Northamptonshire
* The flood incident occurred in the north of the county (north of Corby), affecting land adjacent to the Midland Main Line within Northamptonshire. (p10) [PAG]
* Properties in Northamptonshire were affected by flooding, prompting a Section 19 investigation by the LLFA and the creation/maintenance of an asset register and records of flooding hotspots. (p23)

### Oakham

### Stephenson Way, Corby
* The watercourse flows along the north boundary of properties on Stephenson Way and was within the immediate locality of the flood incident; restricted flow and blocked culverts caused elevated upstream levels and flows that affected the area adjacent to Stephenson Way during the incident. (p14)

### Willow Brook
* {general}: Willowbrook watercourse has historically flooded the railway; National Rail reported Willowbrook flooding the railway every 2-3 years between 2006 and 2016, prompting maintenance and improvements (telemetry, replacement trash screen upstream of the aqueduct). (p10)
* Heavy rainfall increased flows in the watercourse (Willow Brook). Restriction at the flow control culvert/weir caused upstream water levels to rise and water to spill sideways into the balancing ponds; when pond capacity continued to rise flows were routed downstream via the emergency spillway toward Pen Green Lane and the railway aqueduct. The aqueduct and its spillways were at risk of being exceeded, threatening conveyance to the railway drainage at track level. (p15)
* The tributary of the Willow Brook at the location of the Corby flood incident was the watercourse involved in the flooding; it is classified as a Main River. (p24)

### River Nene
* {05 Jun 2019 - 11 Jun 2019}: River levels in the Nene area were recorded as 'Notably High' to 'Exceptionally High' between 5–11 June 2019, contributing to elevated flood risk in the locality. (p8)
* {05 Jun 2019 - 11 Jun 2019}: River levels in the Nene area were recorded as 'Notably High' between 5 to 11 June 2019, indicating elevated river levels that increased flood risk in the catchment. (p14)

### Corby Tunnel
* Floodwater ran down the cutting, increased in depth around the railway tracks and flowed north toward Corby Tunnel on 13 June 2019; standing water reached depths that would affect the safe operation of trains and a landslip occurred onto the tracks. (p17)
* Water flowing from the cutting and overtopping ponds increased water depth on and around the railway tracks and through Corby Tunnel, affecting safe train operation. A land slip from the cutting slope occurred onto the tracks (around 16:00), disabling a stopped southbound passenger train. Passengers were ultimately escorted off the railway on foot and evacuated by coach; emergency services assisted and Network Rail cleared the landslip and drained water via railway drainage. (p18)


## Topics without coordinates

### Network Rail
* Network Rail's railway infrastructure adjacent to Pen Green Lane was flooded, affecting critical infrastructure. (p7)
* Network Rail operated and maintained the trash screen at the aqueduct entrance and received telemetry reporting water levels; NR cleared and monitored the screen during heavy rain to reduce risk to the railway. The aqueduct/trash-screen arrangement is part of the flood pathway that threatens the railway during high flows. (p15)
* Persistent heavy rain and unmanaged overflow from poorly maintained watercourses and control structures caused flooding of the railway. Track and associated railway infrastructure were inundated and slope instability/landslip occurred on the cutting above the railway, prompting an engineering scheme (sheet piling) and coordinated action with risk management authorities. Ponds overflowing contributed to the incident, requiring 24-hour pump operation and restoration of telemetry to manage the pump arrangement. (p19)

### Midland Mainline
* The main area affected was the railway itself: flooding occurred on the Midland Main Line approximately 600 metres south of the south portal of the Corby Tunnel, inundating the track and adjacent disused land west of the railway. (p10)
* Quant: 600 metres south of the south portal of the Corby Tunnel; flooding reported every 2-3 years (between 2006 and 2016) (p10)

### Anglian Water
* The AWS-owned off-line surface water balancing pond (Pond 1) received overflow when the upstream flow control culvert restricted flows during the flood incident; blockage of culverts and build-up of silt, litter and vegetation impeded flow and contributed to water entering the pond system. Pond levels required management (pumping) to maintain capacity and the ponds were directly involved in the conveyance/storage of floodwater. (p14)
* Structures recorded as AWS assets (the flow control culvert, control weir, spillway and the pump arrangement) were reported to be in a poor state of maintenance at the time of the flood incident; silt, litter, debris and overgrown vegetation affected their function and contributed to flooding and reduced capacity to control flows. (p15)

### British Rail
* {general}: Historic flooding affected the railway operated by British Rail: first documented reports of railway flooding in 1968 and repeated incidents led to discussions and infrastructure works (balancing reservoir, aqueduct and drainage improvements) to manage flood impacts on the railway. (p10)

### Homes England
* The Homes England-owned off-line surface water balancing pond (Pond 2) received overflow via the spillway/weir when the flow control culvert restricted downstream flow; culvert blockages and debris/vegetation contributed to elevated upstream levels and flow into the pond system. The interlinked pond arrangement and associated culvert operation directly influenced local flood storage and conveyance. (p14)

### Control Weir
* The flow control culvert/weir (recorded as Anglian Water Services asset in part) was in a poor state of maintenance at the time of the flood incident. Silt, litter, debris and overgrown vegetation reduced its function, causing restricted conveyance, upstream ponding and spill into balancing ponds; because Pond 2's east bank was lower than the emergency spillway, ponds could overflow at the bank before the spillway operated. (p15)

### Culvert Beneath Pen Green Lane
