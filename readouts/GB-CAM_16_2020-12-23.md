## Place names with coordinates

### Godmanchester
* {1998}: Widespread flooding in 1998 affected the town. A flood alleviation scheme was subsequently delivered (1.5 km of engineered defences, river widening/dredging at Cooks Bridge, and two pumping stations) which reduced flood risk to residential and commercial properties and addressed river and groundwater/seepage issues. (p4)
* {1998}: Large areas of the town are at significant risk of fluvial (river) flooding from the River Great Ouse and from surface water flooding; following widespread flooding in 1998 an engineered flood alleviation scheme was implemented to reduce risk. (p5) [PAG, IMG]
* Significant rainfall on 23 December 2020 caused surface-water flooding and increased river (fluvial) flood pressure in the Godmanchester area; drainage systems were placed under substantial pressure and low-lying/residential areas adjacent to the River Great Ouse were vulnerable to inundation. (p6) [PAG, IMG]
* Surface water drainage systems were unable to convey runoff after the 23 December rainfall, leading to excess surface water flowing over ground and following local topography. Ditches, drainage systems and the Great River Ouse experienced high volumes and levels, placing properties at risk of surface water flooding. (p7)
* Surface water drainage systems in Godmanchester were unable to convey runoff, causing excess surface water to flow over the ground following local topography and putting properties at risk of surface water flooding. Some surface water outfalls discharge into the River Great Ouse; when river levels were high the outfalls became submerged, putting pressure on flap valves and causing water to back up within surface water systems. River levels following the 23 December rainfall exceeded previous records (highest on record) and river flow upstream of Godmanchester was recorded at 47.98 m3/s (204PCSYM of the long‑term average), contributing to the back-up and overtopping issues. (p8) [PAG]
* Properties, roads, gardens and fields in Godmanchester were flooded: internal property flooding was reported and external flooding affected garages/outbuildings, gardens and highway areas. (p10)
* Significant surface water flooding when intense and antecedent rainfall caused watercourses throughout Godmanchester to exceed channel capacity and surface water drainage systems to surcharge; at least two properties flooded internally. (p12) [IMG, PAG]
* Watercourses throughout Godmanchester exceeded channel capacity and surface water drainage systems surcharged, causing significant surface water flooding across the town. (p13)
* Severe flooding in December 2020 led to blocked/surcharging highway drainage and stress on sewer/pumping assets. Foul pumping stations in Godmanchester were identified as susceptible to inundation from ingress of surface water and groundwater; priority gully cleansing and highway drainage repairs were required. (p17) [TAB]
* Actions for the town include inclusion of highway drains in the gully cleansing programme and investigation/repair of blocked or collapsed highway drainage networks, reflecting impacts to drainage systems from flooding. (p19)
* {general}: Foul pumping stations may be inundated by ingress of surface water and groundwater during times of extreme rainfall (risk of inundation to pumping station assets). (p20)
* {general}: Foul pumping stations in Godmanchester are noted as potentially susceptible to being inundated during extreme rainfall due to ingress of surface water and groundwater. LLFA actions include sending follow-up riparian maintenance letters to properties adjacent to watercourses. (p21) [PAG]
* {23 Dec 2020 - 24 Dec 2020}: Rapid runoff from intense rainfall on 23rd December 2020 caused ditches to exceed channel capacity and drainage systems to surcharge, resulting in surface water flooding. Flooding of main highway assets (including the A1307) was prolonged and caused significant disruption, and many properties were affected across the eight key risk areas identified in the report. (p26)

### Stoneyhill Brook
