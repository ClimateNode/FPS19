

# Leamington Spa flooding 11 Jun 2023

## Place names with coordinates

### Shuckburgh Grove, Warwick
* Location experienced flooding during the reported event. (p1)
* Internal flooding to residential properties caused by the localised heavy rainfall event. (p3)
* Flooding occurred on 11 June 2023 resulting in internal flooding of residential properties on Shuckburgh Grove; the event prompted a Section 19 investigation by the LLFA. (p4)
* Shuckburgh Grove is identified as the red-point location on rainfall radar and experienced the most intense rainfall in its local catchment between 19:00 and 22:00 on 11 June 2023; this intense convective rainfall is the proximate cause of the localised surface water / flash flooding examined in the investigation (some uncertainty remains whether all impacts were inundation rather than heavy runoff). (p6) [IMG]
* Although local monitored rainfall at Shuckburgh Grove was lower than surrounding areas, runoff from the wider, more intense rainfall in the catchment flowed toward lower‑lying Shuckburgh Grove on 11th June 2023, potentially overwhelming drainage and contributing to surface water/urban flooding impacts downstream in the area. The local rainfall accumulation peaked in the early evening of 11th June 2023, coincident with the wider event. (p8) [PAG, IMG]
* Large volumes of surface water runoff travelled down Pound Lane, pooled at the junction of Loxley Way and flowed across a grass verge into the rear of properties on Shuckburgh Grove (properties sit lower than the highway), causing reports of flooding to properties. (p10)
* Property flooding occurred on Shuckburgh Grove during the event; highway and street drainage discharged into a culverted watercourse on Pound Lane Training Centre land, prompting investigation of the culvert which found it generally in good condition. (p11) [PAG]
* Following intense rainfall on 11th June 2023 surface water drainage became overwhelmed and water pooled towards the lower-elevation Shuckburgh Grove. Residential properties with minimal thresholds experienced internal flooding as surface water exceeded kerb heights and entered buildings. Blocked private drainage (guttering/downpipes) was noted and Warwick District Council arranged for a contractor (Axis) to jet and unblock drainage features around key areas, the car park and shared drying area following the event. (p12) [PAG]
* Surface water exceeded kerb heights and flowed from the highway and pathways into properties with minimal thresholds, entering gardens and causing internal flooding of residential properties. (p13) [PAG]
* Area experienced flooding associated with intense, high‑intensity rainfall across the wider catchment. Drainage network investigations were undertaken and found to be operating as intended, but flooding nevertheless occurred due to the rainfall intensity. (p14) [PAG]
* Surface water runoff entered properties (via low thresholds, rear and front) causing internal flooding; private drains blocked by leaves and debris aided ingress. Properties that back onto Loxley Way and sit at lower elevation flooded internally. (p15) [PAG]
* Internal flooding of residential properties on Shuckburgh Grove. (p16)
* Quant: 12 flood reports of internal flooding to residential properties; five or more residential properties internally flooded (threshold met) (p3)
* Quant: 12 residential properties internally flooded (p4)
* Quant: 12 flood reports (p10)
* Quant: 12 properties confirmed internal flooding (p13) [PAG]
* Quant: 12 properties internally flooded (p15) [PAG]

### Leamington Spa
* The town experienced flooding associated with the event on 11 June 2023 (affecting Shuckburgh Grove). (p1)
* Highly localised internal flooding occurred in parts of Leamington Spa (notably Shuckburgh Grove) during the evening of 11 June 2023. (p3)
* The Royal Leamington Spa catchment experienced intense, localized rainfall during the evening of 11 June 2023 (19:00–22:00) which contributed to surface-water / urban flooding in the Leamington Spa area; HydroMaster radar in the report shows the catchment received the most intense rainfall during that period and is the focus of the flood investigation. (p6) [IMG, PAG]
* Royal Leamington Spa recorded elevated short‑duration rainfall on 11 June 2023 (recorded return period >5 and <10 years for the town-scale observation), producing conditions likely to produce urban/surface water and flash‑flooding impacts in the town during the evening of 11 June. (p7) [IMG]
* Some reports of highway/standing water on highways were received for areas of Leamington during the 11 June 2023 event. (p10)

* Areas within Leamington Spa were affected by surface water flooding on 11th June 2023; notably Shuckburgh Grove experienced pooling and internal property flooding where thresholds were minimal and drainage was overwhelmed. (p12)
* Part of the town (Shuckburgh Grove area) experienced surface water flooding on 11 June 2023, with residential properties internally flooded due to intense localised rainfall and runoff. (p15) [PAG]
* Residential area experienced internal flooding centred on Shuckburgh Grove (internal flooding of homes). (p16)

### River Itchen

(p7) [PAG]
* Intense, short‑duration rainfall over the River Itchen to River Avon catchment produced very high runoff through the River Itchen catchment on 11th June 2023, contributing to rapid river flows and flooding that fed downstream impacts and risk of overwhelming downstream drainage and conveyance systems. ### Warwickshire County Council
* As the LLFA, the council received 12 flood reports from Shuckburgh Grove on 11 June 2023, undertook Section 19 investigations (including for Hurley and Wood End), instructed property services to survey the culverted watercourse at Pound Lane Training Centre and commissioned a CCTV inspection (10 July 2023) which found the culvert generally in good condition. (p10)
* WCC teams visited the site to collect evidence, requested photos/videos, and undertook CCTV/jetting inspections with partners to assess drainage following reports of internal property flooding. (p13)
* Involved in completion of drainage surveys and in arranging maintenance activities relating to highway drainage as part of the response. (p16) [TAB]

### Warwick District Council

* Undertook surveys of the existing drainage network, carried out routine clearance works on drainage assets they maintain, and offered advice to residents affected by internal flooding in Shuckburgh Grove. (p16) [TAB]

### Highways
* Highways officers conducted CCTV and jetting surveys of the drainage network around Shuckburgh Grove and reported no significant blockages; assessed that drainage was operating as intended and that intense rainfall in the catchment caused the flooding. (p13)
* Highway gullies and carriageway drainage on Shuckburgh Grove and Loxley Way were unable to convey intense surface water flows quickly enough during heavy local rainfall, allowing surface water to travel down Pound Lane and into lower-lying properties on Shuckburgh Grove. (p15)
* Responsible for maintaining/cleansing highway gullies (Ongoing) and considering installation of additional gullies at the corner of Loxley Way and Pound Lane; consideration of retrofit SuDS for the location. (p16)
* Quant: 12 properties internally flooded (p15)
* Quant: 12 properties (p16) [TAB]

### Severn Trent
* Severn Trent Water operatives visited and inspected their assets (CCTV/jetting) around Shuckburgh Grove and reported no significant blockages or collapses; defects were assessed as negligible. (p13)
* Surface water on the highway could not enter the STW surface water sewer quickly enough during the intense rainfall. This led to runoff bypassing the drainage network and flowing towards properties at lower elevation (Shuckburgh Grove), contributing to internal flooding. (p15)
* Completed surveys of the existing drainage network as part of the response to internal property flooding in the area. (p16) [TAB]
* Quant: 12 properties internally flooded (p15)
* Quant: 12 properties internally flooded (p16) [TAB]
