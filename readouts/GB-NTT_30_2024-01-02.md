

# Farndon flooding 02 Jan 2024

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Farndon – Storm Henk, January 2024<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/farndon-s19-storm-henk-jan-2024.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Farndon
* Subject of a Section 19 investigation into flooding associated with Storm Henk; the report context links the village to the heavy rain and countywide flooding on 2 January 2024. (p1) [PAG]
* Farndon village was the subject of the Section 19 investigation into the flood event on 2 January 2024; the village experienced flooding linked to heavy rainfall from Storm Henk (maps and risk outputs for Farndon are presented in the report). (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Predicted river (fluvial) flood risk concentrated along the River Trent extending into northern and western parts of Farndon; most of the village lies in Flood Zone 2 (moderate risk). Surface water flood mapping also shows medium–high surface water risk affecting built-up areas and roads. Figure 5 is stated to show areas affected by internal flooding within the area. (p3) [IMG]
* Internal flooding occurred in parts of the village; properties, open spaces and public highways were flooded. Flooding was concentrated in two residential clusters (northwest and southeast) as shown on the map. (p4) [IMG, PAG]
* Riverside properties in Farndon were at risk from River Trent flooding. Staff visited impacted communities and vulnerable individuals; many residents were helped to evacuate their homes, were provided emergency accommodation when necessary, and aqua sacs were delivered. The council carried out cleanup of roads/paths and removal of bulky waste as the event moved from surface water risk to continued river flooding risk. (p7)
* Community affected by flooding associated with the River Trent; local authority coordinated in-person visits to high-risk communities and has supported restocking of community aqua sac stores following the flood. Severn Trent Water investigations for the area indicate River Trent floodwater entered and overwhelmed the sewer network. (p8)

### Newark and Sherwood
* Contains the village of Farndon, which is the subject of this Section 19 investigation following flooding associated with Storm Henk on 2 January 2024. (p1)
* An area of Newark within the Newark and Sherwood district suffered internal flooding (internal property flooding). The report notes this adjacent area is relevant to nearby Farndon and to wider mitigation considerations. (p5)
* Communities in the district experienced flooding that required evacuation support, delivery of flood protection equipment (aqua sacs), provision of emergency accommodation, in‑person visits to at‑risk communities, and post‑flood cleanup of roads, paths and removal of bulky waste from flooded homes. Council staff visited affected individuals to identify humanitarian needs and arrange assistance. (p7)

### Nottinghamshire
* Experienced widespread flooding following heavy rain from Storm Henk on 2 January 2024. (p1) [PAG]
* Widespread flooding occurred across Nottinghamshire as a result of heavy rain from Storm Henk on 2 January 2024. (p2)

### Newark-On-Trent
* An area of Newark adjacent to Farndon suffered internal flooding (subject to its own Section 19 report). (p4)

### Nursery Avenue, Farndon
* Most of the reported internal flooding was in and around this area. A highway surface water pumping station serving Nursery Avenue continued to operate but could not cope with the volume of water, and properties flooded. An electricity substation in the area was not flooded. (p4)

### Fosse Road, Farndon
* Isolated internal flooding occurred along the (Old) Fosse Road. The report notes suggestions that flooding from the River Devon may have contributed to the Fosse/Old Fosse Road flooding as drainage systems and rivers were surcharged during Storm Henk, overwhelming drainage assets and resulting in internal flooding of highways/properties in the area. (p4) [IMG]

### Dorner Avenue, Newark-On-Trent
* Named explicitly in the Environment Agency Flood Warning for the River Trent (riverside properties) issued at 11:43 on 2 January 2024. Flood patrols were instigated and blockages in the area were cleared before and after peak flows. (p7)

### Chapel Lane, Farndon
* The sewage pumping station on Chapel Lane was checked following the flooding incident and actions were completed after that check. This is linked to River Trent flood water entering and overwhelming the sewer network. (p8)

### River Trent
* The River Trent is the main river on the map and the highest flood risk (Flood Zone 3) is concentrated along the River Trent, extending into nearby parts of Farndon — indicating fluvial flood risk from the river to adjacent land. (p3) [IMG]
* The River Trent caused river-flooding risk at Farndon; the Environment Agency issued a Flood Warning for the River Trent at Farndon, instigated flood patrols and cleared blockages, and predicted extreme levels prompted coordinated in-person community visits and multi-agency response measures. (p7)
* Predicted extreme levels and high River Trent flows resulted in flood water entering the sewer network and overwhelming it, causing local flooding impacts. (p8) [PAG]

### River Devon
* Unverified suggestions indicate flooding from the River Devon may have contributed to internal flooding at Nursery Avenue (Farndon) and to isolated flooding along Old Fosse Road. (p4)


## Topics without coordinates

### Old Fosse Road
* Experienced more isolated flooding along the road; River Devon is suggested as a possible contributing source. (p4)

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Acting as the Highway Authority and Lead Local Flood Authority for the area; drainage assets in their responsibility were overwhelmed during the event leading to properties and highways being flooded. The pumping station serving Nursery Avenue was installed/maintained on their behalf. (p4) [PAG]
* The county experienced a Major Incident with internal flooding to properties; the Council verified internal flooding on site, visited flood-affected residents and businesses to establish sources and severity, collated and published verified flood impact data, initiated a Community Flood Recovery Grant for internally flooded properties, and led the Section 19 Flood Investigation and coordination activities. (p6)
* As Lead Local Flood Authority coordinated in-person visits to high-risk communities, conducted internal and multi-agency debriefs, supported restocking of community flood supplies (aqua sacs), and will continue supporting communities (including Farndon) with signage schemes and reviewing Property Flood Resilience suitability. (p8)

### Severn Trent
* Flooding affected the company's sewerage network; surface water sewers, foul sewers and combined sewers required management to reduce flood risk and maintain serviceability. STW managed resilience measures and supported emergency flood response as a Category 2 responder. (p6)
* Received reports of flooding and investigated; investigations indicate River Trent flood water entered and overwhelmed the sewer network. Checked the sewage pumping station on Chapel Lane and completed actions following that check. (p8)

### Environment Agency
* Issued a Flood Warning for the River Trent at Farndon (including Dorner Avenue and Wyke and Marsh Lane) at 11:43 on 2 January 2024; instigated flood patrols and cleared blockages in the area; organised and attended a multi-agency community drop-in event; coordinated community information officers to visit affected communities and collect data to validate and improve the Flood Warning Service. (p7)
* Participated in response to the flood event and will commission an updated flood risk model to test scenarios and inform future works in the area. (p8)

### Via East Midlands
* Maintained the highway surface water pumping station that continued to pump throughout the event but was unable to prevent flooding of properties because it could not cope with the volume of water. (p4)

### Highway Authority
* As Highway Authority responsibility, drainage assets (including the highway surface water pumping station serving Nursery Avenue) were overwhelmed during the event, contributing to internal flooding of properties and public highways. (p4)
