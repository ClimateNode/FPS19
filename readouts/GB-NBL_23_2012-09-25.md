

# Hepscott flooding 25 Sep 2012

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Hepscott<br>091\_Northumberland/Hepscott-FIR-v2.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Hepscott
* Flooding in the village caused by ordinary watercourses, surface water and groundwater on 25/09/2012. Affected areas were concentrated near watercourses and surface water flow paths; one residential property was flooded and one road was affected. No businesses, other buildings or critical infrastructure were reported affected. (p1) [IMG, PAG]
* {2008, summer 2012, 25 Sep 2012}: Hepscott was badly affected by the 2008 floods. In summer 2012 the burn running adjacent to the main road through Hepscott experienced overspill that contributed to surface water flooding in the village area (notably affecting Thornlea on 25/09/2012). (p2) [MUL]
* Quant: 1 residential property; 1 road (p1) [IMG, PAG]

### Park Side, Hepscott
* Park Side (the road) is recorded as the road affected by the Hepscott flood event; flooding was associated with ordinary watercourse flows, surface water and groundwater according to the map summary. (p1) [IMG]
* Quant: 1 road (p1) [IMG]

### Crofts Park, Hepscott
* {06 Sep 2012, 25 Sep 2012}: One property was flooded on 06/09/2012 and 25/09/2012; floodwater entered the building via a basement that did not have waterproof protection. It is unknown whether the water was conveyed from the nearby Coal Burn or was groundwater seepage from adjacent saturated land. (p2) [MUL]
* {06 Sep 2012, 25 Sep 2012}: Quant: one property (p2) [MUL]

### Thornlea, Hepscott
* {summer 2012, 25 Sep 2012}: Thornlea (a residential cul‑de‑sac) was flooded on several occasions in summer 2012, in particular on 25/09/2012. Surface water from the nearby main road combined with overspill from a culvert on the adjacent burn and flowed along Thornlea to a drain at the end of the cul‑de‑sac that was unable to cope. Flooding of one property was narrowly avoided due to existing property‑level protection, private modifications and a decrease in rainfall intensity; additional property‑level protection was later installed on one property perceived to be at highest risk. (p2) [MUL]

### Coal Burn
* {summer 2012, 25 Sep 2012}: Overspill from a culvert entrance on the nearby burn contributed to surface water flooding that flowed along Thornlea (notably on 25/09/2012). The Coal Burn is also a possible source of floodwater to the flooded property in Crofts Park, although that source is not confirmed. (p2) [MUL]
