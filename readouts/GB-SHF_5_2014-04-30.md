

# Sheffield Metropolitan District flooding 30 Apr 2014

Reference document:<br>Investigation of a Flooding Incident Affecting Areas of South East Sheffield on 30 April 2014<br>109\_Sheffield/Section 19 Flood Investigation Report - 30 April 2014 Incident.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Sheffield Metropolitan District
* An extreme storm on 30 April 2014 (~60 minutes, peak ~18:15) overwhelmed local drainage in south‑east Sheffield causing overland exceedance flow on public highways and across open land. Property flooding occurred at Normanton Spring, High Street Mosborough and Richmond Park Rise. (p1)
* The map in Appendix A is a spatial overview showing areas in Sheffield identified as 'affected areas' in the council plan; these areas are presented for further detailed examination in subsequent plans, indicating the city contained locations impacted by the flood event. (p6) [IMG]
* The documented flood incident occurred within the Sheffield administrative area (Mosborough ward) and identifies local drainage infrastructure failures (surcharged manhole, defective culvert) relevant to city-level flood risk management. (p8) [IMG]
* Quant: 24 properties; 3 areas (p1)

### Normanton Spring
* Property flooding occurred in these named locations when the extreme storm on 30 April 2014 overwhelmed local drainage, producing overland exceedance flows on highways and open land; three areas (including Normanton Spring, High Street Mosborough and Richmond Park Rise) are identified as low spots within valleys and at risk on the surface water flood map. (p1)
* High overland flows from Richmond Park crossed Outwood Academy school playing fields and entered properties via rear gardens before issuing onto the public highway; properties flooded and immediate culvert inlet clearance was carried out; sandbags were delivered to affected properties. (p2)
* The settlement area (including Haxby Street and Normanton Spring Court) experienced flooding from converging watercourse flows and overland flow routes, resulting in an area of residential inundation and highlighting the need for culvert/inlet improvements. (p7) [IMG]
* Quant: 24 properties in South East Sheffield (p1)
* Quant: 10 properties (p2)

### High Street, Mosborough
* Flood waters from a surcharging manhole on a watercourse culvert (in the garden of 4 Toll House Mead), with some surface water run-off from High Street, affected properties on High Street; owners of the damaged culvert were informed and sandbags were distributed to affected properties. (p2)
* Flooding at High Street, Mosborough prompted the City Council to commission a catchment and feasibility study into mitigation options and to include the culvert asset on its capital programme for grant funding. (p3)
* Map documents a flood incident originating at a surcharged manhole on High Street; water flowed southeast along the mapped route, passing through a defective watercourse culvert and producing overland flow that reached properties and public areas (toward the Club). Infrastructure weaknesses (surcharged manhole, defective culvert) are directly linked to the observed flow paths. (p8) [IMG]
* Quant: 5 properties (p2)

### Richmond Park Rise, Sheffield Metropolitan District
* Identified as one of three low-spot areas affected by property flooding during the event and shown as at risk on the Environment Agency Flood Map for Surface Water. (p2)
* Surface water run-off from the road (road drainage overwhelmed by storm intensity) flooded properties located at the lowest point of the intersection with Avenue; properties were vulnerable because thresholds were below footway level. (p3)
* Quant: 6 properties (p3)

### Haxby Street, Normanton Spring
* Properties on Haxby Street were affected when overland flows from Richmond Park and the Outwood Academy playing fields entered properties via rear gardens and issued onto the public highway; the City Council delivered sandbags to properties. (p2)
* Residential street inundated by converging watercourse flow and overland flow from surrounding open land; culvert/culvert inlet capacity and overland flow routes contributed to flooding. Map annotations show planned interventions (new inlet grillage, culvert surveys) to reduce future risk. (p7) [IMG]
* Quant: 10 properties (collective across Haxby Street, Normanton Spring Court and Haxby Place) (p2)

### Normanton Spring Court, Sheffield Metropolitan District
* Properties on Normanton Spring Court were flooded by overland flows entering through rear gardens; sandbags were delivered by the City Council following the incident. (p2)
* Residential court/estate area shown within the hatched 'area affected by flooding' where overland flow routes and watercourse culverts converge, causing inundation of properties/grounds; annotations show proposed works (new inlet grillage, culvert surveys) to mitigate future flooding. (p7) [IMG]
* Quant: 10 properties (collective across Haxby Street, Normanton Spring Court and Haxby Place) (p2)

### Smalldale Road, Sheffield Metropolitan District
* Flood waters from surface run-off (road drainage overwhelmed by storm intensity) affected properties; sewage surcharging from an adjacent foul sewer contributed to the flooding. (p3)
* Combined sewer on Smalldale Road identified with root infiltration risk; recommendation for Yorkshire Water to place the combined sewer on an annual check for root infiltration and for Streets Ahead contractor to add the location to the priority drainage list for inspection of road gullies. (p4)
* Quant: 3 properties (p3)

### Haxby Place, Normanton Spring
* Properties on Haxby Place were affected by overland flows that entered gardens and properties; sandbags were delivered by the City Council. (p2)
* Quant: 10 properties (collective across Haxby Street, Normanton Spring Court and Haxby Place) (p2)

### Richmond Park Avenue, Sheffield Metropolitan District

### Hillside, Sheffield Metropolitan District
* The map shows the flood flow path passing adjacent to/through the Hillside area as water moved southeast from the surcharged manhole toward the defective culvert and downstream locations; Hillside is therefore likely to have experienced overland flow or related urban flooding during the event. (p8) [IMG]

### Richmond Park
* Property flooding occurred in these named locations when the extreme storm on 30 April 2014 overwhelmed local drainage, producing overland exceedance flows on highways and open land; three areas (including Normanton Spring, High Street Mosborough and Richmond Park Rise) are identified as low spots within valleys and at risk on the surface water flood map. (p1)
* High overland flows originated from Richmond Park and crossed onto Outwood Academy playing fields, contributing to floodwater that entered properties through rear gardens and onto the public highway. Culvert inlet condition/blocks at the park were identified as a main cause. (p2)
* Properties at Richmond Park Rise were identified as requiring flood protection at thresholds; recommendation for Streets Ahead to include the location on the priority drainage list for gully inspection and for The Housing Company/Great Places to consider installing flood boards at property thresholds. (p4)
* Quant: 24 properties in South East Sheffield (p1)

### Toll House Mead
* {01 May 2014}: A manhole on a watercourse culvert located in the garden of a property on Toll House Mead surcharged, producing floodwaters that affected properties on High Street; the culvert (ageing brick rubble) was found to be in poor condition and owners were advised that renewal works are required. (p2)
* Map annotation indicates overland surface water movement at/near Toll House Mead contributing to the flood route. (p8) [IMG]
* {01 May 2014}: Quant: 5 properties (p2)

### Outwood Academy School
* {01 May 2014}: School playing fields experienced high overland flows from Richmond Park which crossed the playing fields and contributed to floodwater entering nearby properties through rear gardens; culvert inlet blockage at the school playing fields was identified and immediate clearance works were carried out. (p2)
* {01 May 2014}: Quant: 10 properties (p2)
