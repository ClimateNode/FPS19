### Barton Brook
* {01 Jan 2013 - 31 Jan 2013, 01 Feb 2014 - 28 Feb 2014}: Flooding of Barton Brook caused repeated flood events in Barton-under-Needwood, contributing to property and street flooding in the village, including impacts to St Luke's Road during the Jan 2013 and Feb 2014 events. (p22) [PAG, MUL]
* {01 Jan 2013 - 31 Jan 2013, 01 Feb 2014 - 28 Feb 2014}: Barton Brook has been reported to cause flooding in Barton-under-Needwood in past events, with flooding from Barton Brook reported to have impacted St Luke's Road in January 2013 and February 2014. (p23) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* Flooding from Barton Brook contributed to internal flooding of residential properties in Barton-under-Needwood on 16th February 2020; it was one of the watercourses cited as causing the approximately 69 internally flooded homes and also led to flooding of external garages, sheds and driveways. (p24)
* Flows in the Barton Brook rose and overtopped, acting as a primary driver of flooding; culvert sections by the A38 required clearing to address the blockage/flow issues. (p25)
* Modelled rapid rise in Barton Brook flows during Storm Dennis (14–19 Feb 2020) with levels responding quickly then receding. Event magnitude was likely under‑represented in the model because the catchment was already extremely saturated and baseflows were high. (p27)
* High flows in Barton Brook overtopped the channel as it entered Barton-under-Needwood around Park Road on 16/02/2020. Once out of bank, flows moved eastward through gardens, accumulated behind the Wales Lane culvert entrance and contributed to damage to garden fences and a front gate and to internal flooding of properties on Brookside Road. (p28) [IMG]
* Barton Brook overtopped at a footbridge on 16 February 2020 following Storm Dennis; out-of-bank flows moved eastward through gardens and across roads, caused internal flooding of properties (first reported at the footbridge between Fallowfield Drive and Brookside Road), knocked over fence panels, accumulated behind culvert entrances (notably Wales Lane) and discharged as overland flow across roads and into gardens. High Barton Brook levels were reported where it discharges into Barton-under-Needwood around Park Road. (p29) [PAG, IMG]
* Barton Brook flows exceeded channel and culvert capacity at multiple locations (including under Efflinch Lane, St James' Road, Wales Lane and Collinson Road), causing bank overtopping, overland flow across highways and accumulation of surface water at culvert entrances leading to local flooding. (p30) [IMG]
* Culvert geometry (two ~90° bends) and restricted outlet reduced conveyance; losses at bends and restricted outlet forced floodwaters to mount banks at the culvert entrance, causing overtopping and directing flows toward nearby properties (including observed internal flooding of surrounding properties). Channel constrictions and potential blockages of multiple culverts contributed to impaired discharge. (p31) [PAG]
* Barton Brook overtopped and/or exceeded culvert capacity on 16/02/2020. Flows exceeded the culvert under Efflinch Lane (levels above the crown), caused overland flow across the highway, internally inundated properties on Efflinch Lane and to the south of Meadow Rise, knocked over a dwarf garage wall, caused floodwater on Potters Way and adjacent sports fields, and led to overflowing of the local fishing pond. Flows also overflowed the channel at the footbridge between Brookside Road and Fallowfield Drive and accumulated at St James' Road culvert entrance and south of Church Lane; culverts at Wales Lane, Collinson Road, St James' Road and Efflinch Lane may have been blocked or exceeded capacity. (p32) [PAG]
* Out-of-bank Barton Brook flows exceeded channel capacity, flowed across highways, reduced capacity for drainage discharge, and likely entered sewer assets causing surcharges that contributed to internal property flooding. (p35)
* Quant: 69 residential properties (collective across the three brooks) (p24)
* Quant: 0.93 m3/s peak discharge on 16/02/2020 at 14:55 (p27)
* Quant: approximately 450mm floodwater depth observed at the Wales Lane culvert entrance (p31) [PAG]

### Staffordshire
* Highway drainage (gullies, channels and underground sewers/culverts) can be overwhelmed during heavy or prolonged rainfall or when blocked. When the carriageway cannot contain flows water can pond on the highway, overtop kerbs and spill onto adjacent land, entering properties that are at or below road level. Historic variations in sewer design and capacity mean some parts of the drainage network surcharge or are unable to accept highway runoff, contributing to property and highway flooding. (p12) [PAG]

### Severn Trent
* As the sewerage company responsible for public sewers in Staffordshire, the sewer network they operate can be significantly overwhelmed during large storm events. This can cause sewer flow restriction or collapse, resulting in sewage backing up through the network, surcharging and reaching the surface (typically via manholes), and contributing to surface water flooding across the area. Severn Trent Water is therefore directly involved in the operation and maintenance response to these sewer-related flood impacts. (p11)
* {2012, 2013}: Responded to and mitigated sewer flooding in Barton-under-Needwood: installed non-return valves to provide short-term mitigation for properties flooded by sewage after flooding in 2012 and 2013; installed flow monitors on combined foul and stormwater sewers; completed a capacity improvement scheme (around Lichfield Road on the A38) in March 2016; increased sewer cleansing frequency around Mill Crescent; rectified sewer flood incidents impacting St Luke's Road. (p21) [MUL]
* Out-of-bank flows from Knoll Brook caused flooding that affected properties on Main Street; the local flood alleviation scheme was not operational during the event. (p35) [PAG]
* Quant: 5 properties (p35) [PAG]

### Environment Agency
* {15 Feb 2020, 16 Feb 2020}: Environment Agency issued a flood alert for the Burton-upon-Trent area (15 Feb 2020) and upgraded to a flood warning (16 Feb 2020). Following reports, Environment Agency field teams were deployed to Barton-under-Needwood to investigate and respond to the flooding that affected the village. (p24) [PAG, MUL]
* Deployed field teams to Barton-under-Needwood and cleared culvert sections of the Barton Brook by the A38 as part of the flood response to reduce river flow impacts. (p25)
* The Environment Agency conducts annual maintenance of the Barton Brook channel to reduce culvert blockages; an Environment Agency field team was deployed during the event to action clearing of blockages (a reported Short Lane blockage had been cleared before their arrival). (p31)
* {15 Feb 2020, 16 Feb 2020}: Quant: approximately 69 residential properties internally flooded (p24) [PAG, MUL]

### A38

### Highways Agency
* Maintains the culvert sections of the Barton Brook by the A38 that were implicated in the flooding and subsequently cleared by Environment Agency teams. (p25)

### Full Brook Catchment
* Hydrological modelling (ReFH/Hydromaster) for the Full Brook catchment using observed rainfall on 15–16 February 2020 indicates peak flows between a 2-year and 5-year event, which corresponded with Full Brook overtopping and subsequent flooding of Mill Lane and Mill Crescent on 16/02/2020. (p32)

### Highways
* Highways teams undertook clearance of in-channel blockages upstream of the Mill Lane culvert as remedial work to improve conveyance following flooding, reducing the risk of future flood impacts at that location. (p34)

### Canal and River Trust
* Inspected the siphonic culvert under the canal; limited culvert capacity and downstream water levels caused floodwater to back up into the open space between Mill Crescent and the canal during high flows, reducing conveyance and increasing upstream flood risk. (p34)
