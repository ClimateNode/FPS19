## Place names with coordinates

### Bramcote Lane, Wollaton Vale
* Overland flows down Bramcote Lane during an intense summer storm on 17 June 2020 overwhelmed local drainage and flowed toward properties at low spots, contributing to internal flooding of properties in the area. (p4)
* Internal flooding of residential properties (interiors flooded) as part of the flood event. (p6) [PAG]
* Properties on Bramcote Lane experienced internal flooding; the number of internally flooded properties in the area exceeded Nottingham City Council thresholds, prompting a Flood Investigation Report. (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Environment Agency flood map shows High Risk surface water flooding across Bramcote Lane; the street is not at risk from river (fluvial) flooding according to the flood map. (p8)
* {18 Jun 2020}: Overland flow followed the fall of Bramcote Lane; the flow breached the carriageway threshold (likely at dropped curbs) and ran along property frontages, causing internal flooding to some properties and external flooding to others. (p10)
* Properties on Bramcote Lane flooded internally and externally. Residents were displaced as some properties were uninhabitable during repairs; damage included unrepairable furniture, furnishings, floorboards, carpets and items stored in garages. Highway Services were able to deploy some sandbags at Bramcote Lane in time. (p11) [PAG]
* Residential properties on Bramcote Lane were flooded as part of an overland flow event that exceeded drainage capacity; properties experienced internal and/or external flooding during the event. (p14)
* Quant: Part of 6 properties (across Bramcote Lane, Brookside Avenue and Appledore Avenue) (p6) [PAG]

### Wollaton
* On 17 June 2020 an intense summer storm produced significant rainfall over Wollaton that overwhelmed local drainage infrastructure, produced overland flows (including down Bramcote Lane) and resulted in flooding at Bramcote Lane, Brookside Avenue and Appledore Avenue and internal flooding of properties in the area. (p4)
* Parts of Wollaton (Bramcote Lane, Brookside Avenue and Appledore Avenue) experienced internal flooding of properties; the scale of internal flooding exceeded thresholds and prompted a Flood Investigation Report. (p7)
* Wollaton was affected by the flood event on 17/06/2020 when an extremely localised thunderstorm moved northwest from Clifton, producing intense short‑duration rainfall that caused surface water / urban flooding within the Wollaton catchment. (p9) [PAG]
* {18 Jun 2020}: An intense, localised thunderstorm produced very high rainfall leading to rapid inundation of impermeable surfaces across the residential area (within ~30 minutes). Overland flow routes formed (notably down Bramcote Lane and Thoresby Lane) that caused internal and external flooding of residential properties in the area. (p10)
* Quant: more than 5 properties reported internal flooding (p4)
* {18 Jun 2020}: Quant: 7 properties (p10)

### Nottingham Unitary Authority
* Internal flooding of properties occurred within Nottingham City (specifically Bramcote Lane, Brookside Avenue and Appledore Avenue), triggering a Flood Investigation Report because the number of internally flooded properties exceeded council thresholds. (p7) [PAG]

## Topics without coordinates

### Highways
* {18 Jun 2020}: Highway Services responded to the flooding event by deploying sandbags at Bramcote Lane and will add affected road gullies to a 'Hotspot List' for increased cleansing in response to weather warnings; they operate an annual gully-cleansing regime. (p10)

### Nottingham City Council
* {18 Jun 2020}: Council resources were used in the response: it has a limited stock of sandbags reserved for critical infrastructure or vulnerable residents and Highway Services were able to deploy some sandbags at Bramcote Lane during the event. (p10)
