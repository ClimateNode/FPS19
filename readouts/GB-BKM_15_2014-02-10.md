## Place names with coordinates

### The Orchards Residential Park
* {December 2013 to February 2014}: Prolonged above-average rainfall caused groundwater levels to rise, contributing to the overwhelming of drainage and resulting localised flooding. (p4)
* {general}: A flood incident occurred at The Orchards Residential Park that posed environmental and public health risks to residents and prompted an investigation by the Buckinghamshire Strategic Flood Management Committee. (p6) [IMG, PAG]
* {December 2013 to February 2014}: Surface water flooding affected mainly the south of The Orchards residential park during the December 2013–February 2014 event. Flooding resulted from a combination of high watercourse (fluvial) levels, elevated groundwater and surface water ponding which inundated the southern part of the park. (p9) [PAG, IMG]
* {December 2013 to February 2014}: The southern area of the residential park flooded during the December 2013–February 2014 event. Surface water flooding observed on the maps corresponds to flooding actually experienced; high watercourse (river) levels and high groundwater levels, together with surface runoff, combined to cause inundation of the park. (p10) [PAG]
* {December 2013 to February 2014}: The combined drainage system became inundated (full to capacity) due to groundwater infiltration and excess runoff; it backed up and discharged via gullies, causing contaminated floodwater to pond around the residential park and reducing system capacity. (p12) [PAG]
* {December 2013 to February 2014}: The Orchards Residential Park experienced flooding when the combined drainage system could not cope with extreme, prolonged rainfall. Floodwater (contaminated with sewerage) covered roads, gardens and footpaths, making access very difficult and creating environmental and public health risks. (p16) [TAB]
* {December 2013 to February 2014}: Extensive external flooding across The Orchards Residential Park driven by extreme, prolonged rainfall. The combined drainage system backed up and discharged via gullies; floodwater was contaminated with sewage and covered roads, gardens and footpaths, restricting access and creating environmental and public health risk. No properties were flooded internally. (p17)
* {December 2013 to February 2014}: Quant: 0 properties internally affected (p17)

### Buckinghamshire
* {December 2013 to February 2014}: During the December 2013–February 2014 event the Colne Brook tributaries were flowing at full capacity due to prolonged heavy rainfall; following the flood event log debris was observed within the watercourse though no large blockage was reported. (p11) [PAG]
* {December 2013 to February 2014}: Thames Water carried out extra pumping at the pumping station to relieve the sewer/system, an action thought to have caused the rapid receding of accumulated flood water during the event. (p14)

### Wexham
* {December 2013 to February 2014}: An increased volume of surface water contributed to overwhelming the private drainage and causing localised flooding on site. (p4)

### Colne Brook
* {December 2013 to February 2014}: The Orchards Residential Park experienced a prolonged period of flooding from December 2013 to February 2014, with severe flooding in early February 2014. Floodwater ponded around the park after the combined drainage system backed up and discharged via gullies; the ponded water was contaminated with sewerage, posing environmental and public health risks. Due to the height of the homes there were no records of internal flooding. (p12)


## Topics without coordinates

### Tingdene Park Homes
* Roadway and driveway areas were flooded, with water covering the majority of the road and pooling in parking/driveway areas; floodwater encroached on nearby park homes and sandbags were placed to protect property margins. (p1) [IMG]
* {December 2013 to February 2014}: Colne Brook tributaries were flowing at full capacity due to continuous rainfall; following the event log debris was noted within the watercourse though nothing large enough to cause a significant blockage. (p11)
* Residential street in the mobile home park was covered by surface floodwater; photographs on 10th February 2014 show a large puddle and residual water remaining on the road after the flood receded. (p23) [IMG]

### Buckinghamshire County Council
* Surface-water/urban flooding inundated roads, driveways and parking areas within the park home estate; floodwater extended along streets and encroached on adjacent park home plots, with sandbags deployed at property margins. (p1)
* {December 2013 to February 2014}: Localised flooding at low points on the residential park caused by overwhelmed private combined drainage due to high groundwater levels and increased surface water; standing contaminated flood water created serious environmental and public health risks. (p4)

### Middle Green Pumping Station
* {February 2014}: Middle Green pumping station was suspected by locals to have been faulty during the event; Thames Water attended and undertook extra pumping at the station to provide additional support to the sewer network, an action thought to have caused the sudden receding of accumulated flood water. (p13)
* {December 2013 to February 2014}: The combined (surface water + sewer) drainage system could not cope with volumes of groundwater and surface water, causing it to back up and discharge out of gullies and contribute to external flooding and contamination. (p17) [TAB]

### Transport for Buckinghamshire
* {February 2014}: Received two customer calls about flooding close to Middle Green pumping station in February 2014; attended both times and reported the pumping station running normally, and carried out extra pumping at the station to provide additional support to the sewer network which aided system relief. (p13)
* {February 2014}: Quant: two customer calls (p13)
