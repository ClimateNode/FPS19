

# Lowestoft flooding 06 Oct 2019

## Place names with coordinates

### Lowestoft
* At Homeport on Whapload Road there was internal flooding of properties and external flooding of others; the main private forecourt and internal access roads were flooded. The total affected area was 0.09Ha (900m2). (p2)
* {previous events 2010, 2011, 2013, 06 Oct 2019}: An intense storm produced surface water exceedance in Lowestoft resulting in internal flooding of properties at the site (OS Grid Reference TM 55210 94258), flooding of a private forecourt and internal access roads where drainage capacity was exceeded. (p3) [IMG, MUL]
* Quant: 8 properties flooded internally; 2 properties with external flooding; 0.09Ha (900m2) affected area (p2)
* {previous events 2010, 2011, 2013, 06 Oct 2019}: Quant: 8 properties flooded internally; 2 properties with external flooding; total affected area 0.09Ha (900m2); internal flood depths between 100mm–300mm (p3) [IMG, MUL]

### Whapload Road, Lowestoft
* Whapload Road converges to a low spot outside Homeport causing runoff from north and south to accumulate; lack of highway drainage/gullies and drop-kerb levels allowed runoff to top the kerb and convey floodwater into Homeport (road flooding forced water into forecourts). (p4) [PAG]
* Whapload Rd converges to a low depression outside Homeport causing runoff from north and south to accumulate; when runoff tops kerbs it conveys into Homeport. Lack of highway gullies exacerbated surface-water runoff and ponding; moving vehicles forced water out of the highway during floods, worsening impacts to adjacent properties. (p5) [PAG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Homeport
* Surface water runoff and ponding accumulated at the toe of the slope in front of the properties (localised ponding at the front of the buildings). Evidence of foul and surface water mixing indicated by residents reporting toilets 'bubbling out', suggesting backflow/mixing of sewage with floodwater into the private drainage. (p4) [PAG]
* Localised ponding occurred at the front of the building/forecourt as runoff from Whapload Rd collected in a low spot and overtopped kerbs into the site; residents reported toilets bubbling, indicating foul sewer surcharge/mixing with surface water and polluted floodwater entering the property. (p5) [PAG]

### Anglian Water
* A combined sewer owned by Anglian Water exists in Whapload Road; the lack of separate storm disposal and possible private drains connecting to the combined sewer are described as contributing to mixing of foul and surface water and potential sewer-related backflow into properties. (p4) [PAG]
* Combined public sewer in Whapload Rd (Anglian Water asset) is the only surface-water disposal route in the area; evidence (toilets bubbling) suggests stormwater and foul drainage may be mixing and/or the combined system is surcharging, leading to contaminated floodwater entering properties. (p5) [PAG]

### Suffolk County Council
* As highway owner and Lead Local Flood Authority, lack of highway drainage (absence of gullies), drop-kerb levels and no safe exceedance routes are identified as factors exacerbating surface water runoff and ponding that led to flooding at Homeport; recommendations include surveying and improving highway drainage and installing additional gullies. (p4) [PAG]
* Public highway (Whapload Rd) drainage is inadequate (lack of gullies) so surface water ponded and runoff converged to a low spot outside Homeport, topping kerbs and conveying water onto private property; highway ponding and runoff impeded access for emergency vehicles. (p5) [PAG]
