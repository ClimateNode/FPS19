## Place names with coordinates

### Retford
* Experienced extensive flooding during the November 2019 intense rainfall event; town was specifically called out as being affected in the report. (p1) [PAG]
* Parts of the town experienced fluvial and surface-water flooding: internal property flooding, flooded gardens and curtilages, and public highways rendered impassable (local streets listed). (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Significant internal property flooding across the town (internal flooding of dwellings), inundation of gardens and curtilages and public highways made impassable (notably Grove Lane and Blackstope Lane). (p3) [IMG]
* Localised internal flooding occurred in parts of Retford where the Retford Beck is culverted; backing up of water at culvert locations caused overtopping and led to flooding of nearby houses (notably around Grove Lane and Blackstope Lane). (p4) [IMG, PAG]
* An area of Retford (near Carr Dyke and adjacent to the River Idle) experienced internal flooding affecting residential properties, rear gardens, outhouses and allotment gardens; the map highlights an area near Bequel Road as affected. (p5) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: The Environment Agency extracts cover the Darrel Road area of Retford and indicate predicted river and surface-water flooding across parts of Retford; mapping patterns are said to align with known historical flooding in the area. (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Parts of Retford (the Darrel Road area and the Grove Lane / Blackstope Lane area) are shown on the Environment Agency flood risk maps as lying within flood risk zones; the maps indicate higher risk (darker shading) near the centre of the panels and that risk extends into adjacent fields and residential areas. (p8) [PAG]
* Properties in the community were affected by flooding; a public drop-in session in Retford was attended by Bassetlaw District Council, Nottinghamshire County Council Flood Risk Management and Emergency Planning Teams and the Red Cross to provide updates, advice and assistance; recovery grants and resilience measures are being administered/delivered. (p11)
* Quant: 31 reported incidents of internal property flooding; 2 public highways named as impassable (Grove Lane and Blackstope Lane). (p2) [IMG, PAG]

### Grove Lane, Retford
* Highway inundated and rendered impassable during the event; area is in the zone where Retford Beck is culverted and backing-up/overtopping of the beck led to flooding. (p3)
* Areas of Grove Lane are specifically identified as being affected by internal flooding when the Retford Beck becomes culverted and water backs up and overtops, with the local topography directing water onto houses. (p4) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Shown on the Environment Agency flood risk maps as lying within flood risk zones; darker blue shading indicates higher flood risk concentrated near the centre of the map. Flood risk zones extend from Grove Lane into adjacent fields and nearby residential areas. (p8) [IMG]
* Floodwaters affected Grove Lane; the Environment Agency overpumped flood water away from the affected area, cleared debris screens, checked hotspots and requested road closures where necessary. Nottinghamshire County Council replaced a damaged kerb line along the Grove Lane length of the Retford Beck to add capacity and increase bank-side stability. (p10)
* Environment Agency will review screen design at Grove Lane as part of flood-risk management actions (review intended to reduce flood risk at this location). (p11)
* Quant: 31 reported incidents of internal property flooding in Retford (across affected areas including Grove Lane) (p3) [IMG]
* Quant: 7 mobile pumps (p10)

### Darrel Road, Retford
* Internal flooding reported to residential properties on Darrel Road; rear gardens and outhouses of properties were inundated because the adjoining Carr Dyke backed up and the River Idle was surcharging, amplified by a period of significant antecedent rainfall. The front elevations were largely protected by higher ground, limiting internal flooding to lower rear parts of properties. (p5)
* Flooding affected the rear of properties on Darrel Road — gardens and backyards were waterlogged or inundated, with fences, sheds, greenhouses and allotments partially submerged as floodwater from the Carr Dyke encroached onto residential land. (p6) [IMG]
* {general}: Predicted and known flooding affecting Darrel Road: maps show the highest flood risk concentrated around Darrel Road. The report/figures specifically note 'flooding to rear of Darrel Road' and alignment between mapping and known historical flooding (river and surface water). (p7) [IMG, PAG]

### River Idle
* Associated with fluvial (river) flooding in the Retford valley during the November 2019 event; contributed to flooding of parts of Retford. (p2)
* The River Idle was surcharging during the event and this surcharging contributed to the backing up of the Carr Dyke and subsequent internal and garden flooding in the adjacent residential area (including Darrel Road). (p5) [IMG]
* The interaction between the River Idle and the Carr Dyke was identified as relevant to the flooding; its interaction is to be reviewed to reduce future flood risk. (p10)
* Environment Agency will complete a bathymetric survey on the River Idle to inform future maintenance as part of managing flood risk (river identified as relevant to flood impacts and maintenance). (p11)

### Retford Beck
* Retford Beck becomes culverted in the affected area; during heavy or persistent rainfall the culverted sections cause water to back up and overtop, leading to flooding of houses and contributing to the internal property flooding in Retford. Designated as a Main River (Environment Agency responsibility). (p3)
* Retford Beck is culverted at multiple points; during heavy or persistent rainfall the beck backs up at the culverted locations, overtops and contributes to internal flooding of adjacent properties when water cannot pass through the culverts. (p4) [IMG, PAG]
* Flooding/high flows associated with the Retford Beck resulted in damage to the kerb line along its Grove Lane length; the kerb was replaced to provide additional capacity and bank stability. The Environment Agency is pursuing proposals for improvements to the Retford Beck. (p10) [PAG]
* Identified as requiring improvements and the subject of proposals and funding-seeking by the Environment Agency for a flood risk management scheme (i.e. the watercourse was the focus of flood-risk mitigation actions). (p11)

### Carr Dyke
* Carr Dyke lacked capacity and backed up (evidence shown where it passes under an access road), causing flow to inundate adjacent rear gardens and outhouses and contributing to internal flooding on Darrel Road. (p5) [IMG]
* The dyke backed up (including where it passes under an access road) and overflowed, causing floodwater to spread from the watercourse into adjacent land. Flooding affected rear gardens, allotments and adjacent land with water encroaching on fences and inundating sheds, greenhouses and garden plots. (p6) [IMG, PAG]
* The operation of Carr Dyke and its interaction with the River Idle were identified as factors in the incident; the Internal Drainage Board will review and seek to improve Carr Dyke operation to reduce future flood risk. (p10)
