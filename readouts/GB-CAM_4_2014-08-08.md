

# Oakington flooding 08 Aug 2014 - 09 Aug 2014

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Oakington<br>021\_Cambridgeshire County/021\_2014\_Cambridgeshire County/Flood\_Investigation\_Oakington\_2014.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Oakington
* Flooding occurred in Oakington locations listed in the Formal Flood Investigation Report: Cambridge Road, Kettles Close and Arcadia Gardens (and also Longstanton Road, Orchard Way, The Broadway, Water Lane are listed as locations of the incident). The incident was reported in the Formal Flood Investigation (Reference FI/14/000046). (p2)
* Experienced widespread flooding during the 8–9 August 2014 extreme rainfall event: drainage networks and receiving watercourses (including Oakington Brook) were overwhelmed, causing internal flooding to properties and external inundation; property level protection was partially installed for some properties but reported not to have operated as expected. (p3) [IMG, TAB, PAG]
* Extreme rainfall on 8–9 August 2014 caused internal flooding of properties and external flooding to additional properties; community disruption and follow-up investigations and meetings were required. (p4)
* Quant: 57 properties flooded internally; approximately 60 additional properties externally flooded; 5 to 60 cm (depths reported inside properties) (p3) [IMG, TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 57 properties (internal flooded); 60 properties (external flooded) (p4)

### Oakington Brook
* Reported as one of the multiple sources that contributed to the August 2014 flooding in Oakington by overwhelming the receiving watercourse and preventing highway drains and local ditches from discharging, contributing to internal and external property flooding. (p3) [TAB]
* Quant: 5 to 60 cm (depths reported inside properties); 57 properties flooded internally; approximately 60 additional properties externally flooded (p3) [TAB]


## Topics without coordinates

### Environment Agency
* {August 2014, 08 Aug 2014 - 09 Aug 2014}: The Environment Agency is recorded in the report as a reporting party and is identified in the responsibilities table as the lead authority for main river and sea/reservoir flood sources; it was involved in the August 2014 incident reporting and response. (p2) [TAB]
* Reported flood depths in properties (5–60 cm), led a property level protection (PLP) scheme (53 properties signed up) which was partially installed prior to the August 2014 event; investigated why PLP did not operate as expected during the extreme rainfall and organised a post‑flood event for residents. (p3) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 53 properties (signed up to PLP); 5 to 60 cm (depths reported inside properties); 57 properties flooded internally; approximately 60 additional properties externally flooded (p3) [TAB, PAG]

### Anglian Water
* Anglian Water operated its pumping station continuously during the storm (two pumps ran non-stop and a third pump was deployed until surface water entering the pumping station was cleared). Cambridgeshire County Council Highways checked and cleared the surface water drains for which it is responsible; the County (as LLFA) also worked with other RMAs to establish what happened during the event. (p3) [TAB]
* Operated the local pumping station continuously during the extreme rainfall; two pumps ran non‑stop and a third pump was also used until the surface water entering the pumping station was cleared. (p4)

### South Cambridgeshire District Council
* {August 2014, 08 Aug 2014 - 09 Aug 2014}: South Cambridgeshire District Council (SCDC) was the Risk Management Authority reported to for the August 2014 flood incident affecting streets listed in Table 2 (reports received from residents and the Environment Agency). (p2) [TAB]
* {08 Aug 2014 - 09 Aug 2014, December 2014}: South Cambridgeshire District Council (SCDC) is responsible for annual maintenance of awarded watercourses in Oakington; after the August 2014 flood SCDC assessed the awarded watercourses, reported no additional maintenance was required, and completed relevant maintenance by December 2014. (p3) [TAB, PAG, MUL]

### Cambridgeshire County Council
* Cambridgeshire County Council Highways checked and cleared the surface water drains for which they are responsible and will prioritise routine gully cleansing under a new risk‑based approach following the event. (p4) [PAG]
* Following flooding in August 2014, the council supported a post‑flood event, took forward actions from the investigation (including actions relating to the operation of the PLP during the rainfall event), and prioritised routine cleansing of highway gullies as part of a new risk‑based gully cleansing approach. The council also advised flooded residents to contact the National Flood Forum and encouraged establishment of Flood Action Groups. (p5) [PAG]
