### Quinton
* Intense rainfall on a saturated catchment caused excess surface water to flow across Quinton (following natural contours), resulting in internal flooding of at least one property and noted to have occurred on at least one other occasion within the previous five years; historic drainage was ineffective. (p5) [PAG]
* At least one property in Quinton flooded internally during the flood event; flooding resulted from intense short-duration rainfall on a saturated catchment causing excess surface water to flow over ground following natural contours to low points (including School Lane and Preston Deanery Road). Flooding was also reported on at least one other occasion within the previous five years. (p6) [PAG]
* Village experienced flooding during heavy rainfall on 25 November 2012 including inundation of streets (School Lane and Preston Deanery Road) and at least one property flooded internally. (p8)
* Flood incidents occurred in Quinton; these incidents are the subject of LLFA (NCC) investigation and recommendations. The local watercourse in the village is an ordinary watercourse (not a Main River). (p11) [PAG]
* Flooding affected Quinton village associated with the local ordinary watercourse; the Main River (Wootton Brook) about 1.5 km downstream is not considered to affect the village flooding. (p12)
* Intense short-duration rainfall on a saturated catchment caused existing drainage systems and watercourses to be unable to cope. Water overtopped systems and flowed to the low point in the village; drainage at that low point could not drain the quantity of water experienced. Inlets to drainage structures were affected by silt and debris, reducing flow capacity. (p15)
* Flows of water from higher ground overtopped drainage systems and flowed to the low point in the village. The village drainage was not designed to convey the quantity of water experienced, causing overspill of land drainage from watercourses onto the public highway and private property. (p16)
* Quant: at least 1 property; 1 other occasion in last 5 years (p5) [PAG]
* Quant: at least 1 property flooded internally; 1 additional historical occurrence within last five years (p6) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 property (p8)

### Northamptonshire
* The report states that flooding occurred in Quinton (within Northamptonshire) when intense rainfall on a saturated catchment overwhelmed existing drainage systems and watercourses; water flowed to other areas and drainage systems were unable to manage the additional volume, causing flooding. (p14)

### Wootton Road, Quinton
* Runoff and surface flows on Wootton Road (including flow from the start of the culvert) were not removed by the riparian ditch and highway grip drainage systems, so flows from Wootton Road travelled to School Lane and added to the overall surface water flooding; riparian ditches and culverted watercourse at the south of School Lane on Wootton Road were full of stagnant water and accepted runoff and treated foul water from nearby properties. (p10)
* Highway grip drains and adjacent riparian ditches on Wootton Road were identified as locations requiring attention because the drainage was not able to convey the flood flows; this contributed to overspill toward the highway and nearby properties. (p16)
* Riparian-owned roadside ditches alongside Wootton Road (north side) contain standing water adjacent to the carriageway; evidence of highway grip drains and historic riparian ditches west of School Lane are present, indicating drainage features alongside the road are affected by retained water. (p35) [IMG, PAG]

### Quinton Catchment
* Significant rainfall fell on the Quinton catchment on 25 November 2012, giving rise to conditions that caused flooding in the village. (p8)
* Significant rainfall over the Quinton catchment on 25–26 November 2012 resulted in conditions that caused flooding; the report states flooding on 25 November 2012 affected people and one property. (p9)
* Quant: one property (p9)

### Highway Authority
* A culverted ordinary watercourse flowing south–north through the village is reported to risk overtopping and causing flooding of the public highway and flood risk to properties; replacement or upsizing of the conduit and clearance works are recommended to allow free flow during extreme events. (p17)

### Roman Moat
* The pond adjacent to the historic Roman moat is proposed for emergency flood storage; the report highlights the risk that breach or failure of any storage could flood properties downstream. (p17)

### Northamptonshire County Council
* The report describes ordinary watercourse and surface water runoff through the village causing risk of overtopping to the public highway and flood risk to properties. As a result NCC Highways are to undertake regular highway drainage cleansing, develop a detailed plan of highway drainage assets, assess asset capacity and identify where capacity is insufficient (so improvement works can be considered). LLFA and NCC Emergency Planning are to provide advice to residents, instate and liaise with a Flood Warden and produce/update the village community flood plan. (p18) [PAG]

### Wootton Field
* The riparian-owned ditch system on Wootton Field is identified as needing to be adequate to accept runoff from the highway; insufficiency could lead to runoff-related flooding affecting the village and properties. The report recommends working with the LLFA and riparian owners to ensure the ditch system can accept highway runoff to reduce flood risk. (p18)
