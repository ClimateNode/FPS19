

# London Borough of Hillingdon flooding December 2013 - March 2014

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: December 2013-June 2014<br>057\_Hillingdon/Update on the Councils and other bodies responses to flooding in the Borough.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### London Borough of Hillingdon
* The borough experienced its largest flood event for a number of years. Flooding in the borough could affect residents and properties across multiple catchments (River Colne, River Crane, River Pinn and Yeading Brooks) and from surface water; evidence of impacts was collected via numerous calls, emails and letters to the Council. (p4)
* The borough experienced flood impacts requiring coordinated response: highways staff attended sites (including nights/weekends), debris was cleared from river structures to reduce blockages, sandbags were delivered to residents, and the Council and Environment Agency visited known hotspots. (p5) [PAG]
* Across the borough approximately 29 residential properties (and associated gardens and some garages) were affected by flooding; a small number of businesses and educational institutions were also reported affected, with one educational institution severely affected internally. Flooding reports came from across the whole borough. (p6)
* The report states the three electricity substations could have issues with flooded access during the event (access may have been compromised by water over the highway/entrances). (p7) [TAB]
* Flooding at Springwell Lane (Harefield) on the River Colne/ordinary watercourse/surface water pathway caused road inundation. Water could not drain because the River Colne was very high; vehicles were shown standing in water (up to wheel level in photos). Access to one business was cut off. The Council Highways team attended and placed signs to close the road. (p19) [TAB]
* {general}: Multiple highway flooding incidents across the borough; a large number of schools experienced localised surface water and groundwater issues. The Council attended sites and cleared gullies, but some flooding remained ongoing in specific streets. (p20)
* Areas across the borough experienced flooding from local sources (surface water and groundwater) and from Main River flooding. Main River flooding is reported in association with the River Pinn and Cannons Brook. The investigation identified significant flood events at 'The Common' and 'Kings College Playing Fields'. (p23)
* Quant: 29 residential properties; 1 educational institution (p6)
* Quant: 3 Electricity Substations (p7) [TAB]
* Quant: 1 business (p19) [TAB]

### River Colne
* {general}: Identified as a catchment within the borough boundary and a source of flooding contributing to flood risk in Hillingdon during the event. (p4)
* High water levels on the River Colne (linked to high River Thames levels in Feb 2014) meant smaller ditches in the catchment could not drain; as a result water spilled onto adjacent land and onto the highway causing access flooding. (p7) [TAB]
* River Colne levels rose quickly and water flowed onto back gardens, causing inundation concerns; residents contacted the Council and sandbags were provided. The Longford Flood Alleviation Scheme (an overflow channel) appeared to function well and likely reduced inundation. (p8) [TAB]
* Very high River Colne levels prevented an ordinary watercourse from discharging. The high river levels contributed to water flowing into a residential garden, onto the highway and into the entrance/internal areas of a College. (p11) [TAB, MUL]
* River Colne was reported very high and is identified as a source of flooding; high river levels prevented road drainage and likely contributed to overland flow into Stockers Lake and along ordinary watercourses leading to inundation of Springwell Lane. (p19) [TAB]
* Quant: 1 property reported concerns; potentially 23 properties on the island could have been affected (p8) [TAB]
* Quant: 1 garden; internal flooding to an Educational Institution (p11) [TAB, MUL]
