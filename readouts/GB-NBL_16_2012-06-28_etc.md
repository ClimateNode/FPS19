

# Northumberland flooding 28 Jun 2012, Northumberland flooding 07 Jul 2012, Northumberland flooding 04 Aug 2012 and Northumberland flooding 25 Sep 2012

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Investigation of the Summer 2012 Floods<br>091\_Northumberland/FIR-summer-2012-Main-Report-FINAL.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Northumberland
* {general}: Numerous flooding instances occurred across Northumberland in 2012. Saturated ground from prolonged and intense rainfall produced runoff into roads, gardens and potentially into dwellings and businesses. Flooding arose from multiple sources including main rivers, ordinary watercourses, highway drains (road gullies and ditches), sewers and groundwater. (p5)
* {28 Jun 2012, 07 Jul 2012, 04 Aug 2012, 25 Sep 2012}: Widespread flooding across the county from multiple sources during the summer of 2012, resulting in properties being flooded and prompting a Section 19 investigation by the Lead Local Flood Authority. (p7) [PAG, MUL]
* {28 Jun 2012}: Widespread surface water and urban flooding across the county on 28 June 2012 due to very high intensity thunderstorms; drains/gulleys/ditches/sewers could not cope so water pooled in urban and rural areas. Heavy rainfall also caused some ordinary watercourses and main rivers to come out of bank, with instances of dwellings flooded. (p8) [PAG]
* {28 Jun 2012}: The 28 June 2012 floods were widespread across the county. Many communities were affected by excessive surface water runoff and some instances of flooding from ordinary watercourses. Worst-hit urban areas included Alnwick and Seaton Delaval. Numerous dwellings across several villages were disrupted and town centres were inundated by several centimetres of water, causing impacts to dwellings and businesses. (p9) [TAB]
* {28 Jun 2012, 25 Sep 2012}: Widespread flooding across the county on 25 September 2012 (and significant flooding reported after the 28 June 2012 event). Prolonged rainfall caused main rivers to rise; central parts of the county were hardest hit. Settlements on the Wansbeck and the Coquet (and their tributaries) were particularly affected. Several roads experienced flooding (from closures to surface ponding). (p16) [MUL]
* {28 Jun 2012, 25 Sep 2012}: Quant: more than 100 areas (p16) [MUL]

### North England
* {general}: 2012 produced the wettest year on record for Northern England with intense downpours and extended rainfall that saturated ground. Resulting runoff and storage on fields and floodplains caused numerous flooding instances, with water running onto roads, gardens and into potentially dwellings and businesses. (p5)
* {28 Jun 2012, 25 Sep 2012}: Widespread surface water flooding across the county during the 28 June 2012 event; later rainfall (including 25 September 2012) caused main rivers to rise and flood dwellings in floodplains. County-scale saturation meant runoff caused additional flooding across many areas. (p23) [MUL]
* {28 Jun 2012, 25 Sep 2012}: Quant: minimum of 187 dwellings; 37 businesses; possibly over 200 dwellings; 40 businesses (p23) [MUL]

### Tyneside
* {28 Jun 2012}: Tyneside locations (notably Whitley Bay) experienced extremely heavy short-duration rainfall on 28 June 2012 that contributed to surface water and urban flooding in the area. (p8)

### Alnwick
* {28 Jun 2012}: Town centre inundated with several centimetres of water; several dwellings and businesses were affected by flooding during the 28 June 2012 event. (p9)
* {07 Jul 2012}: Alnwick was affected by flooding during the event; flooding in the central areas of the county (including Alnwick) was mostly from excessive surface water runoff with some instances from ordinary watercourses, causing disruption to the area and local infrastructure. (p10)
* {general}: Settlement recorded in the report's maps as affected by the summer 2012 floods; flooding was mapped in parts of the town (buildings, streets and property extents were recorded on the GIS). (p21)
* {28 Jun 2012}: Town centre was under several centimetres of water during the intense 28 June 2012 rainfall, caused by surface water unable to enter watercourses, gullies and sewers quickly enough. (p23)

### Seaton Delaval
* {28 Jun 2012}: Town centre inundated with several centimetres of water; several dwellings and businesses were affected by flooding during the 28 June 2012 event. The report links this flooding to very heavy rainfall recorded at nearby Whitley Bay (less than 5 km away). (p9)
* {general}: Recorded in the report as an affected settlement during the summer 2012 floods and included in the GIS mapping of flooding incidents. (p21)
* {28 Jun 2012}: Town centre was under several centimetres of water during the intense 28 June 2012 rainfall, representing significant surface water/urban flooding. (p23)

### Haltwhistle
* {07 Jul 2012, 04 Aug 2012}: Several properties in Haltwhistle experienced flooding during the 7 July 2012 event; intense short-duration rainfall on 4 August 2012 caused additional flooding in areas that had already been affected earlier in 2012, increasing repeat impacts and vulnerability. (p12) [PAG, MUL]
* {04 Aug 2012}: Settlement experienced flooding from intense short‑duration rainfall on 4 August 2012; local roads were affected (closures and surface ponding). (p13) [TAB]
* {general}: Included among larger settlements where flooding occurred during the summer 2012 events; precise numbers of affected properties were difficult to gather. (p23)

### Whitley Bay
* {28 Jun 2012}: During the 28 June 2012 event Whitley Bay received exceptionally intense, short‑duration rainfall that contributed to the widespread surface water flooding across the north‑east. The high intensity precipitation overwhelmed drainage capacity and promoted rapid pooling of water in urban areas. (p8)
* {28 Jun 2012}: Extreme short‑duration rainfall fell at Whitley Bay on 28 June 2012; this intense rainfall is reported as related to the surface water / urban flooding experienced in nearby Seaton Delaval (within ~5 km), contributing to excessive surface water runoff in the area. (p9) [TAB, PAG]

### Berwick-Upon-Tweed

### River Coquet
* {25 Sep 2012}: The River Coquet rose during the prolonged 24–25 September 2012 rainfall event and caused flooding of dwellings within its floodplain. High antecedent saturation produced rapid runoff into the river, contributing to inundation of floodplain properties. (p14)
* {25 Sep 2012}: Rises on the River Coquet and its tributaries during the 25 September 2012 event led to flooding that particularly affected settlements on the Coquet system. (p16)
* {25 Sep 2012}: On 25 September 2012 the Coquet rose sufficiently to cause flooding to some dwellings within its floodplain, exacerbated by saturated catchment conditions. (p23)

### River Wansbeck
* {25 Sep 2012}: The River Wansbeck rose during the prolonged 24–25 September 2012 rainfall event and caused flooding of dwellings located within its floodplain. Saturated catchment conditions meant rainfall ran off directly into the river, increasing flows and flood depths. (p14)
* {25 Sep 2012}: On 25 September 2012 the Wansbeck rose sufficiently to cause flooding to some dwellings within its floodplain, driven by saturated ground and runoff. (p23)

### River Pont
* {25 Sep 2012}: Ponteland experienced surface water flooding during the 25 September 2012 event; although the River Pont was high it did not overtop, and surface water runoff caused the flooding problems in the area. (p16)


## Topics without coordinates

### North East England
* {28 Jun 2012}: The wider North East region was affected by the 28 June 2012 thunderstorms ('Thunder Thursday'/'Toon Monsoon'), producing very high intensity rainfall that led to widespread surface water and urban flooding. (p8)

### East Coast Main Line
* {07 Jul 2012}: A landslide triggered by the heavy rainfall caused closure of the East Coast Main Line railway. (p10)
* {07 Jul 2012}: Quant: one landslide incident; one railway closure (p10)

### Environment Agency
* {28 Jun 2012}: Widespread surface water flooding across the county on 28 June 2012; this event formed the basis for county-wide flood investigation and prioritisation of funding. (p22)
* {28 Jun 2012}: Quant: 55 major flooding incidents across the county (p22)

### Iceland
* {summer 2012}: The street immediately outside the Iceland store was inundated during the flood; vehicles are shown partially submerged up to their windows and people are wading through the water, indicating the store frontage and access were affected by floodwater. (p1) [IMG]

### Northumberland County Council
* {28 Jun 2012, 07 Jul 2012, 04 Aug 2012, 25 Sep 2012}: As the Lead Local Flood Authority, NCC carried out a Section 19 investigation into the widespread flooding across the county following the summer 2012 rainfall events; the council reviewed which risk management authorities had responsibilities and whether those functions were exercised, and published the results and notified relevant authorities. (p7) [MUL]

### Wansbeck Catchment
* {25 Sep 2012}: Prolonged rainfall on 25 September 2012 caused main river levels across the Wansbeck catchment to rise, leading to river flooding of settlements and disruption to roads. (p16)
