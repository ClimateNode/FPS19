

# St Helens Metropolitan District flooding 08 Dec 2021

## Place names with coordinates

### Brown Heath Avenue, Chadwick Green
* Surface water flooding affected parts of the street during the event; EA surface water mapping indicates low-to-medium risk with highest risk to the area to the rear of properties and the eastern side of the street. The mapping indicates surface water can travel between properties. Local response provided sandbags and, where necessary, temporary accommodation to affected property residents. Flooding in this location related to ordinary watercourses rather than main river flooding. (p9)
* Properties on Brownheath Avenue were flooded both internally and externally during the event; local culverts to the rear of the street have been reported historically to overtop and allow floodwaters to reach property boundaries. (p10) [PAG]
* {general}: External flooding of the highway has occurred historically; anecdotal reports that culverts to the rear have overtopped and floodwaters have reached property boundaries. (p11)
* Floodwaters from overtopping watercourses entered the adopted highway on Brownheath Avenue; the highway drainage network could not drain the volume quickly enough, producing roadway depths of about 2–3 feet. Floodwater progressed southwards toward Powell Drive, damaging fences and enabling a wave into adjacent properties; culvert clearance by MFRS allowed drainage to recover. (p13) [PAG]
* Floodwaters flowed onto properties and into the adopted highway; highway drainage could not remove the volume of water causing roadway inundation. Floodwaters reached depths of between 2 to 3 feet in the roadway; fences between Brownheath Avenue and Powell Drive were knocked down by the flow, allowing a wave of water to move onto Powell Drive. Once culverts were cleared the overtopping ceased and drainage networks were able to drain the floodwater. (p14) [PAG]
* Properties on Brownheath Avenue were internally and externally flooded. (p15)
* Quant: 4 properties affected internally and externally; 12 further properties affected externally. (p10) [PAG]
* Quant: water depth between 2 and 3 feet in the roadway (p13) [PAG]
* Quant: 2 to 3 feet deep in the roadway (p14) [PAG]

### Billinge
* Urban floodwater covered the full width of a residential street, reached up to house entrances and gardens, pooled in driveways up to garage doors, and partially submerged a parked car; emergency response activity is visible in images. (p1)
* A flooding incident occurred in Billinge on 8th December 2021 that met the Council's Section 19 investigation threshold; the event posed or could have posed a risk to human health and caused internal flooding to at least one property used for residential or commercial purposes. (p4)
* The EA surface water flood map for Billinge shows areas of low-to-high surface water flood risk concentrated in low-lying areas and along watercourses; residential streets (including Brownheath Avenue and Powell Drive) and some community assets lie near or within risk zones. Flooding reported in the event related to ordinary watercourses rather than main river flooding. The EA Reservoir Risk Map indicates the affected area of Billinge was not at risk from reservoir flooding. St Helens Borough Council provided sandbags and temporary accommodation to affected residents during the response. (p9) [IMG]
* Residential streets in Billinge experienced flooding that inundated properties both internally and externally; the event prompted local response actions and a catchment-wide EA flood alert was issued after the local flooding had occurred. (p10) [PAG]
* Overtopping of open watercourses north of Brownheath Avenue (caused by blocked culvert inlets containing silt, vegetation and fly-tipped material) led to floodwaters travelling south across a public right of way into the Brownheath Avenue adopted highway. Roadway depths reached 2–3 feet, fences between Brownheath Avenue and Powell Drive were knocked down creating a wave that caused internal flooding of properties on Powell Drive. Once MFRS cleared the culverts the watercourse stopped overtopping and highway drainage was able to clear floodwater. (p13)
* Flooding in the Billinge area associated with the event that caused properties to be internally and externally flooded on Brownheath Avenue and Powell Drive. (p15)
* Quant: Brownheath Avenue - 4 properties affected internally and externally, and 12 further properties affected externally; Powell Drive - 4 properties affected internally and externally, and 1 property affected externally. (p10) [PAG]
* Quant: water depth between 2 and 3 feet in the roadway (p13)

### St Helens Metropolitan District
* Residential streets and private properties within the borough (specifically Billinge) were inundated by surface water; public road surfaces were covered by floodwater reaching up to house entrances and garden areas, and private driveways/garages and vehicles were affected. (p1)
* A flooding incident occurred in Billinge (within the borough) that met the threshold for a formal Section 19 investigation. The incident is recorded as occurring on 8 December 2021 and prompted the council investigation. (p5)
* Maps and text show above‑normal rainfall for December 2021 and notably high 12‑month totals for the St Helens area. This Section 19 report concerns a flood event affecting St Helens on 8th December 2021; the borough was impacted by flooding during that event (investigated under this report). (p6) [IMG]
* Surface water flooding from ordinary (non-main) watercourses affected parts of the borough (notably Billinge) on 8th December 2021. St Helens Borough Council (the LLFA) provided sandbags and, where necessary, temporary accommodation to affected residents. The LLFA were not made aware of the flooding until after floodwaters had receded; the event occurred while staff and vehicle resources were already allocated to winter maintenance. (p9) [IMG, PAG]
* The local authority (St Helens Borough Council) responded to the flooding by providing sandbags and offering temporary accommodation to affected residents; the LLFA were not made aware until after floodwaters had receded and the response took place during winter maintenance operations. (p10)

### Chadwick Green
* Acted as the primary flow path for floodwater traveling southwards; floodwater moved along the green space and then dispersed into adjacent residential streets (including Brownheath Avenue), contributing to the inundation of properties and highways. (p14) [IMG]

### Carr Mill Road, St Helens Metropolitan District
* An ordinary watercourse running alongside Carr Mill Road had culvert inlet blockages (debris of vegetation, silt and litter) which caused flooding affecting residents; MFRS used pumps and cleared the culvert inlets, after which water levels receded and flow within the culvert returned to normal. (p12)


## Topics without coordinates

### Merseyside Fire and Rescue Service
* Attended flooding in the afternoon to assist affected residents; used light portable pumps to remove water, located culvert inlets on the ordinary watercourse alongside Carr Mill Road, and cleared debris (natural vegetation, silt and litter) from culvert inlets. After debris removal water levels receded and flow returned to normal. LLFA were made aware only after MFRS left the scene. (p12)

### St Helens Council
* Responded to the flood by providing sandbags and, where necessary, offering temporary accommodation to affected residents; response carried out during winter maintenance when staff and vehicle resources were already allocated. (p10)

### United Utilities
* {general}: The local United Utilities surface water and combined sewer networks connected to a culverted watercourse that acted as a main drainage point for the affected watercourse during the event; UU outfalls discharge into watercourses upstream and downstream of the flooding location and therefore these assets were implicated in the drainage and flood routing during the incident. (p11)
