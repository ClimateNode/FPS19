

# Lowdham flooding 18 Oct 2023 - 20 Oct 2023

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Lowdham – Storm Babet, October 2023<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/lowdham-s19-storm-babet-oct-2023.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Lowdham
* During Storm Babet internal flooding affected residential properties and businesses; additional properties experienced flooding to gardens and outbuildings; roads and properties flooded more quickly because the ground was already saturated. (p2) [PAG]
* Widespread flooding occurred across the village caused by the Cocker Beck overtopping combined with intense surface water runoff from saturated fields. Properties, roads and open spaces were flooded; major local roads (Epperstone By-Pass, Main Street) acted as channels for overland flow. Sewer systems were overwhelmed in places (foul water rising on Ton Lane West/East; sewage coming up through drains in Blenheim). A lagoon filled and later overtopped, contributing to flooding. (p3) [IMG]
* The village experienced significant flooding from saturated catchment runoff feeding Cocker Beck which overtopped and spilled into the settlement; substantial overland flows routed along roads (including Epperstone By-Pass and Main Street) overwhelmed surface water drainage and caused sewer surcharge in parts of the village. (p4) [IMG, PAG]
* {general}: Large parts of the village (particularly south and east) lie within Flood Zone 3 (highest predicted flood risk) following the main watercourse through the village; residential and infrastructure near the river are identified as at greatest risk; the mapped site boundary near the village intersects Flood Zones 2 and 3 indicating significant flood risk to that site. (p5) [IMG, PAG]
* Experienced surface water flooding which transitioned to a continued river-flooding risk; council staff visited impacted communities and vulnerable individuals at their homes, supported evacuations and provided emergency accommodation where needed, cleaned roads and paths, removed bulky waste from flooded homes, offered recovery support and grants, and restocked local aqua sac supplies; a multi-agency community drop-in was held locally. (p9) [PAG]
* Quant: 135 residential properties and businesses (p2) [PAG]

### Newark and Sherwood
* {1999}: Contains the village of Lowdham which has a significant history of river-related flooding; the report records a 1999 event in which 300 properties in Lowdham were flooded and multiple later events (2007, 2012, 2013, 2019, 2020). The Environment Agency has funded a £25m flood alleviation project for the catchment. (p1)
* The district was affected by the flood event; council staff visited impacted communities and vulnerable individuals and prepared to support evacuated residents. (p8)
* {1999}: Quant: 300 properties (p1)

### Nottinghamshire
* Major flood incident affected the county: internal flooding of properties occurred; sandbags were delivered and 77 roads were closed to reduce further flood damage; a Community Flood Recovery Grant was initiated to support those affected. (p8)
* Quant: 77 roads (p8)

### Ton Lane, Lowdham
* Significant road surface water on Ton Lane West and Ton Lane East caused by foul water rising from the Severn Trent sewer system; Ton Lane East required a road closure. (p4)
* Quant: 1 road closure (p4)

### Main Street, Lowdham
* Main Street acted as a conduit for overland surface water flows during the storm, being mentioned as one of the roads that channelled water through the village and was affected by significant surface water/flooding. (p3)

### Southwell Road, Lowdham
* Water was reported flowing towards cottages on Southwell Road (originating via the Magna car park); the road and adjacent properties were within the area affected by internal flooding during the event. (p3)

### Blenheim Avenue, Lowdham
* Sewage water came up through drains in Blenheim after the Cocker Beck had started to recede, indicating sewer surcharge post-peak. (p4)

### Old Epperstone Road, Lowdham
* Isolated flooding occurred on Old Epperstone Road; authorities responded to the incident and continue to consider options to reduce future flood risk to the area. (p8)

### Willow Holt, Lowdham
* {general}: Area has had drainage works (2021 surface water/highway drainage improvements) and is the subject of ongoing action: ownership concerns over parts of the piped drainage system are being pursued by the council and they are working with affected residents who have been impacted by drainage/flooding issues. (p10)

### River Trent
* {general}: Part of the local catchment receiving flows from local becks (including Cocker Beck); identified as a main river relevant to flood risk in Lowdham and subject to catchment-scale flood alleviation measures. (p1)
* Predicted extreme river levels prompted coordinated in-person visits by the council to high-risk communities to reinforce immediate flood preparedness. (p9)

### Dover Beck
* {general}: Dover Beck runs to the north of Lowdham and is classed as a main river in the catchment; it is therefore linked to the flood risk affecting the village. (p1)

### Epperstone by-Pass
* The Epperstone By-Pass acted as a channel for overland flows during the storm, conveying surface water and contributing to the movement of floodwater through the catchment (i.e. road surface flows increased and helped spread flooding). (p3)

### Magna
* Flood water reached the area adjacent to Magna: a wall from the roundabout to the Magna was leaking slightly and water was observed flowing through the nearby car park toward cottages. The site was within the area affected by internal flooding during the event. (p3) [IMG]

### Magna Charta Pub
* Located within the map's highlighted internal-flooding oval; nearby wall leaking and flows through the adjacent car park were reported during the event, indicating the pub and its immediate area were within the flooded zone. (p3) [IMG]

### Cocker Beck
* Cocker Beck was overwhelmed by runoff from saturated upper catchment, overtopped along its route and spilled into surrounding land and village streets, producing significant overland flows that contributed to the village flooding. (p4) [IMG, PAG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Coordinated the emergency response to a major flood incident that had significant impacts across the county: verified internal flooding to properties, provided advice and support to affected residents and businesses, delivered sandbags, led the Section 19 Flood Investigation and chaired/attended Strategic, Tactical and Recovery coordination groups. Closed roads and shared resources with partners during the response. (p8)
* Responded to flooding in Lowdham by visiting impacted communities and vulnerable individuals at their homes; supporting evacuated residents and providing emergency accommodation; warning and informing the public; providing daily briefings to councillors and community leaders; coordinating clean-up of roads and paths and removal of bulky waste from flooded homes; contacting affected individuals to identify humanitarian needs and offer direct support or liaise with other agencies; conducting in-person visits to high-risk communities due to predicted extreme River Trent levels; commencing recovery activities including delivery of grant funding to eligible households; restocking community flood response supplies (aqua sacs); and participating in multi-agency debriefs and investigations. (p9)
* {general}: As Lead Local Flood Authority, the council was involved in response and investigation of flooding from Storm Babet in Lowdham, supporting affected communities, reviewing property flood resilience options, pursuing ownership/resolution of piped drainage issues (Willow Holt) and providing training/support for local flood warden and road-closure volunteers. (p10)
* Quant: 77 roads (p8)

### Cricket Pitch
* {1999, 2000}: Sustained significant flooding in 1999; following this the Environment Agency carried out flood storage works on the cricket pitch in 2000 to reduce flood risk in the catchment. (p2) [MUL]

### Magna Car Park
* Floodwater was observed coming through the Magna car park and flowing towards cottages on Southwell Road; this flow was reported as part of the first flooding in the village on the day of the event. (p3)

### Lowdham Colts Football Club
* Witness statements describe the cricket/football pitch lagoon (associated with the local cricket/football pitches) filling as the Cocker Beck rose; the lagoon completely filled and then overtopped in all directions. This indicates the football/cricket pitch area, used by Lowdham Colts Football Club, was inundated when the lagoon overtopped due to the excessive river/overland flows. (p3) [IMG]

### Severn Trent
* Severn Trent sewers reportedly lacked capacity during the rainfall; foul water rose from their system into Ton Lane and contributed to flooding, and sewage later came up through drains in Blenheim as the beck receded. (p4)

### Old Tannery Dyke
* Flow out of the village was slow to drain down Old Tannery Dyke because a double culvert (after Harrison's garage) is too high and too small, causing water to be slow in getting away and contributing to prolonged local flooding. (p4)

### Environment Agency
* Issued a Flood Alert for the River Trent Tributaries in Nottinghamshire (19/10/2023) and Flood Warnings for the Cocker Beck at Lowdham (20/10/2023); conducted flood patrols, cleared blockages before and after peak flows, commissioned a structural survey of a flood storage wall, organised/attended a multi-agency community drop-in, and coordinated information officers and surveyors to visit affected communities. (p9)
