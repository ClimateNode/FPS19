

# Lowestoft flooding 24 Jul 2015 - 25 Jul 2015

## Place names with coordinates

### Kirkley Stream
* Computer modelling and the 24/25 July 2015 heavy rainfall indicate the stream and local drainage were overwhelmed, causing river and surface water flooding of residential properties, roads and gardens in the lower part of the stream. (p1)
* The stream was partly blocked by debris (natural material and illegally dumped garden/fly‑tipped waste) and areas of excessive vegetation. Coupled with a large volume of rainfall, this caused the stream to overtop its banks and directly flood lower areas, overflowing into roads, gardens and ultimately getting into homes. (p2)
* The stream was partly blocked by debris (including illegally dumped garden and fly‑tipped waste) and excessive vegetation. Combined with high inflows this caused the stream to overtop its banks and flood lower areas, resulting in water entering homes and gardens. Anglian Water had carried out monthly maintenance but there were occasions when more extensive clearance was required. (p3) [PAG]
* The stream (designated main river) was implicated in the event where highway drains could not discharge into it, contributing to road flooding. A culverted section was jetted to restore flows; the Environment Agency has proposed vegetation clearance (and potentially removal of a large tree) and review of the trash screen to reduce blockages and future flood risk. (p4) [PAG]
* Kirkley Stream is associated with recorded flood incidents along its course; a flood storage area is located centrally along the stream and attenuation ponds/structures are shown to manage flood risk. Flood incident records are concentrated on the stream's southern reaches (near Pakefield Park). The stream is mapped as Main River and Non‑Main River with ownership/responsibility delineated. (p8) [IMG]
* Flood incidents are concentrated along the Kirkley Stream corridor, associated with internal property flooding, garden and outbuilding flooding, and road flooding. Incident records and key watercourse infrastructure (bridges/culverts/trash screens, attenuation pond) are annotated along the stream, indicating repeated flood occurrences where the stream passes through Carlton Colville. (p9) [IMG]

### Carlton Colville
* Flooding affected one home and multiple roads and gardens; incidents included contamination with foul sewage and roads reported flooded. (p1) [PAG]
* Homes, roads and gardens were inundated when drainage and sewers were overwhelmed and Kirkley Stream overtopped its banks; foul sewage was present in floodwater. (p2)
* Flooding was predominantly on the roads where highway drains were unable to discharge into the Kirkley Stream, causing road inundation. Local highway maintenance (gully clearing and jetting of a culverted section) was carried out in response; a planned zebra crossing was postponed until drainage issues are resolved. (p4)
* The map shows modelled Flood Zone 3 (surface water flooding, 1 in 100 year) affecting low-lying land and watercourse corridors in and around Carlton Colville. (p7) [IMG]

### Kirkley
* Flooding affected the area between Bloodmoor Roundabout and Kirkley Ham linked to flows in the Kirkley Stream and problems discharging highway drains; prompted reviews of stream maintenance, a full topographical survey and inclusion in wider flood risk management measures. (p4) [PAG]

### Aldwyck Way, Lowestoft
* Homes on Aldwyck Way had main living areas flooded as the Kirkley Stream and local drainage were overwhelmed by heavy rainfall. Garages and gardens were also reported flooded; nearby public highway in the locality was flooded. (p1)
* Properties in Aldwyck Way experienced flooding on the night in July; local flood-protection measures and a fitted warning alarm did not appear to function adequately during the event. (p2)
* Quant: 33 homes (combined across Aldwyck Way and Velda Close); flood depths 300-500mm in main living areas; ~50m of public highway flooded to approximately 450mm (p1)

### Velda Close, Lowestoft
* Homes on Velda Close had main living areas flooded as the Kirkley Stream and local drainage were overwhelmed by heavy rainfall. Garages and gardens were also reported flooded. (p1)

### The Street, Carlton Colville
* Road flooding occurred because highway drains were unable to discharge into the stream; Suffolk County Council highways cleared local gullies and jetted a culverted section from Bell Vue/The Street to No.73 The Street to restore flow. The installation of a nearby zebra crossing was postponed until drainage issues are resolved. (p4)

### Bloodmoor Roundabout
* Located within the area described as affected when the stream overtopped; flooding of lower areas and roads in this corridor occurred as drainage was unable to discharge and water backed up, affecting roads and nearby gardens/homes. (p2)
* Quant: roads reportedly flooded to about 300 mm (p2)

### Lake Lothing
* Tidal state in Lake Lothing prevented gravity drainage of Kirkley Stream into the lake during the event, contributing to a backup of water in the stream and subsequent overtopping/flooding upstream. (p2)

### Kirkley Ham
* Anglian Water's pumping station at Kirkley Ham was inspected on the night of the flooding and was confirmed to be operating correctly and within capacity; it was used to lift water into Lake Lothing but did not fail. (p2)

### Bell Vue Park
* Part of the culverted section of Kirkley Stream from Bell Vue to No.73 The Street was jetted as part of the response to flooding where drains could not discharge, to ensure the system was running freely. (p4)


## Topics without coordinates

### Suffolk County Council
* As a result of the flooding (more than five adjacent properties flooded in Aldwyck Way/Velda Close), Suffolk County Council was required under Section 19 of the Flood and Water Management Act to undertake an investigation into the 24/25 July 2015 event. (p1) [PAG]
* Following the 24 July flooding the council worked with Anglian Water to clear excessive vegetation, remove bulrushes and open access routes into the stream to restore channel flow. The council is responsible for three drainage basins/ponds alongside Tom Crisp Way, which did not overflow during the 24/25 July event and were not a contributing factor to the flooding. (p3) [PAG]
* Highways responded to road flooding in the Carlton Colville area caused by highway drains being unable to discharge into Kirkley Stream. Suffolk County Council highways cleared local gullies and jetted a culverted section of the stream (from Bell Vue, The Street to No.73 The Street) to restore flow, and agreed to postpone a nearby zebra crossing installation until drainage issues are addressed. (p4)
* Quant: 33 homes; more than five adjacent properties (triggering a Section 19 investigation) (p1) [PAG]
* Quant: three drainage basins/ponds (p3) [PAG]

### Anglian Water
* Debris (including illegally dumped garden and fly-tipped waste) and excessive vegetation partially blocked the stream and its screens/culverts, causing the stream to overtop its banks and flood lower areas (homes and gardens). Anglian Water carried out monthly maintenance since 2002 and undertook clearance after the July flood (clearing access routes, removing bulrushes and other growth, and removing dumped material). They are the responsible organisation for the channel from Bloodmoor Roundabout to Kirkley Ham Park and for ensuring blockages are cleared. (p3) [PAG]
* Anglian Water assets (notably a trash screen on the stream) were identified as a factor in blockages that increase flood risk; Anglian Water was asked to review drainage system designs and to consider redesigning the trash screen and suitability for telemetry monitoring to provide warning of blockages. (p4)
* Quant: six large pipes partially blocked (p3) [PAG]

### Bridge
* A culvert/bridge structure with six circular openings (the piped inlet between the stream and the flood storage area) was partially blocked by vegetation and debris, reducing conveyance between the stream and the storage area; this partial blockage was noted in relation to the 25/26 July event though there is no evidence the storage area failed to store floodwater on that night. (p3) [IMG]
* Quant: six circular openings/large pipes partially blocked (p3) [IMG]
