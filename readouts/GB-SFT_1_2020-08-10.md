

# Sefton Metropolitan District flooding 10 Aug 2020 - 11 Aug 2020

## Place names with coordinates

### Liverpool Road North, Maghull
* Liverpool Road North (Maghull) is the subject of the Section 19 flood investigation for the 10/11 August 2020 event; the map identifies this street as a flood investigation site, indicating it experienced flooding or flood-related impacts during that event. (p1) [IMG]
* Intense, fast-moving rainfall on the night of 10 August 2020 caused surface water/flash flooding on Liverpool Road North (Maghull) when drainage systems were temporarily overloaded. (p3)
* {2018, 10 Aug 2020 - 11 Aug 2020, 18 Aug 2020}: Business units at numbers 1–39 Liverpool Road North and residential properties at numbers 187–197 Liverpool Road North experienced internal flooding on the night of 10/11 August 2020. Number 17 Liverpool Road North was reported to have flooded in 2018 and was close to internal flooding again on 18 August 2020. (p4) [MUL]
* Surface-water (urban) flooding affected the Liverpool Road North corridor. The report states the area to the rear of the businesses (canal side) was at high risk, the highway at medium risk, and that the area that suffered flooding is shown on Figure 1. Topography funnels runoff to the lowest point in front of Nos. 15/17; the public surface water sewer route changes direction outside No.29 and surface water enters a combined sewer behind No.25, creating flow paths under the canal that influenced the flooding. (p5) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface water inundated the low-lying strip between Liverpool Road North and the Leeds & Liverpool Canal, flooding the rear (canal-side) of businesses and causing highway flooding at the low point near Nos. 15/17. Surface water entered the public sewer network; behind No.25 the surface water sewer discharges into a combined sewer and overflowed under the canal. (p6) [PAG, IMG]
* Surface water flooding affected properties on Liverpool Road North (addresses 187–197). Floodwater from the local surface-water drainage network (highway drains that become public sewers) overwhelmed the system and discharged towards Maghull Brook at the junction of Southport Road/Liverpool Road North, allowing water to enter properties that lie below the highway/pavement level. (p7) [PAG, IMG]
* Surface water flooding affected properties on Liverpool Road North; flood maps and the investigation identify flood risk starting at No.189 and affecting the terrace in the 187–197 Liverpool Road North area. Flooding occurred at property low points (properties below the level of the highway and pavement). Drainage from this area discharges to Maghull Brook via the surface water sewer network. (p8) [PAG]
* Identified as a flooding location (addresses 1–39 Liverpool Road North). The southern site (1–39) was affected first during the fast-moving storm on the night of 10–11 August 2020, experiencing intense rainfall that led to localized surface water/flash flooding. (p9)
* Surface water flooding occurred on Liverpool Road North due to intense short-duration rainfall combined with constrained drainage and local topography. Blocked or underperforming gullies and drop kerbs allowed water to pond on the highway and flow over pavements into lower-fronting properties, with ponding reported in front of numbers 15/17 and flooding entering properties at 187–201 Liverpool Road North. (p13) [PAG]
* Short, intense storm caused rapid surface water flooding along Liverpool Road North. Water overtopped the pavement at drop kerbs and flowed into the front of properties at 187–201 Liverpool Road North, ponding at the carriageway and entering premises because groundwater/drainage could not evacuate the water quickly enough. Internal flooding caused damage to businesses and residential properties and financial loss to businesses. Highway runoff was increased by several blocked or silted gullies and an ACO drain clogged with debris, reducing drainage capacity. An officer attended the following morning, distributed sandbags and discussed flooding with property owners. (p14) [PAG]
* Addresses 1–39 Liverpool Road North were affected by flooding during the 10–11 August 2020 event. Flooding was associated with blocked/insufficient highway drainage and drains (including ACO drains outside shops); recommended actions include gully and drain inspections from Westway to Red Lion Bridge and Stafford Morton Way, establishing drain ownership, clearing ACO drains, feasibility of raising dropped kerbs and installing additional gullies, and installing a pipe between No.15 and No.17 to discharge water into the canal. (p16) [PAG]
* Surface-water flooding affected properties and shops on Liverpool Road North (address ranges referenced 1–39 and 187–197). Floodwater entered premises and prompted recommendations for property-level resilience (flood sacks, PLP) and drainage works (clear ACO drains; consider installing a pipe between No.15 and No.17 to discharge to the canal). (p17) [PAG]
* {2018, 10 Aug 2020 - 11 Aug 2020, 18 Aug 2020}: Quant: addresses 1–39 Liverpool Road North; addresses 187–197 Liverpool Road North; address 17 Liverpool Road North (flooding in 2018; near-internal flooding 18/08/2020) (p4) [MUL]

### Maghull
* Maghull is within the Section 19 investigation area for the 10/11 August 2020 event; the investigation map shows multiple flood investigation site icons concentrated in and around Maghull, indicating localized flooding was investigated there during the event. (p1) [IMG]
* Maghull experienced intense rainfall on the night of 10 August 2020 that caused flooding at locations across the town (including Liverpool Road North) as drainage systems were temporarily overloaded. (p3)
* On the night of 10/11 August 2020 parts of Maghull experienced localised surface water and urban flooding which overwhelmed the surface water sewer network and caused internal flooding to businesses and residential properties on Liverpool Road North; the event was sufficient to trigger a Section 19 investigation. (p4)
* Overnight convective storm produced intense, short-duration rainfall across Maghull on 10–11 August 2020, causing localized surface water/flash flooding. Two specific locations on Liverpool Road North (addresses 1–39 and 187–197) were identified as flooding locations during the event. (p9) [IMG]
* Intense convective rainfall produced surface water / flash flooding at two locations on Liverpool Road North (1–39 and 187–197). The intense pulse of rain (lasting about 15 minutes) led to those two locations being recorded as locations of flooding during the event on the night of 10–11 August 2020. (p10) [IMG]

Flooding affected residential sections of Liverpool Road North (noted sites 1–39 and 187–197), inundating roads and properties and causing urban drainage to be overwhelmed. (p9) [IMG, PAG]

### Westway, London Borough of Merton
* Westway lies at the upstream edge of the surface-water public sewer catchment described in the report; the area drained between Westway and Red Lion Bridge (including Stafford Morton Way) is identified as the area that suffered flooding, so runoff from the Westway area contributed to the surface-water flooding on Liverpool Road. (p5)
* Gullies and highway drains in the vicinity of Westway were identified for inspection as part of the response to the 10–11 August 2020 flooding (inspections recommended from Westway to Red Lion Bridge and Stafford Morton Way) because of blockages/operational issues contributing to local roadway and property flooding. (p16) [PAG]
* Blocked gullies and highway drainage in the vicinity of Westway were implicated in surface-water runoff and localised highway flooding; Highways were instructed to inspect gullies from Westway to Red Lion Bridge/Stafford Morton Way to assess and remediate drainage. (p17) [PAG]

### Stafford Moreton Way, Maghull
* Stafford Morton Way is included in the area drained by the surface-water public sewer between Westway and Red Lion Bridge; the report identifies that the area drained by this system suffered surface-water flooding that affected Liverpool Road properties. (p5)
* Highway drainage and gully inspections were recommended for the stretch including Stafford Morton Way because surface water flooding and blocked drainage affected the adjacent Liverpool Road North area; feasibility of highway mitigation (raised kerbs, additional gullies) was also recommended. (p16)

## Topics without coordinates

### Hodgson Avenue
* Properties adjacent to Hodgson Avenue were flooded when surface water overwhelmed the local highway drainage and surface water sewer system. The affected properties lie below the level of the highway/pavement; floodwater pathways were linked to the nearby public sewer and a watercourse (Maghull Brook) where surface water sewers discharge. (p8) [PAG, IMG]

### United Utilities
* United Utilities were asked to check sewer and surface-water systems in affected areas of Liverpool Road North after the 10–11 August 2020 event; ACO drains outside shops were identified for clearing and UU were asked to confirm systems were working as they should. (p16)
* United Utilities were asked to check drainage/sewer systems in the areas affected by the 10/11 Aug 2020 flooding to ensure systems were operating and to address causes of the local flooding. (p17) [PAG]

### A59
* Reported location of flooding during the event: intense convective rainfall (over 20 mm in a 15‑minute period) passed over this southern site, producing rapid surface-water inundation of the carriageway, pavements and adjacent properties/plots at the address range identified in the report. (p10) [IMG]

### Sefton Council
* Highway drainage (gullies and roadside drains) in Sefton performed poorly during the 10–11 August 2020 event, with blockages/insufficient capacity leading to roadway and adjacent property flooding. Sefton Highways were tasked to inspect gullies and highway drains (from Westway to Red Lion Bridge and Stafford Morton Way) and to investigate mitigation measures (raised dropped kerbs, additional road gullies). (p16) [PAG]
