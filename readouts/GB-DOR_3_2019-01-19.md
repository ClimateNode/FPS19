

# Portesham flooding 19 Jan 2019

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Portesham 19th January 2019 Event<br>037\_Dorset/037\_2020\_Dorset/January 2019 Portesham Flood Investigation Report.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Portesham
* Portesham stream flows southwards through the village and splits into raised channels; the village is the subject of the Flood Investigation Report and the catchment’s flashy response to storm rainfall is identified as causing flood risk to the settlement. (p6) [IMG, PAG]
* {18 Jul 1955, 1997, 2000, 2002, 2003, 2012, 2013}: The village has a history of flooding caused by heavy rainfall and the local ordinary watercourse. Significant flooding occurred in 2003 and 2012 caused by heavy rainfall leading to surface water, groundwater and fluvial (river) flooding. Additional flood incidents are recorded in 1997, 2000, 2002 and 2013. The catchment is described as having a flashy response to storm rainfall and is spring-fed; groundwater flooding has occurred historically though it was not experienced in the flood event described in this report. (p7) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Overland flows from higher ground converged on the village leading to surface water inundation of streets and properties; local drainage was overwhelmed. (p8) [IMG, PAG]
* Village-wide flooding during the 19 January 2019 event: property and highway flooding occurred during a highly localised afternoon rainfall. Drainage issues were reported (blocked/restricted culverts, blocked highway gullies, and non-operating flow control sluices) which contributed to flood impacts across the village. (p9) [TAB, PAG]
* Surface water from the saturated catchment (hills to the north and from the C112 road) was conveyed downhill into the village, bringing large amounts of silt and gravel from surrounding hills, Rocky Lane and the eroding verge of the C112 road. (p10) [PAG]
* Surface water runoff from surrounding hills and C112 and overtopping of the local stream caused flooding on Front Street in Portesham, surcharging highway drains, depositing silt and gravel, and resulting in at least one property being flooded. (p11) [IMG, PAG]
* Flood-response and mitigation works implemented after the event: culvert under the track to the Dairy Farm cleared and the upstream watercourse desilted; Malthouse Meadows side weir set to the agreed level and formed in brick; obstructions cleared from the swale on the inlet to the attenuation pond; attenuation pond base level corrected (soil removed and now operating at design capacity); a rain gauge was installed by the LLFA to record storm events. (p17) [TAB, PAG]
* The village experienced surface-water runoff and overland flow during the event, causing highway flooding, silt deposition and drainage problems (references to Front Street, Malthouse Meadows and sluice gate operation are made in relation to managing flood risk). (p18) [TAB, PAG]
* Village experienced surface water flooding associated with intense rainfall on 19 January 2019, prompting a Section 19 flood investigation. (p21) [IMG, PAG]
* Flood extents from a 1:100-year surface water event encroached on built-up areas of Portesham, with infrastructure such as roads and parking areas affected. Appendix photos show flooding in the elevated western channel looking north‑east towards Front Street. (p24) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 9 properties (p8) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 9 properties internally flooded (total); includes 6 properties in Goose Hill, 1 in Rocky Lane, 1 in Malthouse Meadows, 1 on Front Street (p9) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: one property (p11) [IMG, PAG]

### Front Street, Portesham
* Front Street experienced surface water flooding when overland flows and out-of-bank flows from Portesham Stream inundated the road; flooding then conveyed onto the B3157 Coast Road causing major traffic disruption and closure. (p8)
* One property experienced internal flooding due to fluvial (river) flooding during the event. (p9) [TAB]
* Surface water was conveyed downhill towards Front Street, carrying and depositing a large amount of silt and gravel from the surrounding hills, Rocky Lane and the eroding verge beside the C112 road. (p10)
* High-velocity surface water and runoff from the surrounding hills and C112 overwhelmed the highway drainage; the highway drain surcharged and flowed along Front Street. A watercourse on the western side overtopped upstream of several culverts and flowed back onto the road. A locked sluice gate prevented additional flows being diverted to the elevated eastern channel. Obstructions within the channel caused one property to flood. (p11) [IMG, PAG]
* Exceedance flows were conveyed onto Front Street; mud and debris contributed to blocking highway gullies, causing surface water to flow/pond on the carriageway. (p13)
* Front Street was subject to flooding risk that prompted actions to formalise a management plan and coordinate bodies to manage drainage and reduce future surface water flooding. (p18) [TAB]
* Floodwater overtopped boundaries and ran from raised garden areas onto the pavement and carriageway, inundating the road outside residential properties; muddy runoff and soil washed over the pavement and across the carriageway. (p25) [PAG]
* Footpath and adjacent carriageway were inundated; water flowing over the sidewalk beside a stone wall with emergency vehicles on the road responding to the flooding. (p26) [PAG]
* Quant: flood depths up to approximately 400 mm on the highway (p8)
* Quant: 1 property (p9) [TAB]
* Quant: one property (p11) [IMG, PAG]

### Rocky Lane, Portesham
* Experienced surface water flooding from overland flows originating on higher ground after heavy rainfall; runoff from the tarmacked track on Rocky Lane was reported by residents as contributing to surface water issues that reached the village. (p8) [PAG]
* Surface water flooding impacted Rocky Lane during the 19 January 2019 event; runoff issues from Rocky Lane were reported and one property experienced internal flooding. (p9) [TAB]
* Erosion of the verge/hills around Rocky Lane mobilised silt and gravel that were conveyed downhill in the flood flows and deposited on Front Street, contributing debris in the flood. (p11)
* Overland flows from surrounding hills were conveyed via a farm track and the Right of Way onto Rocky Lane, bringing significant silt and gravel which blocked highway gullies and increased silt load in the watercourse. A soakaway at the entrance to the RoW could not cope with the volume of surface water. A recently tarmacked section increased surface water run-off velocity and changed the camber, directing flow towards properties on the southern side of the lane; one property experienced internal flooding. (p12) [IMG]
* Surface-water runoff and silt deposition caused overland flow and flooding affecting Rocky Lane; recommended Natural Flood Management interventions to reduce runoff and silt deposition. (p18) [TAB]
* Surface water flooding affected the lane; the soakaway was inspected because flows were unable to enter the system and the surface water drainage of the adjacent tarmacked track requires improvements to pass flows. (p19) [TAB]
* Quant: 1 property internally flooded (p9) [TAB]
* Quant: one property (p12) [IMG]

### Malthouse Meadows, Portesham
* Fluvial flooding caused internal flooding to properties in Malthouse Meadows during the 19 January 2019 event. (p9) [TAB]
* {2012}: The undeveloped field that became the Malthouse Meadows site flooded during the 2012 flood event; a cut through the redundant railway embankment was made during that event to relieve flood risk to the adjacent Village Hall. Post-development drainage (weir, swale, attenuation pond) was implemented to manage exceedance flows and reduce frequency of overspills onto the highway. (p13)
* {2012, 19 Jan 2019}: Silt build-up and downstream obstructions caused the watercourse to come out of bank, resulting in property flooding. During the 19 January 2019 event the railway culvert was operating at approximately 75PCSYM capacity; the upstream weir was lowered to route more flow into the site attenuation pond (which filled more quickly) and this reduced downstream out-of-bank flows. The attenuation pond did not overspill onto the highway on this occasion. The downstream watercourse was de-silted shortly after the event. (p14) [IMG, MUL]
* The drainage scheme at Malthouse Meadows was identified for maintenance and compliance with an agreed management plan due to flood/drainage performance issues following the event. (p18) [TAB]
* Culvert under the railway embankment was discharging muddy, fast-moving water into the channel, indicating surcharged drainage/runoff that contributed to local flooding and stressed the drainage asset. (p26)
* Quant: 1 property internally flooded (p9) [TAB]

### Goose Hill, Portesham
* Internal flooding of residential properties at Goose Hill caused by a combination of surface water runoff and fluvial (river) flooding; highway flooding is also reported in the event narrative for the afternoon rainfall. (p9) [TAB]
* Floodwaters from exceedance flows were conveyed towards Goose Hill during the event, indicating overland/surface water impacts in that area. (p13)
* Surface water and fluvial floodwater flowed through the village and onto the B3157, bypassing the upstream open ditch due to topography and road camber. Floodwater collected against properties and across the road; vehicles travelling on the B3157 generated 'bow-waves' that pushed water above low external thresholds and into properties. (p15) [IMG]
* Overland flows from Goose Hill contributed to flooding downstream; a flood alleviation scheme was proposed to intercept these flows. (p18) [TAB]
* Quant: 6 properties (p9) [TAB]
* Quant: six properties (p15) [IMG]

### B3157 at Portesham
* Flows from Front Street and surrounding lanes conveyed onto the B3157 Coast Road, causing significant traffic disruption and closure of the road for an extended period; highway inundated by floodwater. (p8)
* Mud and debris from the event contributed to blocking highway gullies along the B3157, impairing drainage and leading to surface water issues on the road. (p13)
* Floodwaters flowed onto and across the B3157; vehicles travelling the road produced bow-waves that exacerbated property flooding. Residents reported delays in road closure during the event. (p15)
* Floodwater reached the bottom of Front Street and flowed onto the B3157 Coast Road, causing carriageway inundation and prompting an emergency response. (p26)
* Quant: flood depths of up to approximately 400mm on the highway (p8)

### Winters Lane, Portesham
* Experienced surface water flooding during the heavy rainfall event; reported as receiving overland flows from the surrounding hills which inundated the highway drainage. (p8)
* Mud and debris from the slippage of the farm track, mixed with exceedance flows, resulted in flooding of Winters Lane. Local ditches/culverts were overwhelmed or blocked during the event. (p13)
* Obstructed ditches and culverts caused runoff to flood the carriageway; actions recommended to inspect and clear obstructions and undertake regular maintenance to reduce flooding onto the highway. (p18) [TAB]

### Portesham Hill, Portesham
* Steep ground on Portesham Hill produced rapid surface runoff that conveyed high-velocity and high-volume flows toward Portesham village, contributing to inundation of highway drainage. (p8)
* Surface runoff and silt deposition from the hillside contributed to local flooding; Natural Flood Management measures (e.g. silt traps, cross drains) were recommended to reduce run-off and silt deposition. (p18) [TAB]

### Cemetery Road, Portesham
* Surface water overland flows were conveyed along Cemetery Road and diverted into a highway grip, which led to diversion through a residential garden and external flooding. (p16)

### Winters Close, Portesham
* A portion of surface water was discharged into a culvert along the back of properties in Winters Close as part of the exceedance flow path during the event. (p13)

### New Road, Portesham
* Floodwater from adjoining areas flowed onto the road surface toward New Road, contributing to inundation of the carriageway and affecting access to nearby residences. (p25)


## Topics without coordinates

### Portesham Catchment
* {general}: The catchment has experienced groundwater flooding in the past (historical groundwater flooding of the catchment is reported). (p6) [IMG]

### Portesham Stream
* Portions of overland flow entered the stream but restrictions in the watercourse caused flows to come out of bank and return onto Front Street, exacerbating street flooding. (p8)
* {2012}: Portesham Stream conveys flows around the Malthouse Meadows site and discharged under the redundant railway embankment; its exceedance flows have historically contributed to flooding of the adjacent field (2012) and informed the design of site drainage (weir, swale, attenuation pond). (p13)

### Dorset Council Highways
* DC Highways reported that overland flows from surrounding hills were conveyed along Cemetery Road and diverted into a highway grip, which routed surface water through a residential garden causing external flooding. DC Highways have since relocated the cut-through to divert surface water away from properties and towards the watercourse. (p15) [PAG]
* DC Highways/LLFA were tasked to explore a flood alleviation scheme to intercept flows at Goose Hill, and to work with the Environment Agency on funding/implementation following the flooding event. (p18) [TAB]

### C112
* Surface water runoff from the hills and from the C112 was conveyed downhill towards Front Street, bringing a large amount of silt and gravel and material from an eroding verge at the side of the C112 into the village. (p10) [PAG]

### Portesham Farm
* Extreme rainfall produced overland flows that flooded external farm buildings. The farm track slipped and eroded, producing mud and debris that mixed with exceedance flows and contributed to downstream road flooding. (p13) [IMG]
