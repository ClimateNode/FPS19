

# Old Amersham flooding 15 Jun 2016

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: Old Amersham, Amersham 15th June 2016<br>018\_Buckinghamshire County/018\_2016\_Buckinghamshire County/s19-amersham-investigation-final-april-2018.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Old Amersham
* High-intensity, localised rainfall led to rapid surface-water runoff on urban surfaces, resulting in flooding of homes and businesses; the event had very little warning and was of short duration during business hours. (p4)
* Internal flooding occurred such that the incident met the council’s Section 19 thresholds: multiple residential properties (including basements) were internally flooded; at least one property experienced internal flooding for one week or longer; business premises were internally flooded; one or more items of critical infrastructure were flooded. (p5)
* {general}: Old Amersham (High Street / Amersham Old Town) experienced repeated flooding: several High Street properties flooded in winter 2000/01 (including basement flooding and flooding to the rear of St Mary's Church), at least one property flooded internally again in winter 2014, and flows of river water ran down the High Street (west to east). In Jan–Feb 2014 prolonged rainfall and exceptionally high groundwater raised river levels; a culvert blockage caused the River Misbourne to back up and blockage of a drainage asset caused a secondary surface-water flow down the High Street. A riverbank collapse also caused property flooding. (p10) [IMG]
* {January–February 2014, January-February 2014}: A documented flood event in January–February 2014 affected Old Amersham: high groundwater and raised river levels, plus a culvert blockage caused the River Misbourne to back up, a blocked drainage asset generated a secondary flow down the High Street, and a riverbank collapse caused property flooding. These impacts are described in a Section 19 Flood Investigation report. (p11)
* Quant: 9 residential properties; 20 commercial properties (internally flooded) (Old Amersham and Amersham) (p4)
* Quant: five or more residential properties; 1km2; one property for one week or longer; two or more business premises; one or more items of critical infrastructure (p5)
* {general}: Quant: at least 1 property; 3 occasions (p10) [IMG]

### River Misbourne
* {March 2014}: The Misbourne (River Misbourne) exhibited a recorded high-water level of 0.46 m in March 2014 (highest recorded). (p8)
* {winter 2000/1, 2003, January 2007, March 2013, August 2013, January-February 2014}: The River Misbourne backed up due to a culvert blockage and raised levels (combined with high groundwater), causing river water to flow down the High Street (west-to-east), a riverbank collapse that led to property flooding, garden flooding for a household backing onto the river, and a secondary flow down the High Street caused by a blocked drainage asset. Historic incidents linked to the river include multiple property and basement floods in winter 2000/1, further flooding in 2003, flooding incidents in Shortway and Grimsdells Lane in January 2007, garden floods in 2013, and the January–February 2014 event when culvert blockage and high groundwater raised river levels and caused property flooding. (p10) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* {winter 2000/1, 2003, January 2007, March 2013, August 2013, January-February 2014}: Quant: one property flooded internally in 2014; two businesses (reported their barriers protected premises in 2014); three garden flooding occasions prior to 2014 (including March 2013 and August 2013) (p10) [PAG, IMG, MUL]

### Amersham
* {winter 2000/2001, 2003, March 2013, August 2013, January–February 2014}: Old Amersham experienced a significant flood event in January–February 2014 after prolonged rainfall and exceptionally high groundwater raised river levels. The River Misbourne backed up due to a culvert blockage and a blocked drainage asset produced secondary flows down the High Street. A riverbank collapse during the same event caused property flooding. The area also experienced earlier recorded flooding (winter 2000/01, 2003, 2013). (p10) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* The 15 June 2016 event produced extremely localised, intense rainfall with a HYRAD cell centred over Amersham (one pocket estimated at 38–41.5 mm in 24 hours). This localized intense precipitation is associated with the 15 June 2016 flood event and is likely to have produced localised surface-water/flash flooding and urban flooding in the Amersham area (report text links the rainfall cell to the 15 June flood event). The text does not give granular property-level impacts for Amersham on 15 June, so this attribution is made with some uncertainty. (p11) [PAG]

### High Street, Old Amersham
* {winter 2000/2001, 2003, March 2013, August 2013, January–February 2014}: Multiple properties along the High Street experienced flooding in several historical events. In winter 2000/01 basement flooding and flooding to properties was reported; at least one property flooded internally again in winter 2014. River water flowed down the High Street from west to east during the March 2013, August 2013 and January–February 2014 events. During January–February 2014 a culvert blockage caused the River Misbourne to back up and a blocked drainage asset caused a secondary flow down the High Street; a riverbank collapse during that event also caused property flooding. Two businesses reported that flood barriers installed after 2000/01 protected their premises in 2014. (p10) [PAG, MUL]
* {March 2013, August 2013, January–February 2014, January-February 2014}: Flows of river water were reported to run down the High Street from west to east during the March 2013 and August 2013 events and during the January–February 2014 event. In the 2014 event a blocked drainage asset caused a secondary flow down the High Street; the street therefore experienced both river-driven flows and secondary surface-water flows. (p11) [PAG, MUL]
* {winter 2000/2001, 2003, March 2013, August 2013, January–February 2014}: Quant: at least one property; two businesses; three occasions (garden flooded) (p10) [PAG, MUL]
