

# Stapenhill flooding 16 Feb 2020

## Place names with coordinates

### Mead Crescent, Stapenhill
* {01 Feb 2020 - 29 Feb 2020}: Street-level flooding covered the visible street area, pooling around vehicles and up to the edge of driveways; parked cars were partially submerged and multiple residential properties/vehicles were affected. (p1) [IMG]
* {2020}: Named in the report title as a location of the 2020 flooding and is the subject of the Section 19 flood investigation. (p3) [TAB]
* Internal property flooding was reported at this location following the storms. (p6)
* Flooding affected Mead Crescent during the Storm Dennis event; the Section 19 investigation summarises the extent of reported flooding and the most likely cause. (p7) [PAG]
* Mead Crescent experienced a flood event on 16th February 2020 associated with heavy, persistent rainfall from Storm Dennis. (p16)
* Residential properties in the Mead Crescent development (housing association-managed) were affected by flooding; the location plan in the report marks the flood at Mead Crescent (green dot). (p19) [PAG, IMG]
* {general}: Identified in the report as having a history of flooding; properties along Mead Crescent were the reason for construction of a bund between the drainage ditch and the properties to prevent water flowing toward them. The outlet from the field drainage (Pipe A) discharges into the Severn Trent Water surface water sewer situated in Mead Crescent (approx. 38 m to the south). (p21) [PAG]
* {01 Feb 2019 - 28 Feb 2019}: Mead Crescent has experienced significant flooding on at least three previous occasions. Photographs from February 2019 show external flooding to the highway. (p22) [PAG]
* Multiple properties along Mead Crescent (the Crescent) reported flooding during the 16 February 2020 event. (p23) [PAG]
* Surface water from nearby fields flowed to the rear of properties then entered homes from both front and rear. Internal flooding damaged furniture, carpets and flooring across ground floors; gardens, driveways and the highway were also flooded. Flood water reached up to front doors and covered the highway. The extent of damage led to residents being evacuated by the Housing Association. Residents expressed concern that floodwater may have been contaminated (unverified). (p24) [PAG]
* Flooding affected properties and the highway; property owners were required to vacate their homes and move into temporary accommodation while repairs were completed. (p25) [PAG]
* Flooding affected properties and the highway at Mead Crescent; incidents recorded as occurring on multiple occasions and specifically investigated following flooding on 16th February 2020. (p26) [IMG]
* Significant volumes of surface water flowed over already-saturated ground and caused flooding on Mead Crescent on 16/02/2020. The Housing Association reported local factors exacerbated the flooding, producing greater impacts than expected for the storm magnitude. (p27)
* Water flowed from the adjacent field into the rear drainage ditch which became overwhelmed; part of the bund between the ditch and houses was washed away and floodwater entered properties via a gap between properties, resulting in internal flooding to properties along Mead Crescent. (p28) [IMG]
* Surface water runoff followed local topography toward Pipe A; the outlet pipe became hydraulically overwhelmed and the drainage ditch filled and overtopped, flowing through rear gardens into properties. An informal soil bund (~0.6m high) provided some delay but was eroded and water infiltrated into gardens; installed property flood gates proved ineffective and properties were inundated from the rear garden side. (p33) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface water runoff from the adjacent field overwhelmed the rear ditch, overtopped the bund and flowed towards properties on Mead Crescent causing flooding to the street and rear of properties. (p34)
* Properties experienced internal flooding; gardens, driveways and the highway were flooded; some residents were evacuated. (p38)
* {01 Feb 2019 - 28 Feb 2019}: Quant: 3 previous occasions (p22) [PAG]
* Quant: 16 residential properties experienced internal flooding (across Mead Crescent and Manor Crescent) (p24) [PAG]
* Quant: temporary accommodation for approximately 3 to 4 months (p25) [PAG]
* Quant: 16 residential properties (total across Mead Crescent and Manor Crescent) (p38)

### East Staffordshire
* Widespread flooding from Storm Dennis led to impacts across the district, prompting grant-support applications and multiple internal property floodings. (p7)
* Widespread localised flooding across the borough triggered a multiagency response; officers received a significant number of emergency calls and moved from response into recovery on 18 February 2020. The borough submitted grant applications and administered property resilience grants to affected properties. (p25)

### Staffordshire
* Storm Dennis caused widespread flooding to highways and properties across Staffordshire. (p7)
* Had exceptionally high rainfall through the period and, during Storm Dennis (15–16 February 2020), experienced heavy persistent rainfall and very high river flows that caused flooding in the county (including the Stapenhill area). (p16)
* A frontal band associated with Storm Dennis swept into Staffordshire by mid‑afternoon on 14 February; a large rainfall front developed on 15 February and sustained/persistent rainfall remained over Staffordshire through to early afternoon on 16 February 2020. (p18)

### Mead Walk, Stapenhill
* Map and site-observed flow routes show overland flow entering from the north and passing through Mead Walk toward Manor Crescent during the February 2020 event. It is not explicitly reported whether properties on Mead Walk suffered internal flooding. (p28) [IMG]
* Properties at Mead Walk are located on the line of a former watercourse; surface water runoff from the adjacent field flowed into the surface water sewer / culvert system during the 16th February 2020 event and likely contributed to local surface water flooding affecting the Mead Walk area. (p32)

### Stanton Road, Stapenhill
* Historical mapping indicates the former watercourse likely connected to a drain situated to the south of Stanton Road; during the 16th February 2020 event that drain received runoff from the field and was implicated in local surface water flooding. (p32)

### Housing Association
* Residential properties owned and managed by the Housing Association (the Mead Crescent / Manor Crescent development) were affected by flooding. (p19) [PAG, IMG]
* The Housing Association reported records and photographs of the event, evacuated residents from affected properties, provided images of flooding on Mead Crescent, and logged damage claims. Their records indicate internal flooding and damage to tenant properties and belongings. (p24)
* Reported that several property owners at Mead Crescent and Manor Crescent had to vacate their properties and move into temporary accommodation until repairs were completed (temporary accommodation lasting approximately three to four months). (p25)
* A representative from the Housing Association provided an overview of the flooding that occurred at Mead Crescent during the February 16th event; site discussions and evidence gathered with the Housing Association indicated other factors exacerbated the flooding impacts at Mead Crescent. (p27) [PAG]
* Representative reported overland flow from the field filled and overwhelmed the rear drainage ditch; part of the bund between the ditch and houses was washed away and floodwater followed topography between gaps in properties, causing internal flooding to the Housing Association's managed properties on Mead Crescent and Manor Crescent. (p28)
* Following the flood event, the Housing Association carried out regular maintenance of the drainage ditch and culverts to keep them free of debris and structurally sound. This includes CCTV surveys of the surface water system from the field and inspection/clearing of the channel and culvert prior to heavy rainfall. (p35) [TAB]

### Severn Trent
* Received discharge from field drainage pipes (Pipe A and Pipe B). Pipe B conveys surface water southerly for ~70 m through a private garden before connecting into a 300 mm diameter Severn Trent Water surface water sewer that runs along Mead Walk; the surface water sewer in Mead Crescent receives the outlet from Pipe A (approx. 38 m to the south). (p21)
* Site investigations were undertaken to determine the connectivity between the drainage ditch and the downstream STW surface water sewer. STW is to be liaised with to confirm ownership of the drainage outlet pipe and downstream surface water network and appropriate future maintenance responsibilities. (p35) [TAB]
* Identified as a responsible maintenance authority to work with the LLFA and other partners to investigate and help reduce flood risk to the affected properties. (p38)
* Quant: 300 mm diameter sewer; approx. 70 m length from Pipe B through private garden; approx. 38 m from Pipe A to sewer (p21)

### Staffordshire County Council
* Received reports of flooding at Mead Crescent and Manor Crescent and acted in its role as Lead Local Flood Authority to coordinate and work with partners to reduce flood risk and support the affected community. (p38)
* Quant: 16 residential properties (p38)

### Culvert
* Described as the low spot / culvert inlet where a drainage ditch intercepting surface water runoff from the agricultural field to the north discharges; the ditch directs runoff toward this culvert inlet and into an outlet pipe (Pipe A) which conveys the water southerly and discharges into the Severn Trent surface water sewer in Mead Crescent. The culvert/pipe forms part of the flow path linked to the flood location. (p20) [IMG]
* The outlet/culvert piping from the field is described as likely having insufficient capacity and possible blockage; the ditch/culvert became overwhelmed which contributed to overtopping of the bund and subsequent flooding to nearby streets. (p34)

### East Staffordshire Borough Council
* East Staffordshire Borough Council coordinated parts of the response (received many calls, mobilised the Town Hall as a potential rest centre, chaired multiagency coordination) and administered grant support schemes for affected residential and business properties. (p25)
* Quant: more than 130 applications; approximately 86 properties; £370,840 (p25)
