

# Staines-Upon-Thames flooding 28 Jan 2021 - 03 Feb 2021

Reference document:<br>Guildford Street Section 19 Report Location: Guildford Street, Staines, Spelthorne<br>126\_Surrey County/126\_2021\_Surrey County/Guildford-Street-S19-Report\_v2.0.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Spelthorne
* Surface water pooled at a low point on Guildford Street (between 14 and 24 Guildford Street) during Storm Christoph; limited drainage on the road contributed to pooling. Thames Water investigation found surface water entered the foul sewer network via pipe joins and a hole in a lateral from one property. (p3)
* {winter 2013/14, 04 Feb 2021}: Large parts of Spelthorne (including Staines) experienced groundwater rise and resultant surface water ponding during prolonged/winter rainfall, leading to flooding in low-lying areas such as Guildford Street. (p4) [MUL]
* Large parts of Spelthorne (including Staines) sit on River Terrace Deposits so when the River Thames was high, groundwater rose to the same level and lower-lying land experienced groundwater coming to the surface. In this event rising groundwater filled the soakaway on Guildford Street, causing surface water to flow overland and pool, and groundwater ingress into the foul sewer plus a hole in a lateral at 21 Guildford Street led to foul contamination of flood water. (p5) [PAG]
* Quant: addresses 14–24 Guildford Street; one property lateral hole (p3)
* Quant: 1 hole in a foul sewer lateral (near the manhole in the front garden of 21 Guildford Street) (p5) [PAG]

### Staines-Upon-Thames
* Flooding occurred in the Guildford Street area of Staines during Storm Christoph, with road pooling at the identified low point and impacts to adjacent residential properties and drainage infrastructure. (p3)
* {04 Feb 2021, 03 Mar 2021}: Guildford Street in Staines experienced surface water ponding at a low point on the highway, contaminated with foul sewage. Floodwater persisted for approximately four weeks and required pumping; rising groundwater (linked to nearby River Thames levels) reduced soakaway capacity and prevented drainage, causing surface water to pool. (p4) [MUL]
* {general}: The Guildford Street area of Staines is shown to lie within mapped surface water flood risk extents; clusters of risk are concentrated near streets, property boundaries and open spaces, indicating parts of the town (Guildford Street and adjacent properties) are susceptible to surface water flooding in varying return periods. (p7) [IMG]

### Guildford Street, Knowle Green
* A low point on Guildford Street (between nos. 14 and 24) pooled with floodwater during the event; limited drainage (only gullies and a soakaway) meant most of the road was susceptible to flooding and photos from residents show water pooling at that location. Surface water also entered the foul sewer system in the area (investigation evidence). (p3)
* {04 Feb 2021, 03 Mar 2021}: Surface water ponded in the centre of the highway on Guildford Street, stretching to the entrance to the allotments to the east. The pooled water was contaminated with foul sewage (residents were unable to flush toilets, indicating ingress to the foul sewer). Flood water remained in situ for about four weeks; Thames Water pumped contaminated water and provided temporary toilet facilities while pumping operations continued until floodwaters receded. (p4) [PAG, MUL]
* Rising groundwater (linked to high River Thames levels) filled the highway soakaway capacity so surface water falling on the highway could not drain, flowed overland and pooled at a low point. Groundwater also entered the foul sewer through joints/cracks and a hole in a foul sewer lateral near the manhole in the front garden of 21 Guildford Street, causing foul contamination of floodwater and causing the foul sewer to exceed capacity. The lateral has since been lined/repaired. (p5) [PAG]
* {general}: Surface water flood risk extents are mapped along Guildford Street and adjacent properties. Areas near intersections, property boundaries and open spaces are shown at risk, indicating potential inundation of the carriageway, frontages and neighbouring properties during surface water flood events. (p7) [IMG]

### River Thames
* {04 Feb 2021}: High River Thames levels were hydraulically linked to elevated local groundwater; this contributed to groundwater emergence and surface water ponding that caused flooding in low-lying areas, including Guildford Street in Staines, and reduced the effectiveness of the local soakaway. (p4)


## Topics without coordinates

### Thames Water
* Thames Water's investigation found it likely that surface water entered the foul sewer via joins in the sewer pipe and a hole in a lateral from one property on Guildford Street, indicating sewer-system ingress related to the flood event. (p3)
* {04 Feb 2021 - 03 Mar 2021}: Thames Water investigated the foul sewer and found likely ingress of surface water into the foul system through pipe joins and a hole in a lateral from a property on Guildford Street. Thames Water pumped contaminated pooling water from the area and provided toilet facilities for affected residents; pumping operations remained in place until floodwater receded. (p4) [PAG]
* Foul sewer experienced groundwater ingress because groundwater levels were higher than the sewer invert. A hole in a foul sewer lateral near the manhole in the front garden of 21 Guildford Street allowed additional clean groundwater into the foul sewer, exceeding its capacity and contributing to foul contamination of the flood water. (p5)
* Quant: 1 lateral from 1 property (p3)
* Quant: 1 hole in a foul sewer lateral (near the manhole in the front garden of 21 Guildford Street) (p5)

### Surrey County Council
* {04 Feb 2021, 03 Mar 2021}: The highway at the centre of Guildford Street (reported to Surrey County Council on 4 February 2021) flooded with foul-contaminated water which remained for approximately four weeks. As part of recovery actions, the council had responsibility for resurfacing the road (work confirmed as done in the following financial year). (p4) [MUL]
* High groundwater filled the highway soakaway on Guildford Street, reducing its storage capacity so surface water falling on the highway could not drain. Surface water therefore flowed overland and pooled at the low point of the road. (p5)

### SCC Highway
* The highway drainage (including soakaway) on Guildford Street was affected by rising groundwater which filled the soakaway and reduced its storage capacity, causing surface water to surcharge and pool at the low point. Recommendations include surveying highway drainage, monitoring surcharge frequency, and recording survey results with the Local Highway Office. (p5)
