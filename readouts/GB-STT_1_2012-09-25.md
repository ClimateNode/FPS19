## Place names with coordinates

### Lustrum Beck
* Communities along Lustrum Beck were among the most severely affected by flooding during the event. (p8)
* Lustrum Beck is identified as a main river in the borough and as part of the network of watercourses contributing to fluvial/urban flooding during the 25–26 September 2012 event. (p11)
* Identified as the receiving watercourse (the tributary is of Lustrum Beck) related to flooding that caused garden/highway flooding on Grays Road and external property flooding on Grays Road and Grange Avenue. (p15)
* {01 Mar 1979 - 31 Mar 1979, 01 Nov 2000 - 30 Nov 2000}: Overtopping of Lustrum Beck caused flooding of nearby properties, specifically inundation of 1, 3, 5 and 7 Burnside Grove and 126, 128 and 130 Oxbridge Lane. (p16) [MUL]
* {29 Mar 1979, 01 Nov 2000 - 30 Nov 2000}: Historic significant floods from Lustrum Beck include the worst flood on 29 March 1979 which caused substantial flooding of land, residential and commercial properties. A severe event in November 2000 flooded properties and community buildings in the downstream area (including Darlington Road, Burnside Grove, Oxbridge Lane, Bishopton Road, Wrensfield Road, the Adult Training Centre, Newtown Methodist Church, Kingdom Hall and Wrensfield School). (p18) [MUL]
* {general}: Flood risk at Lustrum Beck was the subject of proposed defence works that could not proceed after 2003 because the scheme did not qualify for full Flood Defence Grant in Aid (FDGiA). After Partnership Funding was introduced in 2011 some FDGiA might be available for a wider scheme, but the large 'six fields' scheme continued to attract only low FDGiA and was not considered economically viable; discussions on smaller/affordable measures and re-costing were ongoing. (p19)
* Levels in Lustrum Beck rose and overtopped flood embankments, causing much of the widespread flooding across the Newtown and Wrensfield areas and contributing to internal flooding of residential properties, inundation of streets and gardens, and flooding of community facilities. (p28)
* The ordinary watercourse could not discharge into Lustrum Beck (tide-locked), causing backwater that led to garden flooding on nearby streets; Lustrum Beck's inability to accept flow contributed to regular/severe garden flooding near the culvert. (p29)
* Fluvial flooding from Lustrum Beck was the main cause of flooding in the Hartburn area, leading to internal property flooding and inundation of adjacent areas. (p30)
* Referenced as being connected to earlier flooding incidents in the locality; other flood incidents in the area were linked to high flows associated with Lustrum Beck. (p32)
* Lustrum Beck overtopped its banks (fluvial flooding) and caused surrounding inundation: it completely surrounded the two lowest buildings adjacent to the beck, flooded the gardens of properties on Dundas Street and flooded the allotments. (p34) [PAG]
* Overtopping of Lustrum Beck caused fluvial flooding to the rear of Portrack Retail Park, leading to inundation of retail units and car parking. (p35)
* {01 Mar 1979 - 31 Mar 1979, 01 Nov 2000 - 30 Nov 2000}: Quant: 7 properties (p16) [MUL]
* Quant: 2 buildings (p34) [PAG]
* Quant: 3 retail units (p35)

### Browns Bridge
* Described in the report as one of the six locations where flooding was severe; subject of the LLFA investigation into the Sep 2012 event. (p8)
* Community around Browns Bridge was reported as one of the worst affected areas during the 25–26 September 2012 event, experiencing major flooding with internal flooding of properties and associated road closures in the locality. (p11) [PAG]
* {01 Mar 1979 - 31 Mar 1979, 01 Nov 2000 - 30 Nov 2000}: The deck of Browns Bridge was identified as being approximately 170mm below the top level of the existing flood defence, allowing floodwater to spill across Bishopton Road and contributing to overtopping risk during past flood events. (p16) [MUL]
* {general}: Area around Browns Bridge was affected by flooding associated with Lustrum Beck (historic floods referenced in the report). (p18)
* {general}: The Browns Bridge area was identified as high risk with a 1 in 20 year standard of protection; £150k was initially allocated for a small scheme and the Environment Agency consulted residents, but those works were not progressed when Partnership Funding and a wider scheme were pursued. A proposed flood wall funded from the £150k would only have provided a 1 in 30 year standard of protection and would have been exceeded, though it would have afforded some protection against lesser events. The council requested the EA re-consider delivering a scheme with the original £150k. (p19)
* The highway drainage outfall under Browns Bridge became full and floodwater overflowed onto Bishopton Road; the overflow led to closure of the road and contributed to wider overtopping of flood embankments that caused extensive flooding in Newtown/Wrensfield. (p28) [IMG]
* Surface water flooding affected the Browns Bridge area; the report recommends a full investigation of surface water flooding issues, consideration of flood alleviation schemes and assessment of existing local schemes (e.g. alleygates) for potential incorporation of flood risk management; residents are advised to consider property protection measures. (p37)
* Surface water flooding in the Browns Bridge / Newtown area; EA to progress de-silting works to locate the NWL outlet and investigate a localised flood alleviation scheme. (p40) [TAB]
* {01 Mar 1979 - 31 Mar 1979, 01 Nov 2000 - 30 Nov 2000}: Quant: 170mm below top level; existing flood defences were approximately 100mm from overtopping (November 2000) (p16) [MUL]
* {general}: Quant: 1 in 20 year standard of protection; £150k; 1 in 30 year standard of protection (p19)
* Quant: 1 property (94 Bishopton Road) threatened (p28) [IMG]

### A139/A19 Roundabout
* {25 Sep 2012 - 26 Sep 2012, 26 Nov 2012}: The A139/A19 roundabout and the A19 exit slip were flooded when the drainage system surcharged and could not discharge into Billingham Beck because of high river levels. The gully on the roundabout surcharged and formed a growing puddle until the carriageway became impassable. High Billingham Beck levels also overtopped onto adjacent floodplain land (affecting allotments and Billingham Beck Country Park) and caused ordinary watercourses to become tide-locked, preventing discharge and producing severe flooding across the drainage network. Associated impacts included internal flooding of properties in the Mill Meadow Court and Chesham Road areas and severe flooding of gardens at the lower end of Costain Grove (approx. 2 m depth) that threatened properties (none known to have internal flooding). The highway here flooded again on 26 Nov 2012, though no properties were affected on that later occasion. (p32) [IMG, MUL]
