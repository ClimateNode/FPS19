

# Sutton-On-Trent flooding 02 Jun 2018

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Sutton On Trent – 2 June 2018<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/suttonontrentsection19flooding.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Old Great North Road, Sutton-On-Trent
* Internal flooding affected properties and businesses along Old Great North Road. Investigations attribute the flooding to overtopping of the Bulham Lane Drain at culverted sections; TVIDB has commissioned further hydraulic assessment of the drain. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Water from the Bulham Lane Drain (BLD) overtopped and flowed out onto Old Great North Road and surrounding areas; natural topography directed the water towards properties and businesses producing significant internal flooding. (p3) [PAG]
* Water from the overtopped Bulham Lane Drain flowed onto Old Great North Road and surrounding areas; local topography directed the water toward properties and businesses, resulting in significant internal and external flooding (photos referenced showing internal flooding, external flooding and devastation). (p4) [IMG, PAG]
* Floodwater flowed onto and along the road due to overflow from adjacent areas and topography, directing water toward properties and businesses and causing significant internal flooding of nearby premises. (p5) [IMG, PAG]
* Floodwater accumulated in the hammerhead at the bottom of Old Great North Road because a nearby highway underpass (under the B1164) had been blocked and was being used for storage. The blocked underpass prevented floodwater from escaping, increasing flood depths in the hammerhead. (p9) [IMG]
* Quant: 32 properties and businesses (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 32 properties and businesses (p3) [PAG]

### Sutton-On-Trent
* Significant flooding of properties and businesses in the village. (p1) [PAG]
* The Sutton on Trent area experienced both internal and external flooding: internal flooding concentrated near Weston Mill Pottery and Old Great North Road, and external flooding near commercial/community locations (e.g. Lincolnshire Co-op Food Store, Palmer Road). (p2) [IMG]
* A large highway underpass at the bottom of the Old Great North Road hammerhead had been blocked off and used as storage, which prevented flood water from escaping and thereby contributed to flooding in the area. (p8)
* Quant: 32 properties and businesses (p1) [PAG]
* Quant: 32 properties and businesses (p2) [IMG]

## Topics without coordinates

### Bulham Lane Drain
* The Bulham Lane Drain overtopped at multiple locations (including culverted sections), causing internal flooding of properties and businesses in the Old Great North Road area. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* The Bulham Lane Drain overtopped in a number of locations (linked to culverted sections), releasing water onto adjacent roads and areas that led to internal flooding of properties and businesses. (p3) [PAG]
* The Bulham Lane Drain overtopped at multiple culverted sections (examples shown under Old Great North Road, downstream of the railway line and under Grassthorpe Road), causing water to flow out of the drain and onto the Old Great North Road and surrounding areas. (p4) [IMG, PAG]
* Trent Valley Internal Drainage Board carried out weed and debris clearance from the BLD immediately after the flood event and ensured their assets (weed grids and pumps) were operating efficiently during the event. (p8)
* A culverted section of the BLD downstream is being investigated by TVIDB for its legalities and to determine whether it contributed to the flood event; this investigation aims to establish if the culvert influenced local drainage and flood flows during the 2 June rainfall event. (p9)
* Quant: 32 properties and businesses (p3) [IMG]

### Weston Mill Pottery
* Internal flooding occurred in the area adjacent to Weston Mill Pottery as part of the event; flooding is associated with the Bulham Lane Drain overtopping (culverted sections) which caused internal property flooding in this north‑west cluster. (p2) [IMG]
* Quant: part of 32 properties and businesses that experienced significant internal flooding (p2) [IMG]

### Nottinghamshire Fire and Rescue Service
* Provided life‑saving and emergency flood response: water rescue, search and rescue, high‑volume pumping of floodwater, salvage works and assistance to police in recovery of casualties. (p7)

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Led the Section 19 flood investigation and coordinated emergency planning and community engagement after the flood; deployed highways response team to investigate and address highway drainage issues arising from the flood. (p7)
* {02 Jun 2018, 02 Jun 2026}: NCC Highways carried out investigations into the effectiveness of highway drainage systems and carried out remedial works where necessary following the rainfall event of 2nd June. As Lead Local Flood Authority (LLFA) NCC is offering to work with the community to support creation of a Flood Action Group to help manage and reduce future flood risk. (p10)

### Highway Underpass
* The underpass (originally installed as a cattle creep and leased by NCC) had been blocked and was being used as a storage facility. This blockage prevented floodwater passing through the underpass, impeding conveyance and contributing to deeper flood depths in the adjacent hammerhead. (p9) [IMG]

### Marshalls Coaches
* Both external yard and interior of the depot were inundated (water at least knee-deep in visible areas), with staff shown wading through water; depot operations and vehicles/yard areas were directly affected by flooding. (p5)

### Highway Authority
* Public highways and highway drainage assets were affected by surface water flooding; a highways response team was provided to investigate highway drainage issues following the event. (p7)

### Severn Trent
* The flooding was described as primarily surface water and from a watercourse; Severn Trent had no significant involvement in the immediate response but actively engaged in the S.19 Flood Investigation and followed up on issues raised at the drop-in session. (p7)

### East Midlands Ambulance Service
* Worked in conjunction with Nottinghamshire Fire and Rescue Service and police to save life during the flood event. (p7)

### B1164
* A highway underpass beneath the B1164 had been blocked off and used as a storage facility; this prevented flood water from escaping during the event and likely increased flood depths at the Old Great North Road hammerhead. It is noted that had the underpass been left open flood depths in this event would likely have been shallower, though an open culvert might have allowed water entry from the other side of the road. (p9) [IMG]
