

# Weedon Bec flooding 21 Nov 2012

## Place names with coordinates

### Weedon Bec
* Church Street in the village was the subject of a Northamptonshire County Council flood investigation report, indicating that flooding occurred on Church Street and affected the village. (p2)
* Intense rainfall on a saturated catchment caused excess surface water to flow following natural contours to low points in the village, resulting in internal flooding of properties in Weedon Bec during the 21 November 2012 event; at least one property flooded internally and similar internal flooding has occurred several times in the previous five years. (p5)
* Intense rainfall on a saturated catchment caused excess surface water to flow over ground to low points in the village. On 21 November 2012 at least one property on Church Street flooded internally; properties in the village have experienced internal flooding on multiple occasions in the five years prior to the investigation. (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* Significant rainfall and runoff across the Weedon Bec catchment on 21 November 2012 produced surface water flooding and ponding within the village (notably at the junction of New Street, Bridge Street and Church Street), leading to at least one property flooding internally and several other properties being at high risk. (p8)
* Ponding at the junction of Bridge Street, Church Street, New Street and West Street caused water to breach the threshold of a commercial property on Bridge Street and to reach very close to the thresholds of at least two other properties; water depth in Bridge Street was approximately 300mm. (p13)
* Intense rainfall on a saturated catchment on 21st November 2012 overwhelmed existing drainage systems and watercourses in Weedon Bec. Runoff from higher land flowed onto the public highway and into properties; drainage systems and watercourses were in poor maintenance with silt and debris, reducing their ability to convey the deluge. (p16)
* Runoff from higher land flowed onto the public highway where there was no adequate drainage, allowing water to flow to private properties. (p17)
* {01 Jul 2012 - 31 Jul 2012}: Areas of the village experienced street flooding (notably Church Street), causing road inundation, disruption to vehicle access and exposure of building bases to floodwater. (p37) [IMG]
* {general}: Historic photographs record flooding that affected the town. (p41)
* Village streets and residential areas were flooded, causing disruption to the community — residents observed and waded in floodwater and used a cart to move household items to safety; roads were made impassable by the inundation. (p45) [IMG]
* The town experienced urban/ surface-water flooding (illustrated by Church Street), with streets inundated and properties at ground-floor level exposed to floodwater, restricting access and affecting daily activities. (p47) [IMG]
* Quant: 1 property (p5)
* Quant: 1 property (at least) flooded internally; incidents occurred over a five-year period (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* Quant: at least one property flooded internally (p8)
* Quant: one commercial property; at least two other properties; approximately 300mm depth (p13)

### Church Street, Weedon Bec
* During the event on 21 November 2012 surface water flowing overland reached low points on Church Street and caused internal flooding of properties. At least one property experienced internal flooding during this incident; the street has experienced internal flooding incidents multiple times in the previous five years. (p5)
* Runoff from higher ground ponded at the junction of New Street, Bridge Street and Church Street, allowing floodwater to cross building thresholds. At least one property on Church Street flooded internally during the event. (p8)
* {21 Nov 2012, 27 Jul 2013}: Highway at the Church Street/Bridge Street/West Street junction flooded; sandbags were deployed to protect properties. (p10) [TAB, MUL]
* Surface water from fields and Farthingstone Road/New Street flowed to the junction of Bridge Street, Church Street and New Street during the event. (p11)
* Ponding was reported at the junction of Bridge Street, Church Street, New Street and West Street; the junction flooding affected Church Street area and brought floodwater close to property thresholds. (p12)
* {01 Jul 2012 - 31 Jul 2012}: Standing floodwater covered most of the road surface, reached the base of buildings and partially submerged parked cars, impeding traffic and access; emergency responders were on scene assessing/responding to the incident. (p37) [IMG]
* Floodwater covered the entire road surface and reached the base of doors and building thresholds, impeding access to shops and houses and causing inundation of ground-floor entrances. (p47) [IMG]
* Quant: 1 property (p5)
* Quant: at least one property flooded internally (p8)

### Bridge Street, Weedon Bec
* Ponding occurred at the junction of New Street, Bridge Street and Church Street on 21 November 2012; this ponding was associated with at least one property being affected by flood water beyond the building threshold and other properties being at high risk. (p8)
* {21 Nov 2012, 27 Jul 2013}: Highway at the Church Street/Bridge Street/West Street junction flooded; sandbags were deployed to protect properties. (p10) [TAB, MUL]
* Surface water from Farthingstone Road and New Street reached the junction of Bridge Street, Church Street and New Street during the event. (p11)
* Ponding at the junction breached the threshold of a commercial property on Bridge Street; floodwater on Bridge Street reached an approximate depth of 300 mm. (p12)
* Quant: at least one property (p8)
* Quant: 300mm depth; 1 commercial property threshold breached (p12)

### West Street, Weedon Bec
* Runoff from high ground flowed down West Street during the event on 21 November 2012, contributing to surface water reaching the low point at the junction of New Street, Bridge Street and Church Street and placing nearby properties at high risk of flooding. (p8)
* {27 Jul 2013}: Highway flooding occurred at the Church Street / Bridge Street / West Street junction; sandbags were provided to protect properties during the recorded incident on 27/07/2013 (linked to the 2012 surface-water source). (p10) [TAB]
* Surface water reached West Street from Croft Way and was joined there by runoff from Ordnance Road, the school grounds and the recreation ground at the Community Centre. (p11)
* Ponding approximately 75 m in length occurred adjacent to a residential property and Weedon Bec Primary School. The ponding was formed by overland flow from Croft Way and Ordnance Road and by runoff from the school; highway gullies were blocked and did not allow the water to drain away. (p12)
* Quant: 75 m; 1 school; 1 residential property (p12)

### Northamptonshire
* The village of Weedon Bec (within Northamptonshire) experienced a flood incident on Church Street on 21 November 2012 that resulted in internal flooding of at least one property; the village has had repeated internal flooding incidents over the last five years. (p7)
* Quant: one property (p7)

### Lower Weedon
* Runoff from high ground flowed down Farthingstone Road, New Street, West Street and Croft Way and ponded at the junction of New Street, Bridge Street and Church Street, resulting in at least one property being flooded internally and several other properties being at high risk of flooding. (p8)
* {general}: Residents have recorded numerous previous flood incidents in the village (records from 1992). The village contains low points and historic/silted drainage routes (to the River Nene) that have been implicated in past flooding of the settlement. (p9) [PAG]
* Surface water/runoff flooding on 21st November 2012: the main surface water drainage systems in Lower Weedon had limited records of route, condition and outfall, and existing drainage was unable to cope with the intense runoff, allowing water to flow onto roads and toward properties. (p16)
* Quant: at least one property (p8)

### Upper Weedon
* Surface water/runoff flooding on 21st November 2012: the report notes limited records of the main surface water drainage systems in Upper Weedon and indicates drainage systems were unable to cope with intense runoff, contributing to water reaching highways and properties. (p16)

### New Street, Weedon Bec
* Runoff from higher ground flowed down New Street on 21 November 2012 and ponded at the junction with Bridge Street and Church Street, contributing to surface water flooding that led to at least one property flooding internally and left other properties at high risk. (p8)
* Surface water flowed north down New Street from Farthingstone Road; the drainage system in New Street was reported to have failed and a residential property on New Street experienced flooding (resident later excavated a 'moat' to reduce future risk). (p11) [PAG]
* Surface water overtopped fields on the east side of Farthingstone Road and New Street and flowed down New Street towards a residential property. The drainage system in New Street was reported to have insufficient capacity and failed, resulting in overland flow. The affected resident excavated a 'moat' around the property to reduce future flood risk. (p12) [PAG]
* Quant: at least one property flooded internally (p8)

### Farthingstone Road, Weedon Bec
* Runoff from higher ground flowed down Farthingstone Road on 21 November 2012, contributing to ponding at the junction of New Street, Bridge Street and Church Street and the resultant surface water flooding in the village. (p8)
* {2010, 21 Nov 2012}: Residential property on Farthingstone Road experienced repeated surface water flooding (recorded from 2010 through to Nov 2012). (p10) [TAB, MUL]
* High-volume, high-velocity surface water overtopped fields on the east side of Farthingstone Road at multiple breach points along field boundaries and flowed over Farthingstone Road toward New Street. (p11)

### Croft Way, Weedon Bec
* Runoff from higher ground flowed down Croft Way on 21 November 2012, contributing to ponding at the junction of New Street, Bridge Street and Church Street. (p8)
* Surface water flowed north down Croft Way onto West Street as part of the overland flow during the event. (p11)

### Queen Street, Weedon Bec
* Ponding adjacent to a residential property reached approximately 3 m from the property threshold. Vehicles driving through the pond caused a wash that reached the threshold. Residents could not leave the property without wading through water approximately 300 mm deep. (p12)
* Quant: 1 property; 3 m; 300 mm (p12)

### Ordnance Road, Weedon Bec
* Surface water from Ordnance Road joined flows on Croft Way/West Street during the event. (p11)

### River Nene

* {general}: The report notes that works to control flows in the River Nene have reduced the frequency of historical fluvial flooding that previously affected Weedon. For the 21st November 2012 event the source was runoff rather than fluvial flooding from the river. (p16)
* {general}: Historically caused fluvial flooding that affected Weedon; subsequent flow-control works have reduced the frequency of those fluvial floods. (p17) [PAG]

### Weedon Bec Primary School
* Runoff from the school's grounds joined surface water overland flows which ran north down Croft Way onto West Street during the event, contributing to the flood flow. (p11)
* Overland flow from surrounding roads (including flow from the school) contributed to a c.75 m long area of ponding on West Street adjacent to the school; highway gullies were blocked so the water could not drain away. (p12)


## Topics without coordinates

### Weedon Bec Catchment
* Significant rainfall fell on the Weedon Bec catchment on 21 November 2012; resulting runoff from high ground flowed down local roads and contributed to ponding at the village junction, producing surface water flooding that led to at least one property being flooded internally. (p8)
* Significant rainfall on 21st November 2012 produced saturated ground across the Weedon Bec catchment and generated surface water overland flows that led to flooding incidents in the village. (p11)
* Quant: at least one property flooded internally (p8)

### Northamptonshire Fire and Rescue Service
* Attended internal flooding at at least one property during the November 2012 surface-water flooding event. (p10) [TAB]
* Quant: at least one property (p10) [TAB]

### Daventry District Council
* Acted as the local planning and enforcement authority in response to flooding impacts: consulted with the Environment Agency on planning applications to reduce flood risk and committed to use enforcement powers under Section 25 of the Land Drainage Act 1991 where riparian owners failed to maintain watercourses contributing to flooding. (p18)

### Weedon Hill
* Reported source of a large volume and high-velocity surface water flow that crossed field boundaries and travelled north toward Farthingstone Road and New Street. (p11)

### Community Centre
* Runoff from the recreation ground at the Community Centre on West Street joined surface water flows down Croft Way/West Street during the event, contributing to the flood flow. (p11)

### Highway Authority
* Highway drainage assets and the public highway experienced surface water flooding and overtopping due to insufficient capacity of piped drains/culverts and blocked highway drainage, allowing runoff through the village and increasing flood risk to properties. (p18)

### Anglian Water
* Surface water, springs or land drainage entering the public sewerage system increased flows and may have reduced sewer capacity or contributed to local flooding; the report recommends Anglian Water assess and consider disconnecting inappropriate surface water connections. (p18)

### Environment Agency
* Worked with the LLFA and NCC Emergency Planning to support community flood resilience: to instate, train and liaise with a Flood Warden and to provide advice and guidance to residents and local authorities regarding surface water and watercourse-related flooding and planning to reduce flood risk. (p18)
