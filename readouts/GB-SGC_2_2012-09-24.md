

# Filton flooding 24 Sep 2012

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation (Section 19 Report) NHS Blood & Transplant Centre (24th September 2012)<br>114\_South Gloucestershire/Section-19-Flood-report-Filton-v7.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### NHS Blood and Transplant Filton
* Subject of a Section 19 flood investigation following a flood event. (p1)
* Internal flooding of the NHS Blood and Transplant centre — all blood donations were removed and the site was closed until it had dried and been cleaned. The centre's recovery programme returned it to full operation two weeks after the event. (p2)
* Internal flooding of the NHS Blood and Transplant Centre (critical infrastructure) occurred, triggering the Section 19 flood investigation. (p7)
* Evacuated the site of people and blood stock during the flood incident; were involved throughout the incident and produced post-event reviews focused on emergency procedures. (p8) [PAG]
* Flooding inundated the NHS Blood and Transplant Filton site on 24th September 2012. The site surface water drainage network was overwhelmed (due to downstream constraints in the culverted watercourse and attenuation pond threatening to overspill). Blood stocks were moved to other sites and the centre was non-operational during recovery. (p12)
* The NHS Blood organisation's Filton facility was flooded on 24th September 2012, triggering the organisation's emergency response (blood relocated to other sites) and a temporary operational outage until recovery within two weeks. (p13)

### Filton
* Location of the NHS Blood and Transplant centre that suffered internal flooding caused by failure/blockage of culverts on the Stoke Brook watercourse; the centre was closed and donations removed while it was dried and cleaned. (p2)
* Flooding affected Filton, including impacts to critical infrastructure located there (the NHS Blood and Transplant Centre). (p7)
* The local drainage network and Stoke Brook catchment were implicated in a local flood investigation for an event affecting Filton; high water in the Stoke Brook/tributary and failures in culverted sections within the railway/landfill area contributed to flooding problems in the area around the North Bristol Business Park. (p9) [IMG]
* Flooding occurred at the NHS Blood and Transplant Filton site on 24 September 2012, causing internal flooding at the site. The NHS emergency response moved blood to other sites and the Filton site was fully operational again within two weeks. (p13)
* Very localised high rainfall in and around Filton on 24th September 2012 produced surface water flooding conditions that led to the rapid inundation of the NHS Blood and Transplant centre when downstream culverts could not convey the flow. (p20)

### Stoke Brook
* Catastrophic failure/blockage of culverts conveying the Stoke Brook caused water to collect upstream and led to rapid flooding of the NHS centre. Culverts were previously deformed and had emergency remedial measures in place (pumps) that were insufficient. (p2)
* Stoke Brook (the main river / local watercourse referenced) caused or contributed to flooding that affected Filton and the NHS Blood and Transplant Centre. (p7)
* {01 Jan 2011 - 31 Jan 2011, 24 Sep 2012}: The tributary to Stoke Brook runs north of the site, takes drainage from a wider catchment (including Filton Airfield) and is culverted through Network Rail land; high upstream water levels and culvert issues were associated with flooding. The culverted channel and its condition (deformation/fracture of the culvert) contributed to the watercourse behaviour during flood events. (p9) [IMG, MUL]
* Levels on Stoke Brook rose overnight (23–24 September 2012), triggering telemetry alarms. The gauge upstream of the Filton culverts recorded a peak level of 2.8m at 10:00 on 24th September 2012. The raised levels, exacerbated by culvert blockage, contributed to upstream flooding (peak flood level estimated around 50.6–50.7mAOD). (p13)
* Stoke Brook is identified as the watercourse where fluvial flooding occurred and which concentrated flood risk locally. The brook and its culvert were implicated in the flood event; remedial reinforcement works on the culvert and measures (over‑pumping) were implemented and flood zoning boundaries around the brook were reduced after improvement works. (p16) [PAG, IMG]

### Stoke Gifford
* {01 Jan 2011 - 31 Jan 2011}: Experienced unexpected high water levels upstream of the culvert in January 2011; these high water levels prompted an investigation and are associated with deformation and fracturing of the culvert on Network Rail land. (p9) [IMG]
* On 24 September 2012 floodwater ponded upstream of partially blocked railway culverts in the Stoke Gifford area, causing the NHS blood donation centre to be flooded: water entered the building at 09:00, the majority of the ground floor was flooded within 20 minutes, flood depth of 150mm was recorded inside, and the building lost power within an hour; the site was closed, donations removed, and the centre was returned to full operation after about two weeks following drying and cleaning. (p15) [PAG, IMG]


## Topics without coordinates

### Network Rail
* Culverts on Network Rail land conveying the Stoke Brook catastrophically failed/blocked, reducing conveyance capacity and contributing to flooding of the NHS centre; emergency pumps were deployed but could not convey the flood water; subsequent works (large new box culverts) were delivered on Network Rail land to mitigate future risk. (p2) [PAG]
* Network Rail-owned land was flooded during the event; large new box culverts were constructed throughout Network Rail land as a delivered solution to the flooding problem. (p3)
* {01 Jan 2011 - 31 Jan 2011}: Following unexpected high water levels upstream in January 2011, Network Rail investigated a culvert on its land and found the 1.8 m diameter Armco culvert to be deformed and fractured in a number of locations. (p9) [IMG]
* {01 Jan 2011 - 31 Jan 2011, 2011, 01 Feb 2012 - 29 Feb 2012, 01 Apr 2012 - 30 Apr 2012, 24 Sep 2012}: Sections of the culverted watercourse for which Network Rail had responsibility were deformed, reducing conveyance capacity and increasing upstream flood risk. This constraint contributed to the site drainage being overwhelmed; Network Rail commissioned surveys, participated in emergency planning meetings, and installed overpumping provision (with maintenance support) to convey flood flows beyond the deformed culvert. (p12) [MUL]
* Pumps being operated by Network Rail contractors were unable to cope with the volume of floodwater ponded upstream of the culverts. At least one pump had to be turned off because its generator was surrounded by floodwater, reducing pumping capacity and contributing to increased upstream water levels. (p14)
* Partial blockage and deformation of Network Rail-owned Armco culverts caused ponding upstream of the railway; pumps being operated by Network Rail contractors were unable to cope (at least one pump was turned off because its generator was surrounded by floodwater), allowing upstream levels to rise sufficiently to contribute to flooding of the nearby NHS blood donation centre. (p15) [IMG]
* Downstream culverts on Network Rail land were known to be in poor condition and in danger of collapse. Temporary remedial works (including over-pumping) undertaken by Network Rail prior to the event were insufficient to cope with the high localised rainfall on 24th September 2012, and the culvert condition contributed to the flooding risk affecting the nearby NHS Blood and Transplant centre. Design work for long-term replacement of the culverts was ongoing after the event. (p19) [PAG]
* Downstream culverts on Network Rail land were in poor condition and unable to convey the event flow; they were in danger of collapse and emergency remedial works had been undertaken prior to the event. The inadequacy/obstruction of these culverts contributed to upstream floodwater collecting and flooding the NHS centre. Work to replace the culverts with large new box culverts on Network Rail land was delivered after the event. (p20)
* Network Rail-owned land was the location for the culvert works: large new box culverts were constructed across Network Rail land as the implemented solution to the flooding; Network Rail is also recommended to undertake regular inspections of the culverts. (p21) [PAG]

### National Health Service
* Internal flooding of the NHS Blood and Transplant centre at Filton caused removal of all blood donations and temporary closure of the site until it had dried and been cleaned; site reopened after recovery works. (p2) [PAG]
* The NHS centre flooded during the event; post‑event drainage works mean it is now very unlikely the centre would flood if the same rainfall recurred. (p3)
* Flooding occurred at the NHS Blood and Transplant Filton site on 24th September 2012. The site drainage network was overwhelmed (run-off from roofwater and car parks) because of downstream constraints in the culverted watercourse; blood stock was moved to other sites under the emergency response plan and the site was fully operational again within two weeks. (p12) [PAG]
* Floodwater entered the NHS building on 24 September 2012; within 20 minutes the majority of the ground floor was flooded and within an hour all power was lost to the building. Flooding of 150mm was reported inside the building. All blood donations were removed and the site was closed until it had dried and been cleaned; the centre was made fully operational approximately two weeks later. Floodwater was ponded and effectively stationary, held upstream of partially blocked culverts. (p14)
* Quant: 150 mm flood depth; 1 site closure; 2 weeks until fully operational (p14)

### South Gloucestershire Council
* The council worked with partners after the flood event to deliver mitigation works, including construction of large box culverts to address the flooding. (p3)
* Acted in its roles around the flood event: as Lead Local Flood Authority it investigated and recorded details of the flood, maintained a register of relevant structures/features, consented/regarded works on ordinary watercourses, and (as Highway Authority/landowner) was responsible for maintenance of highway drainage systems, gullies, ditches, culverts and other council-owned drainage assets relevant to managing the flood. (p17)
* {2011}: Worked with Network Rail and the Environment Agency to determine a long-term solution for damaged downstream culverts identified in inspections; partook in coordination around temporary remedial works and over-pumping to reduce further culvert damage linked to flooding. (p18) [PAG]
* {general}: The Council were aware of the potential issue with the culvert prior to the 24th September 2012 flood and worked with the landowner (Network Rail) to address the issue. The Council chose collaborative engagement rather than enforcement. The Council continued to work with partners after the event to deliver a solution to the flooding problem. (p19)
* Following the 24 September 2012 flood the Council implemented recommendations related to culverts: regular inspections of trash/safety screens at culvert inlets and outlets, placing culverts on the Council's High Risk Register, reviewing planning applications for impacts on peak flows, and considering installation of a flood level gauge to provide reassurance against future flooding. (p21)

### Armco Culvert
* {01 Jan 2011 - 31 Jan 2011, 24 Sep 2012}: The Armco culvert constructed under the landfill (the culvert conveying the watercourse through Network Rail land) was investigated after unexpected high upstream water levels and was found to be deformed and fractured, indicating structural failure related to flood conditions. (p9) [PAG, MUL]
* {01 Jan 2011 - 31 Jan 2011}: The 1.8 m diameter Armco culvert beneath the landfill was deformed and fractured in multiple locations following unexpected high water levels. Remedial works (internal reinforcement) and provision for over-pumping were put in place to prevent further deformation and blockage. (p10) [IMG, PAG]
* {2011, 01 Feb 2012 - 29 Feb 2012, 01 Apr 2012 - 30 Apr 2012, 24 Sep 2012}: Sections of the Armco culvert were deformed, reducing the culvert cross-sectional area and obstructing flow, which increased upstream flood risk and contributed to the site drainage being overwhelmed; overpumping was required to convey flows past the obstruction until replacement. (p12) [MUL]
* The original 1.8m diameter Armco culverts were partially blocked (photos and modelling assessed blockage), which caused ponding upstream and was critical to the flooding that affected the centre. Following the event the Armco culverts were replaced by new box culverts in the Network Rail-owned land to reduce flood risk. (p14)

### NHS Blood and Transport
* The blood donation centre operated by NHSBT was flooded on 24 September 2012; blood donations were removed and the site was closed until dried and cleaned. NHSBT's operations at the site were suspended and later restored after about two weeks following recovery work. (p14)
* The NHS Blood and Transplant centre was flooded on 24th September 2012 due to very localised high rainfall; temporary remedial works on downstream culverts were insufficient to prevent flooding to the centre. Subsequent remedial and design works by the relevant parties aimed to prevent recurrence of flooding to the centre from a similar rainfall event. (p19)
* Rapid flooding of the NHS Blood and Transplant centre when floodwater collected upstream of downstream culverts on Network Rail land. Pumps deployed onsite were insufficient to convey the floodwater, resulting in removal of all blood donations and closure of the site until it had dried and been cleaned; the centre was fully operational two weeks later. (p20) [PAG]
* Quant: 150 mm flood depth; 1 site closure; 2 weeks until full operation (p14)

### Environment Agency
* Acted as a Category One responder and had a core role in responding to the flood event; provided strategic overview and coordination for flooding from main rivers and tidal areas during the incident in Filton. (p18) [PAG]
* {general}: Worked with Network Rail and South Gloucestershire Council to determine the preferred long-term solution for replacing the downstream culverts that were implicated in the flooding problem; involved in post-event planning and delivery of a solution. (p19)

### Somerset Fire and Rescue Service
* Responded to the flood incident at the site, operating and providing pumps as part of immediate flood response. (p8)

### Birse Rail
* {01 Apr 2012 - 30 Apr 2012}: Provided maintenance support to operate the overpumping protection system (installed April 2012) to convey flood flow beyond a deformed culvert obstruction, helping to manage flood flows affecting the NHS Blood and Transplant Filton site. (p12)

### Hilton Bridge
* Flooding associated with the adjacent watercourse (Stoke Brook) affected the area around Hilton Bridge. Improvement and remedial works (including culvert reinforcement and measures to prevent deformation/blockage and provision for over‑pumping) were undertaken and the revised flood zoning shows a reduction in flood-prone area around the bridge. (p16) [PAG, IMG]

### Highway Authority
* Responsible for ensuring local highways were drained of surface water and for maintaining highway drainage systems during the flood event (operation/maintenance of gullies, culverts and related drainage to manage floodwater on roads). (p17)

### Staffordshire County Council
* Acted as a Category 1 Responder for the 24th September 2012 flood: developed and executed emergency/contingency planning, investigated and recorded the event, regulated and consented works on ordinary watercourses, and maintained highway drainage systems and council-owned assets (open spaces, ditches, gullies, trash screens, culverts) that have roles in managing surface water during the response and recovery. (p17)
