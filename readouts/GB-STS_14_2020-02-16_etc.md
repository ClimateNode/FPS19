### Six Ashes Road, Bobbington
* {16 Feb 2020}: Sustained heavy rainfall caused surface water to flow down Six Ashes Road. A small watercourse to the rear of the properties breached its banks and a blocked culvert inlet was noted as a potential factor. Several properties reported internal flooding and surrounding highways and gardens were flooded; local drainage infrastructure was unable to cope with the volume of water. (p29)
* {15 Feb 2020}: A significant flow event occurred on the watercourse at/adjacent to Six Ashes Road during Storm Dennis, with observed flows exceeding the local 1-in-2-year design and approximating the 1-in-5-year design, indicating elevated flood levels and risk at this location. (p43)
* {15 Feb 2020}: Quant: peak observed flow 0.64 m3/s; 1-in-2-year design flow 0.46 m3/s; 1-in-5-year design flow ~0.65 m3/s; estimated return period between 1-in-2 and 1-in-5 years (20PCSYM–50PCSYM annual probability) (p43)

### Staffordshire County Council
* {15 Feb 2020 - 16 Feb 2020, 01 Jun 2020 - 30 Jun 2020, 01 Aug 2020 - 31 Aug 2020}: As the Lead Local Flood Authority, SCC received a high number of calls reporting property, garden and highway flooding during the 2020 events, coordinated response with other RMAs, issued and processed flood surveys, and carried out site visits and detailed investigations where at least one property had internal flooding. (p13) [MUL]
* {15 Feb 2020 - 16 Feb 2020}: Identified as responsible RMA (Highways and LLFA) to manage highway runoff and gully infrastructure following flooding sourced from main river (fluvial), surface water runoff and highway runoff; instructed to ensure gully drainage infrastructure is free and functioning and to liaise with EA on catchment dynamics and mitigation. (p28)
* {15 Feb 2020 - 16 Feb 2020}: As Lead Local Flood Authority, SCC recorded and analysed incidents of flooding from surface water ponding on highways (which then spilled into properties), river overtopping where culverts caused constrictions, and interaction with sewer outfalls; SCC performed hydrological analysis (e.g. for Malthouses – Great Saredon) and proposed actions in response to the flooding. (p38)
* {15 Feb 2020 - 16 Feb 2020, 01 Jun 2020 - 30 Jun 2020, 01 Aug 2020 - 31 Aug 2020}: Quant: at least one property; three events (February, June and August 2020) (p13) [MUL]

### Environment Agency
* {15 Feb 2020 - 16 Feb 2020}: During the storm (15–16 February 2020) rainwater was directed down Saredon Lane, bypassing drainage and flowing through an access gate into rear gardens; water pooled outside properties and the highway was barely passable. Floodwaters were also unable to return to the Saredon Brook (Main River) due to debris in neighbouring drainage ditches, exacerbating the highway flooding. (p25) [TAB]
* {15th and 16th February 2020}: To be contacted to assess the location, advise on catchment dynamics and recommend appropriate assessment and mitigation following flooding attributed in part to Main River (fluvial) and surface water runoff. (p28)

### Severn Trent
* {15th and 16th February 2020}: Sewer network capacity was overwhelmed during the storm, causing sewage/wastewater to issue out of sewer manholes and highway gullies and contributing to external and internal property flooding and highway flooding. (p38)

### New Peace
* {16 Feb 2020}: Drainage along the north-western boundary of New Peace field (the extension of Letchmere Lane) conveyed flows south-westwards, contributing to runoff and flooding of Moor Lane and nearby properties. The landowner later installed a temporary ditch and hay-bale barrier to help hold back future flood flows. (p22) [PAG]

### Culvert
