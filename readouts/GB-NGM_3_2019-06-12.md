* Properties on Athorpe Grove were flooded during and after the heavy rainfall event; flooding occurred at about 20:30 on 12/06/2019, caused by high runoff and elevated water levels in the nearby Day Brook. (p9) [PAG]
* Properties on Athorpe Grove experienced internal flooding from both the rear and the front during and after heavy rainfall on 12th June 2019. High water levels in the Day Brook overtopped and damaged the rear boundary wall, creating a flow route into rear gardens. Water then travelled through an alleyway to the front of properties where it pooled at the lowest point. Low door thresholds allowed water ingress. Road gullies were unable to drain standing water (later found to be blocked) so floodwater remained above some property thresholds until gullies were cleared. (p10) [PAG]
* Day Brook overtopped a rear wall to the properties, the floodwater damaged the wall allowing more water to pass. Water continued to overtop at the rear, flowed through an alley to the front low point where it pooled, and entered properties through low door thresholds causing internal flooding from both rear and front. Road gullies were blocked so water at the front remained above property thresholds until the gullies were unblocked. (p11) [PAG]
* Internal flooding of residential properties with displacement of occupants; unrepairable damage to furniture, furnishings, floorboards and carpets; external garden damage (outdoor furniture, decking, planting); floodwater damaged the wall separating the watercourse and Athorpe Grove. (p12) [PAG]
* Street and adjacent properties were affected by the Day Brook channel running beside Athorpe Grove; options considered include diverting and re-profiling the Day Brook away from Athorpe Grove, creation of embankments and wetlands, and increased maintenance to remove obstructions and reduce flood flow towards the street. (p13) [PAG]
* Flood flow affected Athorpe Grove due to reduced channel capacity and obstructions in the Day Brook; inaccessible derelict land and vegetation on the brook contributed to water being routed toward Athorpe Grove. * A rapid rise in the Day Brook breached the watercourse walls and flooded Athorpe Grove. Internal flooding occurred in residential properties; blocked road gullies prevented drainage and exacerbated the flooding. (p17) [PAG]

Responses recorded include placing gullies on the Targeted Gully Cleaning Routes, installation of property-level resilience measures to affected properties, and undertaking a Flood Risk Management Scheme for the area. ## Topics without coordinates

### Nottingham City Council
* The flood on 12th June 2019 (Day Brook overtopping) led Nottingham City Council, acting as LLFA, to undertake a formal Section 19 investigation after more than 5 properties reported internal flooding; the Council will seek to improve maintenance of local drainage and the watercourse. (p4)
* Highway Services provided an on-call operative who used a gully wagon to clear blocked gullies on Athorpe Grove; once gullies were unblocked water drained away freely, reducing front-of-property flooding. (p11)
* Acted as the Lead Local Flood Authority investigating the flood incident under Section 19; has responsibilities for investigating the event, making recommendations and for highway drainage maintenance to reduce future flood risk. (p14)
* Responsible for managing highway drainage and investigating the event. Responded by adding Athorpe Grove gullies to the Targeted Gully Cleaning Routes, undertaking a Day Brook flood risk management scheme including natural flood management and property-level resilience measures (flood doors, non-return valves, pumps, sealing service entries), ensuring access for Environment Agency, and progressing the Blue Green Infrastructure project upstream to slow flows and increase storage. (p17) [PAG]
* As the Lead Local Flood Authority, implemented responses to flooding in the Athorpe Grove area: placed gullies on a targeted gully cleaning regime for Athorpe Grove; provided access through the adjacent plot to enable Environment Agency maintenance by the watercourse adjacent to Fox Grove PH; completed a Flood Risk Management Scheme and installed property-level resilience (PLR) measures to affected properties; liaised with planning and developers to seek improvements to Day Brook. (p18) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: more than 5 properties reported internal flooding (p4)
* Quant: 9 residential properties (p17) [PAG]

### Environment Agency
* The Day Brook (designated Main River overseen by the Environment Agency) rose sharply and overtopped on 12th June 2019, causing flooding at Athorpe Grove. (p4)
* Monitored Day Brook and River Leen and issued an Orange Warning for the rivers during the 12 June 2019 event; installed a 'grabbing' system at the High School playing fields to remove debris from a trash screen where the Day Brook becomes culverted and carried out routine checks and debris/blockage clearance to reduce culvert blockages and help manage flood risk. (p11)
* Responsible for managing flood risk on the main river Day Brook; could carry out maintenance and remove obstructions (including tree and bush works up to 8 m inland of the left bank on derelict land) to restore channel capacity and reduce flood impact to Athorpe Grove. (p14) [PAG]
* Responsible for responding to flooding of and maintaining the Main river (Day Brook). Worked with Nottingham City Council to agree an action plan after Day Brook overtopped on 12 June 2019; also regulator for works adjacent to the watercourse. (p17) [PAG]
* Carried out (and were enabled to carry out) maintenance of the adjacent watercourse (Day Brook) to reduce flooding risk and worked to ensure residents are signed up to Floodline for flood warnings. (p18) [TAB]
* Quant: more than 5 properties reported internal flooding (p4)

### Leen Catchment
* Prolonged and intense rainfall produced high water levels and increased discharge into the River Leen and its tributary Day Brook, contributing to the flooding of properties on Athorpe Grove. (p9)

### Thackeray's Lane Recreation Ground
* Described as an upstream storage area that normally stores storm water; during the prolonged rainfall prior to 12th June 2019 the ground is reported as likely to have been saturated and the storage area at capacity, reducing its ability to store further stormwater and contributing to increased flows in the Day Brook during the event. (p10)

### Highway Services
* Provided a 24/7 on-call operative during the event and used a gully wagon to clear blocked road gullies on Athorpe Grove; once gullies were unblocked, floodwater at the front of properties drained away freely, reducing pooling above property thresholds. (p11)

### Highways Authority
* As Highways Authority (NCC) has duty to maintain highways and roadside drainage; responsible for ensuring highway drainage and roadside ditches are adequate and maintained, relevant to reducing flooding impacts on roads and adjacent properties near Athorpe Grove. (p14)
