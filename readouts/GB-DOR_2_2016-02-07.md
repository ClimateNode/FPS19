### Piddletrenthide
* {general}: Piddletrenthide lies on the River Piddle downstream of Plush Brook; it is within the area where river flooding driven by groundwater discharge and tributary runoff is a recognised risk and is the focus of proposed EA catchment modelling and improvements to flood warning accuracy. (p7)
* Internal flooding of residential properties caused by a combination of surface water, high groundwater and main river (River Piddle) flooding. (p8) [TAB, PAG]
* Localised intense rainfall and adjacent-field surface water run-off combined with the River Piddle overtopping upstream of Church Lane bridge, causing internal flooding to properties; the Church Lane bridge was identified as a possible hydraulic restriction to flow. (p12) [IMG]

Post-event inspections by Wessex Water followed to locate sewer defects and inform remedial actions. (p13) [IMG, PAG]

### River Piddle
* {general}: The River Piddle (the main route through the valley) contributes to fluvial flooding of settlements along its course; river flows are influenced by groundwater and springs which can increase flood risk. (p6)

## Topics without coordinates

### Piddle Valley
* {general}: The valley is described as being prone to flooding due to its chalk geology and steep 'v' shaped topography which promotes rapid surface water run-off; high groundwater levels influence river flows and springs, leading to fluvial and groundwater flooding in the valley. Sewer overflows exacerbate problems in some settlements within the valley. (p6)
* Internal flooding of residential properties across the valley as a result of very high intensity rainfall on saturated ground and raised river levels; properties flooded by surface water, groundwater and main river/ordinary watercourse flows depending on location. (p8) [PAG, MUL]
* A peak river flow of 6 m3/s was recorded at the South House gauge in Piddlehinton on 8 Feb 2016; the earlier Piddletrenthide Flood Alleviation Scheme (designed to 5 m3/s) reduced the impact of this event but further actions were identified as necessary to reduce future impacts. (p9)
* {2000, 07 Feb 2016 - 08 Feb 2016}: The valley experienced flooding during the 07–08 Feb 2016 event from fluvial, surface-water run-off and groundwater sources; river gauge at South House recorded a significant peak flow during the event. (p10) [PAG, MUL]
* The River Piddle overtopped within the Piddle Valley catchment; the event prompted investigation of Natural Flood Management and catchment-management options for the upper catchment. (p13)
* Following flooding in the valley, multiple mitigation and response actions were implemented: the Environment Agency changed the River Piddle flood warning threshold; a groundwater flood warning service was made available; Yorkshire Water (WW) inspected 1200 metres of sewer in the valley and identified 4 lengths requiring sealing; the WW Inflow Management Action Plan was published on the parish website; WDDC replenished and established sandbag stores; DCC Highways and EA assessed problematic areas/structures and carried out local works including re-profiling the grass area in front of the Vicarage at Church Lane Bridge; LLFA identified properties that may benefit from Property Level Resilience. (p17) [TAB]
* Flooding from the River Piddle, elevated groundwater and surface water affected communities and infrastructure across the valley. Impacts led to local mitigation works including jetting of Flood Alleviation Scheme channels in Egypt (Piddletrenthide) and Rectory Road (Piddlehinton), replenishment and creation of sandbag stores (Piddletrenthide, Piddlehinton, new store in Plush), review of highway drainage in Plush and Piddletrenthide, and identification of properties for property-level resilience. (p18) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 14 properties (Plush: 7; Piddletrenthide: 4; Piddlehinton: 2; Alton Pancras: 1) (p8) [PAG, MUL]
* Quant: 6 m3/s peak flow; 5 m3/s FAS design capacity; up to 54 properties flooded in 2000 (p9)
* {2000, 07 Feb 2016 - 08 Feb 2016}: Quant: 6m3/s peak flow at South House on 08 Feb 2016; up to 54 properties flooded in 2000 (historical) (p10) [PAG, MUL]

### Plush Brook
* {general}: Plush Brook is a groundwater- and surface-water-fed tributary whose rapid runoff from a steep 3.15 km2 upper catchment can contribute to river and surface-water flooding in the River Piddle system, increasing downstream flood risk near the Cheselbourne junction in Piddletrenthide. (p7)
* On the evening of 7 Feb 2016 a flash flood from intense surface-water run-off and Plush Brook overtopping caused internal flooding of properties in the village; the brook rose rapidly and flowed out of bank, converging with additional run-off. (p10)
* Plush Brook rose by approximately 1.5 m and overtopped its banks, contributing to internal flooding where it converged with surface-water run-off. (p11)
* Plush Brook was identified as a source of flooding where blockages/obstructions contributed to flooding of the centre of Plush; actions include clearing the channel and working with riparian owners to remove obstructions and increase maintenance. (p19) [TAB]
* Quant: 7 properties flooded internally on 07 Feb 2016; 3 properties initially flooded by the brook; brook rose by approximately 1.5m (p10)
* Quant: 3 properties flooded internally (p11)

### Piddletrenthide Flood Alleviation Scheme
* The Piddletrenthide FAS, constructed in 2004 to a design capacity of 5 m3/s, reduced the impact of river flooding during the event but was exceeded by the recorded peak flow (6 m3/s at South House on 08/02/2016), so flooding impacts in the valley were not entirely prevented. The scheme's presence reduced impacts compared with the 2000 event but further measures were identified as necessary. (p10)
* Quant: 5 m3/s (scheme design capacity); 6 m3/s (recorded peak flow at South House on 08/02/2016) (p10)

### Dorset Downs
* {general}: The study area lies within the chalk Dorset Downs; its steep v-shaped valleys and highly permeable fractured chalk transmit rainfall to groundwater and springs, increasing river flows and making the Piddle Valley prone to surface water run-off, fluvial and groundwater flooding. High groundwater infiltration also exacerbates sewer overflows in nearby settlements (Piddletrenthide and Piddlehinton), worsening local flooding. (p6)

### Piddle Catchment
* {general}: The chalk‑fed River Piddle catchment (downstream end including Plush Brook) has a groundwater-dominated baseflow so flood risk increases when groundwater discharge is at maximum; the catchment discharges to Poole Harbour downstream of Wareham, posing downstream flood risk. (p7)
