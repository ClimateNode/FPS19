

# Nottingham Unitary Authority flooding 17 Jun 2020

## Place names with coordinates

### Charles Avenue, Lenton Abbey
* Subject of a Section 19 flood investigation following a flood event; the street was affected by flooding on the event described in the report. (p1) [PAG]
* An exceptional, intense localised rainfall event overwhelmed public and private drainage. Surface water flooded the public highway (Charles Avenue), inundated private gardens and caused internal flooding to residential properties lower down the street. (p4) [PAG]
* Internal flooding of a residential property on Charles Avenue. (p6) [PAG]
* Internal flooding of a residential property located on Charles Avenue. (p7) [PAG]
* Properties on Charles Avenue, located at lower levels in the catchment, were inundated by surface water run-off that the public surface water network could not capture. One building experienced significant flooding with flats and communal lounges suffering water ingress and damage to soft furnishings, carpets, flooring and wallpapers. (p10) [PAG]

* One residential property in Charles Avenue (within Nottingham) experienced internal flooding. (p6) [PAG]

## Topics without coordinates

### Nottingham City Council
* Public (adopted) highway drainage (drains and road gullies) were unable to cope with the intense rainfall, contributing to surface water flooding of the public highway and subsequent internal flooding of residential properties. (p5) [TAB, PAG]
* Internal flooding of a residential property at Charles Avenue, Nottingham. (p6) [PAG]
* Advised residents that council-held supplies of sandbags are limited and cannot be guaranteed for deployment during periods of inclement weather, recommending residents consider personal or permanent defences. (p10)
* {17 Jun 2020, 18 Jun 2020}: Acted as the Lead Local Flood Authority and, as the Highways Authority, responded to the surface water/sewer flooding that internally flooded residential property on Charles Avenue. Responsible for managing highway drainage; committed to regular gully maintenance, adding Charles Avenue gullies to the council 'Hotspot List', and advising residents on property-level resilience measures. (p14) [TAB, MUL]
* Quant: one property (p6) [PAG]
* {17 Jun 2020, 18 Jun 2020}: Quant: 10 flats as part of 1 residential property (p14) [TAB, MUL]

### Severn Trent
* Public drainage systems (both public and private) were unable to cope with an exceptional, localised period of intense rainfall. This resulted in surface water flooding the public highway, flooding private gardens and ultimately causing internal flooding to residential properties. As the adopter of the public sewerage network, Severn Trent Water's drainage assets are implicated in the failure of public drainage to accommodate the event. (p5) [TAB, PAG]
* Met with the Lead Local Flood Authority and inspected their network to identify any damage or blockages and to ensure water was freely flowing through the system as part of the response to the flooding. (p10)
* Investigated the public sewer network in the Charles Avenue area to identify any damage or blockages related to the flood event, completed that investigation, and committed to ongoing maintenance to ensure the sewer network is kept flowing. (p14) [TAB]

### Highway Services
* Provided an emergency 24/7 call-out response during the event; an on-call operative attended and used a gully-clearing unit on site to clear gullies and help prevent further flooding at the front of properties. (p10)

### Highways Authority
* Managed highway drainage in response to the intense rainfall event; committed to continuing regular gully maintenance and to add gullies on Charles Avenue to the 'Hotspot List' so they are prioritised for cleansing before forecast storm events. (p14)
