

# Wood End flooding 11 Jun 2023

## Place names with coordinates

### Wood End
* Surface water flooding on 11 June 2023 caused internal flooding to properties in Wood End (Atherstone). (p3)
* Flooding occurred in Wood End on 11 June 2023. Recovery and investigations were carried out: WCC worked with Severn Trent Water, WCC Highways, councillors and residents to provide advice and guidance, advise on remedial works and to investigate the drainage network. Considerable remedial work has taken place but parts of the remedial works remain ongoing. (p4)
* A short period of very intense localised rainfall on 11 June 2023 caused surface water flooding in Wood End; anecdotal reports indicate numerous properties experienced ingress through building frontages as a result of surface water. (p6) [IMG, PAG]
* Anecdotal reports from 11 June 2023 indicate numerous properties experienced ingress through their frontages due to surface water after a short period of localised intense rainfall. (p7) [PAG]
* Short, localised intense rainfall produced surface water runoff that entered properties through the frontages, causing ingress to residential buildings in Wood End. (p8) [PAG]
* Short‑duration, high‑intensity rainfall caused surface water flows and the inundation/overwhelming of artificial drainage networks (including sewers and highway drainage) across the Wood End area, producing large amounts of ponding on highways and internal flooding of properties in two main areas (Wood Street and Glenville Avenue). (p9) [PAG]
* Short-duration, high-intensity rainfall produced surface water flows from higher ground on Tamworth Road that overwhelmed highway drainage and Severn Trent Water assets, causing manholes and gullies to back up and discharge onto the highway. Flows were channelled along the built environment (notably Wood Street and Glenville Avenue) and ponded on the highway. Internal flooding of properties occurred as a result. (p10) [IMG]
* Surface water flooding affected parts of the Wood End area — mapping and investigation focus show extent of surface water flooding concentrated along low-lying corridors and watercourses, affecting residential streets and contributing to internal flooding of properties in the locality. (p11) [IMG]
* Intense rainfall produced surface water runoff and high flows in ordinary watercourses; highway and STW drainage were overwhelmed leading to manhole/gully backup and internal flooding of properties across the village. (p14) [IMG, PAG]
* {2012, 2018}: Historic surface water runoff flooding affected the settlement. Reports record surface water runoff originating from a farmer's field and at least one incident where sandbags were required to prevent internal property flooding. Local roads (Wood Street and Glenville Avenue) and nearby property boundaries are identified as flood-prone and targeted for drainage and SuDS interventions. (p15) [IMG, PAG, MUL]

### Wood Street, Wood End
* Surface water flowing from higher ground (Tamworth Road) overwhelmed WCC County highways drainage and Severn Trent Water systems; flows backed up out of manholes and gullies onto Wood Street and ponded on the highway, leading to internal flooding of properties. An overgrown watercourse/blocked culvert west of Wood End Primary School also caused one further property to be internally flooded. (p9) [PAG]
* Surface water flows from higher ground on Tamworth Road overwhelmed highway drainage and sewer systems; flows backed up out of manholes and gullies onto Wood Street, causing large amounts of highway ponding and the internal flooding of properties. Overgrown watercourses and a potentially blocked culvert to the west of Wood End Primary School also contributed to one further internal flood. (p10) [PAG]
* Surface water flows originating on higher ground (Tamworth Road) flowed down Wood Street into the drainage network and onwards to Glenville Avenue, contributing to exceedance of drainage capacity and subsequent internal flooding of nearby properties. (p11) [PAG]
* Ordinary watercourses north of Wood Street conveyed significant overland flows; overgrown vegetation and a potential culvert blockage caused flows to back up and resulted in internal flooding of a property on Wood Street. (p14)
* {2012, 2018}: Historic surface water flooding recorded in Wood End affecting the Wood Street area. Reports from 2012 and 2018 attribute flooding to surface water runoff (one report identifies runoff originating from a farmer's field). Sandbags were used in one report to prevent internal property flooding. (p15) [TAB, MUL]
* Quant: five properties; one property (p9) [PAG]
* Quant: five properties; one property (p10) [PAG]
* Quant: 5 properties (p11) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 property (p14)

### Glenville Avenue, Wood End
* Surface water flows routed from Tamworth Road via Wood Street overwhelmed highway and sewer drainage, causing manholes and gullies to back up and flood onto Glenville Avenue, resulting in internal flooding of properties. (p10) [PAG]
* Flooding occurred due to intense rainfall overwhelming the artificial drainage network. CCTV and jetting surveys of the drainage were carried out; small defects in the artificial drainage network were identified and will be rectified by the relevant asset owner. Options to install additional drainage on Glenville Avenue are being explored; some investigation remains outstanding and residents have been updated when requested. (p12)
* Surface water flowed down into Glenville Avenue, overwhelming artificial drainage networks and causing flows to back up out of manholes and gullies onto the street; resulting flows led to internal flooding of properties at this location (combined with Wood Street). (p14) [PAG]
* {2012, 2018}: Glenville Avenue is part of the Wood End area where historic surface water flooding was recorded (2012 and 2018). Actions and opportunities (SuDS retro-fit, additional highway drainage) are targeted at Glenville Avenue, indicating flood-prone conditions in the street. One historic report in the area required sandbags to be used to prevent internal property flooding. (p15) [TAB, MUL]
* Quant: five properties (p10) [PAG]
* Quant: 10 properties (combined Wood Street and Glenville Avenue) (p14) [PAG]

### Warwickshire
* Isolated heavy rainfall on 11 June 2023 resulted in internal property flooding from surface water in parts of Warwickshire, including at least 11 properties in Wood End (Atherstone). Internal property flooding was also experienced elsewhere in the county and will be subject to separate investigations. (p3)
* The county contains Wood End, where flooding occurred on 11 June 2023; county-level LLFA (WCC) carried out the Section 19 investigation and coordinated recovery and remedial actions. (p4)
* Quant: 11 properties (p3)

### Atherstone

### Tamworth Road, Wood End
* Surface water runoff originating on the high ground of Tamworth Road flowed down Wood Street and onto Glenville Avenue, contributing to the overwhelming of WCC County Highways and Severn Trent Water drainage systems. This led to backing up of flows from manholes and gullies onto Glenville Avenue and the internal flooding of properties downstream. (p11) [PAG, IMG]
* Primary surface-water flow originated on higher ground at Tamworth Road and flowed down towards Wood Street, overwhelming highway and sewer drainage and initiating overland flow that led to internal property flooding in Wood Street/Glenville Avenue. (p14)
* Quant: 5 properties (p11) [PAG, IMG]
* Quant: 10 properties (p14)

* WCC (Flood Risk Management and County Highways) attended the location, undertook CCTV and jetting surveys of the artificial drainage network, communicated with residents and partners, identified small defects for rectification, and concluded that the intensity of rainfall would have caused flooding even if systems were operating as intended.
