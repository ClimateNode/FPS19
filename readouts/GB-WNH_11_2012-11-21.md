

# Charwelton flooding 21 Nov 2012

Reference document:<br>Flood Investigation Report: High Street, Charwelton, 21st November 2012<br>090\_West Northamptonshire/Charwelton - November 2012.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Charwelton
* Charwelton experienced surface water flooding driven by intense rainfall on a saturated catchment; at least one property flooded internally and the highway (High Street) was closed. Flooding of a property has occurred on multiple occasions within the previous five years. (p5) [PAG]
* Intense rainfall on a saturated catchment caused excess surface water to flow overland following natural contours to low points adjacent to the River Cherwell and down via highway drainage. At least one property on High Street flooded internally. The highway (High Street) was closed due to the flood. (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* The village experienced flooding associated with significant rainfall on the local catchment: flooding occurred on High Street (south of the River Cherwell), the road was closed and at least one property flooded internally; other properties were at high risk. (p8)
* The village experienced flooding as a result of intense rainfall during the incident. (p15) [PAG]
* Intense short-duration rainfall on a saturated catchment overwhelmed existing drainage systems and watercourses. Silt and debris in drains and inlets reduced flow capacity. Excessive flows overtopped drainage and watercourses and flowed to the low point in the village; the local drainage at that low point could not convey the volume of water to the River Cherwell, contributing to flooding of the village. (p16)
* Flows of water from higher ground overtopped historic drainage systems and concentrated at the low point in the village; local drainage was overwhelmed and outlets to the River Cherwell were rendered unsatisfactory (affected by river water depth), causing overspill of land drainage onto the public highway and private property. (p17)
* Resident photographs document flooding in Charwelton associated with the incident on 21st November 2012. (p36)
* Quant: one property (p5) [PAG]
* Quant: at least one property (p6) [PAG, MUL]
* Quant: at least one property (p8)

### Packhorse Bridge
* The Packhorse Bridge is the low point in the village where the Main River Cherwell and an ordinary watercourse converge. River flow is conveyed beneath the A361 and the bridge via a system of 450mm diameter pipes and a riparian-owned culverted ordinary watercourse discharges to the River Cherwell south of the bridge. These constrained conduits and the flow convergence at the bridge were central to flood routing through the village during the event. (p9)
* Pipe culvert sections at Packhorse Bridge had insufficient capacity during the event; water backed up, filled land north of the A361 and then overtopped onto the A361 and adjoining land west of the A361. (p11)
* Ponding formed immediately south of the Packhorse Bridge extending about 120 metres. Water in the highway exceeded 300 mm depth immediately south of the bridge, causing flooding to an adjacent property. Land immediately west of the High Street at the Packhorse Bridge was completely submerged and overtopped onto the A361. (p12)
* The River Cherwell beneath the A361 at the Packhorse Bridge (and immediately upstream/downstream) was implicated in restricting outlet capacity during the flood; the condition and route of the culverted/channelled river at this location require investigation and maintenance to restore full flow capacity. (p17) [PAG]
* Flows immediately upstream and downstream of the bridge were identified as constrained during the flood and were prioritised for reinstatement and maintenance to restore full hydraulic capacity so flood flows could pass without causing overtopping. (p18)
* Floodwaters nearly submerged the bridge arches and overflowed onto the adjacent road; the road was rendered impassable and nearby properties were threatened by encroaching floodwater. (p38)
* River levels during the flood rose to nearly the base of the stone arches, with sediment‑laden flow passing under the arches. Debris was observed around the bridge and adjacent banks showed muddy deposits and signs of erosion consistent with the flood. (p41) [IMG, PAG]

### High Street, Charwelton
* High Street was subject to surface water flooding; the highway was closed and at least one property experienced internal flooding. (p5)
* Flooding occurred on High Street (to the south of the River Cherwell); the highway was closed to traffic and at least one property experienced internal flooding; several other properties were identified as at high risk of flooding. (p8)
* Flows from the A361 (from culvert overtopping) and from the River Cherwell (overtopping its banks) met at the low point of the village immediately south of the Packhorse Bridge on the High Street. The water was unable to drain into the River Cherwell quickly enough, causing inundation at this location. (p11)
* Ponding on the High Street about 80 metres south of the Packhorse Bridge; water approximately 150 mm deep at a property on the east side causing that property to flood. Other residential properties on the south side were nearly flooded and were affected by waves from passing traffic through the ponded water. (p12)
* Overflow from the fishponds outfall and adjacent ditch exceeded the capacity of an ordinary watercourse culvert in the verge, causing turbid water to pool, overtop the curb and flow onto and across High Street (road surface). (p39) [IMG]
* Flood water was allowed to drain through a purposely created gap in a wall south of Packhorse Bridge; street surfaces were wet with standing water/puddles and the adjacent grass verge showed damage likely associated with vehicular movement during/after inundation. (p42) [IMG]
* A stone-lined ditch discharged into an ordinary watercourse culvert in the verge of High Street; the culvert grate was partially submerged with visible debris and foam, indicating active flow that impeded drainage along the verge. (p47)
* Quant: one property (p5)
* Quant: at least one property (p8)

### River Cherwell
* Excess surface water from intense rainfall flowed to low points adjacent to the River Cherwell, contributing to local flooding and routing floodwater toward highway drainage systems. (p6)
* The River Cherwell flows at the north of the village and the area around it is shown as Flood Zone 3; flooding occurred in the village (High Street) to the south of the river on 21 November 2012 and the river and its tributaries are implicated in the local flood risk. (p8) [PAG]
* The River Cherwell is the Main River passing through Charwelton; it receives two ordinary watercourses (from north and south), was observed to have recently been dredged in places, and bifurcates approximately 30 metres upstream of the Packhorse Bridge where a constructed double plastic pipe splits flow to an unknown outfall. The river's course, its connections to ordinary watercourses and fish-pond systems, and the engineered pipe/bifurcation arrangements influenced river flooding and flood routing in the village during the event. (p9) [PAG]
* The River Cherwell overtopped its banks immediately north and west of Packhorse Bridge on 21st November 2012; flows moved northwest-to-southeast, flowed over the A361 and contributed to flooding at the village low point and to backing up in culverts. (p11)
* River water levels affected the outlets from the village drainage; the depth of water in the River Cherwell made outlets unsatisfactory and prevented effective discharge of floodwater from local drainage, exacerbating flooding in Charwelton. (p16)
* The River Cherwell conveyed flood flows through/adjacent to the village; constrained sections and downstream Main River maintenance issues were linked to overtopping risk to properties and the highway (including concern over the culvert beneath the A361). (p18) [PAG]
* The River Cherwell (Main River) conveyed flow through a culvert beneath the A361 that was at risk of overtopping/overflow, causing or threatening damage to private property in Charwelton. The report recommends increasing conduit capacity, installing a debris screen at the inlet, improving maintenance access and improving the watercourse profile and lining at inlet and outlet to reduce the risk of overflow and improve downstream free flow. (p19)
* The River Cherwell experienced elevated, sediment‑laden flows during the flood, with water levels high both downstream (east) and upstream (west) of Packhorse Bridge. Muddy banks, debris and local bank erosion were observed. (p41) [IMG]
* Channel works recorded: south of Packhorse Bridge the channel was partially trimmed and dredged; further south the channel was fully trimmed and dredged. (p46)


## Topics without coordinates

### A361
* The River Cherwell passes beneath the A361 at the Packhorse Bridge via a piped system; highway drainage for the A361 consists of road gullies and roadside ditches that connect to the culverted watercourse. These drainage and culvert arrangements were part of the flood conveyance through the village during the event on 21 November 2012 and affected the A361 corridor. (p9) [PAG]
* {09 Apr 1998, 20 Jul 2007, 13 Dec 2008, 04 Nov 2012, 21 Nov 2012, 25 Nov 2012, 22 Dec 2012, 24 Dec 2012, 14 Feb 2013}: The A361 was inundated on multiple recorded dates, blocking the highway and contributing to access issues during flood events recorded in the report. (p10) [TAB, MUL]
* A large volume and high-velocity of surface water flowed along the A361 (south to north) from surface inlets and from a culverted watercourse that overtopped; additionally the River Cherwell flowed over the A361—these flows met at the village low point and were unable to drain, causing localised flooding on the road and adjacent land. (p11)
* Areas of land west of the High Street at the Packhorse Bridge were submerged and overtopped onto the A361, causing overtopping of the A361 carriageway. Highway drainage carried ponding away via the system and a hole. (p12)
* {09 Apr 1998, 20 Jul 2007, 13 Dec 2008, 04 Nov 2012, 21 Nov 2012, 25 Nov 2012, 22 Dec 2012, 24 Dec 2012, 14 Feb 2013}: Quant: flooded on at least 9 recorded dates (09/04/1998; 20/07/2007; 13/12/2008; 04/11/2012; 21/11/2012; 25/11/2012; 22/12/2012; 24/12/2012; 14/02/2013) (p10) [TAB, MUL]

### Charwelton Catchment
* Significant rainfall fell on the Charwelton catchment on 21 November 2012, contributing to flooding within the village (notably on High Street). (p8)
* {1998, 2007, 21 Nov 2012}: Significant rainfall produced saturated ground across the Charwelton catchment and generated large-volume, high-velocity surface water and river overtopping that caused multiple flooding incidents affecting people and property in the village. Additionally, outflows from fish ponds west of the village caused flooding of a property (see 1998 and 2007 events) until the outlet route was maintained. (p11) [MUL]
* {1998, 2007, 21 Nov 2012}: Quant: approximately 600mm depth (property flood in 1998 and 2007) (p11) [MUL]

### Highway Authority
* Highway drainage and culverted sections were insufficient to pass extreme flows leading to risk of overtopping to the public highway and adjacent properties; the Authority was tasked with cleansing highway drainage, assessing capacity and considering improvements (including to the A361 culvert) to reduce flood risk. (p18)

### Daventry District Council
* Riparian owners and the council were required to reinstate historic drainage (re-excavate ditches to calculated sections, repair/replace culverts, dredge and trim watercourses) to restore hydraulic capacity and reduce future flooding; responsibilities for a culverted ordinary watercourse through the village were to be established and addressed. (p18)

### Northamptonshire County Council
* Flooding of the River Cherwell affected the channel immediately upstream and downstream of the Packhorse Bridge; these sections were identified as priorities for riparian maintenance to restore full conveyance and reduce overtopping/flood risk to the village. (p18)
