

# Worlingworth flooding 18 Oct 2023 - 21 Oct 2023

## Place names with coordinates

### Worlingworth
* Severely impacted by intense rainfall from Storm Babet: natural flow routes and watercourse/drainage capacity were overwhelmed. Overland flow paths converged through the village, causing internal flooding of properties and disruption to infrastructure and services. (p4)
* Internal flooding of residential and commercial properties caused by multiple sources: surface water runoff from surrounding fields (pluvial), overtopping of local watercourses (fluvial) and overwhelmed sewerage/drainage systems. Flow paths affected multiple streets and zones within the village (West, Central and East). (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* {2012, 18 Oct 2023 - 21 Oct 2023}: Central Worlingworth experienced surface water flooding during the event (flood flows follow a west-to-east surface water flow path through the village). Highway drainage was overwhelmed in the village centre causing highway flooding; the foul sewer network has a history of manhole surcharging during heavy rainfall. A previous internal property flood is recorded in 2012. (p8) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Significant surface-water flow path runs west–east through the village; most of the properties affected in central Worlingworth lie within high-risk surface-water zones. Localised high- and medium-risk surface-water areas to the west of the catchment concurred with flooding experienced during Storm Babet. Fluvial risk is present but less significant and largely isolated to the east of the catchment (adjacent floodplain). (p9) [IMG, PAG]
* Saturated, clayey soils and low infiltration led to extensive overland runoff during the storm period, resulting in surface water flooding/overland runoff affecting the village as reported in relation to Storm Babet. (p12) [IMG]
* Western part of the village experienced overland runoff and local watercourse/pond overtopping. Newtown suffered internal flooding to properties; blocked/poorly maintained channels, debris and fallen trees caused backups and overtopping that routed water to lowest points (houses). Ponds with reduced capacity due to siltation likely exacerbated impacts. (p14)
* Significant rainfall onto already-saturated ground produced high levels of overland surface-water flow across west Worlingworth (around Newtown), causing ponding in low spots and internal flooding of residential properties. Several houses experienced internal flooding with damage to furniture, flooring and personal effects; overland flows also damaged hedgerows, gardens and highways and deposited debris. (p15) [IMG]
* Central Worlingworth experienced widespread flooding: at least 23 properties suffered internal flooding with damage to furniture, flooring and personal effects; overland surface water flows caused ponding in low spots and damaged/deposited debris in hedgerows, gardens and adjacent highways. Manholes surcharged and overtopped watercourses spilled into properties and onto the highway. (p16)
* The village experienced significant flooding concentrated around Shop Street and Willow Green. Heavy, continuous rain caused the drainage network and a culverted watercourse to be overwhelmed; ordinary watercourses between the recreation ground and Willow Tree Yard overtopped, producing the first significant flooding. Floodwater cascaded down Shop Street into Willow Green and properties in the Close, with highway flooding from both surface water and overtopping channels. (p17) [IMG]
* Significant overland flow from surrounding fields and from local watercourses overwhelmed channels, ponds and drainage at pinch points in the village centre. Watercourses exceeded bank, causing pooling near the community centre and flow around the backs of properties on Church Road and Willow Green. Highways and Anglian Water assets were overwhelmed (water forced back up manhole covers). Internal flooding affected residential homes and businesses (internal depths reached 37 cm); some properties remained uninhabitable for an extended period after the event. (p18) [PAG]
* Significant rainfall and saturated ground generated high levels of overland flow toward the village centre, overwhelming water management features and spilling out at pinch points near the community centre. Local watercourses exceeded capacity and overtopped their banks, contributing to overland flows that converged around the Recreation Ground and The White Cottage. Highway drainage and foul sewer networks were overwhelmed, with water forced back up manhole covers and adding foul/surcharged sewer water to the surface flooding. (p19) [IMG, PAG]
* The eastern end of the village experienced overland runoff and watercourse-related flooding: a small number of properties were flooded internally and several properties had gardens and outbuildings flooded as runoff and watercourse flows pooled and overtopped. (p20) [IMG]
* Residential properties experienced internal flooding to significant depths; some residents were forced to move out of their properties for several months. Other properties had water flowing across gardens and into outbuildings but without internal property flooding. Watercourses overtopped and local low spots rapidly filled due to intense rainfall and overland flow; highways drainage assets were overwhelmed. (p22) [IMG]
* Residential yard and adjacent agricultural field were inundated by floodwater during the event in East Worlingworth; water levels in the yard reached nearly to the top of a garden gate and breached property boundaries. Residents were identified for property flood resilience measures (grant-funded PFR). (p23) [IMG, PAG]
* A residential backyard in East Worlingworth was inundated by muddy floodwater during the event. Floodwater covered the garden area, submerging garden features and reaching the bases of the garden shed and hedges. Water encroached onto a wooden deck and threatened outdoor belongings. (p24) [IMG, PAG]
* Floodwater breached the threshold of a residential property in East Worlingworth and entered the interior, covering floors; outdoor furniture and garden items were partially submerged in muddy water, indicating contamination and likely property damage and internal clean-up/repairs. (p25) [IMG, PAG]
* Severe surface-water/urban flooding inundated a residential area in central Worlingworth, with water covering streets, reaching up onto front lawns and threatening houses; vehicles were partially submerged and access along the street was impeded. (p27) [PAG]
* Surface water and watercourse flooding affected the eastern area of Worlingworth during the investigation. Blocked/insufficient highway drainage (gully at junction with Newtown) and suspected blocked piped sections under Shop Street contributed to flows across roads and into low-lying areas (near the church and AW pumping station). Areas were identified for attenuation and maintenance to reduce future flood risk. (p31) [TAB]

### Church Road, Worlingworth
* Church Road (listed in both Central and East groupings) was identified within the investigation as experiencing floodwater during the October 2023 event. Floodwater entered the area from pluvial runoff, overtopping watercourses and overwhelmed sewers/drains, resulting in internal flooding to properties on or adjacent to the road. (p7)
* Homes on either side of Church Road were affected by floodwater as drainage networks became overwhelmed; floodwater cascaded down the street contributing to inundation of lower-lying areas and properties. (p16)
* Homes on either side of Church Road were affected by floodwater that entered from an overwhelmed drainage network and from overtopping watercourses that run around the rear of buildings and through gardens. A culverted section at the highway was quickly overwhelmed, causing overtopping that added to flood depths on the road and into properties. (p17) [IMG, PAG]
* A watercourse running along the south side of Church Road was quickly overwhelmed; water overtopped the banks and added to surface water on the road. Highway low spots pooled and water flowed into properties off the highway. Highway grips were unable to discharge effectively into the overwhelmed watercourse, indicating an overwhelmed drainage system rather than a local blockage. (p21)
* High volumes of surface water overwhelmed highway drainage. A piped section on the south side of Church Road (between the river and the AW pumping station) was identified as a possible contributor to poor conveyance during the event; LLFA recommended the landowner investigate reinstating an open channel to improve flow. (p22) [IMG]
* Areas around Church Road (including a piped/open watercourse south of Church Road and land north of Church Road east of Willow Green) were implicated in flow routes; investigation and potential reinstatement of watercourse/channel and other measures were recommended to reduce flooding. (p31) [TAB]
* Surface water flooding affected the area around Church Road during the 18 Oct 2023 – 21 Oct 2023 event. Land north of Church Road was identified for possible attenuation and investigations were recommended to reinstate an open channel between the AW pumping station and the watercourse to the east to manage flows. (p33) [PAG]
* Quant: over 30 properties across Worlingworth (total for the village) (p7)

### Shop Street, Worlingworth
* Shop Street (within the Central zone) experienced floodwater during the October 2023 event. Flood sources reported in the investigation include pluvial runoff from fields, overtopping of local watercourses and overwhelmed sewer/drainage systems, and properties on Shop Street suffered internal flooding. (p7)
* Shop Street experienced manhole surcharging and reported manhole flooding during high rainfall; surcharging on Shop Street is noted and may be related to surface water infiltration or a sewer leak. Anglian Water investigations into the sewer issue are ongoing. These issues occurred during the storm event. (p8) [PAG]
* Shop Street is shown on the flow-route map around Newtown and was affected by overland surface water flows and ponding, with consequent damage and debris on the highway and adjacent gardens/hedgerows. (p15) [IMG]
* During Storm Babet Shop Street experienced flooding from both overwhelmed drainage infrastructure and overtopping watercourses; continuous rain caused rapid overflow of the drainage network, with floodwater deepening and affecting homes and the highway (including manhole surcharging). (p16) [PAG]
* Prolonged rain and overwhelmed drainage caused floodwater to rise on Shop Street. Floodwater cascaded downhill from Shop Street and Church Road, entered homes set along Shop Street, and contributed to downstream flooding toward Willow Green. (p17) [PAG]
* Suspected blocked piped section of an ordinary watercourse under the highway; investigation recommended to check blockage and confirm outfall after flooding affected the highway area. (p31) [TAB]
* Quant: over 30 properties across Worlingworth (total for the village) (p7)

### Willow Green, Worlingworth
* Properties and immediate areas at Willow Green experienced internal flooding from surface water runoff, overtopping watercourses and overwhelmed sewers/drainage during the October 2023 event. (p7)
* Floodwater cascaded down to Willow Green (the village green), with overland flow coming off the highway into adjacent gardens and properties; contributed to internal flooding of nearby homes and deposited debris and damage to hedgerows and gardens. (p16) [PAG]

### Suffolk
* Storm Babet caused significant disruption to communities across Suffolk between 18th-21st October 2023. Intense rainfall led to widespread pluvial flooding and some fluvial impacts as flow routes and drainage were overwhelmed. (p4) [PAG]
* Experienced the heaviest rainfall in East Anglia during the event, producing saturated land and rivers rising close to or exceeding record levels; significant and rapid flooding affected roads and properties and caused disruption to road and rail networks; a major incident was declared on 20 Oct 2023. (p6)
* Quant: over 30 properties (in Worlingworth) (p4) [PAG]

### East Anglia
* During Storm Babet (18–21 Oct 2023) East Anglia experienced extreme rainfall that produced rapid river rises and widespread, fast-developing flooding. Roads and properties were significantly flooded, river systems rose across whole catchments with several gauging stations recording flows close to or exceeding highest on record, and there was major disruption to road and rail networks. (p6) [IMG]

### Newtown
* Newtown (part of the West zone) experienced floodwater flow through the settlement during the October 2023 event; sources described include pluvial runoff from surrounding fields, overtopping of local watercourses and overwhelmed drainage/sewer systems, resulting in internal flooding to properties. (p7) [IMG]
* Newtown (west zone) was part of the area affected by surface water flooding during Storm Babet; this aligns with the report that localised high and medium surface-water-risk areas to the west of the catchment concurred with flooding during the event. Specific property- or infrastructure-level impacts for Newtown are not detailed in the text. (p8) [IMG, PAG]
* Properties in Newtown suffered internal flooding caused by overland runoff and blocked/poorly maintained watercourses and ponds. Debris and fallen trees in channels contributed to blockages, causing water to back up, overtop features and flow to the lowest points where houses were inundated. Ponds with reduced holding capacity through siltation likely exacerbated the event. (p14)
* Residential properties in Newtown experienced internal flooding, causing damage to furniture, flooring and personal effects. Significant overland surface water flows led to ponding in low spots and caused damage and debris to surrounding hedgerows, gardens and highways. (p15) [IMG, PAG]
* Junction with Water Lane experienced slow drainage and overland flows; surface water ponding and flows from this area contributed to highway damage and ingress of floodwater into nearby properties and gardens. (p16)
* Highway drainage at the gully on the junction with Newtown was not functioning and required repair; impaired drainage contributed to surface water flooding affecting the highway/adjacent area. (p31) [TAB]

### Worlingworth Hall Farm

### Anglian Water
* The Anglian Water foul sewer system became overwhelmed by surface water during the heavy rainfall, contributing to flooding of roads and surrounding properties; investigations indicate likely unregulated private roof connections and groundwater infiltration into unsealed pipes as sources of surface water entering the foul network. (p16)
* Anglian Water assets and the foul sewer network were overwhelmed during the event; sewer surcharging occurred with water forced back up manhole covers, contributing foul water to the surface flooding. The report also highlights potential groundwater intrusion into the foul sewer to be investigated. (p19) [PAG]
* Anglian Water was asked to investigate potential groundwater intrusion into the foul sewer and to plan works to reduce any related foul water flooding; the report also recommends investigating increasing capacity of Anglian Water drainage network assets which were likely overwhelmed by high rainfall. (p20) [PAG]
* The foul sewer network was implicated in the event (surface water ingress/foul flooding concerns). A CCTV survey of foul sewers and private laterals was recommended to identify private roof water connections and any sources of sewer flooding; potential further investment to reduce groundwater/ingress is proposed. (p31) [TAB]
* Quant: 23 properties (p16)

### Recreation Ground
* Inundation of the recreation ground caused by overtopping of the adjacent ordinary watercourse; identified as the first significant flooding location in the village during the event. (p17) [IMG]
* Overland flows from multiple directions converged around the Recreation Ground in central Worlingworth, contributing to system overcapacity; the Recreation Ground was identified as an area where attenuation/infiltration 'overspill' could be provided to draw flood water away from housing. (p19) [IMG]
* The recreation ground was identified as a potential attenuation area because it received/conveyed surface water runoff during the flood investigation; NFM or storage was recommended to reduce local flood impacts. (p31) [TAB]

### Sewage Works
* Flows were directed eastward toward the Sewage Works; highways and Anglian Water assets were overwhelmed during the event, with water forced back up manhole covers and concerns raised about groundwater intrusion into the foul sewer and potential related foul water flooding at Anglian Water infrastructure. (p19) [IMG]
* High volumes of surface water / overland flow interacted with piped sections and the drainage/pumping infrastructure; LLFA actions reference a piped section between the river and the AW pumping station suggesting the sewage/pumping infrastructure was implicated and should be investigated for capacity and connectivity issues. (p22) [IMG]

### Suffolk County Council
* LLFA (Suffolk County Council) led recommendations and will monitor progress; actions reflect SCC involvement in response to flood impacts on drainage, sewers and watercourses in the village. (p31)

### SCC Highway
* Highway drainage assets (notably the gully at the junction with Newtown and a piped section under the highway at Shop Street) were impaired/blocked during the event; SCC Highways Authority was identified as responsible for repairs and investigation to restore functionality and outfall connectivity.
