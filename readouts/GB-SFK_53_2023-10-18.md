

# Worlingworth flooding 18 Oct 2023 - 21 Oct 2023

Reference document:<br>Worlingworth Flood Investigation – Storm Babet October 2023<br>124\_Suffolk County/124\_2023\_Suffolk County/s19-flood-investigation-report-worlingworth.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Worlingworth
* Intense rainfall from Storm Babet overwhelmed natural flow routes and the capacity of watercourses and drainage infrastructure, causing converging overland flows, localised ponding and the internal flooding of properties, infrastructure and services within the village. (p4)
* Storm Babet (20 Oct 2023) produced significant rainfall that caused internal flooding in the village. Flood water entered properties and flowed through defined flow paths from surrounding fields (pluvial runoff), from overtopping local watercourses (fluvial) and from overwhelmed sewerage/drainage systems. The investigation divides affected areas into three zones: West (Newtown and Mill Road), Central (Church Road, Shop Street, Old Stores Close, Willow Tree Yard, Willow Green) and East (Church Street, Worlingworth Hall Farm), indicating multiple streets and clusters experienced internal and external flooding and flow routing through the village. (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* A significant surface-water flow path runs west-to-east through the village centre. During the event this overwhelmed local drainage, causing highway flooding in the centre of the village and affecting properties in central Worlingworth. (p8) [IMG, PAG]
* A significant surface-water flow path runs west–east through the centre of the village. Most of the affected properties in central Worlingworth fall within areas mapped at high risk of surface water flooding; localised ponding is also noted due to shallow topography. (p9) [IMG, PAG]
* During Storm Babet, prolonged antecedent rainfall left ground saturated and the village's clayey, low-permeability soils produced extensive surface water runoff and overland flow, leading to local flooding. (p12) [IMG]
* The western part of the village (Newtown) experienced internal flooding to properties. Blocked and poorly maintained watercourses and silted ponds reduced conveyance and holding capacity; debris and fallen trees in channels caused blockages, leading to water backing up, overtopping features and flowing to low points where houses are located. Reports from Mill Lane were also received but severity is not described in detail. (p14)
* Overland surface water flows caused internal flooding to several residential houses, damaging furniture, flooring and personal effects. Significant surface water flows also caused damage and deposited debris in surrounding hedgerows, gardens and on highways; ponding occurred in isolated low spots on saturated ground. (p15) [IMG]
* Extensive internal flooding of residential properties with water entering from overwhelmed drainage, surcharging manholes and overtopping watercourses. Floodwaters caused damage to furniture, flooring and personal effects; water poured out of manhole covers into road and verges and overtopped watercourses spilled into properties and onto the highway; overland flows damaged hedgerows, gardens and highways. (p16)
* The village experienced flooding during Storm Babet from two sources: prolonged heavy rain causing the drainage network to overflow (bringing water up through highway drains and along Shop Street) and overtopping of local ordinary watercourses (notably the west–east watercourses behind Willow Green and Willow Tree Yard and the south–north watercourse adjacent to Swan Road). Floodwater cascaded down Shop Street to Willow Green, affected properties in the Close, overtopped culverts at the highway, and inundated gardens and properties adjacent to the watercourses. (p17) [IMG]
* Significant overland flow and watercourse overtopping caused pooling in the village centre, overwhelming drainage and ponds and causing internal and external flooding to residential homes and businesses. Internal flooding reached 37 cm in affected properties. Highways and Anglian Water assets were overwhelmed; water was forced back up manhole covers. Some properties remained uninhabitable six months after the event. (p18) [PAG]
* Significant rainfall and saturated ground caused high levels of overland flow toward the centre of the village. Watercourses exceeded capacity and flowed out of bank, contributing to overland flows that overwhelmed water-management features at pinch points near the community centre. Change in topography slowed flows in the village centre, increasing local accumulation and causing drainage systems to be overcapacity. (p19) [IMG, PAG]
* The eastern end of the village experienced flooding during the event: a small number of properties were flooded internally and several properties had flooding to gardens and outbuildings due to watercourses and surface-water runoff overtopping and overwhelming local drainage. (p20) [IMG]
* Significant rainfall and overland flows caused watercourses to overtop and local low spots to fill in east Worlingworth. Residential properties experienced internal flooding to significant depths; some residents had to move out of their homes for several months. Other properties had water flowing across gardens and into outbuildings without internal inundation. (p22) [IMG]
* Residential yard and adjacent open field were inundated; floodwater reached close to the top of a garden gate and covered the yard up to the brick wall, with the neighbouring field extensively flooded, indicating property-level and local area inundation. (p23) [IMG, PAG]
* Residential backyard and garden were submerged in muddy floodwater. Water covered most of the garden area, reached the bases of the garden shed, hedges and a large tree, and encroached onto a wooden deck, putting items on the deck at risk of being overtaken by rising water. (p24) [IMG, PAG]
* Floodwater breached the doorway of a residential property and entered the interior, covering the floor to several centimetres, submerging outdoor furniture in the garden and contaminating the house with muddy runoff. (p25) [IMG, PAG]
* Residential area in central Worlingworth experienced heavy surface-water/urban flooding that covered the road, extended onto verges and lawns and reached up to houses; vehicles were partially submerged and access would have been impeded. (p27) [PAG]
* Surface water and overland flows affecting the eastern edge of the village (south of Church Road adjacent to the Anglian Water pumping station) were identified during the investigation. The report recommends reinstating/reprofiling watercourses and attenuation in this area to reduce flood flows. (p31) [TAB]
* Quant: over 30 properties suffered internal flooding (p4)
* Quant: over 30 properties (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 23 properties (p16)
* Quant: 37 cm internal flooding (p18) [PAG]
* Quant: 2 wheelie bins; 1 garden shed (p24) [IMG, PAG]

### Church Road, Worlingworth
* Properties on Church Road (within the Central zone) experienced internal flooding due to a combination of surface water runoff from fields, overtopping of local watercourses and overwhelmed drainage/sewer systems; part of the observed flow routes through the village. (p7)
* Homes on either side of Church Road were affected by floodwater from overwhelmed drainage networks; floodwater cascaded down the street contributing to flows toward Willow Green. (p16)
* Floodwater from an overwhelmed drainage network and from overtopped adjacent ordinary watercourses reached Church Road. Homes set higher up on either side of Church Road were affected; a west–east watercourse joins north of Church Road and flows around the back of buildings and through some gardens, contributing to inundation. A culverted section at the highway was quickly overwhelmed, causing significant overtopping that added to floodwater on the highway and into properties. (p17) [IMG, PAG]
* A watercourse running along the south side of Church Road was quickly overwhelmed by runoff; highways grips could not discharge effectively and the watercourse overtopped its banks, adding to water on the road. Water routed through a culvert under the road into front gardens on the north side and pooled at low points on the highway, flowing into properties' gardens and outbuildings. (p21)
* Highways drainage was overwhelmed by overland flows in the area. The report specifically recommends the landowner on the south side of Church Road investigate reinstating an open channel where a piped section currently exists between the river and the AW pumping station — indicating the piped/channel arrangement contributed to flow problems during the event. (p22) [IMG]
* Area around Church Road was implicated in the investigation: reinstatement/maintenance of watercourses south of Church Road (adjacent to AW pumping station) and investigation of piped sections/outfalls were recommended to address surface-water flows and drainage issues observed during the flood investigation. (p31) [TAB]
* Areas north and south of Church Road experienced surface water / overland flows. Actions proposed include reinstating an open channel between the AW pumping station and the nearby watercourse and investigating highway drainage to manage surface water. (p32) [PAG]
* Land north of Church Road and land to the south of Church Road were identified as being impacted by flood risk; actions proposed include investigating reinstatement of an open channel between the AW pumping station and the eastward watercourse, and potential highway drainage improvements to manage surface water and reduce surface water ingress/foul sewer flooding. (p33) [PAG]

### Shop Street, Worlingworth
* Shop Street (in the Central zone) experienced internal flooding of properties from surface water runoff, overtopping watercourses and overwhelmed sewer/drainage systems, forming part of the central flow paths through the village. (p7)
* Reported surcharging of the foul network and manhole flooding during high rainfall; surcharging on Shop Street has been observed and may be related to surface-water infiltration or a leak. Anglian Water investigations are ongoing in relation to this sewer/foul-network flooding. (p8) [PAG]
* During Storm Babet Shop Street experienced flooding from both overwhelmed drainage infrastructure and overtopping watercourses; drainage network overflowed, water poured from manhole covers and floodwater deepened to affect homes. (p16) [PAG]
* Floodwater from an overwhelmed drainage network and from overtopping ordinary watercourses cascaded down Shop Street, deepening and affecting homes on either side of Church Road and Shop Street. The culverted section where a watercourse reaches the highway was quickly overwhelmed, causing significant overtopping onto the highway and adding to flood depths on Shop Street. (p17) [PAG]
* Manholes on Shop Street were sealed to reduce sewer surcharge after the sewerage network was overwhelmed by surface water; sewer monitors were installed and CCTV surveys of the foul network were initiated to identify possible infiltration. (p30) [TAB]
* A piped section of an ordinary watercourse beneath the highway on Shop Street was suspected to be blocked as a consequence of the event; an investigation was recommended to confirm the outfall location and clear any blockage to reduce local flooding. (p31) [TAB]

### Willow Green, Worlingworth
* {general}: Floodwater entered properties and inundated the street area due to surface water runoff, overtopping watercourses and overwhelmed drainage/sewer systems, contributing to internal property flooding in the central zone. (p7)
* Floodwater cascaded down to Willow Green (Willow) and affected properties, with water flowing in off the highway into homes and gardens. (p16) [PAG]
* Floodwater cascaded down to Willow Green, with properties in the Close significantly affected as floodwater flowed in off the highway. Nearby watercourses overtopped and contributed to flooding around Willow Green. (p17) [IMG]

### Church Street, Worlingworth
* Parts of Church Street (noted in both Central and East zones) experienced inundation and internal property flooding caused by pluvial runoff from fields, overtopping of local watercourses and overwhelmed sewerage/drainage infrastructure. (p7)
* Included in the investigation zones (central and east) and lies within the village centre flow path; subject to surface-water/highway flooding during the event. (p8) [IMG, PAG]
* Surface-water runoff from surrounding fields routed along a watercourse pooled at the northern end adjacent to the church, overtopped and flowed through the gardens and outbuildings of nearby properties, then pooled at the highway low point and entered properties off the highway. Highway drainage assets inspected before and after the event were reported as functioning, suggesting the system was overwhelmed by rainfall. (p20)
* Properties on the north side were affected predominantly by overland surface water flow and by overtopping watercourses; floodwater flowed across gardens and outbuildings and some properties suffered internal flooding to significant depths, with some residents having to move out for months. (p21)
* Quant: over 30 properties (village-wide reported internal flooding) (p7)

### Suffolk
* Storm Babet delivered significant rainfall across the county between 18–21 October 2023, causing widespread disruption to communities and flooding impacts in multiple locations including Worlingworth. (p4) [PAG]
* Experienced the heaviest rainfall in East Anglia during the period, resulting in rapid flooding of roads and properties, rivers rising rapidly (with flows close to or exceeding record levels) and widespread disruption to road and rail networks; a major incident was declared on 20 Oct 2023. (p6)
* Storm Babet (20 Oct 2023) produced significant rainfall across Suffolk on top of a wetter-than-average October, causing internal flooding to residential and commercial properties county-wide from multiple sources including surface water runoff, overtopping watercourses and overwhelmed drainage/sewer systems. (p7)

### East Anglia
* Extreme and much-above-average rainfall in October 2023 (particularly 18–21 Oct) caused rapid rises in river systems and resulted in significant and rapid flooding of roads and properties across East Anglia. (p6) [IMG]

## Topics without coordinates

### Newtown
* Newtown (part of the West zone) experienced internal flooding to properties driven by surface water runoff from surrounding fields, overtopping of local watercourses and overwhelmed drainage/sewer systems; acted as part of the flood flow routes through the west of the village. (p7) [IMG]
* Newtown was identified as part of the West zone that was affected by flooding during the event; the report links localised high/medium surface water flood risk in the west of the catchment with the flooding experienced during Storm Babet (the October 2023 event). (p8) [IMG, PAG]
* Newtown was the most affected area in the west; a small number of properties experienced internal flooding. Contributing factors reported included blocked/poorly maintained watercourses, debris and fallen trees causing blockages, and silted ponds reducing holding capacity leading to overtopping and floodwater reaching houses. (p14)
* Overland surface water flows and ponding on saturated ground caused internal flooding of residential properties, damaging furniture, flooring and personal effects. Nearby ponds (proximate to Newtown) were silted and had reduced holding capacity, which likely exacerbated local ponding and runoff during the event. Blocked/poorly maintained local watercourses reduced effective egress and worsened flood depths and duration. (p15) [IMG, PAG]
* Junction with Water Lane experienced overwhelmed drainage and contributed to overland surface water flows onto the highway and into nearby properties; linked to a slow-draining gully requiring highway authority action. (p16)
* The highways drainage (gully) at the junction with Newtown was identified as not functioning and repairs were recommended to restore proper drainage; an investigation of a piped section under the highway (Shop Street) and confirmation of outfall location were also recommended. (p31) [TAB]

### Anglian Water
* Acting on reports of foul-network surcharging and manhole flooding on Shop Street; carrying out investigations into the cause (possible surface-water infiltration or a leak) related to flooding episodes. (p8)
* The Anglian Water foul sewerage system became overwhelmed with surface water during heavy rainfall, causing sewer capacity exceedance and contributing to flooding of roads and properties (including water emerging from manholes and watercourses overtopping into properties). (p16)
* Anglian Water assets were overwhelmed during the event; water was forced back up manhole covers, adding foul water to surface flood water. The report also notes potential groundwater intrusion into the foul sewer that may have contributed to foul-water flooding and requires investigation and remedial works. (p19) [PAG]
* Report directs Anglian Water to investigate potential groundwater intrusion into the foul sewer and to plan works to reduce occurrences of related foul-water flooding; also to investigate feasibility of increasing capacity of drainage it owns to reduce sewer/foul water flooding risk. (p20) [PAG]
* Sewer network was overwhelmed by surface water leading to interventions: sealing a number of manholes in Shop Street, installation of sewer monitors to provide early warning of blockages and a CCTV survey of the foul network to identify possible infiltration. (p30) [TAB]
* Anglian Water was recommended to carry out CCTV surveys of foul sewers and private laterals to identify roof-water connections and groundwater ingress following foul/sewer flooding observed during the event; longer-term sewer improvements were proposed to reduce surface water ingress and foul flooding. (p31) [TAB]
* Quant: at least 23 properties (p16)

### Worlingworth Hall Farm

* Located in the eastern investigation zone highlighted on the map; identified as being within the area affected by flooding during the event. (p8) [IMG, PAG]

### Recreation Ground
* The larger watercourse running between the recreation ground and Willow Tree Yard was the site of the first significant flooding on the day; the watercourse conveyance was overwhelmed/overtopped and caused floodwater at the recreation ground and contributed to downstream flooding of adjacent streets (Shop Street) and Willow Green. (p17) [IMG]
* Overland flows from multiple directions converged at the Recreation Ground; watercourses exceeded capacity and contributed to surface water inundation of the ground. The site was identified as a potential attenuation/infiltration 'overspill' area to draw floodwater away from housing. (p19) [IMG]
* Identified in the investigation as a possible location for attenuation works (storage/slow-release) to reduce surface-water runoff and manage flood flows; recommended for NFM measures to reduce future flood impact. (p31) [TAB]
* The recreation ground was affected by surface water inundation/overland flows during the event and is proposed as a site for attenuation measures. (p32)

### Sewage Works
* Flood flows were directed eastward toward the Sewage Works; Anglian Water assets were reported as overwhelmed during the event, with sewer surcharge/manhole surcharging adding to floodwater, indicating sewer-related (foul) flooding risk and surface water arrival at the works. (p19) [IMG]
* Located on the mapped flow routes and adjacent to a piped section between the river and the AW pumping station highlighted by the LLFA; subject to overland flows and pressures on the piped/drainage connection during the event. (p22) [IMG]

### Suffolk County Council
* As Lead Local Flood Authority, Suffolk County Council undertook a Section 19 Flood Investigation in response to the flooding caused by Storm Babet affecting Worlingworth and wider Suffolk. (p4) [PAG]

### Highway Authority
* Highway drainage and sewer-related impacts in Shop Street: gullies and drainage were cleared as part of response actions; manholes in Shop Street were sealed (to reduce surcharge/backflow) and drainage issues were reported for action after the heavy rainfall associated with Storm Babet. (p30)
