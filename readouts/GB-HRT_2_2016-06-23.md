

# Hatfield flooding 23 Jun 2016

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Flood Investigation: Deerswood Avenue, Hatfield<br>056\_Hertfordshire/deerswood-avenue-hatfield-flood-investigation-report.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Deerswood Avenue, Hatfield
* Surface water runoff from intense rainfall pooled on the road around residences and entered living areas; internal flooding of property occurred. Highway drainage could not cope with the scale of the rainfall; no evidence ordinary watercourse or surface water sewers were surcharged. (p1)
* {general}: Map shows surface-water flood risk concentrated along Deerswood Avenue and adjacent low-lying/green spaces; residential properties close to the green-shaded areas may be inundated or at higher risk during heavy rainfall following natural drainage paths. (p2) [IMG]
* Floodwater from the road entered property on Deerswood Avenue, causing dampened carpets in the living area and water to enter the garden. (p4)
* Properties experienced flooding during the event when water came onto the road and entered from the road/front of properties rather than from the rear; Environment Agency hazard rating for properties on Deerswood Avenue is reported as between 1.25 and 2.00 ('Significant'). (p6) [IMG]
* A localized, intense thunderstorm passed directly over Deerswood Avenue at about 18:15 on 23 June 2016, producing very high short-duration rainfall that caused rapid surface-water/flash flooding on the street associated with the incident. (p7)
* Surface water (overland) flow from the road ran downhill into the lower-lying properties, causing those properties to flood; the road acted as the source of the floodwater. (p9)
* Quant: 1 property (p1)
* Quant: 1 property reported dampened carpets; garden flooded to 6 inches (p4)

### Hatfield
* Road-surface water runoff pooled around residences and caused ingress into living areas; at least one property flooded internally. (p1)
* {general}: The mapped area of Hatfield (centred on Deerswood Avenue) is shown to have surface water flood risk in parts of the town, indicating urban/parkland areas may be affected by surface-water inundation during a 1-in-30-year event. (p2) [IMG]
* At least one property on Deerswood Avenue (in Hatfield) experienced surface water flooding on 23 June 2016 — dampened carpets and gardens flooded to 6 inches; flooding was reported to have come from the road. (p4)
* Localised surface water / flash flooding occurred during the intense thunderstorm on 23 June 2016, producing rapid runoff and reports of floodwater entering streets and properties (for example in Deerswood Avenue). (p6) [IMG]
* Hatfield experienced very intense, localised rainfall at about 18:15 on 23 June 2016 (estimated on the radar map to be within the high-intensity band). This extreme short-duration rainfall coincided with the incident and is associated with localised surface-water/flash flooding in parts of Hatfield. (p7) [IMG]
* Quant: 1 property (p1)
* Quant: 1 property (p4)

### Hertfordshire
* At least one residential property reported internal flooding (dampened living room carpets) and garden inundation to a depth of about 6 inches; flooding was reported as originating from the road. (p3)
* {general}: A receptor (property) was flooded by surface runoff in the area covered by Hertfordshire County Council. (p10) [PAG]
* Quant: 1 questionnaire return reporting internal dampened carpets; 6 inches of water in the garden (p3)

### Primrose Close, Hatfield
* A resident letter describes the stream running in culvert between Foxglove Close and Primrose Close; the culverted stream was implicated in the localised flooding that affected properties in the Primrose Close area on 23rd June 2016. (p12)

### Foxglove Close, Hatfield
* The culverted section of the stream located between Foxglove Close and Primrose Close was cited by a resident and is implicated in the localised flooding that occurred on 23rd June 2016; uncertainty over the culvert route was noted as relevant to flood flow. (p12)

### Oxleys Wood
* The ordinary watercourse emerging from Oxleys Wood was identified as playing an important role in the localized flooding on 23rd June 2016, contributing runoff that flowed down the highway and towards downstream properties; culverted sections of this watercourse were noted as relevant to flow paths. (p12)


## Topics without coordinates

### Hertfordshire County Council
* The council, as the Highway Authority responsible for highway drainage, faced an extreme rainfall event on 23 June 2016 that exceeded the capacity of highway drainage; highway gullies and outlet pipes could not discharge the volumes produced, contributing to overland flow from the road into the incident site and flooding of the local topographical low. (p8)
* A receptor in Hatfield was flooded by surface water runoff; Hertfordshire County Council, acting as the Lead Local Flood Authority and as Highway Authority, had responsibilities to identify risk management authorities and manage surface water and highway drainage response for the affected area. (p10) [PAG]
* Carried out a Section 19 FWMA 2010 investigation and published a report into surface water runoff that flowed down the highway in Hatfield; recommended investigating diversion or attenuation of runoff to reduce flow down the highway and into downstream open green space. (p11) [PAG]

### Hertfordshire Fire and Rescue Service
* Responded to a flooding call for internal property flooding; attended and provided advice only (call logged at 18:39; flooding inside noted at 18:45). (p3)

### Highway Authority
* High-intensity rainfall on 23 June 2016 (peak around 18:15) exceeded the capacity of highway drainage (gullies and outlet pipes), resulting in surface water/overland flow from the road into the incident area; the evidence indicates flooding originated from the road rather than the nearby ordinary watercourse. (p8)
