The rainfall event itself had a ~14PCSYM annual exceedance probability (≈7-year return) but the saturated soils made the catchment very sensitive and quick to respond, contributing to the flood impacts modelled at Gawcott. ### Main Street, Gawcott
* Main Street was impacted during the 23 December 2020 event when the culverted watercourse beneath/adjacent to the road exceeded capacity, causing surface water flooding that prompted appraisal of culvert improvements (replacement/enlargement/relining/parallel culvert) and targeted repairs. (p7) [TAB]
* Main Street experienced flooding during the event on 23 December 2020; a manhole was observed to be surcharged and the street was flooded (photographic evidence referenced in the report Figures 8-4 and 8-5). (p18) [PAG, IMG]
* Main Street was inundated during the 23 December 2020 event; the report and figures show flooding of the highway and a surcharged manhole on Main Street during the event. (p21) [PAG]
* Main Street experienced overtopping/surface water inundation during the event; a manhole was observed surcharged and photos show flooding of the carriageway. The surcharging and surface flooding were linked to constrained culverted flows and defects in the surface water culvert (hole in culvert wall, masonry in base, pipe crossings, reductions in culvert diameter and blockages) which reduced conveyance and led to highway flooding. (p22) [PAG]
* Floodwater flowed down both streets (Main Street and New Inn Lane), pooling at a low spot and causing internal property flooding. (p32) [TAB]
* Road closures were put in place and sandbags were delivered to the street; access and transport were disrupted and residents on the street required assistance to dispose of flood-damaged furnishings (skip arranged by the parish council). (p33)
* The culvert running below Main Street surcharged and exceeded capacity during the event, leading to surface flooding along Main Street. Manholes were reported as surcharged and blocked gullies prevented highway drainage, contributing to flooding of the street (photographic evidence in the report shows Main Street flooded during the event). (p34) [PAG, IMG]
* Traffic driving through floodwater on Main Street washed water into properties located on Main Street; culvert surcharge and manhole lifting occurred along the culvert beneath Main Street during the 23 December 2020 event. (p36)
* The culverted watercourse beneath Main Street was significantly over-capacity during the event, causing surcharging along the culvert. Manholes were lifted and began discharging water onto the carriageway from about 13:30, flooding the highway and allowing floodwater to flow west along Main Street. A surcharged manhole on Main Street was photographed overflowing onto the road surface. (p37) [IMG]
* Highway flooding was significant along these roads (Main Street and New Inn Lane), prompting transport disruption and contributing to a road closure. (p38)
* Highway flooding inundated the full carriageway and encroached onto adjacent properties and driveways, impeding vehicle and pedestrian movement. Buckinghamshire Council (the Highway Authority) ordered a road closure after 18:00 on 23rd December 2020. (p39) [IMG, PAG]
* The culverted watercourse running along Main Street was found to have blockages, intrusions and pipe size changes that reduced capacity; short sections were impassable. A Grade 5 defect (a hole) was identified close to the Methodist Chapel between manholes SW01 and SW02, with another similar hole observed on CCTV. (p40) [PAG]
* Survey identified a 300mm foul misconnection between inspection points SW02 and SW01 discharging into the 600mm brick sewer beneath the street; foul debris from this connection has been observed by residents downstream, indicating contamination of surface waters and water-quality impacts associated with the flood event. (p42) [IMG]
* The main culverted watercourse running the length of Main Street was overloaded during the event on 23 December 2020, causing surface water flooding on/around Main Street; culvert blockages and poor condition contributed to the flooding. (p45) [PAG]
* Conveyance under Main Street was a key failure point: the culvert beneath Main Street is in very poor condition with blockages, pipe-size constrictions, sedimentation and rough material that reduced conveyance causing surcharge and flooding onto Main Street (a manhole on Main Street was observed surcharged during the event). Modelling tested removing blockages and smoothing the culvert and also tested increasing culvert capacity and additional gullies to reduce surface water flooding. (p46) [PAG, IMG]
* The culvert under Main Street was blocked/defective and its capacity was exceeded during the 23 December 2020 event, causing flooding along Main Street (surcharging and local ponding). Modelling indicates relining would not have prevented flooding on 23 Dec 2020 (though it would have reduced recession times and reduced impacts in lower‑order events); enlarging or adding a parallel culvert could have prevented flooding caused by culvert capacity being exceeded, though ponding from rainfall may still have occurred. (p49)
* Constrained sections of the Main Street culvert (most of the culvert is 600mm but with short 300mm and 150mm sections and other constrictions) reduced conveyance. During the 23 December 2020 event these restrictions contributed to flooding on Main Street and slowed recession of flood levels. Blockages of masonry and roots were present in problematic sections; targeted clearing/relining and repairs are being proposed to stabilise damaged sections and remove blockages, which would reduce impacts and speed recession though would likely not have prevented flooding on 23 December 2020. (p50)
* The culvert beneath Main Street is identified as a critical infrastructure element whose capacity was predicted to be exceeded in the 1-in-10 annual chance event; exceedance of this culvert contributed to flooding at and around Main Street. The condition of the culvert is noted as an unresolved issue increasing flood risk. (p52)
* The village of Gawcott experienced flooding during the 23rd December 2020 event; flows overwhelmed local conveyance (including the culvert under Main Street), prompting recommendations to appraise upstream NFM measures while noting NFM alone would likely not have prevented the December 2020 flooding. (p53)
* The Main Street culvert was subject to excessive pressure from surface water runoff during the December 2020 flood; restricted culvert capacity and downstream channel/pond condition were cited as factors that increased flood impacts in the village. (p56)
* The main culvert under Main Street surcharged during the event, backing up through highway drainage and manholes and preventing gullies from discharging. Highway flooding was significant and Main Street was impassable during the 23 December 2020 event; culvert blockages and damaged sections were noted and works were proposed to address safety and blockages. (p60)
