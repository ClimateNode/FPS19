

# Lancashire flooding 21 Aug 2016 - 23 Aug 2016

Reference document:<br>21-23 August 2016 Flooding In Lancashire: Section 19 Investigation Report<br>069\_Lancashire County/section-19-flood-investigation-report-august-2016-flooding.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Lancashire
* Western areas experienced intense summer storms that temporarily overwhelmed local drainage systems, causing extensive flooding of properties and highways: 98 properties flooded (85 homes and 13 non-residential properties); 25 homes flooded internally to at least one habitable room; multiple highways flooded. (p3)
* Parts of the county were affected by flooding related to the event described in this report. (p10)
* Widespread heavy rainfall across the county triggered flood alerts and warnings, prompted the Environment Agency Preston Incident Room to open, and generated multiple highway flooding incidents attended by county council emergency teams between 21 and 23 August 2016. (p14) [PAG]
* Widespread intense rainfall on 21-23 August 2016 caused flooding in parts of the county, overwhelming local drainage networks in some locations and causing damage to homes and belongings and significant safety concerns among affected residents. (p17) [PAG]
* The extreme rainfall of 21-23 August 2016 overwhelmed local drainage networks in some locations, causing flooding that damaged homes and household belongings and caused significant concern for residents' safety. (p18) [PAG]
* Quant: 98 properties; 85 homes; 13 other properties; 25 homes internally flooded (p3)

### Wyre
* Within Wyre district the River Wyre overtopped banks at multiple locations (including Scorton, Catterall and Churchtown). The Environment Agency activated the Garstang flood basin (0530 on 22 Aug) and the Catterall flood basin (0945 on 22 Aug); Garstang basin reached full capacity while River Wyre levels continued rising (peaking after 1530 on 22 Aug), and the Catterall basin reached full capacity at 2200 on 22 Aug. Flooding and mud on carriageways required substantial local highway response. (p14) [IMG, PAG]
* River Wyre overtopped its banks causing flooding at Churchtown and overtopping at Scorton and Catterall; Garstang and Catterall flood basins were activated but reached full capacity (Garstang reached capacity at 15:30 on 22 Aug; Catterall at 22:00) and river levels continued to rise, contributing to local flooding. Surface water sewers also failed to operate in parts of the district (Hambleton/Stalmine) due to excessive water levels. (p15) [PAG]

### Lancashire
* Flooding was the most demanding highway defect attended by the county council's emergency call-out teams between Sunday 21 and Tuesday 23 August 2016. Incidents were recorded at Scorton and Churchtown (both in Wyre district) and at various other individual locations around the county; county teams responded to these highway flooding incidents. (p13) [PAG]

### Chorley District
* Local flood response attended by county council out-of-hours teams; time spent on flood incidents in Chorley is shown in the event summary chart. (p14) [IMG]

### Scorton
* River banks were overtopped at Scorton during the event, causing localised river flooding and prompting emergency response between 21 and 23 August 2016. (p14) [PAG]
* River banks were overtopped at Scorton and Catterall, causing flooding in those localities. (p15)

### Churchtown
* Flooding at Churchtown was caused primarily by the River Wyre overtopping its banks upstream of Kirkland Bridge (A6), producing excess water on the A6 at Kirkland Bridge and local river flooding during 21–23 August 2016. (p14) [PAG]
* Flooding in Churchtown was caused primarily by the River Wyre overtopping its banks upstream of Kirkland Bridge. (p15)

### Catterall
* River banks overtopped at Catterall causing local flooding; the nearby Catterall flood basin was activated in response to the heavy downpours. (p14) [PAG]
* River banks were overtopped at Scorton and Catterall, causing flooding in those localities. (p15)

### Hambleton
* Surface water sewers in the Hambleton/Stalmine area failed to operate as normal because of excessive water levels linked to high river and watercourse levels, leading to local drainage/sewering impacts. (p15)

### Stalmine
* Surface water sewers failed to operate normally in the Hambleton/Stalmine area due to excessive water levels associated with high river and watercourse levels. (p15)

### The Avenue, Churchtown
* {01 Dec 2015 - 31 Dec 2015, 21 Aug 2016 - 23 Aug 2016}: Homes on The Avenue were directly in the line of excess flows from the rivers and were affected by flooding (notably in December 2015 and during the August 2016 event); an upstream bund was later built to provide additional protection. (p16) [MUL]

### The Green, Churchtown
* {01 Dec 2015 - 31 Dec 2015, 21 Aug 2016 - 23 Aug 2016}: Homes on The Green were directly in the path of excess river flows and were affected by flooding (including December 2015 and the August 2016 event); protection was later provided by an upstream bund. (p16) [MUL]

### River Wyre
* Overtopped its banks upstream of Kirkland Bridge causing flooding at Churchtown; banks also overtopped at Scorton and Catterall; river flows from the rainfall event exceeded the capacity of the Garstang attenuation basin. (p14) [PAG]
* River Wyre flows rose and overtopped banks upstream of Kirkland Bridge, causing flooding at Churchtown; river banks were also overtopped at Scorton and Catterall. River flows exceeded the capacity of the Garstang flood basin despite its activation. (p15) [PAG]
* Overflow at its confluence with the Brock produced excess flows that contributed to flooding in Churchtown by passing the pinch-point under Kirkland Bridge and threatening/affecting homes on The Avenue and The Green. (p16)

### Kirkland Bridge
* River Wyre overtopped its banks upstream of Kirkland Bridge causing excess water on the A6 at the bridge; road surface was waterlogged and traffic was forced to drive through standing water. (p15) [IMG]
* The bridge location forms a pinch-point under the A6 where river flows backed up and overflowed, contributing to flooding in Churchtown; this issue led to construction of an upstream bund to divert excess water. (p16)

### Garstang Flood Basin
* Activated by the Environment Agency at 05:30 on 22 Aug 2016 to manage runoff; reached full capacity at 15:30 on 22 Aug 2016 but River Wyre levels continued rising until 16:30 so flows exceeded the basin's capacity. (p14)

### Catterall Basin
* Activated by the Environment Agency at 09:45 on 22 Aug 2016 in response to heavy downpours; reached full capacity at 22:00 on 22 Aug 2016. (p14)
