### Pix Brook
* The Pix Brook flows through Stotfold and is implicated in local flooding that affected properties in the village (notably on Coppice Mead and Brook Street). (p10)
* Culverts along the immediate area of Pix Brook were inspected and cleared of obstructions as part of the response to local flooding; culvert condition was a focus for reducing local flood impacts around Stotfold/Coppice Mead. (p13)
* The Pix Brook flowed out of bank and caused flooding in Stotfold on 12 June 2016, leading to inundation of Brook Street and Coppice Mead. (p20) [PAG]
* {04 Jul 2015, 17 Jul 2015, 07 Jun 2016 - 12 Jun 2016}: Out-of-bank flows from Pix Brook caused flooding in Stotfold (notably Brook Street and Coppice Mead) on 12 June 2016. Debris was conveyed along the brook and in the 4 July 2015 event overwhelmed and blocked the trash screen at Brook Street causing rapid upstream water level rise and local flooding. Debris was also photographed at the face of the box culvert at Hitchin Road after the June 2016 event. (p21) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
* Pix Brook overflowed during the June 2016 event causing bank erosion downstream of the confluence of the old and new channels; channel alterations (new channel with the old channel used as an overflow) have been made; evidence of erosion protection was observed and a number of properties constructed brick walls along the left bank in response to bank erosion/flooding. The IDB undertook hydraulic and blockage modelling of the Pix Brook and found that areas affected in 2016 are not well represented on the Environment Agency Flood Map for Planning. (p22) [PAG]
* Pix Brook overtopped/blocked channel flows during the June 2016 event, with debris partially blocking a box culvert at Hitchin Road and water reaching the level of air bricks on new properties. Brick walls were observed along the brook bank (photographed 2016) and floodwaters reached ground‑floor ventilation openings, evidencing property inundation risk. (p23)
* The Pix Brook experienced out-of-bank flows during the event, causing flooding in Stotfold. (p25)
* The channel capacity was exceeded and water flowed out of the Pix Brook at susceptible locations; raised water levels were exacerbated by debris and culvert restrictions causing out‑of‑bank flows. (p27)
* Culverts on Pix Brook were inspected and debris removed by IDB officers to address local flooding and reduce obstruction to flow. (p30) [TAB]
* Flooding along the Pix Brook caused flood risk within Stotfold; the brook's behaviour and catchment response are identified as key drivers of local flooding and the report recommends a catchment-wide study (including upstream Hertfordshire) and assessment of the Standalone Farm Flood Storage Reservoir operating instructions to understand and mitigate future floods. (p44) [TAB]
* Pix Brook overtopped/caused flooding that affected Stotfold and its infrastructure, contributing to debris and blockages on downstream channels and highway assets; prompted recommendations for a catchment‑wide study, modelling review and changes to asset management. (p45) [TAB, PAG]
* Pix Brook carried a significant debris load (including watercress and woody debris) from adjacent conservation woodland; debris accumulation and movement during flood flows contributed to blockages and increased flood risk in Stotfold and along the Pix Brook corridor. (p46) [TAB, PAG]
* The brook has a significant debris load that contributes to blockages and increases local flood risk; debris and blockages at structures (culverts/trash screens) were identified as a cause of flooding risk and must be allowed for in screen design and maintenance. (p47) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 11 properties; 6 properties (p20) [PAG]
* {04 Jul 2015, 17 Jul 2015, 07 Jun 2016 - 12 Jun 2016}: Quant: 11 properties; 6 properties (internal flooding) (p21) [PAG, IMG, MUL]
