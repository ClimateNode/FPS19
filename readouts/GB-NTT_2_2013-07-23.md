

# Calverton flooding 23 Jul 2013

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Calverton 23 July 2013<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/calverton-section-19-report.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Nottinghamshire
* On 23 July 2013 parts of Nottinghamshire were subjected to intense rainfall and many parts of the county experienced major flooding; properties across the county suffered flooding. (p1)

### Calverton
* A number of areas within Calverton suffered major flooding from overland (surface) flow, surcharged public sewers and highway drains, and flooded watercourses; properties in the village were flooded. (p1)
* Surface water drainage systems (surface water sewers, highway drains and ordinary watercourses) were inundated and exceeded capacity. Catchment gradients generated significant overland flows; some watercourses were operating at reduced hydraulic capacity due to lack of maintenance and some highway drainage was blocked, contributing to flooding across the village. (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* The village experienced multiple flood sources across the catchment (overland flows, overwhelmed roadside ditches and ordinary watercourse flooding) causing inundation of Main Street, residential gardens and properties in several neighbourhoods. (p3) [IMG]
* Quant: 10 - 15 businesses and properties (p3) [IMG]

### Main Street, Calverton
* Flooding was worst at Main Street where the catchment flattens out; overland flows converged at this location leading to inundation of properties and businesses. (p2) [PAG]
* Widespread inundation of the town centre: water rose to cover lower parts of buildings and streets, impeding access to shopfronts and public areas and disrupting commercial activity. (p3) [IMG, PAG]
* Quant: 10 - 15 businesses and properties (p2) [PAG]
* Quant: 10 - 15 businesses and properties (p3) [IMG, PAG]

### Crookdole Lane, Calverton
* Areas around Crookdole Lane / Cloverfields experienced flooding caused by the ordinary watercourse that serves as a main outlet for surface water from the village; investigation identified required maintenance by riparian owners. (p2) [IMG]
* Areas around Crookdole Lane (and Cloverfields) experienced flooding caused by the ordinary watercourse that acts as a main outlet for surface water from the village; investigations identified the need for maintenance by riparian owners. (p3)

### Bonner Lane, Calverton
* Flows running down Bonner Lane (together with overland flows from fields and an overwhelmed roadside ditch) resulted in flooding of gardens and a number of properties in the vicinity (Spindle View and Church View). (p2) [IMG, PAG]
* Overland flows running down Bonner Lane contributed to flooding that inundated adjacent gardens and properties; an overwhelmed roadside ditch was a contributing factor. (p3)

### Spindle View, Calverton
* Flooding from overland flows from the steep southern catchment inundated gardens and a number of properties. (p3)

### Cloverfields, Calverton
* Flooding from the ordinary watercourse (the main outlet for surface water at the east of the catchment) caused inundation around Crookdole Lane / Cloverfields; investigations identified a need for maintenance of the watercourse by riparian owners and this was communicated to responsible parties. (p3)

### Dover Beck
* Sections of Dover Beck were investigated and remedial works were carried out (by the Internal Drainage Board) in response to the flooding, indicating the watercourse was implicated in the flood incident and received works to reduce risk. (p4)


## Topics without coordinates

### Church View
* Flooding from overland flows originating in the steep southern catchment led to inundation of gardens and a number of properties. (p3)

### Severn Trent
* Sewer flooding in the catchment: Severn Trent provided emergency response crews, actively investigated sewer-flooding incidents and pursued sewer flooding data which led them to identify areas potentially at risk from the public sewerage system and to include a partnership-based flood alleviation project in their Capital Investment Programme. (p4)
* {23 Jul 2013, autumn 2018}: STW identified a number of areas potentially at risk of flooding from the public sewerage system and included a partnership-based flood alleviation project for the area in its Capital Investment Programme; the project will assess hydraulic performance of drainage assets across the catchment to identify options to reduce flood risk. (p5) [MUL]

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Coordinated emergency planning and response during the flood event (initiated and co‑ordinated emergency planning procedures, provided emergency response crews), initiated and led the Section 19 Flood Investigation, and commissioned/funded a summary report into the catchment flooding and possible future actions. (p4)
* Nottinghamshire County Council liaised with local MP, Calverton Parish Council and community representatives and exercised its functions in response to the flood to clarify responsibilities and provide support to aid future flood resilience. (p5)

### Gedling Borough Council
* Participated in management of the flooding event by providing emergency response support, actively engaging in the Section 19 flood investigation and administering DEFRA Repair and Renew grants to affected parties. (p4)
