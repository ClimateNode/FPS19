### River Irwell
* River levels in the Upper Irwell rose rapidly in response to heavy, long-duration rainfall on saturated ground, producing fast-onset, widespread flooding across the Upper Irwell catchment and contributing to devastating flooding in Greater Manchester (depths in parts of the area were reported as so great that residents had to be evacuated by boat). (p13) [PAG]
* On 26th December 2015 river levels in the Irwell catchment rose very rapidly and reached exceptionally high peak levels (many sites recorded their highest-ever Boxing Day levels), producing fluvial flooding across downstream parts of the Irwell catchment in Greater Manchester and overwhelming planned pre-emptive actions and communications. (p14)
* Multiple gauging stations on the River Irwell across Greater Manchester recorded their highest-ever levels on Boxing Day, producing widespread river flooding along the Irwell corridor (examples include 6.330 m at Kearsley Ultrasonic and 5.668 m at Manchester Racecourse). (p15) [TAB, PAG]
* River Irwell catchment experienced exceptionally high river levels contributing to widespread flooding across the catchment area; record gauge readings correlate with flooded properties in nearby areas. (p20) [IMG]
* The River Irwell catchment was a primary focus of the heavy rainfall and recorded very high/record river levels, causing major river flooding, damage to river channel retaining walls, culverts and bridges, mobilisation of large volumes of gravel downstream, and contributing to extensive internal property flooding. (p29)
* High flows in the River Irwell caused flooding in the local area; the river inundated Prestolee properties resulting in internal flooding of homes (see Prestolee impacts). (p41) [PAG]
* The River Irwell rose to exceptionally high levels at Kearsley (6.33m, a 13‑year record), with river level peaks driven by intense rainfall in the catchment. High river levels were associated with infrastructure damage including the collapse of a United Utilities pipe bridge at Kearsley and damage to Prestolee pumping station. (p42) [IMG]
* The River Irwell overtopped natural banks and caused river flooding that entered properties. Floodwaters from the Irwell internally flooded multiple properties and at least one business; floodwaters affected riverside streets and residential blocks in the local area. (p44) [PAG]
* The River Irwell reached exceptionally high levels at multiple gauging stations and caused flooding that inundated properties (notably in Radcliffe and Redvales). Recorded high water levels coincided with property flooding in the Bury area. (p56) [PAG]
* High River Irwell levels caused residential and commercial property flooding in the catchment; the Waterside Restaurant in Summerseat collapsed into the river causing partial collapse and closure of the associated masonry arch bridge; flood-related infrastructure failures (including the Lomax Street footbridge collapse, gas main rupture and sewage discharge) occurred along the river. (p57) [IMG, PAG]
* The River Irwell reached extremely high levels (estimated rare event) and overtopped/breached banks causing flooding at Bury, Radcliffe and Redvales; flood sedimentation and damage to river monitoring equipment led to scheduled gravel removal and gauging-station repairs; flood warning areas (GM41/GM42) and thresholds were reviewed following the event. (p63)
* The River Irwell overtopped its banks and caused flooding at Pioneer Mill, inundating the mill from three sides. (p65)
* The River Irwell overtopped at Chamberhall, flooding adjacent roads and industrial properties; water also seeped under a raised boundary wall and emerged behind it, and debris and significant inundation were observed at Chamberhall. (p69) [IMG, PAG]
* Extremely high River Irwell levels on 26 December 2015 (reported at Bury and Pioneer Mill) caused river flooding — the raised wall delayed onset but was not a formal flood defence, and river water seeped beneath a boundary wall and emerged behind it. (p70)
* Exceptionally high river levels caused main-river flooding on 26 December 2015, prevented surface water drainage outfalls, contributed to inundation of culverts and overland flows that combined with river flooding in low-lying areas; this contributed to property and business flooding and to damage at culverts (notably at Crow Lane/Bridge Street). (p72)

Bridges were temporarily closed and inspected; gravel removal and channel repairs were part of recovery works. (p74)
* High river levels in the River Irwell overtopped raised defences and natural banks at Cheetham Hill and caused flooding which exceeded the capacity of the surface water network; non-residential properties in the area were inundated. (p83) [IMG]
* River levels at Salford reached a record high of 5.67 m (29.83 AOD), 1.3 m higher than any previous reading in 75 years; a Flood Warning was issued for the River Irwell (Salford Area B). (p84) [PAG]

### Greater Manchester
* Widespread flooding across Greater Manchester resulted from intense, prolonged rainfall on saturated catchments. The event produced very high river levels (many rivers at their highest recorded levels) and caused significant impacts to communities, businesses, transport infrastructure and the environment. (p6)
* Widespread flooding on 26 December 2015 that flooded homes and businesses across the area. Addresses of flooded homes and businesses were collected and verified; people claimed the £500 Community Recovery payment; road closures occurred and emergency services recorded call-outs and responses. (p8)
* Storm Eva (Christmas Day and Boxing Day 2015) brought the heaviest rain over Pennine areas north of Manchester; soils were saturated beforehand and close to capacity, producing high runoff and elevated flood risk across the Greater Manchester area during the 25–26 December 2015 event. (p12) [PAG]
* Widespread and rapid flooding occurred across Greater Manchester due to heavy rainfall on saturated ground. Rivers (notably the Irwell, Roch and Glaze) rose rapidly producing deep inundation in places; parts of Bolton, Salford and Rochdale experienced such depths that residents were evacuated by boat. A Major Incident was declared and recovery operations followed as levels receded by Boxing Day evening. (p13)
* Widespread flooding across the county on 26 December 2015: many river gauges recorded their highest-ever levels (Rank 1) and major reaches of the Irwell catchment experienced extreme river levels and flows leading to river flooding of communities and infrastructure. (p15)
* The Greater Manchester area experienced widespread internal property flooding during the event; the report provides an overview map of flooding across the area. (p19)
* Widespread flooding across the metropolitan area caused internal flooding of properties, issued direct flood warnings to residents, and prompted investment and mitigation planning. (p20) [IMG, PAG]
* Widespread river and surface-water flooding across the Irwell, Roch and Croal catchments: 37 of 44 river-level gauges recorded their highest-ever levels. Flooding exceeded defence levels used for infrastructure resilience. Large-scale electricity supply loss across the conurbation (see local breakdowns); six electrical sub-stations were flooded or damaged, mobile generators were mobilised. Multiple bridges were damaged or displaced; some required extensive permanent repairs. (p21) [PAG]
* Widespread flooding across the region caused transport disruption (tram services suspended on several routes; major rail services disrupted and route checks required; road closures, diversions and impassable roads), damage to Environment Agency assets and river defences (including destruction/damage of river gauging stations), and flooding at wastewater treatment works and pumping stations which affected treatment processes and recovery operations. Local authority offices in town centres were flooded, affecting servers and slowing recovery coordination. (p22)
* Widespread flood impacts across the county included inundation and operational disruption of wastewater treatment works (Bury flooded; Rochdale and Urmston affected), combined sewer overflow discharges and polluted runoff affecting water quality, mobilisation and deposition of large quantities of gravel and masonry rubble altering channels and increasing local flood or erosion risk, habitat damage and potential spread of invasive species, and local slope failures beside watercourses due to waterlogged ground. (p23)
* A major incident was declared and a multi‑agency response operated across the area. Rest centres were opened for displaced people. Significant repair works were required to highways, cycle paths, footpaths, river channels, retaining walls and drainage systems. An extensive cleanup operation collected and disposed of waste and debris. Electricity substations were inundated and defences overtopped causing power losses; Distribution Network Operators undertook emergency repairs. United Utilities saw a large increase in wastewater incidents and attended many sites; properties were flooded from sewers. A Recovery Coordinating Group was formed to manage recovery activity. (p25) [PAG]
* River flooding from main rivers on 26 December 2015 affected the area; many properties received direct flood warnings and others benefited from defences and operational protection; some parts that flooded did not receive a flood warning and are subject to review. (p26)
* River flooding on 26 December 2015 affected multiple boroughs; Environment Agency and other RMAs carried out visual inspections of flood defence assets, recorded damage to assets and programmed emergency and minor repairs to walls and embankments in locations including Rochdale and Littleborough. (p27)
* Largest recorded flood across the area with record high river levels in the Irwell, Roch and Croal catchments; widespread internal property flooding and damage to river channels, retaining walls, culverts and bridges; local wastewater treatment plant performance affected causing effluent discharge to rivers. (p29)
* Greater Manchester experienced extreme rainfall and widespread flooding on Boxing Day that devastated many communities across the area; flooding mechanisms were complex with the majority caused by main rivers and further properties affected by surface water, ordinary watercourse and groundwater flooding. RMAs have continued work across the area on inspections, repairs and community engagement. (p30) [PAG]
* Widespread flooding affected communities across the metropolitan area; risk management authorities (RMAs) and responders worked together in incident response and recovery to support affected communities and have produced a joint flood investigation report with recommendations and next steps. * Widespread flooding across Greater Manchester on 26th December 2015 that caused damage to infrastructure and local flood defences, flooding of communities across multiple boroughs and prompted multi-agency recovery, investigations and repair works (including collapsed river retaining walls, damaged channel walls and collapsed lengths of flood defence in specific locations). (p32) [TAB, PAG]
