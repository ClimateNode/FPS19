### Lincolnshire
* A forecast-led coastal surge event in early December 2013 led to a large multi-agency emergency response in Lincolnshire; the surge caused overtopping and breaches of defences, evacuations, rescues of people isolated by floodwaters, and inundation of properties. (p2) [IMG]
* County‑wide sea defences were severely tested protecting homes, businesses and 220,000 hectares; widespread standing water was managed by drainage and high‑volume pumping; Boston (within the county) suffered the largest number of flooded residential properties. (p5)
* The event required multi-agency coordination in Lincolnshire: reliance on mutual aid and national specialist assets, prolonged deployments of responders, and targeted welfare, evacuation and medical support for vulnerable people. Recommendations were made to the Local Resilience Forum to improve planning, information-sharing and integration of community volunteers. (p6)
* Coastal surge and associated high tides/onshore winds can cause overtopping and defence breaches affecting Lincolnshire: mapped flood depth, velocity and extent have been developed for defence overtopping and defence breach scenarios. Reasonable worst-case (multiple breaches) impacts include large numbers of properties, infrastructure and casualties; overtopping-only scenarios (1:200 and 1:1000 annual chance tide levels) give quantified property and premise impacts. (p7)
* Planning assessments and the December surge response show Lincolnshire exposed to coastal/tidal flooding from overtopping and multiple defence breaches. Large numbers of properties and critical assets were identified as being at risk under defined surge scenarios; contingency planning and multi-agency response arrangements were activated/considered for the county. (p8)
* The tidal surge overtopped around 18–20 km of flood defences, caused large areas of scour (e.g. Mablethorpe), produced four breach locations (Boston Haven, Gibraltar Point/Bull Dog Bank, Tetney Marsh and Friskney), inundated agricultural land, and damaged industrial/commercial sites and local infrastructure (including damage to pumps at Black Sluice Gate and failure of floodwall at Skegness). (p12)
* Defence overtopping and flood damage to coastal visitor centres: Gibraltar Point Visitor Centre and the Wash Study Centre. Preventive action at Donna Nook allowed seals to seek refuge on higher ground in the dunes; reported seal mortalities were low. (p14) [PAG]
* {general}: The Environment Agency raised concern about pre-identified flood-defence seepage at Boston in the run-up to the 5 December 2013 event; this seepage was identified as a potential vulnerability during the precautionary strategic coordination briefing. (p15)
* Lincolnshire was placed under an anticipated Amber flood guidance level for the coastal/ tidal surge on 5 December 2013; multi‑agency contingency planning, pre‑deployment of assets and precautionary SCG arrangements were implemented across the county in response to the threat of spray, overtopping and a predicted River Haven surge. (p16) [PAG]
* The county faced a medium overall risk of significant coastal/tidal flooding (FGS raised to 'Amber'); Flood Alerts were issued for the Lincolnshire coastline, coastal floodgates were patrolled/closed and contingency planning and escalation arrangements (including opening the County Emergency Centre) were activated for the predicted high tides on 5–6 December 2013. (p17) [PAG]
