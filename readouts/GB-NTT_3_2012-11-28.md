

# Carlton-On-Trent flooding 28 Nov 2012

Reference document:<br>Section 19 Report – Carlton On Trent 28th November 2012<br>093\_Nottinghamshire County/carlton-on-trent-section-19-report.pdf

## Place names with coordinates

### Nottinghamshire
* Many parts of the county experienced major flooding following intense rainfall; many properties across the county were affected. (p1)

### Carlton-On-Trent
* Internal flooding of properties in the village; overland flow was the main contributing source. (p1) [PAG]
* The village experienced flooding caused by pluvial runoff from surrounding fields and by highway drainage gullies being at or above capacity; water was channelled through the village (notably toward Ferry Lane, Main Street and Church Lane). (p2) [IMG]
* Identified by the EA as being at significant risk of fluvial flooding from the River Trent; subject to monitoring, community engagement and consideration for potential flood mitigation schemes; NCC and the EA continue to work with the community on flood mechanism understanding and resilience. (p4)
* Quant: 5 properties (p1) [PAG]

### Ferry Lane, Carlton-On-Trent
* Significant surface water from surrounding fields flowed onto and along Ferry Lane, channelling through the village and overwhelming highway drainage infrastructure. (p2) [IMG]

### Church Lane, Carlton-On-Trent
* Surface water runoff from surrounding fields reached Church Lane and was channelled through the village; highway drainage was at or above capacity and was overwhelmed. (p2) [IMG]

### Main Street, Carlton-On-Trent
* Surface water from surrounding fields was channelled onto and along Main Street; highway drainage capacity was exceeded and the road was inundated by surface water during the event. (p2) [IMG]

### River Trent
* The River Trent is identified as the source of fluvial flooding risk affecting Carlton on Trent and other villages in the Trent Valley, prompting monitoring and emergency response. (p4)


## Topics without coordinates

### Highway Authority
* {general}: High levels of surface water collected on roads overwhelmed the existing highway drainage infrastructure; the standard design capacity of the highway drainage was not sufficient to cope with the exceptional rainfall, resulting in road drainage being overwhelmed and roads becoming flooded. (p3)

### Environment Agency
* Provided emergency response crews to assist in management of the flooding event and continued to monitor river levels; identified Carlton on Trent as being at significant risk of fluvial (river) flooding and is considering potential mitigation schemes; engaged with the community on flood mechanism and resilience. (p4)

### Trent Valley
* Area being considered for potential fluvial flood mitigation schemes along the River Trent; communities in the Trent Valley (including villages like Carlton on Trent) are the focus of monitoring, engagement and potential future works due to flood risk. (p4)
