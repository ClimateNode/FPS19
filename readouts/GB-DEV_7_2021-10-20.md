### Seaton

### Axminster
* Axminster was one of the worst affected locations; flooding occurred from an ordinary watercourse and surface water runoff leading to internal property flooding as part of the 20–21 October 2021 event. (p1) [PAG]
* Identified as one of the worst-affected locations during the 20–21 October 2021 event; intense localised rainfall led to overwhelmed drainage and overtopping watercourses, causing severe surface water and river-related flooding and internal flooding of multiple properties. (p4) [PAG, IMG]
* Widespread surface water and ordinary watercourse (Millbrook Stream) flooding across the town. Overtopping of the Millbrook Stream and enhanced surface runoff caused internal flooding of properties and damage to gardens and outbuildings. (p5) [IMG]
* Properties in Axminster were flooded from overtopping and out-of-bank Millbrook flows and from culvert blockage/backing up. Impacts included internal property flooding, deep ground-floor flooding at one property, damage to public open space and footpaths, and large deposits of material. (p7) [PAG, IMG]
* Multiple watercourses and surface runoff caused widespread flooding across Axminster. The Weycroft area had nine properties flooded (three due to the River Axe overtopping and six primarily from ordinary watercourse and surface runoff). The flood bank upstream of Stoney Bridges was overtopped. Surface water runoff from adjacent farmland into Lodge Lane and overtopping of an unnamed ordinary watercourse caused significant flooding in Weycroft around 20:30–21:00 on 20 October 2021, which was then exacerbated by the Axe overtopping around 23:30 with peak river conditions at 00:05 and 03:00 on 21 October 2021. At least two other ordinary watercourses overtopped where culverted under the A358 during peak flows, causing property flooding in the southern urban extent. A local school and church also experienced surface water flooding; garden and road flooding occurred on multiple streets including Musbury Road, Fairy Lane, Lyme Road and Stoney Lane. (p10) [PAG]

### Willhay Lane, Axminster
* A significant flowpath from the overtopping Millbrook conveyed floodwater down Willhay Lane during the 20 October 2021 event, flooding lowest-lying properties via the road and footpaths and causing garden and outbuilding damage. Overtopping at the Willhay Lane culvert was a primary mechanism affecting properties. (p6)

* {2012, 20 Oct 2021 - 21 Oct 2021}: A culvert at Willhayes Lane overtopped during peak flows, producing a concentrated flowpath down the lane and onto adjacent properties, gardens and outbuildings; flooding affected parcels of land north of the stream and flowed via footpaths and the road network to low-lying properties (including Lynch Close and North Street). The culvert had limited conveyance prior to improvements; following replacement works the scale of internal property flooding in 2021 was reduced compared with 2012. (p8) [PAG, MUL]
* Flows overtopping the Willhay Lane culvert and local surface runoff created a significant flowpath down Willhay Lane, conveying water south toward low-lying properties via footpaths and the road, flooding allotments, gardens and outbuildings and affecting properties (some located more than 300 m from the Millbrook). Properties in Lynch Close and North Street were also impacted by out-of-bank flows linked to this flowpath. (p11) [PAG, IMG]

### Devon
* On the evening of 20 October and early 21 October 2021 intense, localised rainfall caused watercourses to overtop and drainage systems to be overwhelmed across Devon, leading to multiple properties flooding across the county. The worst affected places included Axminster and Seaton; other affected places included Kenton, Woodbury, Slapton, Kingsbridge and Modbury. (p1) [PAG]
* Widespread surface water and urban flooding across parts of Devon on 20 October to early 21 October 2021: major highways were affected (A30 closed in sections by police due to crashes associated with surface water flooding; A3052 between White Hart Inn and Axmouth experienced ordinary watercourse overtopping and surface water causing vehicles to become stuck). Significant highway flooding reported in and around Hemyock and Stockland. Road flooding affected East Devon locations including Holcombe, Shute, Whitford and Uplyme. Church Street and Mill Street in Kingsbridge flooded with areas reported up to around 0.5 m depth. (p5) [IMG]
