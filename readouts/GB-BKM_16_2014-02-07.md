

# Willows Estate flooding 07 Feb 2014

## Place names with coordinates

### Willows Estate
* On 7th February 2014 the Willows housing estate was flooded after prolonged above‑average rainfall saturated the catchment. Intense rain in the early hours produced surface water runoff that the surface water drains and nearby watercourses could not convey, leading to flooding of the estate. High river/ditch levels (Stoke Brook and Sedrup Ditch) and possible culvert blockage under the A418, plus debris and fly‑tipped rubbish, exacerbated flows onto roads and residential areas. (p4)
* {24 Dec 2013, 05 Jan 2014, 07 Feb 2014}: The Willows area experienced multiple flood incidents during winter 2013/2014; the most severe on 7th February 2014 caused internal flooding to houses in the neighbourhood. (p6) [MUL]
* The Willows housing estate was affected by flooding on 7 February 2014, prompting a multi-authority flood investigation. Meetings were held (including 14 March 2014) and a site visit to the estate took place on 17 March 2014 where contact was made with local residents to gather information about the flood impacts. (p7) [IMG]
* Gully outfalls from the surface water drains on the Willows housing estate were silted where they discharged to the Stoke Brook; this condition reduced drainage capacity and was a factor during the heavy rainfall on 7th February 2014. (p15)
* Internal and external flooding across the estate: properties and roads flooded, evacuation of residents, rest centre opened and pastoral support provided, sand/gel bags distributed, pumping and clearing of blockages enabled water to retreat. (p19) [TAB]
* Internal property flooding across the housing estate caused by runoff/drainage being unable to discharge to the Stoke Brook which was running very full; silted ditches and limited pipe capacity contributed to ponding and inundation of properties. (p21) [TAB]
* Flow backed up towards the Willows estate from the culvert under Oxford Road, causing water to pond around the area. The ditch alongside Ellen Road was very silted and water ponded there; AVDC cleared and re-profiled this ditch after the flood to help drainage. (p22)
* Raised water levels occurred upstream alongside the Willows estate due to backing up at the A418 culvert and restricted conveyance in Stoke Brook during the heavy rainfall event. (p27)
* The Willows residential estate was affected by flood flows and drainage problems: recommendations note a low embankment alongside the estate, the need to maintain and clear surface water drains and gullies, and measures to prevent backing up of water into the estate during high Stoke Brook levels. (p28) [PAG]
* Floodwater from Stoke Brook and blocked surface-water drains/gullies affected the Willows residential area. A low embankment alongside the Willows estate allowed flow towards properties; gullies and outfalls became silted/blocked requiring routine clearing as part of recovery and to reduce future ponding and backflow into properties. (p29) [PAG]

### Sedrup Ditch
* Prior saturation from prolonged rainfall left Stoke Brook and Sedrup Ditch close to capacity; subsequent heavy rain on 7th February 2014 produced flows the channels could not convey, contributing to flooding of nearby roads and the Willows estate. Elevated levels were worsened by tree/vegetation debris and fly‑tipped rubbish; a culvert under the A418 may have been blocked or undersized, reducing conveyance. (p4)
* Sedrup Ditch joins the Stoke Brook immediately upstream of the culverts under the A418 (Oxford Road) and Pearson Close; it was identified in the location description and investigated in relation to the 7 February 2014 flood event. (p8)
* {general}: Shown on the Environment Agency fluvial flood maps to inundate areas along its course in 1:100 (flood zone 3) and 1:1000 (flood zone 2) events; floodwater would flow from the ditch onto adjacent land. (p9) [IMG]
* On 7th February 2014 intense/peak rainfall fell onto saturated alluvial soils with poor infiltration, so rainfall on that day would have run off rapidly into local watercourses, including the Sedrup Ditch, increasing flow and the potential for flooding in the local drainage network. (p12)
* Sedrup Ditch was described as being close to bank full prior to and during the rainfall on 7th February 2014, indicating it was at or near capacity and contributed to flooding risk in the catchment. (p15)
* Flow was observed to back up at the culvert under Oxford Road (A418), with water backing up from the culvert back towards the Willows estate — likely caused by a blocked or undersized culvert impeding river flow and producing upstream flooding/overland flow into the estate. (p23)
* Raised flows and water levels in Stoke Brook (and the Sedrup ditch) caused or contributed to flooding in the Willows area, caused backflow pressure on gully/outfalls and prompted recommendations for upstream attenuation, embankment works, storage and maintenance to reduce future flood impacts. (p28)
