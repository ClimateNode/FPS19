### River Mole
* Fluvial flooding from the River Mole and its tributaries caused inundation across river floodplains in Surrey during the winter 2013-14 storms, contributing to local flood impacts in the Reigate and Banstead area. (p10)
* The River Mole experienced very high flows during winter 2013-14 that produced fluvial flooding and inundation across river floodplains in Surrey. Peak flows were driven by the 23–24 December 2013 storm (largest recorded), with further significant peaks around 17 January 2014 and 1 February 2014. Gauging stations in the middle Mole (Dorking and Leatherhead) exceeded their capacity during the December/January peaks so peak flows were not fully recorded at those sites. (p11) [IMG, TAB, PAG, MUL]
* The middle Mole experienced extreme river levels during the 23–24 December 2013 storm that exceeded the capacity of gauging stations (Dorking and Leatherhead). The river exhibited flash behaviour — levels rose rapidly and returned to normal about 10 hours after peak. Significant high flows were also recorded in the middle Mole area on 17 January 2014 and 1 February 2014. (p12) [MUL]
* {December 2013 - February 2014, 01 Dec 2013 - 28 Feb 2014}: High flows in the River Mole caused flooding across Horley and the catchment, contributing to road closures and internal property flooding; river levels in Horley reached record highs. (p21) [TAB, MUL]
* {01 Dec 2013 - 28 Feb 2014}: Extremely high river flows at the Horley gauge during December 2013–February 2014; river levels in Horley were the highest since EA records began (approximately 1985). (p22) [TAB]

* High river flows in the River Mole catchment produced fluvial flooding and inundation across river flood plains in Surrey. The Mole experienced its maximum peak flow as a consequence of the 23 December 2013 storm and a rapid succession of high-flow events through winter 2013-14, triggering fluvial flood warnings across the area. (p11) [MUL]
* {December 2013 - February 2014, winter 2013/14}: During winter 2013/14 (notably the storms of 23–24 December 2013) the catchment experienced a sequence of high flows that caused road closures, disruption on the A23 (24 December 2013) and internal property flooding in Horley and Salfords.
