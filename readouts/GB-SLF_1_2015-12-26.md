* EA services were under pressure during the Boxing Day river flooding: the Flood Warning Service issued widespread warnings in a short time and some warnings were not issued due to rapid forecast changes; communication between EA officers and responders was poor, delaying recognition and escalation of the major incident and contributing to operational confusion (including flood basin activation failures). ### Littleton Road Flood Basin
* The basin activated for the first significant time since construction; significant volumes of floodwater entered and were stored there until River Irwell levels subsided, but storage was insufficient to prevent flooding in Lower Broughton. (p3) [PAG]
* The presence of the basin mitigated the impact of the Boxing Day flood event, reducing the flood impact to properties in Salford. (p4)
* Floodwater overtopped a footpath and flowed into the Littleton Road Flood Basin on 26th December 2015, submerging the path and depositing debris as the basin received excess water. (p12) [IMG]
* {26 Dec 2015, 27 Dec 2015}: Flood water was stored within the Littleton Road Flood Basin following the event; aerial photography on 27th December shows the basin containing flood water. (p14) [MUL]
* {26 Dec 2015, 27 Dec 2015}: Flood water was stored within the Littleton Road Flood Basin during the event; by 27th December flood water remained held in the basin. (p15) [IMG, PAG, MUL]
* Was operated and stored a significant volume of flood water during the incident. (p35)
* Operated as a monitoring/defence asset during the event; telemetry from this basin is cited as a source of warnings that should be shared with Salford City Council to enable better preparedness and action. (p40)
* Activation procedures for the Littleton Road Flood Basin were not followed during the event: delays occurred in closing footpaths, there were insufficient resources to manage site security, and a second basin activated by default and was flooded without plans, warnings or security in place. (p46)

### Manchester Ship Canal Company
* Sluice gates on the Manchester Ship Canal were relevant to managing water levels; EA contacted MSCC and MSCC attended site to ensure sluices operated (operation noted as automatic). (p14)
* The company was contacted late in the flooding event about high water levels and reports of flooding from the canal in the vicinity of Irlam and Latchford locks and north of Lymm (Thelwall). MSCCo attended a Cheshire Incident Management teleconference, shared information on how flows were being managed down the canal and contributed to the response conversation. (p21)
* MSCC (the canal operator) managed flows down the Manchester Ship Canal during the event and the canal experienced bank erosion adjacent to the Valero Energy oil storage facility and on the Trafford bank. (p22) [PAG]
* Issued a public statement describing actions taken in response to the flooding and engaged with local authorities on incident response arrangements for flooding from the Manchester Ship Canal. (p23)
* As riparian owner and navigation authority for the Manchester Ship Canal, MSCC/Peel issued a statement following the flooding to demonstrate how their duties were fulfilled in relation to their structures and responsibilities. (p36)
* {general}: Identified as a coordinating organisation during the river flood response; recommended for regular meetings with the EA and LLFAs to improve coordination and develop action plans for future flooding events. (p40)

### Castle Irwell Flood Basins

### Frederick Road Bridge
* Water levels rose and overtopped flood defences to the south east of the bridge, resulting in flooding of the adjacent area (adjacent to Heath Avenue). (p3)
* Flood water overtopped flood defences in the vicinity of Frederick Road Bridge (photographic evidence shows water flowing over the barrier), raising concerns about overtopping of the river wall nearby and inundation of adjacent areas. (p13) [IMG]
* River levels rose to close to the base of the bridge and encroached onto adjacent grassy banks. The bridge acted as a hydraulic restriction, contributing to a localised rise in upstream water levels and creating a risk of overtopping; water was fast-flowing and turbid around the structure. (p26) [IMG]

### Greater Manchester Police
* Contacted Salford City Council/out-of-hours service and were involved in reporting flooding at the Littleton Road Flood Basin on 26 December 2015 as residents reported inundation. (p12)
* Operational flood response: informed residents by 22:00 that it was safe to re-enter properties after floodwaters receded. (p14)
* Police resources were directed quickly and appropriately using live information from Salford City Council CCTV to respond to flood-affected locations. (p42)
* {general}: GMP officers from other boroughs who were unfamiliar with the local area provided incorrect locations to residents; limited numbers of emergency officers in the borough hindered evacuation decisions during the flood incident. (p44)

### Irwell Catchment
* Heaviest rainfall occurred in the upper parts of the River Irwell catchment, producing an extreme catchment response that led to flooding in downstream areas including Salford. (p2) [PAG]
* Excessive rainfall on Boxing Day fell across the catchment, running off saturated ground into tributaries and increasing flows and water levels, causing flooding across the catchment. (p3) [PAG]

### Salix
* Salix assisted with housing displaced residents, gathered information from affected residents, provided housing officers to support those impacted, and worked with other services on the cleanup following the flooding on 26th December 2015. (p43) [PAG]
* Salix tenants were perceived to have received a different level of response compared to non-Salix occupants during the flood, with a lack of consistency in response and service reported. (p44)
* Tenants of Salix affected by the Boxing Day flooding received inconsistent and unequal post‑flood information during the recovery phase; different quality of information was fed back to residents, hindering coordinated support. (p46)

### Latchford Locks
* Flooding from the Manchester Ship Canal occurred in the vicinity of Latchford Locks (high water levels and reported flooding downstream of the canal). (p21)

### Peel Holdings

### United Utilities

(p27)
* The area affected by the 26 December 2015 flood is served largely by combined public sewers; United Utilities, as the sewerage undertaker responsible for the public sewer network, has ongoing duties and works in response to the flooding. (p36)

### Greater Manchester Authorities

### Salford Flood Management Plan
* Plan deficiencies were exposed by the flood response: it lacked specific evacuation process guidance (including for vulnerable people), did not include response guidance for businesses, lacked reception/rest centre suitability guidance linked to flood risk, did not document recovery actions, and did not clarify sandbag issue protocols or Emergency Control Centre activation procedures. (p47) [PAG]
