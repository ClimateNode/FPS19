* On 23 December 2020 intense rainfall over a saturated catchment produced a rapid catchment response; radar and gauge data show substantial rainfall (radar total ~48 mm; local gauge record nearby up to 52 mm) falling on soils with very low moisture deficit (0–10 mm), which led to rapid runoff and surface-water/flash flooding across the Tingewick catchment. Residents were affected and received assistance from Buckinghamshire Fire and Rescue Service and Transport for Buckinghamshire. (p33)

A culvert running under Main Street was surcharged and its capacity exceeded, several manholes along Main Street surcharged, blocked gullies and a collapsed surface water pipe (below West Well Lane) exacerbated flow. Surface water followed Main Street and West Well Lane, leading to internal property flooding (including properties adjacent to the Education Facility). (p34) [PAG]
* The ordinary watercourse through the village rose rapidly and Townsend Pond and the culverted watercourse were overwhelmed on 23 December 2020. The culvert under Main Street was surcharged and its capacity exceeded, preventing the pond and surface water drainage systems from draining. This led to surcharging of private drainage, manhole flooding in rear gardens, pooling of water in gardens and flows along Main Street from the western toward the eastern end of the village. (p35)
* Overtopping of Townsend Pond led to floodwater flowing down Main Street and extensive internal and external flooding across the village. At least 20 properties were flooded internally (including one commercial property, The Royal Oak, and 19 residential properties). Many residents were forced into temporary accommodation, reported insurance claims and financial burden, and experienced mental-health impacts and stress. Internal flooding entered properties through front doors and in some cases through floors; floodwater depths were worst towards the western end of Main Street. Flooding began after pond overtopping around 17:00 and dissipated rapidly from about 23:00, with no remaining flooding after 02:00 on 24 December as the pond level fell and the culverted watercourse drained the road. (p37)
* Internal and external flooding across the village: extensive internal flooding of properties (including whole-ground-floor inundation in worst cases), prolonged drying times for homes, damage to carpets/flooring/furniture, one property’s boiler damaged by flooding which subsequently caused a house fire. At least some households were displaced or lived on upper floors while repairs occurred. (p38)
* The culverted watercourse through Tingewick (along Main Street) and connecting surface water sewer were obstructed during the observed flood event, which slowed discharge to the downstream open watercourse. A 35PCSYM vegetation blockage was identified at the downstream end of the culvert and the downstream connection from the West Well Lane surface water sewer into the culvert was partially blocked by masonry bricks and root growth; these conditions were reproduced in the hydraulic baseline model for the 23 December 2020 flood. (p39)
* Surface water flooding affected the village on 23 December 2020; the surface water drainage system was overloaded (model baseline calibrated to reported flood depths for that date). A collapsed culvert on West Well Lane prior to the event caused backing up of water that contributed to the flooding. (p41) [PAG]
* Flooding from the ordinary watercourse affected the village; modelling of the 23 December 2020 event considered an upstream storage area to the west of the village which, in the model, operates online and captures water from the ordinary watercourse as a mitigation measure. (p42)

(p44)

Floodwater entered many properties through doors and floors; a number of affected properties had suspended timber floors and low-level airbricks which contributed to internal entry. (p45) [PAG]

Internal flooding entered mainly via rear doors; many ground-floor rooms flooded and six properties reported the entire ground floor flooded. ### Main Street, Tingewick
* A 600 mm culvert conveying discharge from Townsend Pond runs beneath the entire length of Main Street; during the 23 December 2020 event the pond overtopped and routed flows into this culvert and the village surface water system, making the culvert and highway corridor a pathway for floodwater. (p17) [PAG, IMG]
* {general}: Highway gullies along Main Street were observed to be blocked (site visit May 2021), preventing drainage from the highway at those locations. The culverted watercourse runs under the entire length of Main Street and the highway gullies are reported to drain to that culvert, meaning blocked gullies caused local drainage failure and surface water accumulation on the highway. (p19) [PAG, IMG]
* Main Street is shown as having a high risk of surface water flooding and flood extents increase downstream of the Main Street culvert where the watercourse becomes open channel. Properties along Main Street experienced flooding during the December 2020 event, with reports that water rose through floors and that shallow groundwater/wells were present in rear gardens (south side of Main Street), suggesting both surface-water and groundwater pathways affected properties. (p23) [PAG]
* {2014}: During the 2014 intense hailstorm surface water flooding occurred on Main Street and West Well Lane, causing internal flooding of properties. (p24) [PAG, TAB]
* Manholes surcharged, causing water to flood onto the highway and into gardens. Sandbags were delivered to Main Street and an additional road closure at Main Street was requested by Thames Valley Police during the event. (p32) [TAB, PAG]
* Received delivered sandbags as part of the flood response, indicating direct exposure to flooding risk during the event. (p33)
* The 600mm culvert running under the entire length of Main Street was surcharged and its capacity exceeded. * Floodwaters pooled and flowed along Main Street after additional surface water from adjoining downhill roads (including West Well Lane) and after Townsend Pond overtopped. * The culverted watercourse beneath Main Street was found mostly in good condition but had a vegetation blockage (35PCSYM obstruction) at its downstream end discharging to the open watercourse; this partial blockage is likely to have slowed discharge and exacerbated flooding during the 23rd December 2020 event. ### West Well Lane, Tingewick
* {2014}: During the 2014 intense hailstorm surface water flooding occurred on Main Street and West Well Lane, causing internal flooding of properties. (p24) [PAG, TAB]
* Surface water flowed down West Well Lane during the event; the surface water pipe below West Well Lane had collapsed causing a blockage that contributed to local flooding and overland flow. (p34) [PAG, IMG]
* Initial reports (around 13:00 on 23 December 2020) describe flooding from manholes in rear gardens of properties at the western end of Main Street and West Well Lane due to private drainage backing up from the surcharged culvert and surface water sewer. A collapse and blockage of the surface water sewer on West Well Lane (just upstream of the junction with Main Street) exacerbated flooding, and the street's downhill slope sent additional surface water flows toward Main Street. (p35)
* The surface water sewer along West Well Lane had a significant partial obstruction (masonry bricks and root growth) at its downstream end where it connects into the Main Street culvert; this obstruction was included in the baseline model to replicate the conditions during the 23rd December 2020 flooding and would have restricted flow, contributing to flooding. The loose bricks and root growth were subsequently removed and the sewer confirmed structurally sound. (p39)
* {general}: A surface water sewer/culvert in West Well Lane was recorded with a Grade 5 obstruction comprising a pipe and cement washings that made the culvert inaccessible to the CCTV survey (survey abandoned beyond the obstruction). Brickwork rubble and root growth in the same surface water culvert were later removed, reducing the risk of backing up or causing flooding. (p40)
* {general}: The surface water culvert under West Well Lane had collapsed prior to the 23 December 2020 flood, resulting in backing up of water (surface water/sewer flooding). Anglian Water later restored the pipe and removed brickwork rubble and root growth (works carried out since January 2021), and the culvert was concluded to be structurally sound. (p41) [PAG]

(p50)

### Townsend Pond
* The embanked pond at the western end of the village overtopped on 23 December 2020 (figures in the report show flooding at 13:40 and further overtopping in the evening), discharging excess water via its outlet pipe into the downstream culvert and contributing to flooding in the village. (p15) [PAG, IMG]
* Townsend Pond overtopped (annotated as occurring at approximately 17:00 on 23 December 2020). The pond is embanked at its eastern end and discharges via a 300mm pipe into a 600mm culvert; the culvert was surcharged and its capacity exceeded during the event, contributing to downstream overland flow and flooding in the village. (p34) [PAG]
* Townsend Pond overtopped: water rose above the 300mm outlet pipe (visible by 13:40) and, by approximately 17:00, overtopped the overflow structure and the top of the embankment. The overtopping released additional water down Main Street, triggering a second rapid rise in water levels and causing internal flooding of nearby properties. Water levels fell rapidly later that night and flooding had dissipated by 02:00 on 24 December. (p36)
* Townsend Pond overtopped at approximately 17:00; water overtopped the overflow structure and the top of the embankment and additional water flowed down Main Street, coinciding with a second wave of rainfall and causing rapid rises in water levels that led to internal flooding of nearby properties. As pond levels fell below the embankment later in the event the culverted watercourse allowed the remaining floodwater on the road to drain relatively rapidly, with flooding largely dissipating by about 23:00 and no remaining flooding after 02:00 on 24 December. (p37) [IMG, PAG]
* A modelled scenario doubling the pond's storage capacity would not have prevented the culvert surcharging during the 23rd December 2020 event because flows from the west were too large. The pond could potentially overtop in extreme events and increased storage might worsen downstream impacts. Management changes (maintaining a clear small channel to the outlet pipe to reduce silt/debris) were agreed. (p44)
* During the 23 December 2020 event pond water rose above the 300mm outlet pipe and exceeded the overflow route into the 600mm culvert. This caused water to flow over the top of the embankment and down Main Street, contributing to downstream inundation. (p50)
