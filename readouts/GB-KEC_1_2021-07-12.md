### London Borough of Kensington and Chelsea
* Extreme rainfall on 12 July 2021 caused internal flooding across the borough, resulting in flooded properties and overland flows that overwhelmed local drainage. (p5)
* Internal flooding of properties within the borough. (p14)
* On 12 July 2021 the borough experienced flooding that led to properties being flooded and road closures. Residents submitted photographs and reports of flooded properties which were used to document impacts. (p16)
* Council officers undertook site visits to the worst-affected areas of the borough following flooding. (p18)
* During the intense rainfall event on 12 July 2021 the borough experienced combined sewer and surface water flooding. The combined sewer system reached capacity, causing sewers to surcharge and stormwater to overflow via manholes onto roads, with roads then conveying water downhill according to local topography. The report identifies sewer and surface water flooding as the main cause of flooding in the July 2021 event. (p22) [PAG, IMG]
* During the 12 July 2021 event, sewer and surface-water flooding occurred across the borough: combined sewers reached capacity and surcharged, causing overflows via manholes and overland flow along roads; sewer flow was pushed up into properties (including basement properties) through toilets, sinks and shower drains as sewers backed up. (p23)

* During the intense rainfall on 12 July 2021 the borough experienced widespread surface water ponding and sewer surcharging. Sewers and drains reached capacity causing surcharges through manholes onto roads (roads acted as conveyance pathways) and floodwater entering properties, with basement properties particularly affected by backflow through toilets, sinks and shower drains. (p27) [PAG, IMG]

* On 12 July 2021 the borough experienced surface-water and sewer (urban) flooding: sewers surcharged, causing sewage and floodwater to back up into basements and via shower/toilet drains, manholes/fountains formed where sewers were surcharged, roads conveyed floodwater, floodwater collected in basements and internal flooding damaged business stock (including flooding inside Sloane Square station). (p41) [PAG, IMG]
* {general}: The borough contains Critical Drainage Areas where surface water and sewer flooding interact, resulting in flooding impacts to streets and properties within the borough. (p45) [PAG]
* Intense convective rainfall on 12 July 2021 caused surface-water and sewer-related flooding across parts of the borough. The event prompted hydrological analysis (radar and gauge data) to quantify rainfall totals and understand the surface-water flooding response in the borough. (p47) [PAG, IMG]
* {general}: An intense band of rain produced very high localized rainfall over the borough between 15:00–18:00 on 12 July 2021. The extreme rainfall led to localized surface-water/urban flooding across parts of Kensington and Chelsea and overwhelmed drainage in central areas. (p52) [IMG, PAG]
* Intense convective rainfall on 12 July 2021 produced very high local rainfall totals across the borough (15:00–18:00), which led to surface water flooding across streets and public areas and caused sewer surcharge (fountains at manholes and internal sewer flooding) and internal flooding of properties and businesses. (p53) [PAG, IMG]
* Intense, short‑duration rainfall on 12 July 2021 (starting around 15:00) produced surface water/urban flooding across the borough. Areas experienced highly variable return periods (southern parts <1 in 2 years; locations of heaviest rainfall in the borough up to around a 1 in 185‑year storm). A high Thames tide at Chelsea (15:45, 3.80mAOD) coincided with the heavy rainfall on 12 July. (p55)
* Interceptor and local sewers surcharged, causing flooding at street level; tide-locking impeded sewer discharge and increased manhole depths; operational failures at pumping stations contributed to borough-wide flooding. (p60)
* Intense rainfall and surcharged sewers produced overland flow across the borough. Roads acted as channels conveying floodwater (examples given: A3220, A40, Portobello Road). Surcharging sewers caused fountains at manholes (photographed at the junction of St Marks Road and Lancaster Road, and at Cornwall Crescent). High urban and hydrological connectivity synchronised flows across streets in the Notting Hill area and adjacent neighbourhoods—Portobello Road, Arundel Gardens, Elgin Crescent, Blenheim Crescent, Lower Clarendon Walk, St Marks Road, Camelford Court, Clarendon Road—and a wider corridor including Royal Crescent, Holland Villas Road, Bramley Road, St Anns Road, Stoneleigh Street, St Anns Villas, St James's Gardens, Norland Square, Queensdale Road, Princedale Road, Holland Park Avenue, Addison Road, Warwick Road, Napier Road, Edwardes Square, Cope Place and Scarsdale Villas, all of which contributed to and experienced surface water/sewer-driven flooding on 12 July 2021. (p63) [PAG, IMG]
* Properties recorded as flooded; floodwater entered properties via backflow through property drains into basement flats (through toilets, sinks and drains). (p64) [TAB, PAG]
* Flooding across the borough affected properties and basements: surface water and sewage-contaminated water entered properties (including basements and bathrooms), causing internal flooding, sewage contamination and damage to fixtures, finishes and fittings on multiple streets. (p69) [PAG]
* Council-owned properties were internally flooded with sewage and overland floodwater; sewage staining required replacement of walls and flooring and posed health risks. The Council incurred substantial costs responding and repairing flood-damaged properties and providing temporary accommodation. (p70) [PAG]
* Within the Royal Borough of Kensington and Chelsea, 76 streets were reported to have flooded on 12 July 2021; properties were internally flooded (receptors), business stock was damaged (e.g. Portobello Road), and three primary schools (Colville Primary, Thomas Jones Primary, Avondale Park Primary) experienced internal flooding. The borough’s records note that many flooded streets had at least one FLIP installed yet some properties still flooded. (p73)
* Widespread intense rainfall caused surface water and combined sewer systems to exceed capacity across the borough, leading to sewers surcharging, manhole overflows and multiple basement and ground‑floor property inundations on several streets. (p79) [TAB]
* Widespread internal flooding across the borough during the intense rainfall on 12th July 2021. Multiple streets and properties experienced internal flooding; sewer surcharge and surface-water flow pathways contributed to internal property flooding and sewer-related issues. (p90) [PAG]
* Widespread flooding across the borough on 12 July 2021: 76 streets were reported to have flooded, with internal flooding of properties (including basements), sewer surcharging (manhole fountains and sewage entering buildings) and roads acting as channels conveying floodwater. Businesses suffered internal flooding and stock damage (for example reported at Portobello Road) and Sloane Square Station experienced internal flooding. Many flooded streets had FLIPs installed but some of those properties still experienced internal flooding. (p91) [PAG, IMG]
* The borough experienced intense surface-water/urban flooding on 12 July 2021 that triggered the Section 19 investigation and ongoing engagement with Thames Water over sewer and asset performance; RBKC has been actively engaged with Thames Water's independent review on behalf of residents. (p92)
* Surface water / urban flooding on 12 July 2021 produced very short warning lead times for the borough, affected properties and local emergency decision-making, and led to recommendations that the borough seeks funding for Property Flood Resilience (PFR) schemes, develops local surface-water forecasting, and coordinates with other Risk Management Authorities. (p95) [PAG]
* The borough collected reports of internal property flooding following the 12 July 2021 event and ran the Section 19 investigation questionnaire; over 300 responses were submitted to the RBKC questionnaire. Initial reports of flooding within the borough were received by local teams during the event. (p96)
* As the local authority, RBKC was required to update and consolidate three existing Multi-Agency Flood Plans into a single Multi-Agency Flood Plan, to review it annually and after events, and to introduce a training and exercising framework. RBKC is also tasked with supporting London-wide actions on rest centre standards and ensuring critical infrastructure sites (points of contact, contingency plans, protection resources and business continuity arrangements) are included in the MAFP. (p100)
