

# Surrey Heath flooding December 2013 - January 2014

## Place names with coordinates

### Surrey
* Widespread flooding across the county in winter 2013/14 included impacts within the Surrey Heath Borough area: seven sites across two sub-areas flooded, resulting in internal property flooding incidents. (p7) [PAG]
* Widespread flooding occurred across the county in winter 2013/14 from multiple sources including groundwater and surface-water runoff; prolonged rain on saturated ground led to widespread inundation and disruption. (p9) [TAB, PAG]
* Widespread flooding across Surrey resulted from a sequence of Atlantic storm events and prolonged rain on saturated ground. Peak flows from the 23 December storm caused River Wey flooding; lower reaches (Byfleet, Old Woking, Weybridge) reached their highest levels on Boxing Day. River levels in Guildford rose about 2.5m above normal winter levels. River levels rose again on New Year's Day and the saturated catchments led to flooding after subsequent smaller rainfall events. (p10) [PAG, MUL]
* Widespread flooding occurred across the county during winter 2013/14; strategic response and asset operation were carried out by the Environment Agency and other RMAs. (p14) [PAG]
* The county experienced flooding during Winter 2013/14 (Section 19 reports were produced for flooding across the county); roads, properties and drainage systems were affected, prompting inspections, repairs and clearance works. (p15) [PAG]
* Experienced raised river levels (including the River Thames and its tributaries) during prolonged heavy rainfall in autumn and winter 2013/14. (p21)
* Exceptional and unprecedented rainfall over Dec–Feb 2013/14 caused widespread flooding across Surrey; many districts and boroughs within the county were impacted to a greater extent than Surrey Heath. (p22) [PAG]
* Surrey experienced widespread historic flooding during the winter 2013/14 event. The report records that lack of maintenance of some riparian-owned ditches and watercourses contributed to the impact of the winter 2013/14 floods and that data from that event was used to identify areas most at risk and to prioritise drainage maintenance and flood defence bids. (p23) [PAG]
* Quant: 7 sites; 10 incidents of internal property flooding (p7) [PAG]
* Quant: 2.5m above normal winter levels in Guildford (p10) [PAG, MUL]
* Quant: 860 blockages and storm damage incidents cleared; 1087 pollution incidents reported; 125 Flood Ambassadors visits involved; 70 flood data recorders deployed to more than 100 locations. (p14) [PAG]

### Windle Brook
* Windle Brook (the Mill Bourne / Hale Bourne) is part of the Mill Bourne catchment draining the eastern part of Surrey Heath. During winter 2013/14 the catchment became saturated after repeated storm events (notably the 23 December 2013 event), reducing its capacity to absorb further rainfall and contributing to river-level rises and flooding in the eastern parts of the borough. (p10) [MUL]
* Windle Brook (named as part of the Mill Bourne system) was part of the Main River network and main surface water flow routes through the sub area during the winter 2013/14 events, contributing to surface water and fluvial flooding that led to road closures in Lightwater. (p20)
* {December 2013 - January 2014, 02 Feb 2014}: The Environment Agency issued Flood Alerts for Windle Brook (with Hale, Mill and Addlestone Bournes), indicating elevated river levels and flood risk in the Lightwater sub area. (p21) [TAB, MUL]

### River Wey
* After heavy rainfall the River Wey experienced peak flows that caused flooding along its course. The lower reaches (Byfleet, Old Woking and Weybridge) reached their highest levels on Boxing Day and river levels remained high thereafter, reducing catchment capacity and contributing to further flooding after subsequent rain. (p10) [PAG, MUL]
* Quant: 2.5m rise above normal winter levels (recorded at Guildford) (p10) [PAG, MUL]

### River Blackwater
* River levels on the Blackwater rose during the late-December/early-January period of winter 2013/14 (water levels rose again on New Year’s Day), contributing to flooding in the borough’s western catchment and reducing the catchment’s capacity to absorb further rainfall so that subsequent smaller events also caused flooding. (p10) [PAG, MUL]
* Water levels along the Blackwater River rose on New Year's day (following the intense rainfall and earlier peaks over the Christmas period), contributing to high river levels and reduced catchment capacity that increased likelihood of flooding after subsequent small rainfall events. (p11)

### Hale Bourne
* The Hale Bourne (alternative name for the Mill Bourne) formed part of the main river network through Lightwater that acted as a surface water and fluvial flow route during the winter 2013/14 floods; this contributed to flooding in the sub area and associated road closures. (p20)


## Topics without coordinates

### Surrey County Council
* Internal property flooding reported at multiple addresses across the borough; roads were closed due to the flooding. (p2) [TAB]
* Acted as the Lead Local Flood Authority and Highway Authority, carrying out strategic flood risk management functions before, during and after the winter 2013/14 floods affecting Surrey Heath; managed surface-water and highway drainage responsibilities for the affected area. (p13) [TAB]
* {December 2013 - January 2014, 01 Feb 2014 - 28 Feb 2014}: As LLFA and Highways Authority SCC responded to the Winter 2013/14 floods by inspecting flood-affected roads and commissioning defect repairs via contractors (Kier); assessing damaged carriageways for the Project 400 repair programme; cleansing and re-opening roads; providing and recycling sandbags; clearing trees and debris and carrying out ditching works to restore drainage function; operating a call centre for residents; administering Repair and Renew grants (up to £5,000) for flooded residents and businesses; and participating in strategic coordination ahead of the February 2014 event. Surrey Fire and Rescue Service pumped flood waters to protect people and infrastructure. (p15) [MUL]
* Identified as the responsible Risk Management Authority acting as both Lead Local Flood Authority and the Highway Authority and undertook actions prior to and during the winter 2013/14 flooding that caused internal property flooding and affected roads and properties in the Camberley sub area (including Robins Bow, Middleton Road, the Maultway and a short section of London Road). (p18)
* As LLFA and Highway Authority, carried out temporary road closures during the winter 2013/14 flooding, implementing transport disruption measures on affected roads. (p21)

### Thames Water
* Internal property flooding reported at multiple addresses in Camberley; associated road closures occurred. (p2) [TAB]
* Named as the water company responsible for sewer flooding and described as carrying out flood risk management functions during the winter 2013/14 floods affecting Surrey Heath, managing sewer-flooding response and related drainage issues. (p13) [TAB]
* During the Winter 2013/14 floods TW protected and maintained water and wastewater service assets: deployed flood barriers and sandbags to TW sites; carried out regular (often daily) checks of unmanned sites; optimised sewerage network operations (investigations and sewer cleaning) and increased engineers on the ground to investigate flooding reports. Network Engineers visited internally flooded properties where sewer flooding was the primary cause. TW also undertook wide-scale clean-ups of flooded properties (whether foul/sewer or river flooding), provided sewer-flooding information leaflets and supported Affinity Water with risk assessments and contingency planning for sites at risk of inundation. (p15) [PAG]

### West Thames
* Prolonged and repeated Atlantic storm rainfall across the West Thames Region produced high river flows and widespread flooding in parts of the region (notably in Surrey). The cumulative effects of saturated catchments reduced absorption capacity so subsequent smaller rainfall events caused flooding. (p10) [IMG, MUL]

### Blackwater Catchment
* Water levels on the Blackwater River rose during the winter 2013/14 storm period (levels rose again on New Year's Day 2014), contributing to sustained high river levels across the catchment and reducing the catchment's ability to absorb further rainfall, which led to flooding after subsequent smaller rainfall events. (p10) [MUL]

### Surrey Fire and Rescue Service
* {December 2013 - January 2014, 01 Feb 2014 - 28 Feb 2014}: Pumped flood waters away to protect residents, property and infrastructure during the winter 2013/14 flooding. (p15) [MUL]

### Mill Bourne Catchment
* The Mill Bourne (main river through the sub area) and its catchment acted as a main flow route during the winter 2013/14 floods; flooding in the catchment contributed to surface water and fluvial flooding in the Lightwater sub area which resulted in a number of road closures. (p20)

### Windlesham Ditch
* The Windlesham Ditch, a Main River draining areas to the north of the sub area and joining the Mill Bourne, formed part of the main surface water flow routes during the winter 2013/14 floods and contributed to surface water/fluvial flooding in the Lightwater area. (p20)

### Lightwater Stream
* The Lightwater Stream, classified as a Main River draining the south of the sub area and joining the Mill Bourne, was a main surface water flow route during the winter 2013/14 flooding and contributed to surface water and fluvial flooding impacts in the sub area. (p20)
