### Kingston Upon Hull
* City‑wide flash flooding in August 2012 caused extensive urban street flooding across Hull. Roads had standing water deep enough to force vehicles to drive through floodwater and left parked cars partially submerged; floodwater contained foam and debris and disrupted transport and streetscape. (p1) [PAG]
* Widespread highway flooding across the city and surface water inundation; drainage infrastructure was overwhelmed in many areas leading to flooding before drains could convey flows. (p4)
* Multiple locations across Hull were flooded on 25th August 2012 (Table 1 lists numerous street-level incidents and the map shows a cluster of flooded points within the urban area). (p6) [PAG]
* Multiple streets within the city were flooded on 25th August 2012. Flooding affected Stockholm Road (full length), Copenhagen Road, Sutton Road, Garrick Close, Corona Drive, Ullswater Close, Lowgate, Malham Avenue, Kendal Way, Hawkshead Green, Foster Street, Princes Avenue and Calvert Lane. Flooding was associated with the local drainage system being overwhelmed, producing surface-water/urban flood impacts on the streets listed. (p7) [IMG, PAG]
* {2007, 25 Aug 2012}: Widespread flooding across the city. In 2007 drains, ditches and watercourses were overwhelmed causing flooding across large areas of Hull; flood flows exceeded drainage capacity. On 25th August 2012 an intense, localised storm caused surface water flooding concentrated in a central belt of the city, with roads affected, sandbagging and pump operations deployed by council and fire crews. (p8) [PAG, MUL]
* Intense, highly localised rainfall on 25th August 2012 caused surface water (pluvial) flooding across Hull; residents and operating authorities were unprepared, prompting investigations and planned remediation measures. (p17) [TAB]
* Quant: 21 properties (p4)
* {2007, 25 Aug 2012}: Quant: more than 1300 businesses; 8600 residential properties; 19 residential properties; 2 commercial properties (p8) [PAG, MUL]

## Topics without coordinates

### Yorkshire Water
* Identified as a main operating authority involved in the surface water event; carried out investigations and implemented works to improve the drainage network; call-handling improvements were identified and actions taken. (p4)
* Operated pumping infrastructure during the intense rainfall to manage high sewer flows; telemetry shows pumps at West, East and Bransholme were run. Undertook investigations after the event and identified de-silting, blockage removal and jetting works (majority completed) to restore and improve conveyance. (p11)
* Carried out inspections and remedial sewer works across the investigation area after the 25 August 2012 flood: visual inspections, CCTV surveys, de-silting, blockage removal, jetting and flushing to restore flows; identified a broken combined-pipe on Fortune Close to be repaired and reported blocked private gullies for resident action. (p12) [TAB, PAG]
* Carried out visual inspections and CCTV on multiple surface water and combined sewers in the Hull area; removed silt and soft blockages in Nidderdale and Cotterdale sewers; removed a large metal tube and tree roots from a surface water sewer by man-entry and jetting; flushed combined sewer at junction with Stoneferry Road as a precaution; CCTV revealed a broken pipe in a combined sewer on Fortune Close (dig required); identified and reported a blocked private gully to a resident; repeated jetting removed fat blockages and cleaning work ongoing. (p13) [PAG]
* Operated pumped drainage and sewer infrastructure during the event; systems worked as designed but had limited effectiveness due to isolated, intense rainfall and became surcharged. Carried out investigations and remedial works in response to a Section 19 request; where defects or blockages were found they were not considered solely responsible for flooding. (p15)
* Yorkshire Water Services is identified as an operating authority involved in investigations after the intense/pluvial rainfall on 25th August 2012; investigations led to remediation measures and planned interventions, and Yorkshire Water was recommended to resolve problems (notably at Hawkshead Green) and improve customer contact procedures. (p17) [TAB, PAG]
* Quant: 1 broken pipe; 1 large metal tube; 1 blocked private gully; 1 gully reported to resident (p13) [PAG]

### Hull City Council
* Acted as Lead Local Flood Authority and responder to the surface water flooding event; call-handling procedure implemented that weekend caused problems that led to protracted responses and was investigated and addressed. (p4)
* Investigated the pluvial (surface water) flooding across the city following the 25 August 2012 event, identified locations for road/public‑realm modifications (including Noddle Hill Way and Cotterdale), provided a list of required investigations to Yorkshire Water and coordinated remedial works (de‑silting, blockage removal and jetting) which were largely completed. (p11) [PAG]
* Hull City Council carried out investigations and is developing capital schemes to manage identified surface water flood flow routes (drainage, storage and new sewer connections) and working with residents to rectify blocked gullies. (p12) [TAB, PAG]
* As Lead Local Flood Authority, Hull City Council received advance warnings for 25th August 2012, investigated flood routes reported by residents, worked with residents to rectify blocked gullies, and is developing a capital scheme (drainage, storage and a new connection to the surface water sewer) to manage an identified flood flow route along the footpath network. (p13)
* Managed local response and made a formal Section 19 request leading to investigations and remedial works. The council manages the non-arterial highway network where flooding occurred and maintained regular contact with residents affected (supported further Yorkshire Water work in Hawkshead Green). (p15)

### Highways Authority
* The intense storm on 25 August overwhelmed many roadside drains and gullies on non-arterial roads managed by the council; the storm exceeded the design standards of much highway drainage infrastructure. Investigations showed the vast majority of gullies were clear, indicating storm intensity rather than routine blockages was the key cause. (p15)
