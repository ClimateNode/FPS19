

# Arnold flooding 12 Jun 2019

## Place names with coordinates

### Arnold
* Following heavy rainfall on the evening of 12 June 2019, parts of Arnold were affected by surface water/urban flooding. Internal flooding occurred to residential and commercial properties and many gardens and outbuildings were flooded. Specific streets affected include Hall Drive, Brook Gardens, Bentwell Avenue, Bonnington Drive, Brookfield Road, Gedling Road, Front Street, Market Place and Arnot Hill Road. (p1) [PAG]
* Significant internal flooding of homes and businesses across the Arnold area caused by overland flow from a failed storage attenuation area. The attenuator was full following heavy rain and its bank breached, allowing excess surface water to flow along a natural valley (rear gardens of Bentwell Avenue) and cause extensive damage. (p3) [PAG, MUL]
* {1998, 1999, 2006, 2010, 2013}: The area of Arnold has experienced flooding following the line of the historic watercourse now occupied by streets such as Bentwell Avenue, Brook Gardens and Brookfield Road. Predicted surface-water flow routes (Environment Agency surface water map) follow this route, indicating those residential streets are at risk and have been affected during heavy rain. (p4) [IMG, MUL]
* Quant: 24 residential properties; 20 businesses (p1) [PAG]
* Quant: 24 residential properties; 20 businesses (p3) [PAG, MUL]

### Brook Gardens, Arnold
* Properties on Brook Gardens experienced internal flooding; gardens and outbuildings were also flooded. (p1) [PAG]
* Map annotations and narrative show the storage attenuation area is located between Malin Close (NE) and Brook Gardens (SW) and that the bank broke with flow directed to the southwest. This indicates overland flow from the breach moved toward Brook Gardens, inundating nearby gardens and contributing to local property flooding in the area. (p3) [PAG]
* {1998, 1999, 2006, 2010, 2013}: Brook Gardens is located on the historic watercourse line now overlain by housing; the area has flooded along this route in past events and is shown on the EA surface water map as a predicted flow route during heavy rain. (p4) [MUL]
* Floodwater from a breached storage/attenuation area flowed along the route, channeling through rear gardens and entering some houses on Brook Gardens, causing internal and external flooding. (p5)
* Quant: 24 residential properties; 20 businesses (p1) [PAG]
* Quant: up to 4ft depth (p5)

### Bentwell Avenue, Arnold
* Properties on Bentwell Avenue experienced internal flooding; gardens and outbuildings were also flooded. (p1) [PAG]
* {12 Jun 2026}: When the storage attenuation area breached on the evening of 12 June, excess surface water ran overland and followed a natural valley along the rear gardens of Bentwell Avenue, channeling downstream and causing extensive damage along that path. (p3) [PAG]
* {1998, 1999, 2006, 2010, 2013}: Surface water floods follow the line of the historic watercourse now occupied by Bentwell Avenue. The street and adjacent residential areas have flooded during heavy rainfall when predicted flow routes follow the historic channel. (p4) [IMG, MUL]
* The flow route followed the line of the historical watercourse now Bentwell Avenue; properties along Bentwell Avenue experienced concentrated internal and external flooding as floodwater moved westward along the avenue, with water channeling through gardens into houses. (p5) [IMG, PAG]

### Market Place, Arnold
* Internal flooding to properties and businesses in the area as part of the wider Arnold event; associated flooding to gardens and outbuildings. (p1) [PAG]
* Floodwater collected (ponded) at the Market Place, where flows from Front Street combined with other runoff, causing flooding that affected businesses in the area. (p6) [PAG]
* A contiguous area of internal and external flooding affected the streets and properties along Upper Front Street, through the Market Place and on to Lower Front Street. Floodwater was channeled from Upper Front Street into the Market Place where it combined with water from Front Street, increasing the extent of the flooded area. (p7) [IMG]
* Identified as an area where NCC and STW will investigate how the highway drainage system worked with the public sewer system following the flood event, implying it was affected by the flooding. (p9)
* Quant: 24 residential properties; 20 businesses (across the listed areas) (p1) [PAG]

## Topics without coordinates

### Day Brook Catchment
* During periods of heavy rain the catchment produced surface water flow routes that followed the line of the historical watercourse (now Bentwell Avenue), contributing to flooding of the adjacent residential area. (p5)
* {2007, 12 Jun 2019}: A flooding event affected the Day Brook catchment. The operation of a pond within the catchment during the flood is reported as requiring joint investigation by GBC and STW. Historical flooding (2007) led STW to install a foul water tank to reduce mixing of foul and surface water; during this (DOC EVENT DATE) event there was no evidence of foul water mixing, indicating the tank worked effectively. Emergency response support was provided in the management of the flooding event. (p9) [MUL]

### Nottinghamshire County Council
* Initiated and led the S.19 Flood Investigation and emergency planning procedures; provided a highways response team to close Front Street during the flood and to investigate highway drainage issues; continues to work with STW to survey the wider Day Brook catchment. (p8)
* {general}: Secured funding (£99k Local Levy) for a study into the Upper Daybrook surface water assets and is involved in planned joint investigations (with STW) into how highway drainage interacts with the public sewer system in affected areas following the flood event. (p9)

### Nottinghamshire Fire and Rescue Service
* {general}: Described flood-response roles include saving life (with ambulance and police), implementing water rescue operations, conducting search-and-rescue, pumping floodwater (using high-volume pumps) and carrying out salvage/essential damage control operations. (p8)

### East Midlands Ambulance Service
* {general}: Works in conjunction with the fire service and police to save life during flood incidents (providing emergency medical response and casualty care). (p8)

### Severn Trent
* {2007, 12 Jun 2019}: Provided emergency response support managing the flooding event; inspected the condition of the pond immediately after the event; actively engaged in the Section 19 Flood Investigation. STW's previously installed foul water tank (installed after the 2007 flood) prevented foul water mixing with surface water during this event. (p9) [MUL]

### Gedling Borough Council
* {general}: Participated in the study into the catchment's surface water assets and will carry out a joint investigation with STW into how the pond operated during the flood event; will input to partnership work on the wider Day Brook catchment to address flooding. (p9)
